# 1.vision.yield — the self-review gate stops to charge the driver for good work

## 🔴 .the one-line answer

**the timer was never the defect. the hash key was — and the clock goes anyway.**

the self-review trigger report is keyed `(stone, slug, hash)`. the promise it produces is keyed
`(stone, slug)` — hashless, *"firm checkpoints that don't invalidate"* (`getStonePromises.ts:12`).
**that asymmetry is the whole harm.** drop the hash from the key and four separate complaints close
at once.

🔴 **and then the clock is re-purposed, which is a SECOND and separable call.** the *"what's the
rush?"* prompt is a **message**; the clock was only the trigger that decided when to show it. `S11`
dropped the trigger, and `S13` restored it on a narrower job — so the prompt now reaches **only** a
promise that lands inside 30s of the ask, and it holds **no one** for any duration.

⇒ **so the round ships two independent changes to one gate, and a reader must not fuse them:**

| the change | what it repairs | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **the hash leaves the key** | the reset, the unreachable hatch, the serial ladder, the blocked fork — one defect seen four ways | 🔴 the trigger's **invalidation**, which `S10` hands to the rewind |
| 🔴 **the clock leaves the GATE and becomes a CUE** | the wish's title and requirement 1, restored — and the confrontation stays **targeted** | a driver who waits idle presents as thorough (`case=2 [t3]`) |

🔴 **the second row is a THIRD state, and the round passed through the middle one.** the arc is kept
visible because a reviewer with a mid-round read holds a claim this yield no longer makes:

| | what the clock costs the driver it fires on | how often | who waits | can it part the thorough driver from the hasty one? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B** — as built | a **wait**, then a re-run | every fresh hash — unbounded | 🔴 everyone who arrives early | ✅ yes |
| `S11` alone | naught — no elapsed read at all | never | ✅ no one | 🔴 **no.** `case=1` and `case=2` merged |
| ✅ 🔴 **`S13`** — `attempts == 0 ∧ elapsed < 30s` | 🔴 **one command** — the promise IS refused, and a paragraph renders | **at most once per slug** | ✅ **no one** | ✅ **yes.** the merge is un-done |

⇒ **`a clock that costs a command is not a clock that costs a wait.`** that one line is the round's
final answer on the timer, and it is stated in the cost rather than in the verdict on purpose:
`challenge:rushed` returns `challenged: true`, the cli turns that into exit 2, **so the promise is
refused and the driver re-runs.** what it never does is hold them for a duration.

⚠️ `0.wish.md`'s `.what is NOT wanted` has no word for this — it forbids *"a cleverer clock"* — so a
reader must be pointed at the wait-vs-command split rather than left to find it. ⇒ and the split is
bounded on both sides: **at most once per slug, only inside the window of the ask, one command, no
wait.** a reader who takes *"it only speaks"* from this section has taken the wrong half.

## .the outcome world

### a day in the life

a driver is asked for eight self reviews on a blueprint. the guard hands them **all eight** — eight
slugs, eight articulation paths, eight triggers minted together. they fork eight lanes.

three lanes find defects and repair the blueprint. all eight write what they found and promise, each
with `--into` that states the path they wrote to. **every one clears on its first command. no prompt,
no wait, and the three repairs cost naught** — they promised forty minutes after the ask, and the
confrontation reaches only a promise inside 30s of it.

🔴 **and the load-carrying moment of that paragraph is one the prose passes over.** lane 3 writes to
the blueprint at T+5min, while lanes 1, 2 and 4–8 are **still open** — asked, unpromised, mid-read.
today that write re-keys all seven of their trigger reports and they are confronted for work they did
not do. after the change **lane 3 is invisible to them**: each finds the report minted at T+0, byte
for byte. ⇒ that is the property the fork is made of, and it is asserted in `case=8 [t4]` — *the one
step no sequential run can express* (`S16`).

another driver, on another stone, promises eight seconds after the ask with a one-line `looks good`.
**they** get `patience, friend` — the identity reframe, the four when-then cues, the exact path. they
read the cues, recognize that `looks good` answers none of them, go back and actually read, then
promise again. it clears.

⇒ 🔴 **the gate confronted the second driver and not the first, and it held neither.** that
conjunction is the whole shape of this round's answer, and it is the property no option the drive
tabled had: `B` bought the target condition with a wait, and an unbounded `E1` bought the absence of
a wait by a confrontation aimed at everyone.

### the "aha"

> **`hashbar: 0` was never a config. it was a bug report, written in yaml.**

every author who set it had hit this defect and routed around it. the knob is what let the defect
survive — it handed its victims a place to hide it, so the signal never reached the repo.

⇒ and the second aha, which is the wisher's and is sharper than the wish's own frame: the gate
**discourages yield updates**, and it does so **invisibly**. it did not merely delay a promise — it
taught the driver not to improve the deliverable, and gave no clue why.

## .mental model

### the analogy that fits

**a street meter that resets every time you wash your car.** the meter is fine; its trigger is
