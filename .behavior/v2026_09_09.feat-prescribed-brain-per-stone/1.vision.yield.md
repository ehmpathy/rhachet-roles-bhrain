# 1.vision.yield — a prescribed brain per stone

a `.guard` declares `brain: <the brain-cli's own /model argument>`. on stone entry the driver-role
hook applies it into the live driver clone. no manual `/model`.

🟡 **the value's vocabulary was ruled at the 2026-09-10 council** (`F10`) — it is what `/model`
accepts, passed through verbatim, never a rhachet brainslug. *"for now"*, in the wisher's own words.

⇒ seed: `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain#377`, archived at `.seeds/inventory.of=seeds.case=S1-*`.

## .the outcome world

### a day in the life

a route runs fourteen stones. **the hard ones are at the FRONT — vision and blueprint. every stone
from the roadmap onward is mechanical.**

⇒ 🔴 **vision and blueprint are where every decision is decided.** execution stamps what the
blueprint already settled; verification checks what the criteria already named. neither judges.

today the driver runs one brain for all fourteen, chosen by whoever launched the drive, before
anyone had read the work.

after: the drive opens on opus for vision and blueprint, drops to sonnet at the roadmap, and **stays
there for the rest of the route**. the driver never types `/model`, and never has to remember to.

### 🔴 the shape of the route decides how this pays — and it sharpens `F6`

the hard stones are **front-loaded and contiguous**, never scattered. that is no detail of the
narrative; it decides how the feature pays and how it leaks:

| | the consequence |
|---|---|
| the expensive region is **short and early** | one lift, one drop. the switch count is low, so the mechanism's own cost is near zero |
| the mechanical region is **long and terminal** | 🔴 **the majority of every route**, and where the whole economy lives |
| a brain is **sticky** (`F6`) | ⚠️ **so an opus set at the blueprint bills every stone after it** — which is most of the route, never a fraction of it |

⇒ 🔴 **the leak is worse than `F6`'s file states, and for a structural reason.** `F6` reads it as
*"an expensive brain set on one hard stone bills every routine stone that follows"* — true, and it
sounds like a tail. **it is not a tail. it is the body of the route**, because the hard region ends
early by construction.

⚠️ **that inverts which half of this feature carries the value.** the lift to opus reads as the point;
the **drop back at the roadmap** is where the money is — and that half depends on bhuild's templates,
which must stamp a cheap brain on the mechanical stones rather than only a rich one on the hard ones.

### before / after

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| who decides the brain | whoever launched the drive | whoever wrote the guard, once, for every drive that walks this route |
| when | before any work was read | per stone, at the moment the stone is entered |
| how it is applied | a human types `/model` and hopes they remembered | the hook applies it, and reports what it applied |
| what a cost claim rests on | a bill, and a memory | a record that names the stone and the brain |

### the aha

> **the brain is a property of the work, not of the session.**

a stone is worth what it is worth. that judgment is reviewable and belongs in the route — not in a
decision made once at launch and drifted for four hours.

### 🔴 the aha overclaims by one word — it is the STONE, not the WORK

corrected at self-review r3. the line above once read *"that judgment is **stable**"*, and that word
carried an assumption nobody had stated:

> **a guard is a property of the route TEMPLATE. it is reused by every wish that walks the route.**

⇒ so a trivial wish's blueprint stone and a gnarly wish's blueprint stone **share one `brain:`**. the
field captures how hard that **stone** usually is, never how hard **this drive's passage** through it
turns out to be.

| the claim | true? |
|---|---|
| better than a per-**session** choice | ✅ yes, and that is the whole win — a stone's typical weight beats a guess made before any work was read |
| a property of the **work** | 🔴 **no.** it is a property of the **stone**, averaged over every wish that walks it |

🟡 **the distinction the word `stage` was reached for is real, and the glossary already holds both
halves:** a **stone** is the slot in the route template; a **passage** is one drive's transit of it.
⇒ no third word is owed, and the third word was the defect.

⚠️ **the overclaim is not cosmetic — it hides a per-drive override question.** if difficulty varies by
wish, someone will want to raise one stone's brain for one hard wish, and the design as stated offers
no surface for it. **that gap is invisible while the aha says "the work."**

⇒ **not a fulcrum, and deliberately so.** an override is a scope addition the wish did not ask for and
the issue does not raise. it is named as a **cost of the design** here rather than reserved as a
choice — a wisher who wants it wants a second feature, and should know that before the blueprint
stone rather than after.

## .evaluation

### 🔴 the size of the mechanism — stated, because ten cases do not imply a large change

this vision demoes 10 experiences and raises 14 fulcrums. **the mechanism they describe is small:**

| the change | where |
|---|---|
| one optional key on a parsed object | `RouteStoneGuard` — a fifth field beside `path`, `artifacts`, `reviews`, `judges`, `protect` |
| one branch in the parser | `parseStoneGuard.ts` — ⚠️ **not a fifth literal**, see the correction below |
| one branch in a driver hook | 🔴 absent key → return; present key → **dispatch, unconditionally** (`F14`) |

⇒ **the case count is a property of the SURFACE, not of the diff.** the surface mutates a live
session, so it has many edges; the code that touches it is one field and two branches.

🔴 **the hook row shrank on 2026-09-11 and is now the smallest of the three.** the r1 design read
*"compare, and dispatch on a mismatch"*, which carried a live-brain read, a value-form bridge, and
two failure modes behind it. `F14` deleted all of it: **one key check, one dispatch, and no belief
about the clone's current state.**

### 🔴 the parser row was WRONG, and the correction is a real blueprint constraint

corrected at self-review r5, from a line-by-line read rather than a summary one.

**measured — `parseStoneGuard.ts:199-222`.** every top-level key is matched by **exact equality on a
bare header**, with its value on the lines that follow:

```ts
if (trimmed === 'artifacts:') { currentKey = 'artifacts'; … continue; }
if (trimmed === 'reviews:')   { … }
if (trimmed === 'judges:')    { … }
if (trimmed === 'protect:')   { … }
```

⇒ 🔴 **so `brain: opus` matches none of them.** `trimmed` is `'brain: opus'`, never `'brain:'`, and
an exact-equality branch cannot see it. **a fifth literal in that list would parse `brain:` bare and
silently drop its value** — the same failhide shape `case=4` is about, arrived at from the other side.

| the extant shape | what `brain:` needs |
|---|---|
| a bare `key:` header, then `- ` list items beneath it | 🔴 **an inline `key: value` on one line** |
| exact-equality match | a **prefix** match plus a value extract |

⚠️ **no top-level key in the guard format carries an inline value today.** inline `key: value` parses
exist (`slug:`, `run:` — `:258+`), and **only nested inside a `- ` peer-review item**, never at the
top level.

⇒ **the change is still small — but it is a new branch SHAPE, not a fifth entry in an extant list.**
that distinction is what a blueprint stone needs and what a summary-grain read cannot supply.

🟡 a reader who sizes the work from the artifact count will over-price it, and a reader who sizes it
from the diff will under-scrutinize it. **both numbers are real, and they measure different things.**

### 🔴 why the key lives in the `.guard` and not the `.stone`

> 🔴 **a guard guards the BUDGET a stone may spend — that is its subject, beside the schema. and a
> brain IS spend.** so `brain:` is not merely *hosted* by the guard; it belongs to what a guard is
> already for.

⇒ **the key joins the guard's own concern rather than borrows its file.** that is the decisive
argument, and every mechanical one below is secondary to it.

the guard already bounds spend in exactly this vocabulary: `route.guard.budget` meters **review
rounds per stone**, and each peer review's `run:` line carries its own `--brain <slug>` (`case=9`).
⇒ **a per-stone brain is the same act one level up** — it bounds what the *driver* costs where
`budget` bounds what the *reviewers* cost.

🟡 **the two compose into one honest account of a stone's price**: `budget` caps how many rounds are
bought, `--brain` prices each reviewer's round, and `brain:` prices the driver's turns. the guard is
the one file where all three sit together.

#### the mechanical argument, which is secondary and still holds

a reviewer may still ask why not the stone, since a brain prescription reads as an **execution**
property against the guard's **validation** framing:

> **a `.stone` has no schema.** `RouteStone` carries `name`, `path`, `guard`
> (`src/domain.objects/Driver/RouteStone.ts:9-26`), and the `.stone` file itself is free prose read
> whole and emitted — measured: `route.peer.budget/1.vision.stone` is the single line
> `declare the vision for this feature`.

⇒ to put `brain:` on the stone is to invent a stone parser, and it would put a machine-read key into
a file whose whole contract is that a human wrote prose in it.

🔴 **but note what that argument alone would have said: *"the guard is the only structured surface a
stone has."*** true, and it prices the guard as a **convenient host** — which invites a reviewer to
ask what happens once a stone does grow a schema. **the budget argument does not have that weakness**,
because it names *why the guard is the right home* rather than *why the stone is not*.

⚠️ **and that is a distillation defect worth naming, since this yield committed it.** the first draft
reached the mechanism and stopped, one layer short of the motive that was available
(`rule.forbid.surface-speak`). ⇒ **a mechanism argument answers "how", survives review, and leaves
the design unjustified** — the exact failure a drill to the root exists to catch.

### against the goals

| the issue's goal | met | how |
|---|---|---|
| a stone declares its brain | ✅ | a top-level `brain:` key |
| applied automatically, not by hand | ✅ | the driver-role hook, at stone entry |
| applied **reliably** | ⚠️ | reliably **or loudly**. 🔴 **re-graded 2026-09-10** — a refused slug IS detectable today: `rhx clone get` reads the brain's own refusal from its transcript. `F5` buys the **silent**-refusal corner only |
| cheap brains on routine stones | ⚠️ | needs bhuild's templates to stamp the cheap brain too (`case=7`) |

### 🔴 what continues if this is never built — and why that answer is self-sealing

a vision owes the do-not-build baseline, and this is it:

| what continues | and |
|---|---|
| the brain is chosen once, at launch, before any work was read | no mechanism corrects it, so it drifts for the whole drive |
| the guard author knows which stones are hard | that knowledge has **nowhere to live**, so it is re-derived by whoever launches next, or lost |
| a human who remembers types `/model` | the ones who forget are the norm, and a forgotten switch leaves no trace |

⇒ 🔴 **the sharp part is the last column of row 3: the cost is UNMEASURABLE today.** no record says
which brain ran which stone, so the spend cannot be attributed, so it cannot be shown to be large,
**so the fix never gets prioritized.**

> **a defect that hides its own cost is a defect that argues for its own deferral, forever.**

⇒ that reframes what is most valuable here. it is tempting to read `case=8`'s one output line as the
cosmetic part of the feature and the switch as the substance. **it is the reverse:** the switch saves
money on the drives that adopt it, and the **record** is what lets anyone prove the saving was real —
which is the only argument that gets the rest adopted.

🟡 and it is why `F5` matters more than its "downgrade, not a stall" grade suggests. a refusal leaves
the feature shippable and weakens the cost claim, which is the state of affairs this row describes.
**the mechanism ships either way.**

🔴 **corrected 2026-09-10 — this read *"leaves the cost claim a request log"*, and that overstated it
by two grades.** `rhx clone get` reads the brain's own reply, so a refused switch is caught without
`F5`; the claim drops to *verifiable-to-the-reply* rather than to a log of what was asked for. ⇒ the
three-grade table is in `case=8`'s own re-grade.

### pros

- **the decision is made once, by the person best placed to make it**, and reused by every drive
- **it is declarative** — a guard is readable, diffable, and reviewable; a `/model` typed at 2am is none of those
- **it composes with the extant precedent** — the guard already prescribes a model per L3 review. this is the same idea one level up
- **it costs naught when unused** — a guard with no `brain:` behaves exactly as today

### cons

- **it mutates a live session in place**, which is a fundamentally less safe surface than a fresh spawn. every sharp path here descends from that
- **the applier and the applied are the same process** (`case=5`) — a re-entrancy the extant `clone say` model does not anticipate
- 🔴 ~~**it depends on a surface that does not exist** (`F5`), and on another repo to build it~~ — **struck 2026-09-10.** it depends on a read of the brain's **reply**, and `rhx clone get` ships that today. what does not exist is a read of the brain's **state**, and that buys one corner: a **silent** refusal
- **stickiness leaks cost** (`case=7`), and the containment lives in a third repo's templates

### the edge cases, and the pit of success

| edge | today | the contract that keeps a human in the pit |
|---|---|---|
| driver has no clone address | the common case | `whoami` **first**, halt, name the `rhx enroll` fix (`case=2`) |
| the brain refuses the slug | `delivered: true`, and a lie | compare live against declared; the **mismatch** decides, never the exit code (`case=3`) |
| the field name is misspelled | dropped in silence, forever | 🔴 a **near-miss warn** + the `model:` alias — the key set stays open, per the `F4` ruling (`case=4`, `F2`) |
| the hook says into itself | deadlock, read as a slow hook | hand off, never wait (`case=5`) |
| the brain is already right | — | 🔴 ~~compare before dispatch~~ → **set unconditionally; the applier holds no belief** (`F14`). the record still reports what happened (`case=6` `[t3]`) |
| a later stone declares none | — | sticky, and attributed to the stone that set it (`case=7`) |
| a guard already declares reviewer brains | — | `brain:` bounds the **driver alone**; `--brain` flags untouched (`case=9`, `F12`) |
| a stone declares no brain at all | — | 🔴 the key check runs **first**; an absent key returns before any other check (`case=10`) |

⇒ **the through-line: every fix here converts a silent outcome into a loud one.** that is the whole
safety story, and `rule.require.safe-by-default` is what it serves.

### 🔴 the one bound that must be READ narrowly, or it breaks every extant route

the wish states: *"fail loud, never a silent no-op."* taken at face value that binds every stone
entry — and `case=10` shows it must not.

| the stone | what the bound requires |
|---|---|
| **declared** a brain, and it could not be applied | 🔴 fail loud. this is the wish's bound, and it holds |
| declared **no** brain | ✅ **be entirely silent** — no line, no warn, no subprocess, no turn |

⇒ **the bound is about a switch that was ASKED for and did not happen.** it is not a bound on stone
entry, and a build that reads it as one adds an output line to every route in every repo and a
`whoami` rejection to every unenrolled drive. `case=10`'s `[t0]` is where the narrow read is fixed.

### 🔴 the requirement the wish settled, and what it cost

the cons above name in-place mutation as the source of every sharp path here. that is the honest
diagnosis, and it leaves one question unasked: **why mutate at all?**

`enroll --brain <cli>` spawns a clone with a brain already set. a per-stone brain could be a
per-stone **clone** — which is the "build matrix" model, and it has none of this space's sharp edges:
no re-entrancy (`case=5`), no submit-versus-accept gap (`case=3`), no live-brain surface to ask
rhachet for (`F5`), no stickiness (`case=7`).

**it is not taken, and the reason is decisive rather than a preference:**

| | mutate in place | spawn per stone |
|---|---|---|
| the drive's context | ✅ **continuous** — the driver carries what it learned at stone N into stone N+1 | 🔴 **discarded at every stone.** a route is one thread of comprehension; a fresh clone starts from the readme |
| what the wish asked for | ✅ the specified sequence, verbatim | 🔴 a different feature |
| sharp paths it creates | 🔴 five | ✅ near zero |
| cost per stone | one turn | a full boot, a full re-read, on every stone |

⇒ **context continuity is the whole value of a route**, so a design that discards it at every stone
does not deliver this wish cheaply — it delivers a different feature. the sharpness is the price of
the thread, and it is paid knowingly.

## 🟡 .what is awkward

three honest discomforts, stated rather than smoothed:

### 1. the wish's own sequence deadlocks

`clone whoami` then `clone say @:driver`, run from a hook, in that order — the sequence the wish
spells out — hangs on its own happy path (`case=5`, **F-d**).

**the sequence is not wrong; it is right for a caller outside the clone.** the wish did not
anticipate that the applier and the applied are one process. that is the single most awkward result
here, because the specified design and the correct design differ in a way no command-level read
surfaces.

### 2. `clone say` is a keystroke channel dressed as an api

the switch is delivered by **typed characters submitted into a pty**. it is verified, ack'd, and
genuinely reliable at what it promises — and what it promises is that a **human-shaped input arrived**,
not that a **machine-shaped instruction was accepted**.
