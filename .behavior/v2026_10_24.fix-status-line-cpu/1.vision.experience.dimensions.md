# 1.vision.experience.dimensions — the axes of a status-line repaint

three actors meet the status line:

- the **crew seat** — the harness repaints its line; a human or a driver glances at it
- the **box** — the system actor; it experiences the cpu of every seat summed, and the pile-up
  when repaints overlap
- the **operator** — runs `rhx upgrade` and owns the settings

each repaint is one point in the product of five axes:

| axis | positions | what it varies |
|---|---|---|
| **A. bind** | `unbound` · `bound` · `multi-bound` (a fault) · `bound, route unreadable` (a fault) | which route the line names, and whether it can be read — a STATE |
| **B. change since last repaint** | `unchanged` · `passage written` · `meter row appended` · `promise written` · `stone/artifact added or removed` · `bind flag changed` (set or removed) · `branch switched` · `hook write, no render input` | what moved on disk; a bind TRANSITION is a change, so it lives here, never on A |
| **C. cache** | `cold` (none) · `warm` · `aged past max` · `corrupt` · `unwritable dir` | the state of the one new artifact |
| **D. command** | `new subpath` · `old cli/route` (pre-upgrade settings) | what the settings invoke |
| **E. concurrency** | `single` · `concurrent` | whether another writer touches the same cache file in the same instant. the writer is a seat's repaint or a bind op; WHICH write it is lives on B, never on E |

a sixth axis, found at execution, varies how the new command reads its branch:

| axis | positions | what it varies |
|---|---|---|
| **G. checkout** | `plain repo` (a `.git` dir) · `linked worktree` (a `.git` gitdir pointer file) · `detached HEAD` | where HEAD is read from; only `detached HEAD` may spawn git |

## .the instruments — a fourth actor, the verifier

a verifier (a human, or a driver at a verification stone) runs the repo's own instruments to
prove a claim about the status line, or to ship its fix. they meet each instrument on one axis
of outcome:

| axis | positions | what it varies |
|---|---|---|
| **F. instrument** | `route.status.line.show` · `bench.status-line` · `settings.permissions.diff` · `gen.consumer-patch` | which claim the verifier proves: one repaint's hit or miss · the cpu per repaint · that a settings regen moved no grant · the pnpm patch a consumer applies while the release lags |
| **H. outcome** | `help` · `refused` (exit 2) · `faulted` (exit 1) · `shown` (exit 0) | what the instrument tells the verifier |

F × H is walked in the inventory's `.the instruments` table. the instruments read the status line
and never change it, so F and H are orthogonal to A–E and G.

## .the sub-type — B, C, and E exist only under D=new

the old command keeps no cache and no memory of its last repaint, so for `D=old` none of B, C,
or E varies its experience: each repaint is a full render at full cost. that is a discovery,
not a gap — **the cache, and every axis that acts on it, are attributes of the new command
only**. so the product is two branches:

- `D=new` × A × B × C × E — walked in the inventory
- `D=old` × A — today's render, today's cost, whatever A is; the one transition is `rhx upgrade`

## .the one tie between axes — a bind op drops the cache

`B = bind flag changed` forces `C = cold` on the next repaint, since `route.bind.set` / `del`
delete the branch's cache file. so the bind rows sit at `C=cold`. the one exception is a repaint
that read the cache before the drop landed — that is `E=concurrent`, and it shows `C=warm` once.

## .void cells, each with its kind and reason

| cell | kind | why |
|---|---|---|
| `A=unbound × B ∈ {passage written, meter row appended, promise written, stone/artifact added or removed}` | impossible (nature) | no route is read, so no route file is watched; such writes cannot reach the line |
| `A ∈ {multi-bound, bound route unreadable} × C=warm` with a cached fault | forbidden (nurture) | a fault is never cached — a cached fault would hide a fix the operator just made |
| `E=concurrent × C=corrupt` from a torn write | impossible (nature) | writes go to a temp file then `rename`, which is atomic on one filesystem: a reader opens the old file or the new one, never a half |

## .the densest walk

`A=bound × B=passage written × C=warm × D=new × E=single` crosses the most boundaries one seat
meets: a warm cache must notice a change it did not author, recompute, and rewrite — the one walk
where a defect shows a stale stone (`case=2`). the densest walk for the **box** is
`E=concurrent` across many seats at once (`case=7`).
