# case 7 — the-next-stone-declares-none

**actor** = driver-clone · **feel** = happy · **care** = alterpath
**cell** = `driver-clone × apply × absent-after-switch × live`

> this case settles the issue's fourth open question: **default + stickiness.**

## .the narrative

i launched on sonnet. `3.3.1.blueprint` declared `brain: claude-opus-5[1m]`, so i switched, and the
blueprint was better for it.

now `3.4.roadmap` is next, and its guard declares no brain at all.

what should i run?

there are two honest answers and they lead opposite ways:

- **restore** — i drop back to sonnet, the brain i launched on. the case: opus was scoped to the
  blueprint, and a stone that asked for naught should not inherit an expensive brain by accident
- **sticky** — i stay on opus. the case: the wish's own bound says *"a stone with no `brain:`
  field keeps the inherited brain"*, and after a switch the inherited brain **is** opus

i stay on opus. sticky, and stated:

```
🗿 3.4.roadmap
   └─ brain claude-opus-5[1m]   (inherited; last set by 3.3.1.blueprint)
```

that line is the part that earns the choice. sticky without attribution is a cost that appears from
nowhere; sticky **with** attribution is a cost a human can trace to the stone that caused it and fix
in one edit.

⇒ **the "aha": `brain:` declares a floor, not a lease.** it says "this stone is worth at least
this"; it does not say "and then give it back". a route controls cost when it declares the cheap
brain on the cheap stones — explicitly — rather than by trust in an invisible restore.

## .the bdd `[tn]` timeline

```
given a driver launched on claude-sonnet-5[1m], enrolled as @:driver, reach=LIVE
  and  a stone 3.3.1.blueprint whose guard declares `brain: claude-opus-5[1m]`
  and  a stone 3.4.roadmap whose guard declares no brain

  when [t0] the driver enters 3.3.1.blueprint
    then the switch lands, and the stone runs on claude-opus-5[1m]

  when [t1] the driver passes 3.3.1.blueprint and enters 3.4.roadmap
    then no switch is attempted — 3.4.roadmap declares no brain, so it inherits
    and  the brain it inherits is claude-opus-5[1m], NOT the sonnet the drive launched on

  when [t2] the driver reports the stone it entered
    then the line names the live brain AND that it was inherited
    and  it names the stone that last set it — 3.3.1.blueprint

  when [t3] a human asks why 3.4.roadmap ran on an expensive brain they never asked for
    then the answer is in [t2]'s line, not in a bill
    and  the fix is one edit: declare `brain: claude-sonnet-5[1m]` on 3.4.roadmap

  when [t4] 3.4.roadmap declares the cheap brain explicitly
    then the switch lands as in case 1, and the leak is closed at its source
```

## 🟡 .the call this case best-guesses — `F6`

**sticky, with attribution.** the alternative — restore-on-exit — was weighed and set aside:

| | sticky | restore-on-exit |
|---|---|---|
| matches the wish's stated bound | ✅ *"keeps the inherited brain"* | 🔴 requires a read of "inherited" as "launch-time", which the wish does not say |
| cost hazard | 🔴 an expensive brain leaks into later cheap stones | ✅ cost is bounded to the stone that asked |
| state to carry | ✅ none — the live brain IS the state | 🔴 must remember a per-stone prior brain, and restore it on a path that may halt, block, or rewind |
| failure mode | a stone runs richer than needed — costly, never wrong | a restore that does not fire on a halt path leaves the state false — **the same class of defect as case 3** |

the decisive line is the last one. **restore adds a second dispatch on a path that can halt**, and
every path that can halt is a path where the restore silently does not happen. that is a new instance
of the exact seam cases 3 and 5 are already spent on.

⇒ sticky is chosen because it holds **no state**, and state that does not exist cannot drift.

⚠️ **the cost hazard is real and is not waved off.** it is answered by the cross-repo work the issue
already names: bhuild's guard **templates** must stamp the cheap brain on routine stones, not only
the expensive brain on hard ones. a template that declares opus and stays silent elsewhere is a
template that bills opus for the whole route.

## .why alterpath and not critipath

**freq** is high — most stones declare no brain. **cost** is bounded and recoverable: a stone runs
on a richer brain than it needed, which is money, never a wrong result. the workable route around it
is one guard edit, shown at `[t4]`.

⇒ tolerable `freq × cost` with a workable fallback = **alterpath**. it is demoed because the choice
it settles is an open question in the seed issue, and a settled question deserves its argument on
record.
