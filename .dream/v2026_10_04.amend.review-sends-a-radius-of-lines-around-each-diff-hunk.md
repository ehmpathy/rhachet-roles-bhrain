# 🌙 dream: a review sends a radius of lines around each diff hunk, not the whole file

- kind = amend
- owner = rhachet-roles-bhrain
- caught 2026-10-04, in `.behavior/v2026_09_20.review-cost-meter` (stone `5.3.verification`, round i001)

## .the cue that fired

8 of 9 peer reviewers overflowed the window on stone `5.3.verification`. the wisher proposed, as a
fundamental option: show only the radius of lines around the diffs.

## .the gap, precisely

- today a modified target is sent as its full body plus its diff; there is no option between "the
  hunk" and "the whole file"
- ⚠️ measured caveat on this branch: 831 of 1098 changed files are NEW (~1.05M tokens); a radius saves
  naught on a new file, since its hunk is the whole file. the 261 tracked edits are where it bites

## .the shape of the fix

1. per modified file: each diff hunk plus N lines on each side; adjacent windows merge; line numbers
   kept so concerns still cite `file:line`
2. a variant to measure beside the fixed radius: the hunk plus the whole function or block that holds
   it (less arbitrary than N)
3. the rubric declares the resolution it needs (radius vs full body); a rubric that grades whole-file
   properties (dead code, name consistency across a file) opts out
4. new files take another representation (outline, or full body under a partition) — see the peer
   dreams caught the same day
5. measure first: tokens per resolution across a real branch, at N = 5, 15, 40, and the whole-block variant

frame: this sets the **first resolution** a target is sent at, in the order path → outline → hunk →
hunk+radius → hunk+block → full body (an unsettled frame, not a glossary term). pairs with progressive disclosure (the reviewer may climb past
the radius) and the parallel partition (the last resort).

## .why it is not done in this round

- SAFE — yes, mostly: a render change behind a rubric opt-in
- CLEAN — no: it moves the prompt's byte layout, which the cache-anatomy measurements and every
  composition snapshot pin; it owes its own measurement round
