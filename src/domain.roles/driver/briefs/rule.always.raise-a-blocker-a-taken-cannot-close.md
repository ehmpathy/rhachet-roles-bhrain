# rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close

> **when a review round yields no point you are PERMITTED to close, signal `--as blocked` with the
> ask — never re-arrive.**

`rule.always.converge-with-reviewers` says settle it yourself and escalate only at a real wall. it is
silent on the one question a stalled driver actually needs answered: **how do you recognize the wall
while the budget still reads healthy?** that silence is where the rounds go.

## 🔴 .the mechanism — a `.taken` converges an ARGUMENT, never an ABSENCE

this is the whole rule, and it is a distinction about what the objection *is*:

| the reviewer's objection | what closes it | can a `.taken` do it? |
|---|---|---|
| *"this is wrong"* | a repair, or a refutation with cited evidence | ✅ **yes** — that is exactly what a `.taken` is for |
| *"this is not covered"* | the coverage itself — a test, a file, an artifact | 🔴 **no.** an argument about an absence does not fill it |

⇒ so a rejection can be **correct, unanswerable, and permanent all at once**. and if the artifact
that would close it needs a grant you cannot make yourself — a credential, a commit quota, a scope
decision — then **more rounds cannot converge it, and the budget will not tell you so.**

⚠️ **budget left is not evidence of a remedy.** a lane rejected for absent coverage re-raises
identically at 45/107 and at 105/107. **exhaustion is terminal; rejection is not** — so a lane can
block forever with budget to spare, and from the inside that shape reads like progress.

⚠️ **and to run the lane dry is no exit either.** exhaustion ends its *rounds*, never the blocker
it already raised — that debt outlives its budget (`rule.always.converge-to-terminal`). a coast to
the bottom buys a debt rather than a discharge.

## .the test

> **"is there a change I am PERMITTED to make that would close this point?"**

| the answer | you |
|---|---|
| **yes** | `--as contemplated`, then re-arrive. the ordinary loop |
| 🔴 **no** | `--as blocked`, **with the ask stated as a command the human can run** |

⚠️ *permitted* is the load-bearing word, and it is narrower than *possible*. a test you could write
but cannot run without a key you do not hold is **not permitted**. nor is an artifact whose
authorship the wisher fenced.

## ⚠️ .this does NOT loosen `rule.forbid.unanswered-exits-from-a-blocker`

that rule lists `--as blocked` among the forbidden exits, and the two are easy to read as opposed.
they are not — they govern **different states**, and the test above is precisely what parts them:

| the state | the rule |
|---|---|
| a critique you have **not answered** | 🔴 `rule.forbid.unanswered-exits-from-a-blocker`. **`--as blocked` is forbidden.** write the `.taken` |
| a critique you **have answered** and cannot close, because the close is not yours to make | ✅ **this rule.** `--as blocked` is the correct exit |

⇒ *"a wall is a wall; a critique you have not answered is not one"* is unchanged. **what this rule
adds is that a critique you HAVE answered can still be a wall** — and the forbid rule, read alone,
leaves a driver with no word for that case, so they re-arrive.

## 🔴 .the test is an OR over points, never a SUM — and a `.taken` does not lift a halt

**the test above asks about *this point*. a stone halts on *any* point that fails it.** those are
different quantities, and a driver who answers the first and acts on the second re-arrives.

| you have | the halt |
|---|---|
| 5 points, 5 closeable | ✅ lifted. close them, re-arrive |
| 5 points, 4 closeable, **1 not** | 🔴 **stands.** close the four **while halted** |
| 1 point, not closeable | 🔴 stands |

⇒ **a halt is retired by its own remedy, never by a neighbour's.** four walls that fall is not the
wall that falls.

### ⚠️ the two operations are orthogonal, and that is the whole repair

this rule already says *"the halt does not excuse the `.taken`."* **the converse holds equally and
was never written down:**

> 🔴 **a `.taken` does not lift the halt.**

`--as contemplated` discharges a **debt to a reviewer**; `--as blocked` reports an **absent grant**.
neither is the other's inverse, so a driver may — and should — answer points while halted. **what
they may not do is read that progress as permission to re-arrive.**

### 🔴 the measured case — a headline that refuted itself in consecutive sentences

`rhachet-roles-bhrain` @ `beav/fix-contemplation-gate-on-entrance`, stone `5.3.verification`,
2026-09-07. three gates held it: a suspended brain provider, a guard-bind seal, four absent test
credentials. two fell to driver acts — a re-probe cleared the provider, a re-read showed the seal
was already `allowed`. the credentials did not move.

the blocker artifact was then amended to read, in consecutive sentences:

> *"a guard round can run again, so the halt is lifted and the stone is re-arrived. **gate 2 still
> stands between this stone and passage.**"*

**both clauses were true. the conclusion did not follow from them**, and the driver re-arrived past
its own sentence. `passage.jsonl` records the shape: `blocked → arrived → blocked → contemplated`.

⇒ 🔴 **the halt was raised twice and abandoned twice.** the failure was never a halt left unraised —
it was a halt treated as a round to get past rather than a terminal state to wait in.

⚠️ **and the wisher had to supply the correction**, in one question: *"if you need absent
crednetials, shouldn't have you raised a blocker already?"* the rule was **cited on the page it was
broken on**, so no further read would have found it. a rule a driver can recite is not a rule a
driver obeys, and the recitation is what makes the gap invisible from the inside.

## .the counter-bound — it fires only after every lever is spent

this rule would otherwise mint an early-exit license, so it is gated by the two that precede it:

1. `rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation` returns **no** — every driver lever spent, each
   on the record. a budget top-up is yours; a diagnosed reviewer malfunction is yours
2. `rule.always.get-a-second-opinion-before-foreman` — a peer read on whether it is truly a wall

⇒ **a blocker raised with unspent levers is the defect this rule would create.** name the levers you
spent, in the blocker artifact.

| when… | then… |
|---|---|
| a lane's count **rises** while the defect surface shrinks | 🔴 the sharpest cue. it converges on no point at all |
| you answered a point and the **same** point returns unchanged | ask whether your answer was an argument against an absence |
| two lanes read one artifact and **diverge permanently** | one grades soundness, the other coverage. a sound deferral is still uncovered |
| the close needs a **credential, a quota, or a scope call** | not yours. halt with the exact command |
| ⚠️ the reviewer raises a **structural** question at fulcrum-council time | halt. see below |
| the budget still reads healthy | 🔴 **irrelevant.** re-read the test |
| 🔴 you are **already halted** and a *different* gate clears | **the halt stands.** re-probe the gate that raised it, and no other |
| you are already halted and write a `.taken` | correct conduct — and it lifts no halt. answer while halted |
| you re-probe the halt's own gate and it has **cleared** | ✅ **now** re-arrive. a halt is a measurement with an age (`rule.require.trust-but-verify`) |

## 🔴 .the fulcrum-council case — a structural objection is often a wisher question in a review's coat

`rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last` gathers every deferred fork into one council at the end. so at
that moment the drive holds a list of calls **explicitly marked as the wisher's**, and a reviewer that
raises a structural objection is very often one of those calls, re-asked from the outside.

⇒ **do not answer it with a `.taken`.** an itemized fulcrum is a call you already declined to make;
to argue it down in a `.taken` settles by side effect a question you had reserved, and the wisher
never learns it was asked twice.

**halt, and hand up both** — the reviewer's objection and the fulcrum row it lands on. that pair is
worth far more to the wisher than either alone: the row carries what you chose and why; the objection
carries an independent reader who disagrees.

⚠️ **the question that parts the two cases is not "is it structural?"** — it is *"does an itemized
fulcrum already reserve this call?"* a structural objection with no fulcrum behind it is an ordinary
critique, and it is yours to answer.

## 🔴 .the loop this closes — the work that clears a reviewer is the work that blinds the next one

the same drive measured a second, structural cause, and it explains why *"just one more round"* feels
survivable and is not:

```
no commit quota → zero commits → `since-main` unions the whole staged+untracked set
                → ~293 files → 9 of 11 lanes overflow their context window → dark
                → and every artifact written to ANSWER a reviewer enlarges the diff again
```

a `.taken`, a dream, a term cluster — each is the **correct** response to a review, and each makes
the next round harder to review. so the loop is not a driver's carelessness; it is the honest work,
compounded.

⚠️ **the driver-owned remedy for a dark lane is a GUARD EDIT** — narrow that lane's `--paths-with`,
trim its `--conversation`, so it fits and the guard can run it
(`rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions`). ⛔ **never a hand-run `rhx review`**: it draws no
budget and its verdict gates naught (`rule.forbid.hand-run-reviews`).

### 🔴 .a commit quota is NOT, on its own, a legitimate `--as blocked`

the arithmetic above holds — a commit really is the only move that collapses `since-main`, and the
quota really is human-gated. **the leap that does not follow is from *effective* to *permitted*.**

| the question | who answers it |
|---|---|
| *may I commit?* — the quota | a **human**, via `git.commit.uses set` |
| 🔴 *does this route ASK me to commit?* | the **stone**, and a quota answers it not at all |

⇒ **two questions, and the easy error is to collapse them into one.** a commit no stone asked for
changes what the reviewers can see, mid-review, by the hand of the party under review — so to
escalate for it is to ask a human to pay for an act that stays forbidden once paid for
(`rule.forbid.commits-the-route-did-not-ask-for`).

⚠️ **measured** — a driver halted on exactly that ask, and the wisher struck it: *"you do not need
to commit yet. no one approved your stone."*

⚠️ **so the halt this rule sanctions is narrower than it read.** a dark lane is a wall you must name;
the ask that names it is a **guard edit you already made and the blocker you still cannot close** —
never a commit quota, unless a stone asked for the commit or the route is finished.

`ehmpathy/rhachet` @ `beav/fix-node-pty-install`, stone `5.1.execution.from_vision`, i069 → i073.
two l3 lanes read one artifact each round:

| round | `enroll-impl-behavior-intent` | `enroll-impl-arch-defects` |
|---|---|---|
| i070 | 1 blocker | approved |
| i071 | 1 | approved |
| i072 | 2 | approved |
| i073 | **4** | **approved** |

at i073 the four resolved to: one refuted (it demanded a clamp green since i071), two closeable only
by a human, one disclosed + dreamed since i070. **five rounds spent; budget was never the wall** —
45/107 and 42/107, neither exhausted.

🔴 **and the lane was not wrong.** it wrote both halves in one sentence — *"the deferral itself is
reasonable"* — and blocked on it. a reasonable deferral is still an uncovered behavior, and coverage
is the only quantity its rubric measures.

## .what the blocker artifact owes

- **the ask, as a command the human can run** — never a description of a difficulty
- **the levers you spent**, each named, so the counter-bound is auditable
- **the reviewer's point, verbatim**, and your answer to it — the halt does not excuse the `.taken`
- the fulcrum row, where one applies

## .enforcement

blocker: a re-arrival while **any** point fails the permitted-change test · a `--as blocked` raised
with a driver lever unspent · a structural objection answered by `.taken` where an itemized fulcrum
reserves the call · a blocker artifact with no runnable ask · 🔴 **a halt lifted because a
*different* gate cleared**.
nitpick: a blocker artifact that omits the levers spent.
false positive: points closed **while halted** — that is the correct conduct, not a re-arrival.

### 🔴 mind the QUANTIFIERS above — the near-miss form licenses the exact defect this rule names

*"a re-arrival whose **every** point failed"* paired with *"false positive: a re-arrival where
**any** point was closeable"* reads as a sane pair and is not. four points closeable and one not
satisfies the carve-out and never trips the blocker, ⇒ **the un-closable point is invisible to
both** — which is precisely the measured case above.

a halt is an **OR** over points, so the blocker line needs **any**, and the false positive needs no
quantifier at all: a closeable point is closed *while halted*, which is not a re-arrival.

⚠️ 🔴 **an enforcement clause carries a rule's whole weight and gets its fewest re-reads.** a rule
whose enforcement contradicts its own test is worse than one with no enforcement, since it reads as
a check while it licenses the breach.
