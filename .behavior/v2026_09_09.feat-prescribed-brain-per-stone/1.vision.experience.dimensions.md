# 1.vision.experience.dimensions

the experience space for **a prescribed brain per stone**, factored into orthogonal axes.

the product of these axes is walked in `1.vision.experience.case=_.md`.

## .the subject

a `.guard` declares `brain: <brainslug>`. on stone entry, the driver-role hook reads it and applies
it into the live driver clone:

```sh
rhx clone whoami                                   # confirm the addressed clone
rhx clone say @:<addr> --what '/model <brainslug>'  # land the switch
```

## .the axes

### A. actor — who encounters it

| value | who |
|---|---|
| `driver-clone` | the clone on the route. it authors guards (`route.mutate.guard`), it drives stones, and it is the clone whose brain swaps |
| `human` | the foreman who enrolled the drive, watches it, and pays the bill |

**orthogonal because the two actors share every goal.** a driver mutates its own guards; a human
hand-edits them. a human drives a route directly; a clone drives one under enroll. neither actor
implies a goal, a declaration, or a reach state.

### B. goal — what that actor came for

| value | what they came for |
|---|---|
| `prescribe` | write `brain:` into a `.guard`, so the stone runs under the right brain |
| `apply` | enter the stone and have the switch land |
| `audit` | know which brain ran, and that the switch happened |

**orthogonal to actor** — see A. **orthogonal to declaration and reach** — a goal names an
intent; it constrains neither what a guard already says nor whether a socket answers.

### C. declaration — what the entered stone's guard says

| value | the guard says |
|---|---|
| `absent-from-launch` | no `brain:`, and no switch has happened yet on this drive |
| `absent-after-switch` | no `brain:`, and a prior stone already switched the brain |
| `same-as-live` | a `brain:` equal to the live brain |
| `other-known` | a `brain:` that names a brainslug the brain-cli accepts |
| `other-unknown` | a `brain:` that names a slug the brain-cli rejects (a typo, a retired model) |
| `misspelled-key` | the field itself is misspelled — `model:`, `brains:`, `Brain:` |

**closed set.** the axis factors on **three** independent facts:

| # | the fact | which values it splits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | does a `brain:` key parse at all? | `absent-*` and `misspelled-key` against the rest |
| 2 | does its value differ from the live brain? | `same-as-live` against `other-known` / `other-unknown` |
| 3 | 🔴 **has any prior stone on this drive switched?** | `absent-from-launch` against `absent-after-switch` |

### 🟡 .fact 3 was unstated until peer review r1, and its absence made the axis unauditable

the axis once claimed to factor on **two** facts — and its own `absent-*` split is identical on both
of them (no key parses, so no value can differ). ⇒ **a reader who audited the axis against its stated
basis would find a split the basis does not produce**, and could not tell whether that was a third
fact or a defect.

🔴 **it is a third fact, and it is the DRIVE's state rather than the guard's.** the two `absent-*`
values differ in **stickiness** — the issue's open question (`F6`) — and a reader cannot tell them
apart from the guard alone, because the guard is identical in both.

⚠️ **so the axis name `declaration` is one word short of its content.** it carries *what the guard
declares* plus *what the drive has already done*, and only the first is in the name.

⇒ **the values were NOT moved onto a drive-state axis**, and that is a deliberate call: a separate
axis would multiply every one of the other five `C` values by a drive-state that only matters when
`C` is absent — 🔴 **which produces cells where fact 3 is undefined.** the bundle keeps the product
free of undefined cells, and the cost is one word in a name.

🟡 and fact 3 is precisely what the `C ↔ E` section below turns on: **fact 3 counts the DRIVE's
history, and `E` counts THIS STONE's entry count.** two clocks, so the axes stay orthogonal.

**orthogonal to reach** — a guard is text on disk. it reads the same whether the driver clone
answers a socket or does not exist.

### D. reach — can the driver clone be addressed

| value | the driver clone |
|---|---|
| `live` | enrolled, socket answers — `clone say` lands |
| `deaf` | enrolled, process alive, no dispatch socket — `clone say` is refused |
| `unenrolled` | never spawned by `rhx enroll` — no `RHACHET_CLONE_SERIAL`, no identity record, no address |

**closed set** — it takes two of rhachet's three reach states (`computeCloneReachState`:
`LIVE` / `DEAF` / `DEAD`), plus `unenrolled`, which that model has no value for **because a clone
that enroll never spawned is not in the registry to have a state at all**.

### 🔴 .D-`DEAD` is dropped, and the drop is ARGUED rather than assumed

added at peer review r1. the axis once claimed it mirrored rhachet's model *"exactly"* while it
carried two of three states — **an overclaim that hid whether the space had a hole.**

it collapses on all three goals, and for a **different reason on each**:

| the goal | why `DEAD` hosts no distinct experience |
|---|---|
| `apply` | 🔴 **the hook runs INSIDE the driver process** (`case=5`, fact **F-d**). a dead driver fires no hook, so no stone is entered and no switch is attempted. **the cell cannot be reached from within it** |
| `prescribe` | author time is reach-blind (`F3`) — a guard is text on disk, and it reads the same under every reach state. `DEAD` is identical to `live`, `deaf`, and `unenrolled` here |
| `audit` | the record is on **disk**, not in the process. a dead clone's brain line reads exactly as a live one's, because the line was written when the stone was entered |

⇒ 🔴 **so `DEAD` is a genuine value of rhachet's model and hosts no cell of this space** — which is
the collapse-with-an-argument shape axis `E` uses below, applied to one value rather than a whole
axis.

⚠️ **the honest residual:** `apply × DEAD` is unreachable **by the hook**, and a **human** could still
observe a drive that died mid-stone. that observation is `audit`, never `apply` — the human reads a
record, and row 3 covers it. **the axis has no hole; it had an overclaimed word.**

⚠️ `unenrolled` is the value most likely to be dropped, and it is today's **common** case. verified:

```
$ rhx clone whoami --output json
{"class":"ConstraintError","message":"not run inside an enrolled clone", ...}   # exit 2
```

## 🔴 .the hidden-dimension check

per `rule.require.dimensional-decomposition`: a slice that cannot vary means two axes were not
orthogonal. one slice looks invariant, so it gets checked rather than waved past.

> **`goal=prescribe` appears invariant over `reach`.** at author time no switch is attempted, so
> the guard reads the same whether the driver is live, deaf, or unenrolled.

the check: **is that invariance a fact of nature, or a property of today's design?**

it is a property of the design. an author who writes `brain:` into a route whose driver is
`unenrolled` has written a field that **can never apply** — and naught tells them so. an author
surface that read reach would vary over this axis.

⇒ so the axes ARE orthogonal, and the invariance is a **real experience the current design hides**.
it is itemized rather than dropped — `case=_` row `prescribe × any × unenrolled`, and
fulcrum `F3`.

## 🔴 .the probe-malfunction dimension — added at peer review r5 (verification)

`reach` (axis `D`) names the driver clone's TRUE state — `live`, `deaf`, `unenrolled`. the shipped
`StoneBrainHaltCause` type (`src/domain.operations/route/brain/StoneBrainHaltCause.ts`) names seven
reasons a switch was never dispatched, and only one of them — `unenrolled` — is a clean, typed read
of that true state (`clone whoami` rejects with `ConstraintError`, exit 2, **F-a**). the other six
— `killed`, `timed-out`, `unreadable-clone`, `unreadable-address`, `unreadable-payload`,
`spawn-failed` — are cases where the driver's true reach may be `live` or `deaf`, and the
INSTRUMENT used to observe it (`clone whoami`, or the `clone say` child it hands off to) failed to
report cleanly.

**is this a fourth value of `reach`, or a fifth axis?** neither. it is orthogonal to `reach` in the
same sense the hidden-dimension check above found `prescribe` orthogonal to `reach`: a probe
malfunction says naught about the driver's true state, and the true state says naught about
whether the probe that reads it will function. a genuinely `live` driver can still present as
`killed` if an oom killer reaps the probe process; a `live` driver can still present as
`unreadable-payload` if the probe exits cleanly and writes garbage.

⇒ **it does not multiply the `108`-cell product, and for the same reason `dispatch mechanism`
above does not:** it is not a state the world presents that the actor then experiences at a fixed
cell — it is a property of whether the ONE shipped instrument, on this run, did its job. unlike
`dispatch mechanism` (where exactly one of two candidate builds ships), all six malfunction causes
coexist in the one shipped build and can each occur on separate runs — so this is closer to `axis
E`'s shape than to the dispatch-mechanism region: a real dimension that is **argued to collapse**
rather than multiplied, because its six values do not each need their own cell — they need one
family, that shares `case=2`'s halt shape and differs only in fix line.

**where it applies**: only within `apply` goal, and only where a switch is attempted at all — the
same footprint as `reach` itself, since a probe cannot malfunction on a cell where no probe runs
(`absent-*`, `misspelled-key`).

**the two sub-groups**, split by which step fails:

| group | causes | what fails |
|---|---|---|
| probe malfunction | `killed`, `timed-out`, `unreadable-clone`, `unreadable-address`, `unreadable-payload` | `clone whoami` dies, caps, or answers unusably |
| dispatch-launch malfunction | `spawn-failed` | the probe succeeds; the `clone say` child never launches |

⇒ demoed as one family, `case=11` (`the-probe-cannot-answer`) — itemized here, in `case=_.md`'s
slice 2, and demoed once rather than six times, per the same proportionality clause `case=7`'s
rewind-region cell invokes: the six causes share one argument for why the family is a critipath
(the fix line must be distinct per cause, on pain of `case=4`'s exact harm), and one demo carries
that argument for all six.

## 🔴 .the one cell that hosts TWO experiences — and why the axis under it is not an axis

added at peer review r1, which found it and was right to: **`driver-clone × apply × other-known ×
live` carries `case=1` (happy) and `case=5` (sharp).** one cell, two experiences, opposite feel — and
`rule.require.dimensional-decomposition` says a latent dimension must be surfaced rather than left
implicit.

**the latent dimension, named:** the hook's dispatch mechanism — `{wait-for-the-ack, hand-off}`.
`case=1` is the hand-off; `case=5` is the synchronous wait, which deadlocks because the applier and
the applied are one process (**F-d**).

### 🔴 .why it is recorded here rather than multiplied into the product

> **every other axis's values are states the WORLD presents to the actor. this axis's values are two
> candidate BUILDS, and exactly one of them ships.**

| axis | who chooses the value | how many values are live at once |
|---|---|---|
| `A` actor · `B` goal · `C` declaration · `D` reach · `E` ordinality | the world, per encounter | **all of them**, across encounters |
| dispatch mechanism | 🔴 **the implementer, once** | **one.** the other stops to exist at the moment the build lands |

⇒ a product over it would generate 108 cells that **cannot coexist**, and a walked space whose cells
are mutually exclusive is not a space — it is two versions of one space.

### 🟡 .so the cell is a REGION, and the region is why `case=5` exists

**both experiences are demoed**, so no coverage is hidden — `case=1` walks the outcome the build must
produce, and `case=5` walks the outcome it produces if the wish's own specified sequence is taken
literally.

⇒ 🔴 **`case=5` is not a second experience of one cell so much as the FORK in that cell, demoed so the
build cannot take it by accident.** the wish spells out `whoami` then `clone say`, in that order, from
a hook — which is the deadlock. **a design fork this sharp earns a demo rather than a column.**

⚠️ **the honest cost of this call:** the `108` count over-states the resolution of exactly one cell,
and it is the densest cell in the space. **recorded rather than smoothed** — a reader who counts cells
should know that one of them is two.

## .the product

`2 × 3 × 6 × 3 = 108` cells.

walked in `1.vision.experience.case=_.md`, one slice per `actor × goal`, each a `6 × 3` table.

🔴 **axis `E` below is a fifth axis and it does NOT multiply this product** — it collapses onto the
walked cells everywhere but one region, argued there rather than asserted.

## 🔴 .E. entry ordinality — added at self-review r3, and it COLLAPSES except in one region

**{first entry, re-entry after a halt, re-entry after a rewind}**

it was never in the axis list and never in the rejected list — **a third state, neither walked nor
argued away.** and it is used in two demos: `case=2`'s `[t3]` and `case=3`'s `[t4]` both close on a
re-entry, so the value appears in the artifacts while the space denies it exists.

⇒ **it is independent of `C` and `D`.** what the guard declares and whether the clone is reachable say
naught about whether this stone has been entered before, so it is a real fourth-and-a-half axis
rather than a function of the others.

### 🟡 .C ↔ E was challenged at peer review r1, and the axes hold — here is the argument

the challenge: `C = absent-after-switch` reads as *"a prior stone already switched"*, which sounds
like it **implies** a non-first entry, and an implication between axes means they are one axis.

🔴 **the two clocks are different, and that is the whole answer:**

| axis | what it counts |
|---|---|
| `C` | the **DRIVE's** switch history — has any stone, anywhere on this route, switched yet? |
| `E` | **THIS STONE's** entry count — is this the first time this particular stone is entered? |

⇒ so all four combinations occur, and none is empty:

| | `E = first entry` | `E = re-entry` |
|---|---|---|
| `C = absent-from-launch` | ✅ stone 2, first entry, no stone has ever switched | ✅ stone 2 re-entered after a halt, still no switch anywhere |
| `C = absent-after-switch` | ✅ **stone 5, FIRST entry**, where stone 3 switched earlier | ✅ stone 3 re-entered after a rewind, where stone 5 switched |

🔴 **the top-right cell is the one that settles it.** a stone entered for the first time can sit at
`absent-after-switch`, because the switch belonged to a *different* stone. ⇒ **`C` says naught about
`E`.**

⚠️ **the challenge was reasonable and the words invited it** — *"a prior stone already switched"*
reads as history, and a reader has to supply *whose* history. the table above supplies it.

### .why it does not multiply the product to 216

🔴 **the collapse is argued PER GOAL, never once.** one argument stood here until peer review r3, and
it was an `apply`-goal argument — which left the other two goals with an unargued collapse, the exact
shape this deck rejects for `D-DEAD` and for `C`'s fact 3.

| the goal | why entry ordinality collapses on it |
|---|---|
| **prescribe** | a guard is **text on disk**, authored once, before any drive walks it. the author cannot observe an entry count that does not yet exist — ⇒ no value of `E` reaches this goal at all |
| **audit** | a record is **read after the fact**, and `case=8`'s line is emitted per stone entry. a re-entry adds a **row**; it changes no row's shape — ⇒ `E` moves the record's length, never any experience in it |
| **apply** | the argued case, below — the hook's act is invariant across entries |

⇒ **`prescribe` and `audit` collapse by CONSTRUCTION** (neither goal observes an entry ordinal);
**`apply` collapses by ARGUMENT** (the act does not vary). three goals, three reasons, and two of
them were silent.

#### the `apply`-goal argument

**the hook's act is identical on every entry**: read the key, compare, dispatch on a mismatch. so for
`declaration × reach` the cell's verdict does not move — **a re-entry is the same experience the first
entry was, or a `case=6` no-op.**

| the ordinal | what changes |
|---|---|
| first entry | naught — the walked cell holds |
| re-entry after a halt | the switch usually already landed → `case=6`'s no-op, already demoed |
| 🔴 **re-entry after a rewind** | **the live brain is a LATER stone's, and it is likely richer** |

### 🔴 .the one region that does not collapse — a rewind into a stone with no `brain:`

`case=7` establishes stickiness on **forward** motion: a later stone with no `brain:` keeps the
inherited brain, attributed to the stone that set it. **a rewind inverts the direction and the
argument does not survive the inversion:**

```
stone 5 declares opus  →  brain is opus
rhx route.stone.set --stone 3.roadmap --as rewound
stone 3 declares NO brain  →  sticky keeps opus  →  🔴 a routine stone runs on the rich brain
```

⇒ **`case=7`'s leak, on a path `case=7` never considered**, because a forward walk cannot reach a
cheaper stone with an inherited rich brain — only a rewind can. the containment `case=7` proposes
(bhuild stamps the cheap brain on routine stones) closes this one too, which is why it is itemized
rather than demoed.

⚠️ **and it is a real experience, not a hypothetical** — `route.stone.set --as rewound` is a shipped
status (`define.passage-statuses`).

⇒ verdicted `📋 alterpath` — a rewind is uncommon, its cost is one stone at the wrong price, and it
shares a fix with a demoed critipath. **itemized, per `rule.require.experience-coverage`'s
proportionality clause; a demo would restate `case=7`.**

## .the axes considered and rejected

| candidate axis | why it is not an axis |
|---|---|
| **switch outcome** {landed, refused, silent} | derived from `C × D`, not independent. an axis that is a function of two others adds no cells |
| **stone kind** {vision, blueprint, execution, verification} | the behavior does not vary by stone kind. the kind decides which brain an author picks; it changes no experience |
| **concurrency** {one driver, two drivers on one route} | a route binds to one branch and one driver. two concurrent drivers on one route is a route-engine concern this behavior does not touch |
| **brain vendor** {claude, other} | `/model` is a brain-cli command. a brain-cli with no `/model` is covered by `other-unknown`, which is the same felt outcome |
| 🔴 **entry ordinality** | **not rejected — ADMITTED above as axis `E`.** it was absent from both lists, which is the defect a rejected-list is meant to prevent |
| 🔴 **dispatch mechanism** {wait-for-the-ack, hand-off} | **rejected, and argued above.** its values are two candidate **builds**, never two states the world presents — exactly one ships, so a product over it generates cells that cannot coexist. it is demoed as a fork (`case=1` vs `case=5`) rather than walked as a column |
| **reach = `DEAD`** (a value, not an axis) | **dropped from axis `D`, and argued above** — it collapses on all three goals, for a different reason on each |
| 🔴 **probe-malfunction dimension** {clean-read, killed, timed-out, unreadable-×3, spawn-failed} | **not rejected — ADMITTED above.** absent from both lists at the vision stage; found at peer review r5 against the shipped `StoneBrainHaltCause` type, per `rule.require.experience-catalog-evolution` |

🟡 **the rejected list is what made the gap findable, and it is also what hid it.** four candidates
argued away reads as a thorough sweep — and a sweep can only report the candidates someone thought
of. **an absent row and a rejected row are indistinguishable to a reader**, which is why `E` — and
now the probe-malfunction dimension — is admitted here with its own heading rather than added
quietly to the table above.
