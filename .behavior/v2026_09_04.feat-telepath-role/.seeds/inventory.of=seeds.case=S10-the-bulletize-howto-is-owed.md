## .settled

**a brief is owed that teaches the outline as the default prose structure**, and it is absent from the wish's ten issues.

the structure it teaches:

- a **`.what` / `.why`** header pair opens the passage
- beneath each, a **bullet outline**
- **one dense line per concept** — a bullet is one concept, stated whole
- **a subconcept is a subbullet** — nesting carries the concept hierarchy, so the reader never reconstructs it from prose

⇒ the claim is not that an outline is *a* clear structure. it is that it is **the clearest**, so it is the **default** — and a paragraph is what you reach for when the outline genuinely cannot hold the thought.

### the gap this fills, and it sits between two extant rules

| scale | rule | governs |
|---|---|---|
| the **sentence** | `rule.require.brevity` · `rule.prefer.short-sentences` | how many words a sentence takes |
| **the passage** | **absent — this seed** | what SHAPE the passage takes |
| the **document** | `rule.require.place-a-passage-by-its-subject` · #425's four | where a passage sits, and how the whole is shaped |

⇒ **the sentence rules can all pass on a paragraph that should have been a list.** brevity grades each sentence and never asks whether prose was the right container.

### the relation to #415

`#415 howto.densify-a-passage` asks for *"strict one-sentence bullets"*, which is one line of this seed. **the seed is wider**: it names the `.what` / `.why` frame, the outline as the default, and the subbullet as the carrier of a subconcept.
