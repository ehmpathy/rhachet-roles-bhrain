## .settled

**a telepath bulletizes by default** — one concept per line, a subconcept as a subbullet, depth as
the decomposition.

four benefits, in the wisher's own order:

- **scannable readability** — a reader descends only the branch they want
- **clear decomposition of concepts** — the nest carries every edge, so no connective must be decoded
- **it drives summarization and terseness forward** — a bullet has room for one sentence, so the
  form holds the discipline the author would otherwise hold by hand
- **it is a CHECK, never a layout** — *"if it doesnt fit the structure, it probably doesnt need to
  be said or the structure needs revisited"*

### the fourth is what earns it a rule

a passage that will not fit a bullet is one of exactly two defects, and the outline surfaces both:

- **surplus** — it belongs to no concept in the tree, so cut it
- **an absent branch** — the concept it belongs to was never named, so add it

⇒ **prose absorbs both silently.** the outline is what makes them visible, and it makes them visible
**to the author, at write time**, before a reviewer is spent.

### the artifact split the wisher prescribed

| artifact | holds |
|---|---|
| a **brief** | what bulletize means |
| **demos** | a few, to drive it home |
| a **trait** | that the telepath always bulletizes |

⇒ three artifacts, not one — and the split is the wisher's, not a decomposition I chose.
