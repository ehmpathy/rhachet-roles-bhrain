# 1.vision.experience.dimensions

the orthogonal axes the review-cost instruments vary along. the cells are the product;
`1.vision.experience.case=_.md` verdicts every one.

## .dimension 1 — `breadth` (3 positions)

how far a figure reaches.

| position | the unit | the source |
|---|---|---|
| `run` | one `rhx review` invocation | `metrics.compiled.json` + `metrics.realized.json` |
| `tree` | one worktree's review history | that tree's `.log/bhrain/review/**` |
| `grove` | every tree discoverable on this machine | the same, joined |

the axis is `breadth`, never `scope` — `term=review.scope` already owns `scope` for the file
set one review reads (rules + refs + targets), a different concept at a different layer
(`rule.forbid.domain-term-ambiguity`).

## .dimension 2 — `quantity` (2 positions)

| position | the question | why it is its own axis |
|---|---|---|
| `chars` | *"what was the prompt MADE OF?"* | knowable **before** the brain is called; never changes |
| `cost` | *"what did it CHARGE?"* | knowable **only after**; carries the honesty grade |

genuinely independent: a prompt's composition is a fact about the prompt, its cost a fact
about the call — which is why `metrics.compiled.json` is a peer of `metrics.realized.json`
rather than a field inside it.

`cost` carries an honesty **grade**:

| grade | what it means | how it renders |
|---|---|---|
| `metered` | the cost was measured | the figure |
| `refused` | the zero is a **fact** — the brain charged naught | `$0` |
| `unknown` | the cost was **unrecorded** | **absent** — never `$0.00` |

### .why `grade` is a sub-property and not a fourth axis

not because `chars` cannot carry it — the rule handles that with `impossible` cells. the
real reason is **collinearity with `intent`**, and the rule's own orthogonality bar refuses
a correlated pair:

| the `intent` position | the grades it is about |
|---|---|
| `observe` | `metered` — read the figure and act on it |
| `defend` | `refused` and `unknown` — be caught when the figure would have lied |

`intent` IS the grade axis, read from the reader's seat rather than the datum's. promote
`grade` beside it and every `cost × observe × {refused,unknown}` cell is a contradiction,
while `cost × defend × metered` is empty by construction. a walked product whose cells are
settled by a related axis is not a decomposition — it is one axis, written twice.

the grade is still surfaced, in the place a walked product surfaces things: `case=2` and
`case=5` are the two `cost × defend` demos, and they exist because of it.

## .dimension 3 — `intent` (3 positions)

| position | what the reader wants |
|---|---|
| `observe` | read the number and act on it |
| `defend` | be caught when the number would have lied |
| `trace` | see whether a change moved the number |

`defend` is the position easy to miss and costliest to skip: an instrument built only for
`observe` prints a plausible number over a corrupt measurement, and nobody can tell.

### .why `trace` is a third position, and not a flavour of `observe`

the bar this file sets for any axis is that a related axis must not settle it. `trace`
clears it by measurement, not by taxonomy:

> a surface can serve `observe` perfectly and destroy `trace`. `byReviewer` averages a
> lane's rounds, so the series `0, 0, 8.4, 41.2, 7.3` reports as one 29.7% — a correct
> number, and the step that proves a lever fired is gone from it.

an `observe` reader wants *"what did this cost"*; a `trace` reader wants *"did my repair
move it"*, and the same measured datum answers one and cannot answer the other. a marginal
ranks; a cross-tab traces. no re-sort of a marginal recovers the series, because the fold
that made it summed the axis away.

the walk that first omitted `trace` is why `case=6 [t3]` sat open with no surface owed: its
`[t3]` — re-drive a stone after a repair, assert the second round's cache share exceeds the
first's — is not an observe act, so the catalog carried the demo but named no cell for it. an
unnamed position does not merely leave a cell empty; it hides the absence of the instrument
that cell would have required.

`trace` does not track `breadth`: it is impossible at `run` (one invocation has no
second point) and real at both `tree` and `grove`. both `tree` cells are served, by two
different instruments — `tree × cost × trace` by `--trace`'s cross-tab, `tree × chars × trace`
by `rhx census.review-shared-prefix`, which reads the byte prefix consecutive rounds share.

## .dimension 4 — `agency` (2 positions)

added when the wisher ruled that a guard must warm its own review caches.

| position | who acts | when |
|---|---|---|
| `driver` | a human invokes a surface and reads what it says | at the moment they ask |
| `engine` | the system acts on its own, **between** invocations | while nobody watches |

### .why this is an axis

the sharper point is what it reveals about the other three: every one of the 18 cells above
assumes a human asked a question. `breadth` is how far *their* figure reaches, `quantity` is
what *they* asked for, `intent` is what *they* wanted. the reader was a constant, so it was
invisible. a constant is not an axis until a case varies it — the warmer varies it: it
spends real money, on a timer, with no human in the loop and no invocation to attach the
spend to. the prime meet varies it a second time: lanes that share a prefix elect one primer
with no driver in the loop.

the `defend` column is where the two positions come apart hardest. a `driver` cost that goes
unrecorded is a figure someone asked for and did not get — a nuisance, bounded by their
attention. an `engine` cost that goes unrecorded is **money spent that nobody knows was
spent**, and it is unbounded by construction, because the party who would notice never
invoked a surface.

`engine` is not a flavour of `intent`: intent is what a reader wants from a figure, agency is
who caused the figure to exist. an engine act can be observed, defended against, and traced
— so the two axes are crossed, never nested.

## .the product

3 × 2 × 3 × 2 = **36 cells**. the `driver` half is the 18 walked above; the `engine` half is
walked in `case=_`. an engine act has no `trace` at `run` breadth for the same reason the
driver half does not. the engine ACTS at `run` breadth alone — a warmer is spawned by one
guard, for one lane, on one tree, and no mechanism warms across trees. but the read of its
spend rolls up: the warmer value verdict renders at `tree` and `grove`. a warmer replays one
lane's prefix, so no composition exists past `run`, and every `{tree, grove} × chars` cell is
impossible.

so the engine half holds 10 real cells (6 served, 4 unserved) and 8 impossible:
`run × * × trace` and `{tree, grove} × chars × *`. that asymmetry is
the point rather than a weakness: an axis that generated a full, symmetric product would be
suspect, and this one marks precisely where the system is allowed to act alone.

two cells are impossible for the same shape: `run × {chars, cost} × trace` asks whether a
change moved a figure across a corpus of one invocation. there is no second point to
difference against, so the cell is unaskable rather than unserved.

the walk found four unserved cells and they shared one column: `chars` above `run` breadth —
a reader could attribute one prompt's chars to eight named inputs and could not ask *"across
this tree, what share of chars is target files?"*; the roll-ups carried tokens, never
composition. the honest fill was a fold of the `metrics.compiled.json` files every run
already writes, with no new measurement owed; it landed at `grove` and then at `tree`, one
render shared by both, and filled all four.

| state | cells — `agency = driver`, 18 of 36 |
|---|---|
| served | 8 + `{grove, tree} × chars × {observe, defend}` + `tree × {cost, chars} × trace` = **14** |
| unserved | `grove × {chars, cost} × trace` = **2** |
| impossible | `run × {chars, cost} × trace` = **2** |

across both halves: **20 served · 6 unserved · 10 impossible · 0 forbidden = 36.** the engine
half contributes 6 served (one of them `case=7`), 4 unserved, and 8 impossible.

served and demoed are different columns, and the sum only closes when they are kept apart:
all 7 demoed cells are served, and 13 of the 19 itemized are too — `7 + 13 = 20 served`,
against `19 itemized = 13 served + 6 unserved`.

the `trace` column opened with one of its four askable cells already served — `--trace`
shipped the same day the position was named. the surface was built, and only then did the
walk acquire a cell for it — a decomposition that lags its own instrument set cannot surface
an absence, which is the same failure the first walk made with its `question` axis, below.

the fill was cheap for the same reason the grove one was: `getAllBucketsByKey` and
`asReviewMeterBucket` already served three marginals, so the cross-tab needed a key and a
sort option — no new arithmetic and no new measurement. and the walk is what made the fill
cheap: the product named the two grove cells before any surface existed, so when a peer
review later forced a fold of `metrics.compiled.json` for an unrelated reason, the cells it
filled were already named and verdicted.

## .the first walk drew its axes around the tools it had

it used `question` as an axis, with a `declared` position that existed only because
`review.corpus` filled it. that is not a decomposition — it is an inventory of what was
built, with an axis fitted after the fact, and it cannot surface an absence, because every
position was defined by a tool already in it.

the rewalk drops that axis and four unserved cells appear at once. the retired position was
a stand-in for exactly the column that turned out empty — answered by estimate from declared
guard globs rather than measurement of what rendered, which is why it drifted (`F3`).

half that column is now filled by measurement, which is the vindication rather than a
footnote: the same question `review.corpus` answered by estimate, `--of grove` now answers
from `metrics.compiled.json` — what the renderer actually sent, per run, summed. the axis was
right to retire; the question under it was right to keep.

## .the bound — this space enumerates reports, never every experience

**every one of the four axes is about a figure**: `agency` is who caused it, `breadth` is how
far it reaches, `quantity` is which figure, `intent` is why a reader wants it. so the product
enumerates **report** experiences — a reader and a number — and it is complete over exactly
that. it is **not** a census of every experience in this behavior, and the clamped classes
below prove it:

| the experience | its clamp | why no cell holds it |
|---|---|---|
| a driver discovers `--warmer off`, sees which warmers are still awake, and can refuse a spender | `reviewWarmerFlag.acceptance.test` · `reviewWarmerSilence.acceptance.test` · `asReviewWarmerPlan.test` · `emitReviewWarmersAwake.integration.test` · `setReviewWarmerOfRound.integration.test` | a **control** experience. a flag has no breadth and reports no figure |
| an abandoned lock is cleared, so a lane is not left cold forever | `genReviewWarmer.integration.test [case5]` · `emitReviewWarmerDisowned.integration.test` | a **liveness** experience. no question was asked, and no number is returned |
| a lane slug cannot plant a file outside the tree's cache dir | `asReviewWarmerPaths.test [case2]` — proven red at `etc/cron.d/x` | a **containment** experience. the slug reaches `asReviewWarmerDir` from a filename via a `.+` capture, so it carries separators; no question, no figure |
| concurrent lanes meet, one primes the shared prefix, a prime fault pours cold and never hangs | `genReviewPrefixPrime.integration.test` · `asPartOfMeet.test` · `withPrimeFaultSurvived.test` · `asReviewPrimeFaultLine.test` · `getOneReviewPrimeMeetStopMs.test` · `asEnvWithReviewPrimeMeet.test` · `setOneReviewLanePrime.integration.test` | a **liveness** experience. its spend is a run (`case=_`, the engine half); the meet itself asks no question and returns no number |
| a driver previews, declares, and is refused for an unclaimed file in a partition set; a partition fault prints its own section | `review.partition.acceptance.test` · `driver.route.partition-journey.acceptance.test` · `driver.route.guard-partition.acceptance.test` · `getAllStoneGuardPartitionGaps.integration.test` · `getAllStoneGuardPartitionCoverage.integration.test` · `stepReviewByPartitions.integration.test` · `asPartitionFaultSection.test` · `asPartitionFaultLaneSection.test` | a **control** experience. a declaration has no breadth; the per-partition cost in the plan is the `run × cost` cell, read before the spend |
| `onStop` stays quiet while a fresh repairs-in-flight watermark names the stone, and nudges past 60m | `routeDriveHelp.test` snapshot | a **liveness** experience. a timer acts; no figure |
| a driver meets a malfunctioned lane and reads "still yours to clear" with its levers | `routeStoneSetFeedbackAbsorption.acceptance.test [case8]` | a **guidance** experience. it names a remedy, never a number |
| a caller-fixable constraint renders `✋ ConstraintError:` with its cause and fix; a bare value flag sets naught and is named | `asOpenerFailureNote.test` · `asConstraintErrorHeader.test` · `asConstraintErrorHeadline.test` · `asTreeCauseLines.test` · `asCliOptionsOfArgs.test` | a **refusal** experience. no breadth, no figure |
| a shipped skill runs when typed, and a review reaches its brain | `agentSkillsExecutable.integration.test` · `blackboxBrainLinks.integration.test` | an **install integrity** experience. a failure here is loud before any figure exists |

`asExecResultOrThrow.test` and `contract/cli/index.integration.test` guard the test harness and
the lazy-load seam; no actor meets them, so no row is owed.

the honest disposition is to declare the bound, never to force-fit a position: a `quantity =
control` would make the product incoherent (`control × grove × trace` asks whether a change
moved a flag across every tree on the machine, which is not a question). an axis that
generates meaningless cells is a relabel of another axis — the same test `agency` had to
pass. per `rule.require.experience-catalog-evolution`'s own caveat: *"a discovery that is
genuinely out of the wish's bounded scope is flagged to the wisher, not force-fit into the
contract."* these sit inside the wish (bars 13, 14, 15) and outside this space.

`rule.require.dimensional-decomposition` earns completeness "by construction" from the
walked product — so a reader who takes `36 cells` for *"every warmer experience, verdicted"*
would conclude the control, liveness, refusal, and integrity surfaces were never examined. each
was, with clamps,
and their absence here is a property of the axes rather than a coverage gap. a complete space
still owes a statement of what it is complete over.
