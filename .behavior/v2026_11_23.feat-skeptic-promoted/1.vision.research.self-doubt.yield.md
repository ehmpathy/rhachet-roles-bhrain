# research yield: make the agent doubt its own thought (not block its acts)

All sources read 2026-11-26. Most reads are web search summaries; a figure from a summary alone is marked `(search-summary)`. A claim with no source is marked `guess:`.

## 1. bottom line

- Doubt that is fed only by the model's own view does not help, and often harms. Doubt helps when it is fed by a signal the model did not author: a tool result, a fresh independent context, a stronger critic, or the later outcome of a commitment the model made earlier.
- Human debias work agrees on the mechanism: "consider the opposite" beats "be unbiased" because it forces a search that the first view skipped. A bare reminder to be careful does not.
- The best fit for this role is a ledger of commitments (state a guess, then compare to the outcome) plus a fresh-context counter-take injected as context at a few commit points. Both feed the doubt with an outside signal and neither blocks.
- A bare prompt of the form "are you sure?" or "could you be wrong?" has no measured gain in the sources found, and in one study a human-style debias prompt did not remove a bias and made it stronger.

## 2. does self-correction work with no new outside information?

- Huang et al. 2023 (arXiv 2310.01798): after intrinsic self-correction (no outside feedback), accuracy fell on every benchmark tried. Llama-2 on GSM8K fell 62.0% to 36.5%; GPT-3.5 on CommonSenseQA fell 66.8% to 55.4%. Models flip right answers to wrong at a rate near or above the rate they fix wrong ones. Earlier gains used oracle labels that told the model when to stop. Authors' diagnosis: the model cannot judge whether its own answer is correct. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01798 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Kamoi et al. 2024 (TACL): no prior work shows success of self-correction fed by a prompted LLM, except on tasks unusually suited to it; self-correction works when reliable outside feedback exists (tools, knowledge) and after large-scale fine-tune. Many positive reports used unfair set-ups (a weak first answer, or oracle stop signals). Sources: https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/ and https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Olausson et al. (ICLR 2024), code: self-repair is held back by the quality of the model's feedback on its own code; a stronger model as the critic, or human feedback, gave much larger gains; GPT-4's own feedback was inaccurate in 32 of 80 sampled cases; gains are modest and variable once the cost of repair is counted. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09896 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023): 91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80.1% for GPT-4, but the loop is fed by unit tests (self-written, up to six) and env reward; self-written tests gave more false-positive labels on MBPP than on HumanEval. The gain relies on an outside signal and its noise. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11366 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Self-Refine (Madaan et al. 2023): about 20% absolute average gain over 7 tasks (averaged over three models); the gain is large on style tasks judged by a model (dialogue 25.4% to 74.6% by GPT-4 preference) and small on tasks with a hard answer (code optimize +8.7 points). A later paper ties the modest gains on logic tasks to the model's weak ability to find its own errors. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651 and https://github.com/PatrickWiloak/ai-zero-to-hero/blob/main/papers/techniques/99-self-refine/summary.md (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Newer RL-trained thinker models: the picture is mixed and mostly cautious. A study of over 1M traces finds mid-trace "shifts" are symptoms of unstable runs, not a real fix path (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00514v2); vision-language RL models rarely show a real wrong-to-right switch (https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/93397b489222f1e66a862f795fc32b96-Paper-Conference.pdf); one paper does find a hidden critique skill in R1 models on GSM8K-Error (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16331v1). A HackMD note says o3-mini has near-zero error-introduction; unchecked, treat as `guess:` (https://hackmd.io/@BE82wIFmQzuVS__XrwkYWA/HJBPK8IrGg). All read 2026-11-26 (search-summary).
- What the doubt must be fed: (1) a tool or env result (Kamoi, Reflexion); (2) a stronger or separate critic (Olausson); (3) for fact lists, independent re-ask of narrow questions (CoVe, below). Absent these, the sources show no reliable gain.

## 3. techniques that make a model doubt its output: measured effect

- Chain-of-Verification (Dhuliawala 2023): draft, plan check questions, answer them in separate prompts (factored), revise. List-question precision more than doubled for Llama 65B (0.17 to 0.36). The factored form beat the joint form because the check answers are not tied to the draft's own errors; yes/no check questions did worse than open ones. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11495v1 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). Takeaway: the doubt works when each check is posed with the draft hidden.
- Self-consistency (Wang et al. 2022): sample many, take the majority; PaLM-540B on GSM8K 56.5% to 74.4%. It is a vote, not a self-critique, and it needs a task with one checkable answer. Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359390115_Self-Consistency_Improves_Chain_of_Thought_Reasoning_in_Language_Models (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). Cost is the sample count; it does not fit open-ended agent work (`guess:`).
- Multi-agent debate: at an equal count of answers it did worse than a plain majority vote (Huang et al.); later work says a vote explains most of the gain; debate between identical models raised confidence from 64.1% to 75.2%, so it can add overconfidence. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01798, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19184, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19921v3 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Critic models: CriticGPT-style trained critics caught more bugs than paid human reviewers, and their critiques were preferred over human ones in 63% of cases; this is a trained critic that helps a human, not a fresh-context copy of the same model. Source: https://www.alphaxiv.org/resources/2407.00215v1 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). No source found that measures a same-model fresh-context reviewer; judge models are known to favor their own output (self-preference) per https://leiwang84.com/2026/10/12/multi-agent-debate-failure/ (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- "Could you be wrong?" prompt (Hills 2025, arXiv 2507.10124): evidence is qualitative (three demo cases, no control prompt in the original); a secondary site gives big drops that the paper text did not confirm. A separate study on negotiation found neither a perspective-take nor a debias chain-of-thought prompt removed the anchor bias, and the theory-based prompt made it stronger. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10124 and https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2026.2732484 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Verbalized confidence: models are systematically overconfident when they state a number (Xiong et al.); a prompt advisory about overconfidence is the main lever in one 2026 paper, so a stated confidence is a weak soft signal at best. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10996v1 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19184 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Net for this repo: an in-context "doubt yourself" nudge has no reliable measured gain; the gains seen are tied to an outside signal.

## 4. human debias with measured effect on overconfidence

- Consider-the-opposite: Lord, Lepper & Preston 1984 (JPSP 47, 1231), two experiments: the instruction to consider the opposite cut biased reads of new evidence more than the instruction to be fair and unbiased. Mechanism per a summary: the bias is a one-sided memory search and the prompt forces the skipped search. Source: cited and summarized in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10124 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary); the original page (a ResearchGate copy) was seen only in search results.
- Pre-mortem: Mitchell, Russo & Pennington 1989 (prospective hindsight): imagine the outcome as certain and explain it; about 30% more reasons generated, but the study counted reasons, not their quality (Klein's popular "30% more correct" is a mis-quote). Klein, Veinott & Wiggins study (178 students, a flu plan): the pre-mortem gave the largest cut in overconfidence vs a plain critique or a pros-and-cons list; a secondary source says about twice the cut of pros-and-cons (single secondary source). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-mortem and https://resources.rework.com/libraries/project-management/pre-mortem (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Calibration drill and score: a drill of under one hour improved forecast accuracy (Brier) 6 to 11% over control across the Good Judgment Project years and cut overconfidence, per Chang, Chen, Mellers & Tetlock 2016. Source: https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~baron/journal/16/16511/jdm16511.pdf (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). The loop that does the work is: state a probability, see the outcome, see the score.
- Predict before you look: a guess before the answer improves later recall (pretest effect, Kornell 2009); a guess made ahead raised surprise at the true answer (pupil size), which fits a prediction-error account, vs a guess made after the fact. The learner under-rates the benefit. Sources: https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-01904-1 and https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292081/ (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). Not found: a direct test that a prior guess cuts overconfidence.
- Common thread: each human method either forces a second search path (opposite, pre-mortem) or ties a committed guess to a later outcome (calibration, predict-first). None is a bare reminder.

## 5. mechanical delivery without flags that cry wolf

Principle from the alert-fatigue yield (1.vision.research.enforcement.yield.md): people and agents tune out a signal that is wrong often. Here the delivery is context, not a block, so a wrong counter-take costs one read, not a stop. The residue risk is rote use (aviation: challenge-response decays to a reflex reply when it costs no effort; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910017830/downloads/19910017830.pdf, read 2026-11-26, search-summary). Each design below has a rule to keep the doubt priced.

### A. predict-then-check ledger
- What it is: before a run whose result is not certain (a test, a probe, a diagnosis step), the agent records a one-line guess of the outcome (`expect: exit 0` or `expect: fails with X`) in a typed field or tool call. A PostToolUse hook compares the guess to the result (exit code, a named token in output) and, only on a mismatch, injects one line: "you expected X, got Y".
- Outside signal: the tool result itself, a recorded fact; the compare is on exit code or a token, not on reply text.
- Why it can resist rote: the guess is the agent's own and the score is real; a lazy `expect: ok` on every call shows up in the score (a hit rate near the base rate). A surprise line is rare when the guess is honest and is true by construction (a mismatch did occur), so it is not a false flag.
- Cost: one short line per tool call that matters; a hook run of milliseconds; no model call. Risk: the agent can guess the safe outcome each time; limit by confining the ledger to a closed set of runs (tests, builds, probes) and by the scorecard in C.
- Strongest source: pretest and predict-first work (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-01904-1) for why a prior guess makes the miss register; Reflexion for the outside-signal loop (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11366). `guess:` effect size on an LLM agent is unmeasured.

### B. fresh-context critic at commit points
- What it is: at a commit point (a diagnosis stated, a plan chosen, a first failed fix), a separate model call gets only the artifact and the question, with the agent's own chain of logic hidden, and returns a counter-take of a fixed shape: the strongest alternative cause and the one cheap check that would tell them apart. A hook injects it as added context. It never blocks.
- Outside signal: a context that did not author the claim (factored CoVe logic: checks answered with the draft hidden); a stronger model for the critic if cost allows (Olausson: a stronger critic gave larger gains).
- Why it can resist rote: the counter-take names a concrete check, so to follow it is a tool call, not a reflex reply; fire it only at a closed set of commit points so it is rare.
- Cost: one extra model call per commit point; the counter-take is kept to a few lines. Risk: a same-model critic favors its own family (self-preference) and a debate form can raise confidence; use the separate-context form, not a chat between two copies.
- Strongest source: https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11495v1 (factored form beat joint) and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09896 (critic quality limits the gain). No source tests a same-model fresh-context reviewer; `guess:` the gain is modest and task bound.

### C. calibration scorecard
- What it is: an offline or per-session tally from the ledger in A: hit rate of guesses, and for the agent's stated confidence, a Brier score, shown at session start (UserPromptSubmit) as two lines plus the three largest misses.
- Outside signal: outcomes from the ledger.
- Why it can resist rote: it is a score of the agent's own past guesses, not a rule to obey; the drill that worked for people (Chang et al.) is exactly state, see outcome, see score.
- Cost: low; data from A. Risk: with few entries the score is noise; show it only past a floor count (`guess:` 20).
- Strongest source: https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~baron/journal/16/16511/jdm16511.pdf (6 to 11% Brier gain from a short drill, for people). Not tested for LLM agents. The agent has no memory of prior sessions unless the scorecard is placed in context, which the hook does.

### D. consider-the-opposite and pre-mortem as a forced second search
- What it is: at the same commit points as B, a typed prompt requests "two other causes that would produce the same symptom, and the check for each" (consider the opposite), or "assume this plan failed; list the three likeliest reasons" (pre-mortem), with the answer slots in a typed form.
- Outside signal: none by itself; it is a second search path from the model's own store. So it is weak by the Huang result unless a slot demands a check that is run. Strengthen by a link from each listed cause to a probe, with the probe result recorded in the ledger (A).
- Why it can resist rote: slots need a named check, and the probe result is recorded; a list that is never probed shows in the ledger.
- Cost: a few lines of output per commit point.
- Strongest source: the human evidence via https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10124 (cites Lord et al.); the LLM test of a debias prompt was null or worse (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2026.2732484). So rank it below A and B.

### E. verification questions posed with the draft hidden (CoVe at the reply edge)
- What it is: before a reply with a stated cause or state, a helper call is given only the narrow questions ("what does `git log origin/x -1` show?"), not the draft, and the tool or model answers; mismatches with the draft are injected as context.
- Outside signal: the independent answer; better if it is a tool call, which is then the plain re-derive of a fact.
- Cost: one call per checked claim; use only for the few typed claim kinds.
- Strongest source: https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11495v1 and the deterministic verifier https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464 (0 of 1,825 healthy runs flagged).

## 6. ranked mechanisms for skepticism of one's own thought

1. Predict-then-check ledger with a mismatch line (A). The outside signal is a recorded result; the surprise line is true by construction; cheap. Supported by the pretest and predict-first work and by the Reflexion loop; effect on an LLM agent is untested.
2. Fresh-context counter-take at commit points, injected as context (B), with the draft hidden. Supported by factored CoVe and by the critic-quality result; same-model fresh-context is untested.
3. Calibration scorecard (C), fed by A. Supported by the human drill data (6 to 11% Brier gain); needs the ledger to exist.
4. Question-with-draft-hidden check on typed claims (E). Strong where the answer is a tool call; narrow scope.
5. Consider-the-opposite or pre-mortem slots linked to probes (D). Good human evidence, null or worse in the LLM test found; useful only as a feeder for A.

## 7. what the evidence says does NOT work

- Bare self-review prompts with no outside signal: "check your answer", "are you sure", "could you be wrong" (Huang 2023; Kamoi 2024; the Hills paper has no measured gain).
- Debias prompts copied from human work, with no probe: no removal of anchor bias, one case worse (tandfonline link above).
- Multi-agent debate between copies of one model: no better than a vote at equal count and it can raise confidence.
- A stated confidence number taken at face value: models are overconfident.
- A same-model judge of its own output: self-preference and a weak ability to judge its own correctness.
- A reminder repeated after each miss: no source supports it as a fix; the Replit case shows orders ignored (https://rafter.so/blog/incidents/replit-agent-trust-and-guardrails, read 2026-11-26, search-summary).
- A confirm step that costs no effort: it decays to rote (NASA report above).

## 8. gaps

- No test found of a predict-then-check ledger on an LLM code agent; the whole of A and C rests on human work and on the tool-loop logic of Reflexion. A replay over saved transcripts, to score how often an honest guess mismatches, would be the first test.
- No test of a same-model fresh-context reviewer for a code agent.
- The 30% figure for pre-mortem is a count of reasons, not accuracy; the "twice the cut" figure is one secondary source.
- Several arXiv items carry 2026 ids and were read through search summaries only.
