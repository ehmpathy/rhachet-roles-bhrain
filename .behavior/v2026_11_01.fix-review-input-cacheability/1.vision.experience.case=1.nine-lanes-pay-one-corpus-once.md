# case=1 — nine lanes pay one corpus once

## .narrative

the driver finishes a repair at `5.1.execution.from_vision`, two hours after its last round, and
types `rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution.from_vision --as arrived`. the terminal shows the
guard pour level 1: nine reviewer lanes, each with its own spinner. a few minutes later nine
verdicts land, the driver answers them, and it moves on.

**before.** the next day the driver runs `rhx review._.cost --of tree` to see what the stone cost.
the `5.1` round reads **0.8% cached**. the round paid for its 60 target files nine times: each
lane's prompt opened with its own rubric, so the bytes nine lanes shared began behind the first
byte two lanes did not.

**after.** the same arrival, the same nine spinners, the same nine verdicts. one difference shows,
and only in the meter: the stone's `5.1` round now reads most of its corpus from cache, because
eight of the nine lanes compute one head — the instructions, the output format, the targets — and
each lane's own rubric, refs, and conversation trail it. the first lane pokes that head; the other
seven read it back. the prior round, two hours old, contributes naught; the peers alone suffice.
with no affinity pin in the adapter, which replica each lane meets is still chance; the pin is a
dispatch owed to `rhachet-brains-openrouter` in `5.1`.

the aha is in what did NOT change: the driver typed the same command, waited the same wait, and read
the same verdicts. only the bill moved.

## .[tn] sketch

```
given a guard level of 9 lanes, 8 of which bind one target corpus
  and the lanes' prior round ran past the cache reach
  when the level pours
    [t0] each lane compiles its prompt
      then the 8 shared-corpus lanes compute one byte-identical head
      then the head holds the instructions, the output format, and the targets
      then each lane's rubric, refs, and conversation sit in its tail
    [t1] the first lane to claim the head's watermark leads: it finds peers and sends one poke
      then the other lanes follow: they wait on the watermark, never on a lock
    [t2] all 9 lanes ask the brain
      then the 8 shared-corpus lanes send one byte-identical system prompt
      then those that meet the primed replica read the target mass at the cache rate
  when the reviewer reads its prompt
    then it holds the same rules, refs, targets, and conversation as before (`case=4`)
    then its verdict contract is unchanged
```

the branch where a lane misses — a cold meet, or a replica the poke never reached — is its own
spotlight, `case=5`.

## .the measure that proves it

`rhx review._.cost --of tree` on a route whose `5.1` level pours 9 lanes: the by-round cache share
of each `5.1` round, before and after. an offline clamp asserts the head of two lanes with different
rubrics over one corpus is byte-identical, and fails on today's layout.
