## .settled — the driver's derivation, not a wisher ruling

**an inventory's axes emerge from its entries.** the mechanism is comparison: entries accrue on
whatever axis is declared so far — often just `case=` — you set two side by side and ask *"what
differs?"*, and each answer is a dimension.

three claims the article had implied the opposite of:

| the article said | it now says |
|---|---|
| *"generated before entries are collected"* | before **the next** entry, against the axes as currently declared |
| test 1 as a one-time pass/fail gate | *"can you name them **now**?"*, re-asked as the set grows |
| *"decomposition finds the space"* | decomposition finds it from the axes down; comparison finds it from the entries up |

### the shape of the fix, and why this one

the correction **adds an inverse**; it does not relax the rule:

| the box is built | by | when |
|---|---|---|
| **forwards** — axes first, cells generated | dimensional decomposition | the shape is known up front |
| **backwards** — cells first, axes inferred | comparison across accrued entries | the shape is learned as entries arrive |

⇒ a relaxed article would have blurred dimensional decomposition; a named inverse keeps it intact
and gives the second direction its own name.

### the cost, kept explicit

**a newly discovered dimension changes every entry's coordinates — so it is a rename of the whole
set.** that rename is mechanical only because the axes sit declared on one page.

⇒ **this is the argument for the summary that the article had lacked.** an inventory whose axes
live in the author's head cannot be re-walked at all.
