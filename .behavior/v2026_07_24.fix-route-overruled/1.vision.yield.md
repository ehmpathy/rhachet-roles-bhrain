# 1.vision — fix: an overruled lower level must not let a driver skip a ready higher level

> wish: "drivers can mark `--as passed` and skip the l3 reviewers if the l1 reviewers were
> overruled. that shouldn't be possible. they should be forced to continue through the guard
> reviews if l3 is ready after l1 is terminal." + "cover with exhaustive boundary cases."
>
> context from wisher: **this is a regression from ~2 days ago**. the ~2-day marker points at
> #335 `b0d32de` (2026-07-24, "fix(guard): exhausted peer level unlocks the next"), which made a
> ready higher level *reachable* after a terminal lower level. but the **structural passage hole**
> it exposes — the judge's file-based, level-forgiven tally — predates it: `reviewFilesToCount`
> was introduced by **#294 (`1e7c620`, 2026-07-04)**. #335 widened the blast radius; #294 dug the
> hole. (not fully bisected — the exact runtime trigger is the execution stone's red-repro job.)

---

## the one-line vision

**every peer-review guard, at every level, must be terminal before a stone can pass.** a terminal
*prior* level (overruled, exhausted, malfunctioned, or constrained) does NOT let you skip the reviews
above it — to unlock the next level is not to clear it. a reviewer still **queued / ready /
rejected** (non-terminal) is a hard blocker, never a silent zero.

> wisher, 2026-07-24: "they should be forced to go through all reviews, regardless of whether a
> prior level is terminal … all reviewers must be terminal before a stone can be moved past …
> you can't pass a stone until all the peer review guards are terminal." — **this is the core
> invariant.**

the ladder still *orders* execution (a dearer higher level runs only once the cheaper lower level is
terminal), but the order is not the gate — **all reviewers terminal** is the gate.

## the outcome world

### day-in-the-life (with the fix)

a driver is stuck: the l1 reviewer (`basic-checker`) perma-rejects a plan it deems flawed, but
the human judges the reviewer overzealous.

```
human> rhx route.stone.set --stone 1.plan --route . --as overruled
🦉 ...
   ├─ level 1, overruled
   └─ level 3, ready          # ← the l1 overrule UNLOCKS l3; l3 is now the live gate

driver> rhx route.stone.set --stone 1.plan --route . --as passed
🦉 ...
   ├─ l1  basic-checker    overruled ✓   (human waved it through)
   ├─ l3  premium-checker  1/5  rejected  🔴 2 blockers
   └─ passage = blocked      # ← l3 RAN, found real blockers → the stone does NOT pass
```

the driver keeps driving l3 — fixes the code, re-arrives, converges the l3 reviewer — and only
**then** does the stone pass:

```
driver> rhx route.stone.set --stone 1.plan --route . --as passed
🦉 ...
   ├─ l1  basic-checker    overruled ✓
   ├─ l3  premium-checker  2/5  approved ✓  0 blockers
   └─ passage = allowed
```

### before / after contrast

| moment | before (regressed) | after (fixed) |
|---|---|---|
| l1 overruled, l3 ready, driver runs `--as passed` | stone can pass while l3 is unproven — l3 skipped | passage blocked until l3 is **approved** or **overruled** |
| what the human trusts when they overrule l1 | "the whole stone is waved" (wrong — they only waved l1) | "l1 is waved; l3 still guards" (right) |
| the meaning of an overrule | leaks upward: forgives l1 AND silently forgives the not-yet-run l3 | scoped: forgives exactly the level named, unlocks the next, clears no level above |

### the "aha"

**an overrule is a key for one gate, not a skeleton key for the corridor.** waving l1 through
opens the door to l3; it does not walk you past l3. the review ladder is a *sequence of gates*,
and each gate is passed on its own terms.

## user experience

### usecases & goals

- **driver** — wants to make honest forward progress. goal: never accidentally pass a stone whose
  higher-level reviews are unproven; be *forced* to drive l3 to a real verdict.
- **human (foreman)** — wants to wave a *specific* overzealous reviewer without lowering the bar
  for the rest of the ladder. goal: overrule l1 and trust that l3 still guards.
- **reviewer author** — wants the ladder's guarantee to hold: "my l3 rule cannot be bypassed by
  someone overruling a cheaper l1 rule."

### contract inputs & outputs

the surface is unchanged — the fix tightens the **passage decision**, not the CLI shape:

| input | meaning |
|---|---|
| `rhx route.stone.set --stone <s> --route <r> --as passed` | driver: "work done, try to pass the guard" |
| `rhx route.stone.set --stone <s> --route <r> --as overruled` | human: "wave the **active** level through" (level-scoped) |
| `rhx route.stone.set ... --as arrived` | driver: "re-run the guard reviews, converge" |

| output token | meaning (unchanged, but now honest) |
|---|---|
| `passage = allowed` | every unlocked level approved (or cleanly overruled) |
| `passage = overruled` | passage was *enabled by* an overrule, and no un-overruled level blocks |
| `passage = blocked` | **at least one unlocked level is not yet approved/overruled** ← now fires for a ready l3 |
| `level 1, overruled` / `level 3, ready` | overrule confirmation names the waved level + the unlocked-next level |

### the invariant, stated as a contract

> **PASS ⟺ every peer-review guard, at every level, is terminal.** terminal =
> `approved | exhausted | malfunction | constraint | overruled`. any reviewer still
> **`queued` / `ready` / `rejected`** (non-terminal) blocks the stone.
>
> a terminal *prior* level does **not** substitute for a later one: `l1` terminal (by any route —
> overrule, exhaustion, malfunction, constraint) unlocks `l3` but leaves the stone blocked until
> `l3` is *also* terminal. the ladder orders *when* a reviewer runs; the gate is that *all* have run
> to terminal.

**per-verdict consequences (unchanged) once all are terminal**: `approved` clears; `exhausted` →
human approval; `malfunction`/`constraint` → human overrule; `overruled` → forgiven. the fix adds
the missing precondition — you cannot even reach those consequences while any reviewer is
non-terminal.

the regression violated this by letting a terminal `l1` (overruled) pass the stone while `l3` was
still non-terminal — an *unrun / ready* higher guard counted as a silent zero.

### timeline the driver goes through (fixed)

```
l1 rejects ─▶ driver stuck ─▶ human overrules l1 ─▶ l3 becomes READY
     └─▶ driver --as passed ─▶ l3 RUNS ─▶ l3 rejects ─▶ passage = BLOCKED
     └─▶ driver fixes code, --as arrived ─▶ l3 re-runs ─▶ l3 approves ─▶ passage = ALLOWED
```

## mental model

- **to a friend**: "someone let me skip the easy reviewer, and the tool wrongly let me skip the
  *hard* reviewer too. now it makes me actually pass the hard one."
- **analogy**: airport security. a supervisor waving you past the *boarding-pass check* (l1) does
  not wave you past the *metal detector* (l3). each checkpoint clears on its own.
- **analogy 2**: a ladder. cutting a lower rung's requirement lets you *reach* the next rung; it
  doesn't teleport you to the top.
- **terms**: users say *"skip"* / *"waved through"*; we say **overrule** (level-scoped forgiveness),
  **terminal** (a level that no longer blocks *unlock*), **ready** (unlocked but not yet passed),
  **clear-for-passage** (approved or overruled). the key distinction the fix leans on:
  **terminal-for-unlock ≠ clear-for-passage** — this distinction already lives in the code
  (`computeReviewLevels.ts`: `isReviewLevelClearForPassage` vs `isReviewPeerLevelTerminal`), the
  passage judge simply failed to honor it for a ready higher level.

## evaluation

### how well it solves the goals

- **directly closes the wish**: A1/A2 (the reported skip) flip to BLOCKED; the driver is *forced*
  to continue through l3.
- **restores the ladder guarantee**: an overrule can never lower the bar for a level the human did
  not name.
- **surgical**: the CLI, the overrule mechanic, and the unlock/tier-escalation behavior all stay —
  only the passage decision for a *ready-but-unproven higher level* changes.

### pros

- honors the pre-extant **terminal-for-unlock ≠ clear-for-passage** distinction the codebase
  already models — the fix is "make the passage judge respect a distinction that already lives here."
- pit-of-success: the default (bare `--as passed`) does the safe thing (block on unproven l3); the
  unsafe thing (skip l3) requires a *deliberate* second overrule (C2).
- exhaustive boundary coverage means the next regression here is caught by a red test.

### cons / costs

- a driver who *wants* to wave the whole ladder now needs an explicit overrule per level (C2) —
  slightly more ceremony, but that is the point (safety over convenience).
- more acceptance journeys to author and snapshot (the wish asked for exhaustive coverage — this
  is the intended cost).

### pit-of-success edgecases

- **A1 (ready l3)**: bare `--as passed` blocks — the driver cannot *accidentally* skip. ✓
- **B4 (legacy overrule)**: preserved on purpose so old routes stay usable; it is opt-in via a
  level-less marker, not a default. **shipped scope**: the level-less marker maps to the judge rung
  (`JUDGE_LEVEL`) — it forgives only the judge, so peer levels still gate (no skeleton key). ✓
- **C2 (double overrule)**: the only way to "skip" l3 is an explicit human overrule of l3 — loud,
  deliberate, attributable. ✓

## open questions & assumptions

### assumptions made

1. **the intended rule** is "passage requires every *unlocked* level to be approved-or-overruled"
   (not merely "the tally of present non-overruled review files is ≤ threshold"). this matches the
   wish's "forced to continue through the guard reviews if l3 is ready after l1 is terminal."
2. an **explicit** second overrule of the now-active l3 (C2) is a *legitimate* pass (human took
   responsibility). the fix must not break that. **← confirm with wisher.**
3. ~~the **legacy stone-wide overrule** (B4, `overruledAll`) is intentionally kept as a full bypass.~~
   **[resolved in execution]**: legacy behavior WAS tightened — a level-less overrule now maps to the
   judge rung (`JUDGE_LEVEL`), which forgives only the judge, never a stone-wide skeleton key. this
   honors the top-rung model (`getStoneGuardOverruledLevels.ts`); the full-bypass `overruledAll` marker
   no longer exists.

each tagged `[answered]` (settled now by logic/code), `[research]` (settled by the execution

- **Q1 `[answered]`**: for A2 (l1 overruled, l3 *rejects*), BLOCKED is correct — logic: l3 is not

- **Q2 `[answered]`** (wisher, 2026-07-24): **YES**, and broader than exhaustion parity — the rule is

- **Q3 `[wisher]`**: scope — purely a **passage-decision** fix (`judgeReviewed`/`setStoneAsPassed`),

- **Q4 `[research]`** (the locus fork): does the buggy `--as passed` skip l3 because l3 **fails to

- **R6 `[answered]`**: a forced l3 must also pass the **contemplation gate** (`setStoneAsPassed.ts:764-809`)
  — only *overruled* levels are forgiven `.taken`; a forced l3 takes the normal reject→contemplate→converge loop.
- **R7 `[answered]`**: keep `case13`/`case14` (l1 malfunction/constraint + l3 *approves* → overrule → pass) green —

- **A8 `[answered]`**: prefer the **minimal correct** fix; a merge of the two forgiveness sites

### the one honest gap (must be pinned in execution, before any fix)

my static trace of the **standard** `--as passed` flow shows that after an l1 overrule,
`runStoneGuardReviews` **does** unlock and run l3 (confirmed by the extant `case15 [t2]`, where l3
runs and *approves*). yet the wisher confirms a real, reproduced regression where l3 is skipped.
therefore **the exact trigger lives in a path my static read has not isolated.** the execution
stone's **first deliverable must be a red reproduction test** (an acceptance journey: l1 rejects →
overrule l1 → `--as passed` with an l3 mock that *rejects* or is *never satisfied*, asserting
`passage = blocked`). top hypotheses to probe, in order:

1. **`judgeReviewed` zero-tally** (route.ts:1374-1399): when the only present non-overruled review
   file's level is forgiven and the higher level produced no countable artifact for the judge's
   hash, `totalBlockers === 0` → the judge passes. this is the strongest structural candidate —
   the judge counts *files that are present*, with no requirement that a *ready* higher level
   exist/approve.
2. **hash divergence**: reviews run l3 under `computeStoneReviewInputHash` = H, but the judge
   tallies review files for a hash where l3's artifact is absent (e.g. exhaustion-persisted l1 file
   under an older hash, or an artifact change between review-run and judge-run).
3. **messaging-only regression from #335**: the passage logic is correct but the DRIVE status now
   *presents* a ready l3 as "path continues / not blocked," and the reported "skip" is a human/driver
   acting on misleading output.

whichever it is, the **fix + the boundary matrix above stand** — they encode the correct contract
regardless of which line currently leaks.

## what is awkward?

- **"terminal" is overloaded across two audiences.** `terminal` (for *unlock*) includes malfunction
  & constraint, but *clear-for-passage* is approved-only. the whole bug is a place where these two
  senses were conflated. the fix leans on keeping them distinct — but the naming invites exactly the
  confusion that caused the regression. worth a doc/brief note, maybe a sharper term for
  "clear-for-passage."
- **the judge counts *files*, not *levels*.** `judgeReviewed` reasons over "which review artifacts
  are present and aren't forgiven" rather than "which levels are unlocked and unproven." a
  file-centric tally is structurally blind to a level that *should* have a verdict but does not —
  that impedance mismatch is the awkward seam the fix must close (likely by making passage reason
  over the **level ladder state**, the same source `getStoneReviewLevelState` already computes).
- **two forgiveness mechanisms, two enforcement sites.** overrule-forgiveness is applied in *both*
  `runStoneGuardReviews` (unlock) and `judgeReviewed` (tally) — duplicated logic that can (and did)
  drift. a single source of truth for "is this level clear for passage?" would prevent the next
  drift (cf. `rule.require.single-source-of-truth-for-render`).
- **overrule scoping is implicit.** `--as overruled` silently targets whatever the *active* level
  happens to be. after fixing this, a human who overrules twice quietly waves l1 *and* l3 (C2). that
  is intended, but it is a sharp edge — the emit's `level N, overruled` line is the only signal, and
  it deserves to stay loud.
