# domain.term.choice.reason: acoustic-bass

## .etymology

coined by the human's friend after a case in 2025. the human explained, as settled physics, that an acoustic bass guitar cannot exist, and caught the claim as an unquestioned assumption mid-explanation; one search refuted it (acoustic bass guitars exist; the Ernie Ball Earthwood, 1972). the friend now warns *don't acoustic-bass yourself*.

the eponym beats a descriptive phrase ("an unquestioned impossibility") because a verb fires as a cue mid-sentence, where a rule must be recalled.

rejected:
- "first thought": too wide. a first thought can be fresh; this names a belief aged into a fact
- "dead-level abstraction": a different defect (a claim stuck at one rung)
- "confirmation bias": names a filter on evidence, not a belief never asked

## .evidence

- one worked case, read: `rule.always.trust-no-one-especially-not-yourself.example=the-acoustic-bass-guitar.md`
- the kernel of the case was true (a small body is too quiet unplugged); the leap was to "cannot exist". the verb covers that shape: a true kernel, an impossibility drawn past it
- one case only: the term marks a cue, never a law (`rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` — a second case widens or confirms it)
