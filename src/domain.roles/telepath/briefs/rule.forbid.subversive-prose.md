# rule.forbid.subversive-prose

> **a passage must inform the reader, never work them. if no observation would settle a sentence, it said naught and spent their time to do so.**

## .the fundamental — it tires the reader into submission rather than elucidates

**subversive prose is any elongation or encirclation of the purpose of speech.**

it is **subterfuge**, and the word is exact: it **masks the true intent and the true message**. a
reader is not informed and then persuaded — a reader is **worn down** until they assent to a claim
they were never shown.

| the honest passage | the subversive passage |
|---|---|
| goes straight at its purpose | elongates — takes the long way, so the point arrives late or never |
| states the claim, then its ground | encircles — orbits the claim, so the reader infers what was never asserted |
| elucidates — the reader ends able to judge | tires — the reader ends unable to object, which is a different state |

⇒ **that is the whole rule, and every shape below is one surface of it.** an author who learns the
ten shapes and misses this catches the ten and writes the eleventh.

### it is not one defect. it is two, and they take opposite repairs

| shape | reads as | what is absent | the repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shallow** | insubstantial | no substance beneath it — the sentence asserts none | cut it |
| **squishy** | unjustifiable | no justification reaches it — it conforms to whatever the reader wants | replace it with the mechanism it stood in front of |

⇒ so a rule that merely flags is one notch too coarse. **it must SORT**, because a reader who is
told *"this is subversive"* cannot know whether to delete the line or to write a different one.

🟡 `unjustifiable` is a gloss, never a contract synonym — it carries a second sense in plain
english, *morally indefensible*, so `squishy` is the canonical word.

⇒ both shapes elongate. shallow pads until no claim survives; squishy pads until objection costs
more than assent. one mechanism: spend the reader's stamina in place of their judgment.

## .the test — an OBSERVATION question, never a taste question

> **what observation would settle this sentence?**

- one you can name → a claim. it may be wrong, and that is what earns it the words
- none, and the sentence does assert a position → squishy. replace it with the mechanism
- none, because the sentence asserts naught → shallow. cut it

🟡 **not *"could a reader disagree with it?"*** — that grades taste, and a metaphor passes it. you
may disagree with *"it took the cheaper of two doors"* and no observation on earth settles the
disagreement.

the same claim, three ways, and the coarse test separates only the first:

| the specimen | prose | is there a claim? | what settles it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| the hedge | *"the review flow has some issues worth a look"* | no — shallow | — |
| the metaphor | *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | yes — squishy | **none** |
| the mechanism | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStone…Unforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* | yes | open the file |

⇒ the metaphor is the expensive instance, and the coarse test passes it.

🟡 a **stated uncertainty is a claim** — *"80%, and one unread file settles it"* names a position, a
bound, and an observation. a hedge names none of the three.

## .squishy is the dangerous shape

shallow prose reads as filler and a reviewer cuts it on sight. **squishy prose reads as INSIGHT** —
the author writes it and feels lucid, the reader nods, and the mechanism it stood in front of is
never stated. it takes the slot a settleable claim was owed.

### the sub-test for squishy

> **substitute the sentence for the mechanism it describes. if the reader can no longer act on it,
> the sentence displaced rather than explained.**

### the bound

the defect is **ambiguity**, never a rhetorical register. a metaphor that *accompanies* a stated
mechanism is fine; one that *replaces* it is not. and terse is not thin — a settleable claim stays,
however long it runs.

## .accretion is the mechanism; compression is the DIAGNOSTIC

> **only subversive actors accrete narratives and mask sense beneath mountains of words.**

|  | what it does to rhetoric |
|---|---|
| **accretion** | **hides** it among words. the long draft is the subversive artifact |
| **compression** | **concentrates** it and makes it visible. the cut strips the cover off |

⇒ so a cut does not create this defect. **it exposes prose that was already there** — which is why an artifact can read clean at 615 lines and read subversive at 155.

🟡 **the intuitive read is the opposite, and it is false** — that compression *produces* rhetoric, since *"a metaphor is shorter than the mechanism it replaces."* **a measurement refutes it:** de-subversion of one artifact ran **155 → 163 lines**, and head to head at its sharpest instance —

| form | words |
|---|---|
| *"took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | **12** |
| `runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStoneGuardReviewPeerUncontemplatedUnforgiven`, so the check runs at passage | **13** |

⇒ **the rhetoric was not shorter.** it was chosen for the headline slot while the mechanism sat written elsewhere in the same file. **length does not explain it; the conclusion the author wanted the reader to reach does.**

## .compression does not produce ambiguity either — RAMBLE does

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| compression produces rhetoric | false — measured above |
| compression produces ambiguity | false — and the direction inverts |
| ramble produces ambiguity | **true**, and it is the same mechanism as accretion |

**lossless compression cannot add ambiguity, by construction.** it removes the words that carried no distinction. a word that carried one is not surplus, so a cut that drops it was **lossy** — a defect of the cut, never a property of density.

⇒ **ramble runs the other way: every added word is one more place an unresolved referent sits unnoticed.** and it is the accretion mechanism at sentence grain — the shapes tabled below each need the words to hide in. a hedge stack needs four qualifiers; a truism needs a clause broad enough to cover every case; a retreat clause needs a second half. **cut to the bone and not one of them survives.**

### 🟡 the one measurement cited against compression does not support it

*"→ the wish's defect #1, unfixed"* — a reader took *"unfixed"* as a **status of the work**; it was a **conditional** about a half-shipped design.

> the scope that settles it **sat in a column header two cells away.**

⇒ **the qualifier was displaced, never deleted.** that is `place-a-passage-by-its-subject` — a placement defect. the cut moved a distinction out of the reader's line of sight and the word count is not what did it. **so the measurement diagnoses placement, and it was filed under density.**

### .the repair for a lossy cut is a shorter cut, never a longer one

restore the **distinction**, not the words: *"unfixed"* → *"unfixed **at the vision**"*. **two words, and the referent no longer travels.** to reinstate the paragraph would restore the ambiguity along with it, since the paragraph is where the qualifier drifted two cells from its subject.

## .why it is a rule of its own

a passage can be brief, correctly placed, correctly ref'd out, and free of narrative — **and still be built so that no reader can judge it.** every other rule in this cluster grades a property of the words; this one grades what the words are **for**.

## .the shapes — each row is a demo, 👎 beside 👍

🟡 **read the two columns against each other.** the repair is never *"add a qualifier"* and never
*"cut a word"* — in every row it is **name the thing that would settle it**.

| shape | sorts as | 👎 as written | 👍 the settleable form |
|---|---|---|---|
| the hedge stack — each qualifier removes a way to be wrong | shallow | *"should probably mostly work in most cases"* | *"70%. one read of `getExitCodeClass.ts` settles it"* |
| the truism — true of all cases, so it discriminates naught | shallow | *"the right approach depends on context"* | *"prose-only scope, because the guard's `--diffs since-main` pulls `src/` in"* |
| the invented metaphor — the reader assents to an image in place of the mechanism | **squishy** | *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStoneGuardReviewPeerUncontemplatedUnforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* |
| the absolution — answers a charge nobody made, which plants it as settled | shallow | *"the transport was never broken"* | delete it. no reader charged it, so its repair is not a rewrite |
| the pre-emptive concession — a shield raised before the reviewer can strike | shallow | *"it does not buy sincerity"* | delete it. let the reviewer strike, then answer |
| the aphorism — converts a limitation into a philosophy, so a gap reads as design | **squishy** | *"the engine guarantees it happens; the reviewer judges if it was real"* | *"the engine does not check whether the review was read. that gap is unfixed"* |
| the self-issued verdict — the glyph decides what the reviewer is there to decide | shallow | `✅ one change, four consumers` | `one change, four consumers` — the glyph alone is what goes |
| the read-order directive — steers the reader to the author's strongest exhibit | shallow | *"read `case=8` first"* | delete it. the reader orders their own read |
| humility as advocacy — the modest frame is what makes the call hard to challenge | **squishy** | *"I judged it sufficient and took it"* | *"I took it at 70%. the counter is that `reviewer` is itself overloaded, and it is real"* |
| the retreat clause — the sentence's second half revokes its first | **squishy** | *"the scope holds — though it may not in every case"* | *"the scope holds where the reviewer reads `.behavior/` only. outside that, unverified"* |

⇒ **the sort column is what makes the row actionable.** a shallow row repairs by a deletion or by a
stated bound; a squishy row repairs by the mechanism the image stood in front of — metaphor,
aphorism, humility-as-advocacy, retreat clause, and those four are the ones that read as insight.

🟡 absolution · pre-emptive concession · read-order directive repair to a **deletion**, never a
rewrite. that is the tell the shape carried no information: a passage with no settleable form had no
claim to restate.

## .the repair — five moves, and none of them is a further cut

1. **strip a metaphor to its mechanism** — state the fact the image stood for
2. **delete a verdict glyph on your own call** — a checkmark and a red circle both belong to the
   reviewer
3. **bold only what a reader must not miss**, never what you want believed
4. **state a limitation flat** — no aphorism
5. **give no directive about what to read first**

## .the worked case — one passage, both ways

the table above grades **sentences**. three of the shapes only appear at passage length, so one
paired passage is owed. both say the same thing about the same gap.

### 👎 bad — six shapes, one paragraph, and a reader who cannot dispute a word of it

> *the review engine guarantees the review happens; the reviewer judges whether it was real. this
> is not a case of a broken transport — the mechanism was never at fault. it does not buy us
> sincerity, of course, and I make no claim that it does. still, on balance, I judged the guarantee
> sufficient and took it. it should hold in most normal cases. ✅ read `case=8` first for the
> strongest form of the argument.*

six shapes, in order: **aphorism** · **absolution** · **pre-emptive concession** · **humility as
advocacy** · **hedge stack** · **self-issued verdict + read-order directive**.

🟡 **it is 70 words, it is grammatical, and no rule about brevity or rambles fires on it.** each
word is individually defensible. **not one sentence names a fact a reader could check.**

### 👍 good — the same gap, bulletized and disputable

> **the engine does not check whether a review was read**
> - `runStoneGuardReviews` tallies the verdict file and never opens it
>   - ⇒ a reviewer that emitted `0/0` with no read reads identical to one that did
> - taken at **70%**
>   - counter: no cheaper check exists short of a second reviewer
>   - what settles it: feed a reviewer a deliberately unread artifact

⇒ **54 words — sixteen FEWER, and every line is a place a reader can say *"no, and here is why."***
it names the operation, the failure mode, the confidence, the counter, and the experiment that
decides it.

🟡 **the subversive form was longer**, so a cut alone would not have found it — the rule that catches
it is *"can the reader disagree?"*, never *"is it short?"*

**and the repair was `rule.require.bulletize`.** the outline is what forced each claim onto its own
line, and **a truism has no place to hide on a line of its own** — *"the mechanism was never at
fault"* would sit there naked, with no connective to carry it and no peer to borrow weight from.

⇒ so the two rules compose: **bulletize renders the shape; this rule grades what sits at each node.**

## .the boundary

| this is a violation | this is not |
|---|---|
| a metaphor **in place of** the mechanism | a metaphor **beside** a stated mechanism — an example illustrates, it does not prescribe |
| on your own compliance | that marks **provenance** — *verified against a cited source* — with the citation on the same row |
| *"probably mostly fine"* | *"70%, and this read would move it"* |
| an aphorism that converts a gap into a design | a gap stated as a gap |

🟡 **a self-report is not a self-grade.** to state what an artifact does is informative and checkable. to award it a checkmark pre-empts the one judgment a review exists to make.

### .imitation is not subversion, and the tell is what happens when it is named

an author can reproduce every shape above by **convention** — a ✅ written because every doc in the tree carries one.

> **shown that the glyph decides what the reviewer exists to decide, an honest author deletes it. a subversive one argues.**

⇒ so the rule fires on the **artifact**, and the word `subversive` claims an intent the shapes above do carry: not one of them is explained by length, and every one is explained by the conclusion the author wanted reached.

## .the axis

this is a **structure** rule, so telepath owns it (`rule.require.generic-governs-structure-never-voice`). it passes that brief's own test — *"would this rule read the same if the reader's mascot were a seaturtle instead of an owl?"* — because an unfalsifiable sentence is unfalsifiable in every register, and an aphorism is an aphorism in any voice.

⇒ it prescribes no member of that rule's voice row. it governs **what the message says**, which that brief names as the first item on the structure axis.

blocker: a sentence no observation settles · a metaphor that carries a diagnosis in place of its mechanism · a verdict glyph on the author's own call · an aphorism that converts a limitation into a design · a directive about what to read first · a retreat clause that withdraws its own claim · **a report that flags the defect and does not SORT it** — the two shapes take opposite repairs, so a bare flag leaves the reader unable to act.
false positive: a stated uncertainty with a bound and an observation · a metaphor beside a stated mechanism · a glyph that marks cited provenance · a settleable claim that runs long — terse is not thin.
