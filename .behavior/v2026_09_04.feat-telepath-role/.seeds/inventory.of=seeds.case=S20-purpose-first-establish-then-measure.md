# S20 · purpose-first — establish the purpose, then MEASURE the prose against it

## .said

verbatim, 2026-09-04, mid-drive at `1.vision`:

> also, we need a separate enbriefment of a trait and rule to always lead with purpose. first, before any written prose, understand why it needs to be said at all. and then measure the prose against how directly it fulfills that purpose.

## .settled

**two acts, in order, and the second is the one no extant rule performs.**

| # | the act | when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **establish** — *why does this need to be said at all?* | **before any prose exists** |
| 2 | **measure** — *how DIRECTLY does this prose fulfill that purpose?* | after it exists |

⇒ the wisher's words are precise: *"measure the prose **against** how directly it fulfills that
purpose."* the purpose becomes a **yardstick**, not merely a prompt.

### it is the other half of the first fundamentalism utterance

S17's utterance carried **two** drills, and the wisher later asked for this one **separately**:

| drill | question | rule |
|---|---|---|
| the **content** drill | what root truth does this stand on? | `fundamentalism` (S17) |
| the **passage** drill | why does this even need to be said? | **`purpose-first`** |

⇒ so the two were **parted rather than fused**, on the wisher's own instruction — one grades the
**concept**, the other grades the **passage's right to exist**.

### what "directly" catches that no other rule does

a passage can be brief, bulletized, fundamental, and condensed — **and serve a different purpose
than the one it was written for.** every other rule grades a property of the words; this grades
whether the words are aimed at the target.

⇒ that is why *"directly"* sits in the utterance and not merely *"fulfills"*: a passage that reaches
its purpose by a lap is a violation, even where each word survives every other rule.

## .landed

- `rule.require.purpose-first` (say) — owed
- ⇒ it fires **first** in the canon pipeline, before `fundamentalism`
- 🟡 it is the peer that gives `forbid.subversive-prose` its teeth: **elongation and encirclation are
  defined relative to a purpose** (S19), so a rule that never establishes one has no baseline against
