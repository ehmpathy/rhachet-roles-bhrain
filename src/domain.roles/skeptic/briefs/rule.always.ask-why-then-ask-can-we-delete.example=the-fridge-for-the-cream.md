# rule.always.ask-why-then-ask-can-we-delete.example=the-fridge-for-the-cream

## .what happened

a café's staff asked the owner for a second fridge. the cream spoilt each week, they said, and the one fridge was full.

the owner was about to order one. she asked first: why does the cream spoil? the answer: each week a full case went in, and most of it came out unopened, past its date. two drinks on the menu used cream, and almost no one ordered them.

she asked the second question: can we delete those two drinks? she did. the cream no longer spoilt, the fridge had room, and no fridge was bought.

## .what the ask did

- it arrived as a solution ("a second fridge"), not a problem
- the problem it named (spoilt cream) had a cause the ask did not hold (no one orders it)
- the cheapest cure deleted the requirement; the bolt-on would have stored the waste in more space

## .the lesson

a requirement is a claim that a cost is owed. ask why it exists before you build for it, then ask whether it can go.

## .the questions that caught it

1. **why is this a problem?** — the answer moved the cause from "no space" to "no demand"
2. **if we deleted the requirement, who would feel it, and how?** — almost no one
