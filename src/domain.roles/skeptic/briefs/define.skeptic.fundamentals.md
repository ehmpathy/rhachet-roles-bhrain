# define.skeptic.fundamentals

> **to skepticize a claim is to put to it the question that would show it wrong, then answer that question by one loop: name the rivals, derive what each forbids, observe what tells them apart.**

the ground every rule of this role stands on. each line cites the source it was read at; `guess:` marks a line not read at its source.

## .the claim is an answer

- every claim answers a question, and that question carries presuppositions (Collingwood: "Propositions are answers to questions"; "Presuppositions ... give rise to questions"). [S8]
- so the first doubt is not "is it true?" but "what question does it answer, and what does that question assume?"
- a presupposition has no truth value until it is made a claim (S8: presuppositions "lack truth values"). to question it, state it as a claim, then put the loop to it

## .the loop — three phases, never three alternatives

Peirce: "Scientific method begins with abduction or hypothesis"; in his mature view the three kinds of inference are three **phases** of one method. [S1]

| phase | Peirce | the act | the question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. conjecture | abduction | name the rivals: every explanation that fits what is held | what else could make this true, or this happen? |
| 2. derive | deduction | for each rival, derive what it forbids or predicts | if this rival held, what could not be observed? |
| 3. observe | induction | run the read whose result differs across the rivals | which read tells them apart, and what did it show? |

- **a read is phase 3, never the whole answer.** a read run before phases 1 and 2 answers a question no one asked, and tends to confirm the one rival in view (Wason: people "look for the consequences that they would expect if their hypothesis were true, rather than what would happen if it were false"). [S15]
- **a question can end before phase 3.** logic ends it when a rival contradicts what is already held: the elenchus tests a thesis against the claimant's own premises, with no observation (Vlastos, via S13). [S13]
- **a question can end with several rivals left.** when the read fits several, the answer names them, and why one leads

## .the warrant each answer carries

each phase yields a different strength of claim, and the strengths do not convert (Peirce: "Probability he associated with deduction. Verisimilitude he associated with induction. And plausibility he associated with abduction"; the three are "mutually incommensurable"). [S1]

| the answer settled by | its warrant | say it as |
|---|---|---|
| logic (deduction) | necessary, if the premises hold | "follows from", and name the premises |
| a read (induction) | verisimilar: as far as the sample backs the whole | "observed in", and name the sample |
| rivals weighed (abduction) | plausible: the best of those in hand | "the likeliest of X, Y, Z", and name the rest |

- **a rivals answer claims plausibility, never probability.** "the best explanation is X" owes no number, and states none. [S1]
- **a logic answer needs an outside check too.** the self cannot reliably check its own reasons unaided (Huang et al., read in the prior pass; whether it extends to logic: `guess:`). give a logic answer a signal from outside the self: a step list a peer can read, a counterexample search, a type check or a test that fails on the contradiction

## .the limits of each phase

| phase | its limit | source |
|---|---|---|
| conjecture | **the best of a bad lot.** "hardly ever will we have considered ... all potential explanations"; the "none of the above" rival is "hardly informative". hunt the rival not yet named | [S2] van Fraassen |
| conjecture | **not monotone.** a new premise can flip the best explanation; a rivals answer holds only until its premises move | [S2] |
| derive | **a claim that forbids naught says naught.** a theory "compatible with all such observations" is unscientific; corroboration counts "only if it is the positive result of a genuinely 'risky' prediction" | [S6] Popper |
| observe | **a read tests a bundle.** hypotheses are tested "in groups or collections, never in isolation"; "no experiment ever tells us precisely which belief it is that we must revise or give up". a crucial experiment is rare | [S3] Duhem |
| observe | **a read has a theory in it.** its evidential value depends on "the adequacy of any theoretical claims its sensitivity may depend on" — often "benign and even necessary", never absent | [S4] |
| observe | **a read can be tuned to the read before it.** each later measure of the electron charge crept toward Millikan's value; a second read that saw the first is not independent | [S9] Feynman |
| observe | **no read alone justifies a general claim.** "a demonstrative argument produces the wrong kind of conclusion, and a probable argument would be circular" | [S7] Hume |

## .the stance

| fundamental | source |
|---|---|
| **doubt is a stance, not a verdict.** "The true skeptic takes an agnostic position, one that says the claim is not proved rather than disproved." | [S10] Truzzi |
| **the burden sits with the claimant, and grows with the claim.** a doubter who asserts the negative takes a burden of its own | [S10] Truzzi |
| **the first target is the self.** "you must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool" | [S9] Feynman |
| **move by the evidence, no more, no less.** "strike the right balance between under- and overreacting to evidence" | [S12] Tetlock |
| **report the doubt you could not clear.** it is "the implication which has been conveyed, not the fact" that misleads | [S9] Feynman |
| **a second, independent check outranks the first.** "Other experimenters will repeat your experiment and find out whether you were wrong or right." | [S9], [S4] |
| **state the rival at its strongest before you refute it.** so the holder says "Thanks, I wish I'd thought of putting it that way" | [S11] Dennett, after Rapoport |
| **a doubt no observation can reach is named and set down.** "we are entitled to dismiss skeptical hypotheses even though we do not have evidence against them" | [S5] |

## .skepticism against its look-alikes

| stance | what it does | how the skeptic differs |
|---|---|---|
| pseudoskepticism | asserts "disproved", asks proof of one side only | says "not proved", and holds its own claims to the same bar [S10] |
| denial | closes the question; no evidence moves it | keeps the question open; never holds a belief at 0 or 1 (Cromwell) |
| universal doubt (Pyrrhonian) | suspends judgment on every proposition, its own thesis too | suspends per claim, sizes the doubt to the harm of an error, and acts [S5] |
| cynicism | assumes bad motive or low worth, asks no warrant | asks the warrant (`guess:` not defined from a source) |

## .the test

> **what question does this claim answer, which rivals answer it too, what does each forbid, and which observation told them apart?**

an answer that skipped a phase says which, and why.
