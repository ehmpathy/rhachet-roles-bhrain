# rule.always.say-only-what-is-needed.example=the-note-for-the-sitter

## .what happened

a friend left a note for the person who would mind the house for a week. it ran to fourteen lines: where the spare towels sat, the wifi password, a tip on the coffee machine, the bin day, the plants. line nine said: "water the plants on tuesday. not the cactus, it rots, and the fern in the hall is plastic."

the sitter read the note once, on arrival, and watered every plant on tuesday. the cactus rotted.

## .what the note did

- the one clause that changed an act sat in the middle of thirteen that changed naught
- it shared a line with a clause that was a joke (the plastic fern), so it read as one too
- the sitter acted on what the note said loudest: water the plants

## .the lesson

a reader acts on a message once. each clause that changes no act dilutes the one that does, and a reader cannot tell which one mattered.

## .the question that would have caught it

per clause: **what will the reader do differently because of this line?** none → cut it. one line left: put it first, alone.

the note, at that bar: *"do not water the cactus. all else: see the fridge door."*
