## .the fork, stated fairly

a dispute defers a human verdict to the end-of-route council. **the cost of that deferral is not
uniform** — it scales with how much work settles on the disputed call before the council rules.

the fulcrum inventory already carries the column that measures exactly this: `rework`,
`clean`/`dirty` (`rule.always.itemize-the-fulcrums-you-best-guess`). so a predicate is available,
free, and already understood by every reader.

| fork | the stance gate would… |
|---|---|
| **A — uniform** *(taken)* | drive on for every dispute. `rework` is a column the council sorts on, never a gate |
| **B — graded** | drive on for a `clean` dispute · **halt** for a `dirty` one, since a late verdict on it costs most |

## 🔴 .the extant rule appears to mandate B — and does not, by a test easy to miss

`rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last` carries this table, and a reader who stops at it reads **A as a
rule violation**:

| the fulcrum is | you |
|---|---|
| open, rework **clean** | best-guess, note why, flag for the end |
| open, rework **dirty** | 🔴 `--as blocked` — last, and named |

⇒ `--as blocked` **is** a halt. so line-for-line, the rule already says a dirty fulcrum stops the
road, which is precisely fork B.

**it does not, and the clause that narrows it sits four lines below:**

> *"a block needs all three: no defensible guess · a dirty rework · every other question already
> addressed."*

⇒ **three conditions, conjunctive.** and a dispute fails the first by construction: the driver
reached it *after* a `.taken` that states a repair or a cited refutation
(`rule.always.converge-with-reviewers.via-a-taken-per-point`). **a defensible guess is exactly what a
dispute is.** so the rule does not owe a block, and A stands.

🟡 **but note which way the discovery cuts, in full.** it removes an objection to A — and it also
shows that **a halt graded on a compound predicate that includes `rework` is already the extant
shape of this system.** B is not alien to the design; it is the same move with one condition
dropped. ⇒ the two effects roughly cancel, which is why the confidence below stays at 70% rather
than rise on a resolved objection.

## .taken, and why at the time

**A.** three reasons, in the order they weighed:

1. 🔴 **B re-introduces the very halt the wish exists to remove.** `0.wish.md:15` — *"a route halts
   **only** when the driver concedes."* a `dirty` dispute that halts is a dispute that did not drive
   on, so B contradicts acceptance #2 for a subset the driver itself defines
2. **the predicate is self-graded.** `rework` is the driver's own clean/dirty call
   (`rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last`: *"unsure → treat it as clean and flag it"*). B would make
   a **halt** turn on a field the halted party fills in, and that field's own rule biases it toward
   `clean` — so B buys a gate that is trivially and *sanctionedly* avoided
3. **the sort already delivers most of B's value.** a council that opens the inventory and sorts on
   `rework` reads the dirty rows first. B buys **earliness**; the column buys **priority**, at no
   cost to the drive

## 🔴 .the assumption A rests on is now MEASURED, and it came back against A

A's third reason and the yield's § 7 both rest on one unstated premise: **that a deferred verdict is

**this does not flip the call, and it moves it a long way.** A's three reasons stand: B still

this fulcrum first assumed, so the rework A exposes is a live cost rather than a tail risk.

⇒ **and F04 fell 65% → 55% on the same evidence**, for a related reason: two of the observed

## 🔴 .why the confidence FELL to 60% — was 70%

the honest case **for** B, which A does not answer:

- the deferral's cost is genuinely non-uniform, and A prices it as though it were
- a `dirty` dispute is exactly the one where a late overturn is most expensive, so A concentrates
  its whole risk in the cell it does least about
- reason 2 argues the predicate is gameable — **but a driver who mis-grades `rework` to dodge a
  halt has done a visible and countable act**, which is the same restraint this design leans on
  everywhere else. so reason 2 proves less than it appears to

⇒ **A rests on the assumption that disputes are rarely overturned** (yield § 7). that assumption is
unmeasured. if it is false, B is the better design and A is the expensive one.

🟡 **and this call favours the party that made it.** A lets a driver drive on in every case; B
would hold it in the hardest ones. `rule.always.get-a-second-opinion-before-foreman` applies here for
the same reason it applies to F04 — **the two should be sought together, since a verdict on F04's
asymmetry moves this one's arithmetic.**
