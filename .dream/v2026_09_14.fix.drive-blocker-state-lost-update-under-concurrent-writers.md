## .the cue that fired

`arch-hazards-behavior` graded the non-atomic read-modify-write of `.drive.blockers.latest.json` a
behavior hazard. the torn-read half closed first (temp file + `rename`); the lost-update half was

## ✅ .how it closed

- `fs.open(lockPath, 'wx')` (`O_CREAT | O_EXCL`) around the whole read-project-write, released in a
  `finally`; a bounded acquire (`LOCK_ACQUIRE_TIMEOUT_MS`) fails loud with the lock path and its fix
- 🔴 **no age-based steal, and a later reader must keep it that way.** two writers can both judge
  one lock stale and double-unlink it — a silent double grant. the one reap is
  `delAbandonedDriveStateLock`, on the os's verdict (`ESRCH` from `kill(pid, 0)`), behind its own
  `wx` claim

## 🔴 .the deferral reason was wrong on BOTH halves

| the claim | what was measured |
|---|---|
| *"a lock not released would stall the next drive"* — so not SAFE | true, and the SAFE outcome: a wedged lock throws with a named fix; a lost update silently un-arms the 21-block cutoff or suppresses a brain dispatch |
| *"it adds a lock lifecycle with stale-lock recovery"* — so not CLEAN | an age-based steal is the one piece that must NOT be built, and its absence removed most of the cost |
| *"gated on a concurrent-clone flow that does not exist"* | the gate was open: this feature added `setDriveEntryStone` as a second writer in the same round |

⇒ **an estimate written into a dream is a guess, and a dream is the artifact that stops anyone from
a re-check of it.** the `spawn-failed` rename (`.dream/v2026_09_15…retry-backoff.md`) closed on the
same pattern.

## 🔴 .what the repair found beyond the blocker

with the lock bypassed, two concurrent mutations in ONE process both wrote `…json.<pid>.tmp`, and the
second `rename` threw ENOENT. **a pid suffix parts two processes, never two calls in one.** so the
lock is also what makes the temp-file write safe against a same-process writer — the torn-read fix
was narrower than its own note claimed.
