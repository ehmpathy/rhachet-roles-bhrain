# case 10 — the-stone-declares-no-brain-at-all

**actor** = driver-clone · **feel** = happy · **care** = **critipath**
**cell** = `driver-clone × apply × absent-from-launch × any reach`

> the highest-frequency cell in the whole space, and the one the wish states as a bound:
> *"a stone with no `brain:` field keeps the inherited brain."*

## .the narrative

i am a driver on a route that has never heard of this feature. not one of its fourteen guards
declares a brain. neither does any guard in any other repo — because until this ships, the key does
not exist.

i pass a stone. i read the next one.

**and not one thing is different.** no new line in my output. no turn spent. no socket opened. no
warn about an absent field. the drive is byte-identical to the drive i would have had yesterday.

that sounds like a description of naught happening, and it is the opposite: it is a **guarantee**,
and it is the one this feature is most likely to break, because the hook now fires on **every stone
entry of every route everywhere** and must decide, every time, to do naught.

the three ways it breaks are each a small, reasonable-looking choice:

| the choice that tempts | what it costs |
|---|---|
| print a line — *"brain: inherited"* — for symmetry with `case=6` | **every route in every repo gains an output line.** every stone-entry snapshot in every repo breaks at once |
| run `clone whoami` first, unconditionally, to confirm the address | a subprocess on every stone entry, on routes that declared no brain — and on an unenrolled driver it **rejects**, so `case=2`'s halt fires for routes that asked for naught |
| dispatch a re-assert of the live brain, so state is explicit | a full turn of context per stone, on fourteen stones, to change no state at all |

⚠️ **the second is the dangerous one**, because the wish's own sequence puts `whoami` first, and
`case=1` `[t1]` makes that confirmation the guarantee of the happy path. read carelessly, that says
*"always whoami first"* — which turns `case=2`'s correct halt into a blanket halt on every drive on
this host today.

⇒ **the "aha": the absent field is not the absence of the feature — it is the feature's largest
input.** the blast radius of this cell is every drive that exists; the blast radius of every other
cell in this space is the drives that opt in.

## .the bdd `[tn]` timeline

```
given a route whose guards declare no `brain:` at all — every extant route, in every repo
  and  a driver on that route, reach unimportant (live, deaf, or unenrolled alike)

  when [t0] the driver enters any stone
    then the hook reads the guard and finds no `brain:` key
    and  it returns at once — before any reach check, any whoami, any socket

  when [t1] the driver's stone-entry output is compared to the pre-feature output
    then it is byte-identical — no brain line, no warn, no marker
    and  every extant stone-entry snapshot in every repo still passes

  when [t2] the driver is unenrolled — today's common case (F-a)
    then no halt fires, because no switch was ever attempted
    and  case=2's halt is reserved for a stone that ASKED for a brain and could not get one

  when [t3] the driver takes its first turn on the stone
    then it runs on whatever brain it was already on
    and  no turn, no token, and no transcript line was spent to establish that

  when [t4] a later stone on the same route declares a brain
    then that stone switches as in case=1, and this one is unaffected in retrospect
    and  the absent key is a per-stone fact, never a per-route mode
```

## .the fail-safe grade

this is a **happy** critipath, so the grade is not fail-safe — it is whether the guarantee holds
against each way it could be broken:

| the guarantee | broken by | held at |
|---|---|---|
| no new output on an undeclared stone | a symmetry line with `case=6` | `[t1]` |
| no subprocess on an undeclared stone | an unconditional `whoami` | `[t0]` |
| no halt on an undeclared stone | a reach check before the key check | `[t0]`, `[t2]` |
| no turn spent on an undeclared stone | a defensive re-assert | `[t3]` |

⇒ **`[t0]`'s order is the whole case.** the key check runs **first**, before reach, before identity,
before any dispatch. every failure above is the same failure: a check that ran before the one
question that could have skipped it.

## .why critipath, against `case=6`'s alterpath

`case=6` (`same-as-live`) is an alterpath because its cost is one wasted turn with a workable route
around it. this cell reads similar and grades opposite:

| | `case=6` — same-as-live | this case — absent |
|---|---|---|
| who is affected | routes that opted in | 🔴 **every route that exists**, including every one that never heard of the feature |
| cost if the check is absent | one redundant turn | a broken snapshot in every repo · a halt on every unenrolled drive · a turn per stone |
| workable fallback | ✅ the work still lands right | 🔴 none — a drive that halts on a stone it never annotated has no route around it |

⇒ a core usecase — **every extant drive** — fails, with no fallback = **critipath**.

🟡 it is graded happy rather than sharp because when the contract holds, the actor feels naught at
all. the sharpness lives entirely in the build that gets `[t0]`'s order wrong.

## .the invariant this case establishes

> **the `brain:` key check is the FIRST thing the hook does, and an absent key returns before any
> other check.** reach, identity, and dispatch are all downstream of a key that parsed.

⚠️ this constrains `case=1` `[t1]`: *"the hook confirms the target BEFORE it dispatches aught"* is
correct **within** the branch where a brain was declared. it is not a rule about stone entry.
