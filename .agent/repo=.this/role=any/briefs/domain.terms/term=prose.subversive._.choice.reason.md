# domain.term.choice.reason: subversive

## .etymology

from `subvert` — to overturn from beneath. the word is chosen for exactly that sense: the prose does
not argue against the reader's judgment, it works underneath it, so the reader assents to a claim
they were never shown.

`subterfuge` is its close neighbour and the reason the word is exact rather than merely vivid: both
name **a mask over the true intent and the true message**.

## .the two coinages, dated

| date | what the wisher settled |
|---|---|
| 2026-09-04 | the root — *"forbid subversive prose"* · seeds `S05` · `S06` · `S19` |
| 2026-09-06 | the split — *"i preffer shallow and squishy"* · seed `S30`, pulled from `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain#444` |

⇒ so the root shipped first and was **found one notch too coarse in use**. that is the whole reason
the cluster has children: a rule that flags without a sort tells the reader a defect exists and
leaves them unable to act on it.

## .the rejected synonyms

| word | the case for it | why it lost |
|---|---|---|
| `rhetorical` | it is what the prose *does* | it names a register, and the bound is ambiguity. a metaphor that accompanies a stated mechanism is legal, and `rhetorical` would forbid it |
| `weasel` | vivid, and in common use | it grades the author. the rule must fire on the artifact, since an author can reproduce every shape by convention |
| `fluffy` · `hand-wavy` | both read plainly | each smuggles a volume claim, and the measurement refutes it: de-subversion of one artifact ran 155 → 163 lines, and at its sharpest instance the rhetorical form was 12 words against the mechanism's 13 |

## .the evidence — two independent derivations

the same rule was derived in a second repo with no reference to this one:

> `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhuild#357` — *"`rule.require.brevity` and `rule.forbid.rambles` cut
> surplus words; each word in **'this approach is robust and scalable'** is individually defensible,
> so neither rule fires. the sentence still asserts none."*

⇒ **two independent derivations is the strongest evidence a term names a real concept**, and the
bhuild form supplied the better test name: falsifiability, never word count.

## .disputes

### dispute: unjustifiable — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (gloss only, for the `squishy` child)

- raised.by  = the wisher, in `#444`
- claim      = `unjustifiable` states the defect plainly — no justification reaches the sentence
- counter    = it carries a second sense in plain english, *morally indefensible*, so a contract that
               used it would read as a charge against the author rather than a grade of the prose
- resolution = `squishy` is canonical; `unjustifiable` is its **gloss**, never a synonym to use in a
               contract. recorded in `term=prose.subversive.squishy._.choice._.md`

### dispute: is `subversive` a synonym of `obfuscation`? — raised 2026-09-04 — status: RESOLVED (distinct)

- raised.by  = `term=prose.elucidation._.choice.reason.md`, and it was recorded as open arrears in
               `term=prose._.choice._.md` until 2026-09-06
- claim      = both name prose that leaves the reader misinformed, so one word should serve
- counter    = they grade different grains, and `rule.forbid.obfuscation` declares the seam:
               `subversive` grades *what is on the page* (could an observation settle it?);
               `obfuscation` grades *what is absent from it* (is what the reader needs even here?).
               a passage can be fully settleable and still omit the caveat that changes the reader's
               action, and one that carries every caveat can still be squishy throughout
- resolution = distinct terms, and the overlap is deliberate — see the peer table in
               `term=prose.subversive._.choice._.md`

### dispute: is the split two terms or a sharper definition? — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (two terms)

- claim      = one word with a sharper test would cost less than a cluster with two children
- counter    = **the repairs are opposite.** shallow repairs by a deletion; squishy repairs by the
               mechanism it stood in front of. a single word cannot carry two repairs, so a reader
               who receives it cannot act
- resolution = two children, and the root is a boundary that must never appear alone in a grade

## .the lesson the split taught, and it is about RULES rather than prose

> **a test that answers YES on both the good and the bad case is not a test — it is a filter one
> notch too coarse.**

the root's original test was *"could a reader disagree with this?"*. it separates `shallow` from a
real claim and **passes `squishy` whole**, because two readers can disagree forever about a metaphor
and no observation settles them.

⇒ that is `rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` applied to a rule's *scope* rather than to a
word, and it is the sharper case: a wrong word costs a rename; a wrong scope ships a gate that
passes the defect it was built for. the amendment is caught at
`.dream/v2026_09_06.amend.a-rules-scope-is-enumerated-before-the-rule-ships.md`.
