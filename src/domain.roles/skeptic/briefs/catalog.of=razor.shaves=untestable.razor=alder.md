# razor: alder

shaves: a claim that cannot be tested · settled by: logic

## .the question

> **what observations would settle this claim, and does the claim entail them by logic?**

the ask goes to whoever makes the claim. they derive the observable consequences; no read runs yet.

## .the source

- Mike Alder, "Newton's Flaming Laser Sword", *Philosophy Now* 46, May/June 2004, pp. 29-33: "we should not dispute propositions unless they can be shown by precise logic and/or mathematics to have observable consequences"
- the first question: "What set of observations do you consider would establish the truth of your claim?"
- read at https://philosophynow.org/issues/46/Newtons_Flaming_Laser_Sword

## .fails when

- the claim is about value, not fact. Alder concedes the sword also cuts out ethics: run `razor=hume-is-ought`
- a count is taken for the outcome (McNamara fallacy, `razor=goodhart`)
