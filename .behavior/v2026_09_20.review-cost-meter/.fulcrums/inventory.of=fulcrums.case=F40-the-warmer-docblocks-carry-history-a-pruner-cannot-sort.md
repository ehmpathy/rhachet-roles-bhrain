## .the fork

the warmer subsystem's `.ts` files carry docblocks several times the length of the code beneath
them. the lane's complaint, stated fairly:

> *"a future editor can't easily tell load-bearing invariant from historical narration to prune."*

that is correct, and **this branch is its own evidence**: several warmer docblocks carry a dated
`AMENDED` marker precisely because their prose had drifted from the mechanism below it, and a
reader who trusted the prose would have had the wrong model
(`0.wish` bar 17 · `i015` passes 30–31).

| fork | what it buys | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A — leave it** (taken for now) | no sweep; every argument stays beside the code it binds | the editor's sort problem stands, and grows with each amendment |

## 🔴 .why (b) of the same concern was REFUTED, and why that does not settle (a)

🟡 stated with its bound: brief-boot cost has not been measured against target-cache cost here, so

⇒ **but the editor's sort problem is untouched by any of that.** (b) is about money; (a) is about a
future reader's ability to tell a law from a story, and the cache says naught about it.

## .why it is the wisher's

three reasons, and the third is the one that makes it a halt rather than a dream:

1. it is a **sweep** — `rule.prefer.wickup-touched-prose` bounds a fix-forward to the prose your
   change already touches, and this reaches ~20 files a single change does not open
2. the repo **already has the rule** (`rule.require.timeless-lessons`: the concept stays, the
   episode ejects to `example=$slug.md`) — so fork C is a mechanical application, not a new policy
3. 🔴 **only the wisher can settle what carries load.** the arguments in these docblocks are what
   made four repeat defects catchable, and a cut made by the author who wrote them is a cut made by
   the party with the least distance from them
