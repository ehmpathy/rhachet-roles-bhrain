## ✅ .how it closed

`withDriveStateLock` takes an `O_CREAT | O_EXCL` lock (`fs.open(path, 'wx')`) around the whole
read-project-write, and releases it in a `finally`. the acquire is bounded and fails **loud**, with
the lock path and the `rm` that clears it.

🔴 **there is deliberately NO stale-lock steal**, and that is the one design call a later reader
must keep: a steal reads a lock's age and unlinks it when it looks abandoned — which is **itself the
race the lock closes**, since two writers can both judge one lock stale and the second unlinks the
first's freshly-won lock. ⇒ a steal converts a loud wedge into a silent double grant, which is the
exact trade this repair was asked to reverse.

the clamp is `mutateDriveBlockerState.integration.test.ts`, 16 rows over 5 cases.

## 🔴 .the deferral reason was wrong on BOTH halves — the durable part

the record read **SAFE — no** and **CLEAN — no**. neither held.

| the claim | what was measured |
|---|---|
| *"a lock not released (a crashed hook) would stall the next drive until it times out"* | 🔴 **true, and it is the SAFE outcome rather than the unsafe one.** a wedged lock throws with a named fix; a lost update silently un-arms the 21-block cutoff or suppresses a brain dispatch. the reason ranked a **loud** failure above a **silent** one |
| *"it adds a lock lifecycle (acquire, release, stale-lock recovery)"* | 🔴 **stale-lock recovery is the one piece that must NOT be built**, and its absence removed most of the estimated cost. the whole lock is 40 lines in one new file |
| *"gated on a concurrent-clone flow that does not exist today"* | ⚠️ **the gate was already open and the dream did not notice.** this feature added `setDriveEntryStone` as a SECOND writer, so the window widened in the same round the dream was filed |

⇒ **the general form, and it is the same one the `spawn-failed` dream recorded four days earlier:
an estimate written into a dream is a GUESS, and a dream is exactly the artifact that stops anyone
from a re-check of it.** two dreams in one route, both deferred on a mis-estimate, both closed in
minutes once someone measured rather than read.

## 🔴 .what the repair found that the blocker never named

the dogfood (lock bypassed, 8 of 16 rows red) surfaced a **second** fault: two concurrent mutations
in ONE process both wrote `…json.<pid>.tmp`, so the second `rename` threw ENOENT on a path the first
had already consumed. **the pid suffix parts two processes and never two calls inside one.**

⇒ so the lock does more than serialize the read-modify-write — it is what makes the extant temp-file
write safe against a same-process writer at all. the atomic-rename fix of i012 was **narrower than
its own note claimed**, and no test would have found that without the bypass run.
