# domain.term.reason: education.pursuit.course.greats

## .etymology

"the greats" is the word a student and a teacher both say for the masters of a field: one reads
the greats. it names people and their works together, and it carries the bar: not any source,
the ones who paid for the lesson first.

rejected:
- `sources`, `references` — any citation qualifies; the word drops the bar the course holds
- `experts`, `authorities` — name the people only, never the works read

## .evidence

- the narrative move maps "the masters' treatises" to the greats; the apprentice reads them
  before the practice at the bench
- the pedigree lists each course's greats, so a reader sees whose lessons a role carries

## .invariants

- every great a course reads is named with the work and where it was read
