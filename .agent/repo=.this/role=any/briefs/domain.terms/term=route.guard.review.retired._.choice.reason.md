# domain.term.choice.reason: retired

## .etymology

**retired** — from the french *retirer*, "to withdraw". a retired reviewer has been withdrawn from
service. the word carries three senses this concept needs and the alternatives do not:

1. **it is deliberate.** somebody edited the config. a retired reviewer is not a defect, an
   accident, or a corrupt record — it is a config change that happened to leave a debt behind
2. **it is permanent.** a retired reviewer does not come back on its own. that is exactly why the
   halt tells the driver *"it will not speak again, so your answer is what discharges it"* — the
   usual instinct (wait for the reviewer to re-run and drop it) can never succeed here
3. **it does not void the past.** a retired worker's completed work still stands. so does a retired
   reviewer's critique — which is the whole point, and the property every rejected synonym loses

## .the rejected alternatives

| word | why it was rejected |
|---|---|
| `removed` / `deleted` | both suggest the reviewer's **record** was removed — but the given file is still on disk and still gates. the config entry went; the critique did not. a driver who reads `deleted` looks for an absent file |
| `absent` | 🔴 already load-bearing for a different concept in this same subdomain — `contemplation absent` names a `.taken` that was never written. to reuse it for a reviewer out of the config is `rule.forbid.domain-term-ambiguity` verbatim: one word, two concepts, in one tree |
| `gone` | vague, and it reads as though the debt went with it — the exact misread the halt exists to prevent |
| `orphaned` | names the GIVEN's plight, not the reviewer's place in the config. it also implies the record is unowned, when in truth the debt has a very clear owner: the driver who owes the answer |
| `stale` | 🔴 already taken, and by a near neighbour — `stale` marks a `.taken` that answers an earlier given from the same reviewer. two adjectives one line apart in the same render must not blur |

⇒ the two strongest reasons are the collisions. `absent` and `stale` are both live words in the
contemplation vocabulary, and both appear in trees a driver reads beside this one.

## .evidence

- **the discovery**: `[case7]` of `routeStoneSetContemplation.acceptance.test.ts` — a reviewer
  `departed` speaks, is removed from the guard config, and still halts the stone. the case was
  written at 5.3 to clamp a critipath the vision catalog had not named (`case=11`, *the reviewer
  that left*), and the adjective was coined to render its status line
- **the invariant it carries**: a debt is keyed to the reviewer, so no edit to the artifact — and
  no edit to the config — can clear it. only an answer or a human overrule discharges it
- **the orthogonality**: `retired` and `unreadable` are independent axes, clamped together by
  `formatRouteGuardReviewPeerContemplatePrompt.test.ts [case6]`, the stacked render
