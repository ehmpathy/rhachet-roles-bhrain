# case 8 — the-switch-is-visible

**actor** = human · **feel** = happy · **care** = critipath
**cell** = `human × audit × other-known × live`

## .the narrative

i am the foreman. i declared `brain: claude-opus-5[1m]` on `5.3.verification` for one reason: i want
verification done by a brain that will actually argue with the work. i pay a premium for that, and i
pay it on every drive that walks this route, forever.

so the question i will ask — not today, but the first time a verified stone ships a defect — is
exactly one question:

> **did 5.3 really run on opus?**

if the only place i can look is the bill, the answer is a guess. a bill tells me tokens were spent on
opus somewhere in a four-hour drive. it cannot tell me *which stone*, and the stone is the whole
claim.

so the switch says so, where i already look — in the drive's own output, at the moment it happens:

```
🗿 5.3.verification
   └─ brain switched → claude-opus-5[1m]   (prescribed by 5.3.verification.guard)
```

three facts in one line: **which stone**, **which brain**, and **that a guard asked for it** rather
than a human at a keyboard. that third fact is the one i cannot reconstruct later from anything else.

and the case that matters more than the happy one: when the switch does **not** land, the record
must say so with the same clarity. cases 2, 3, and 4 each end in a stone that ran on a brain its
guard did not prescribe. if any of those reads the same as this line, then this line means naught —
a record that reports success identically to failure is not a record, it is decoration.

⇒ **the "aha": a prescription nobody can verify is a preference, not a control.** the feature's
entire justification is cost against quality. an unverifiable cost claim cannot be traded against
anything.

## .the bdd `[tn]` timeline

```
given a foreman who declared `brain: claude-opus-5[1m]` on 5.3.verification
  and  a driver enrolled as @:driver, reach=LIVE

  when [t0] the drive reaches 5.3.verification and the switch lands
    then the switch is announced in the driver's own stone-entry output — where the foreman
         already reads, not in a log they must know to open
    and  the line names all three: the stone, the brain, and that a guard prescribed it

  when [t1] the foreman later asks whether 5.3 truly ran on opus
    then the answer is readable from the drive's record
    and  it is not inferred from a bill, which cannot attribute spend to a stone

  when [t2] a stone declared a brain and the switch did NOT land (cases 2, 3, 4)
    then the record says so plainly, in the same place
    and  an unswitched stone can never read as a switched one

  when [t3] a stone declared a brain that was already live (case 6)
    then the record reads the same as every other stone — the applier ran the same act
    and  a no-op can never read as a switch, nor a switch as a no-op — because there are
         no no-ops to confuse (F14)

  when [t4] a stone inherited a brain from an earlier stone (case 7)
    then the record names the stone that set it
    and  an unasked-for cost is attributable to the guard that caused it
```

## 🔴 .`[t3]`'s distinction is DISSOLVED — `F14` resolved 2026-09-11

`[t3]` says the record must read *"already"* rather than *"switched"*. **that verdict existed only if
the applier compares**, and `F14` deleted the compare:

| the applier | what `[t3]` renders |
|---|---|
| ~~compare-then-set (`case=6` r1)~~ | ~~`already` on a match, `switched` on a difference~~ |
| 🔴 **converge** — the build | **`set` — every stone, every time.** there is no *already* to report |

⚠️ **and the guarantee `[t3]` exists for SURVIVES that**, in a stronger form. its clause reads *"a
no-op can never read as a switch"* — with a convergent applier **there are no no-ops**, so the
confusion it guards against cannot arise.

⇒ so `[t3]` is not deleted; **its distinction dissolves.** the record still names the stone, the
brain, and the guard — it simply never has two words to choose between.

🟡 **the same move strengthens `case=6` `[t3]`'s concern**: with a line on every stone, an **absent**
line is unambiguously a dead hook rather than a silent skip.

## .the fail-safe grade

this is a **happy** critipath, so the grade is not fail-safe — it is whether the guarantee holds
across every neighbour case:

| the record must part | from | covered at | 🔴 needs `F5`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| switched | not-switched | `[t2]` | 🟡 **for the strong form only** — re-graded below |
| switched | already-correct | `[t3]` | no — 🔴 and **the distinction is dissolved** (`F14`): no compare, so no no-op to part from |
| switched-here | inherited-from-there | `[t4]` | no — provenance is the route's own state |

⚠️ **`[t2]`, `[t3]` and `[t4]` are the case.** `[t0]` alone — a cheerful line on the happy path —
is the version that ships and then lies, because it prints on the one path that needed it least.

### 🔴 `[t2]` is `F5`-dependent, and the word `switched` is where it bites

added at peer review r3, found when a claim about **this** file was checked rather than recalled.

with no live-brain surface the hook knows the switch was **requested**, never that it **landed** —
which is `case=3`'s whole seam. so `[t2]`'s guarantee holds in two grades:

| | with `F5` | 🔴 without `F5` |
|---|---|---|
| the record's word | `switched` — a fact, confirmed against the live brain | `requested — unconfirmed` (`case=3` `[t5]`) |
| what a reader learns | 5.3 **ran** on opus | 5.3 was **asked** to run on opus, and nobody checked |
| what the record can never do | assert a switch that did not happen | ✅ **the same** — by construction, and that is the point |

⇒ **the guarantee this case exists for survives a refusal of `F5` intact:** no surface asserts a
switch that did not happen. what degrades is the record's *strength* — a verifiable ledger becomes an
honest request log, which is exactly the cost the yield names in the fulcrum council.

### 🔴 re-graded 2026-09-10 — the table above is one column short, and the absent one is buildable

the two columns read as *"a fact"* against *"a label"*, which made a refusal of `F5` look like a
collapse to a request log. **a third build exists and it ships today** — `rhx clone get` reads the
brain-cli's own transcript, so the hook learns whether the brain **refused**, with no ask on
`rhachet` (`case=3`, *the open question*).

| | detect-state — `F5` | 🔴 **detect-reply — `clone get`** | declare |
|---|---|---|---|
| the record's word | `switched` | 🔴 **`switched`, on the brain's own reply** | `requested — unconfirmed` |
| what a reader learns | 5.3 **ran** on opus | 5.3 asked for opus and **the brain did not object** | 5.3 was asked, and nobody checked |
| this case's cost claim | ✅ a **verifiable** ledger | 🟡 **verifiable to the reply's honesty** | 🔴 a request log |
| a **silent** refusal | ✅ caught | 🔴 **missed** — this is `F5`'s whole remaining purchase | 🟡 labelled |

⇒ 🔴 **so this case's cost claim does NOT degrade to a request log where `F5` is refused.** it drops
one grade, to *verifiable-to-the-reply* — and the yield's council section overstated the price of a
refusal by two grades, on the strength of a candidate list that was one option short.

⚠️ **the middle column is where the word `switched` gets its sharpest edge.** it is honest only where
the check reads a real refusal; a brain-cli that refuses **silently** leaves `switched` on the page
with naught behind it — the one lie this case exists to prevent. ⇒ a build that takes the middle
column **must** bound the word on the line: `switched ⟨no objection from the brain⟩`, never a bare
`switched`.

🟡 so `[t3]` and `[t4]` are `F5`-free, and a reader who reaches for that distinction now has it: the
`already` and the `inherited-from` verdicts are computed from the route's own state, and only the
`switched`-versus-`not-switched` verdict needs a fact the hook cannot hold alone.

## .the bound — this is a line, not a subsystem

no audit store, no per-stone brain ledger, no new artifact.

the wish asks for a brain switch; a record nobody can read is not a switch that happened. so the
scope here is exactly: **the stone-entry output names the brain and its provenance.** that is
`rule.require.status-feedback` applied to a mutation — *"a mutation that does not report what changed
= blocker"* — and nothing beyond it.

⇒ a durable per-stone brain ledger is a **dream**, not this route's work. it is caught as one rather
than smuggled in here.

## .why critipath

**freq** — every drive, every stone with a declared brain. **cost** — the feature's justification is
a cost-versus-quality trade; without attribution the trade cannot be evaluated, so the whole feature
becomes unfalsifiable. there is no workable route around it: no other surface can attribute a brain
to a stone.

⇒ `freq × cost` is intolerable, and no fallback exists = **critipath**.
