# domain.term.choice.reason: contrastive

## .etymology

the word is **linguistics' own**, and it was adopted rather than coined: a *contrastive* construction
is one that sets a term against a named alternative. that is exactly the clause shape
`rule.forbid.absolutes` needs a word for.

⇒ so the choice is an adoption, and the candidates below were weighed against it and rejected because
each drops the property that makes the shape permitted — **the binary**.

| candidate | why it lost |
|---|---|
| `comparative` | ranks two things on an axis, where this **excludes** one outright |
| `oppositional` | grades the author's stance; this term must grade the clause |
| `exclusionary` | names the exclusion and not the binary, so it admits a clause that excludes a whole set |
| `bounded` | already spoken here for what a claim STATES about its scope, so it would overload a live sense |

## 🟡 .the overload it is qualified against — a method of definition, not a clause shape

the two senses are tabled in `term=prose.contrastive._.choice._.md`. what belongs here is the corpus
behind that table and the reason the pair is worth a record at all.

- the foreign sense is spoken 6 times across 5 files, all in `role=thinker` — `contrastive
  coordinates` · `contrastive implication` (×4) · `contrastive analysis` · `contrastive spotlight`
- 🟡 **the two are close relatives, which is what makes the overload worth the record.** both turn on
  a named alternative — the thinker's to *reveal* a concept, this one to *bound* a claim
- ⇒ so a reader who holds one dereferences the other wrong and never notices

⇒ the overload is not blocker-grade: every thinker use sits in prose or in `.scratch`, and
`rule.forbid.domain-term-ambiguity` fires on a contract. the qualification is what keeps it that way.

🟡 this is the same discharge `prose.absolute` takes, and it is cheaper here — that one's foreign
sense sits in a published CLI flag (`symlink --mode absolute`), so the overload is already IN a
contract and the qualification is a repair. here it is a preventive.

⇒ **the walk is what made the term affordable, and it is the same walk that made its parent rule
affordable.** without the sort, `rule.forbid.absolutes` opens with a 2900-line backlog and a rename of
a booted published rule (`rule.require.generic-governs-structure-never-voice` carries the word in its
filename). with it, the rule opens with a candidate set of 1626 and no rename.

🟡 **so the 1274 is what this term EXEMPTS**, and it is a candidate set rather than a verified one — a
regex parts a contrastive shape from a quantifier shape, and it cannot part a contrastive shape from a
quantifier dressed in one.

### the discovery move

not a decomposition and not a citation — **a false positive that needed a name.** the term was not
sought; it was forced by the enforcement line of a rule authored the same round.

⇒ that is the cheapest kind of term to miss: a rule can state a carve-out in prose and ship, and the
absence shows only when two reviewers grade the same line differently.

## .invariants

- a clause that names **no** alternative is not contrastive, whatever its shape — it is an absolute
- a clause whose named alternative is itself a **set** is not contrastive — the binary is the property
- the thinker's sense is untouched by this term, and a bare `contrastive` dereferences to neither
