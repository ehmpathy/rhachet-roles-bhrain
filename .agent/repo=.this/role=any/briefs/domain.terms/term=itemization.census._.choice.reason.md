# domain.term.choice.reason: census

## .etymology

latin *census*, from *censere* — to assess, to register. a roman census enrolled **every**
citizen, and its authority came from that claim. the word carries completeness in its root,
which is the one property that parts it from a count.

## .rejected peers

- `tally` — a count kept as it grows. it makes no claim about who was not reached
- `survey` — a sample, and a sample's whole point is that it is incomplete
- `sweep` — already canonical for the ACT of a walk over a glossary (`glossary.sweep`). a sweep
  yields a census when it can report its own gaps; the two are act and claim, never synonyms

## .boundary

`itemization`, never `review` and never `prose`. the word is what an inventory or catalog asserts
about its own coverage (`term=itemization.inventory`: *"an inventory claims a census"*). the
review surfaces use it in that exact sense — a grade census counts every run by its grade, so a
reader of a sum can see how many runs it covers and how each was graded.

## .evidence

- the gap row `census` in `inventory.of=gaps._.md` named the cluster owed and its boundary; this
  cluster closes that row
- 18+ uses across `src` and the briefs, each a completeness claim over a declared set
- two measured instances where an instrument delivered a set of hits and could not deliver a
  census: `grepsafe` skips dot-prefixed files, so its silence is a lower bound
- the `repo-rules-briefs` lane on the review-cost-meter route named it un-itemized beside its
  peer instrument `meter`, 2026-09-27
