# case=6 · the human valve

- **coordinates** = `overrule × carried-unanswered × exhausted`
- **feel** = happy · **care** = critipath · **verdict** = demoed

the escape valve that makes case=5 survivable rather than stuck.

## .the narrative

*human, called in by a driver out of moves*

the driver escalated. the mechanic is exhausted and holds one blocker it says it cannot honestly
answer: a refactor that belongs to a different repo. I read the given — the driver is right, the
critique is true and out of scope.

so I take responsibility for the passage:

```sh
rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as overruled --level 1
```

it answers through the shared stone emit (`setStoneAsOverruled.ts:129-138`), so the confirmation
names the level I forgave **and the level now ready** — I need not work out what my own command
unlocked.

the driver re-enters and proceeds, and owes no `.taken` to the mechanic, because I answered for it —
with my name on the passage record.

**the aha:** the gate never needed to be un-escapable. it needed the escape to cost a *human
decision* instead of a whitespace edit. the same door exists; it now has a name on it.

## .the bdd timeline

```gherkin
given a stone `5.1.execution` with peer reviewer `mechanic` at level 1, exhausted
  and the mechanic holds 1 unanswered blocker at hash 7c04b9
  and the driver has escalated via --as blocked

[t0] when the driver runs --as passed
     then the entrance gate holds it                           # case=5

[t1] when the human runs --as overruled --level 1
     then passage.jsonl records status='overruled', level=1

[t2] when the driver runs --as passed
     then the entrance gate admits it
      and no .taken.by_self.mechanic is required                # forgiven at that level

[t3] given a second reviewer `architect` at level 3, with its own unanswered blocker
     when the driver runs --as passed after the l1 overrule
     then the entrance gate STILL holds, and cites `architect` alone
                                                               # per-level, never stone-wide

[t4] given instead EVERY level is terminal and no unanswered blocker remains
     when the human runs --as overruled
     then it is REFUSED, loudly: 'no blocked level to overrule — every review
          level is already terminal', and it names `--as passed` as the next move
```

`[t3]` keeps the valve honest — an overrule forgives the rung it names and no other, so a human
cannot wave a whole ladder through with one command.

⚠️ **`[t4]` is a refusal, never a silent idempotent success, and that is deliberate.**
`setStoneAsOverruled.ts:77-83`: a fresh overrule with no target *"would fall back to the terminal
level and mint a bogus 'forgiven by human' record on a level that cleared on its own merit"*, which
*"flips a later passage label from `allowed` to `overruled`"*. ⇒ **a false-provenance marker.** the
loud no-op protects the honesty of the record this whole case depends on.

## 🔴 the seam — P2 is what makes the valve hold

the overrule's target derivation is **not** a pure level-state read. `:77-79` scopes it: a target
exists when a level is un-terminal **AND** an unanswered blocker remains.

⇒ so `hasTarget` reads the very state P2 governs. under the hash key, an exhausted reviewer's
carried blocker evaporates on the next edit, and the human's valve then has **no target to forgive**
at the exact moment case=5 needs it. P2 keeps the debt on disk, so the target is still there.

**the valve and the gate are one mechanism seen twice** — the gate holds the driver because the debt
persists; the valve can forgive because the same debt persists. break P2 and both fail together, a
compound neither case alone reveals.

⚠️ the entrance gate must read **that same operation**, never a hand-rolled copy, or the two gates
drift on which reviewers a waved level forgives.

⇒ **the asymmetry is the whole safety property:** the driver cannot forgive itself; the human can
forgive the driver. the cheap exit is gone, the legitimate exit remains, and it leaves a signed
record.
