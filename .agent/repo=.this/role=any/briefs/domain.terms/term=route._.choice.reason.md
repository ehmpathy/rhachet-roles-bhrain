# domain.term.choice.reason: route

## .etymology

**route** — from latin *rupta (via)*, a **broken way**: a path forced through terrain by those who
went first. the etymology is the concept. a route is not a plan drawn in advance; it is a way that
was **walked**, and the breakage was paid by the first traveler so the next needs only follow.

that is exactly the pavement claim (`philosophy.pavement-saves-nature`): paid once, walked without
bound. the word carries it natively, which is why no coinage was needed.

## .why not the rejected synonyms

- **workflow** — the closest miss, and the reason the line must be stated. a workflow implies
  **steps a system executes**; a route is walked by a **brain that judges at every stone**. to
  call a route a workflow invites the assumption that it could be run unattended — the exact
  category error `rule.always.entool-the-skills-you-touch` warns against (*a tool that pretends
  determinism over a fluid process*)
- **pipeline** — implies data flows through fixed stages, each of which transforms it and hands it
  on. a route's stones do not transform a payload; they gate a traveler. and pipelines run to
  completion unattended, the opposite of a guarded stone that **halts** for judgment
- **process** — overloaded past usefulness: an os process, a business process, a legal process. a
  term that reads three ways cannot mark one concept (`rule.forbid.ambiguous-labels`)
- **playbook** / **procedure** — both name a *document to read*, not a path with **guards that
  refuse passage**. the guard is what separates a route from prose about a route; a playbook has
  no mechanism to stop a traveler who skips step 4

## .the line — route vs skill

both are enskilled work. they differ in **what walks them**:

| | paves | walked by |
|---|---|---|
| **skill** | a sequence of commands | an invocation |
| **route** | a sequence of judgments | a brain, stone by stone |

**the decisive case:** `declapract.upgrade`. the brain still performs every repair — no judgment
was removed. what the route retired is the need to recall that a reflection follows each repair,
and that hazards are detected before any repair begins. a linear skill could not have carried that,
because the steps are not commands to run but decisions to make in an order.

⇒ they are distinct concepts, not synonyms. see `philosophy.entoolment-is-the-pinnacle` for where
each sits.

## .disputes

none. the noun predates this capture by the whole of the driver role.

## .evidence

- **code**: `RouteStone.ts`, `RouteStoneGuard.ts`, `RouteBouncerCache.ts`,
  `RouteStoneDisposition.ts` and a dozen more declare it as a domain object prefix; the
  `src/domain.operations/route/` namespace and every `route.*` skill declare it as an operation
  prefix. it is arguably **the** central noun of this repo
- **the driver's own briefs** treat it as settled vocabulary — `howto.create-routes.[ref]`,
  `define.routes-are-gardened.[philosophy]`, `howto.drive-routes.[guide]`
- **the wisher named the concept's role in externalization (2026-08-13)**: *"a common way to
  enskill, if brains are required along the way, is to pave a route … offroad brains are best when
  they pave reuseable routes that can later be driven"* — which prompted this capture, and tied
  `route` to the learner's enskill/entool pair
- ⚠️ **the blind-spot instance, worth a permanent record.** `route` is among the most-used nouns
  in this repo and had **no cluster** until 2026-08-13 — the same day `entool` and `enskill`, coined
  hours earlier, got theirs. it is the second such find in one day (`enbrief` was the first), and
  it confirms the pattern: **the glossary's gap is never the novel term. it is the word so
  fundamental that nobody thinks to question it.** `template.domain-term.md` uses `term=stone` as
  its worked example, and no `term=stone` cluster existed either
- **invariant:** `route` names a domain object and operation namespace declared in THIS repo — not
  generic english like `path` or `way`
