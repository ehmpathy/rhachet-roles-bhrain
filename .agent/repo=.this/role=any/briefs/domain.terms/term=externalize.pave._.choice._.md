## .what

to **pave** is to lay a concept down as a durable, shared artifact, so a **future traveler** walks
it without re-derivation.

it is the operator-name for the learner's whole itemization discipline, and it carries a property
the alternatives lack: **a paved path is malleable at first and hardens with traffic.** a term can
always be re-paved — a dispute reopens it, a synonym is settled, a cluster is rewritten — but the
more contracts and briefs lean on it, the more expensive that repave becomes.

⇒ **pave early while malleable; repave deliberately once trafficked.**

## 🗿 .a ROUTE is what `pave` is named after

the four paved artifact kinds are not peers. they sit on a gradient, and **`route` sits at the one
end where the metaphor is no metaphor at all:**

| paved artifact | how literal the pavement is |
|---|---|
| 🗿 a **route** | **literal** — an ordered path, with markers (`stones`), that a traveler walks in sequence and may not skip |
| 💪 a **skill** | near-literal — the same path, with the turns already taken |
| 📚 a **brief** | figurative — prethought a reader picks up |
| 📜 a **term cluster** | figurative — one word settled, so nobody re-derives it |

⇒ **`route` is the paradigm case, and every other row borrows its vocabulary.** *path*, *traveler*,
*marker*, *wrong turn*, *offroad* — each is literal in a route and analogical everywhere else. that
is why `philosophy.pavement-saves-nature` reads as vivid rather than strained: it describes a route
plainly, then extends the description outward.

⚠️ **the two words are not rivals and they are not a pair.** `pave` is the **verb**; `route` is the
**noun that most fully instantiates it**. you pave a route, and you also pave a brief — but only a
route is a path in fact.

⚠️ **and the seam is easy to miss.** a road glyph 🛣️ for *"pave a route"* is a second marker for
what 🗿 already marks (`define.bhrain-repo-mascot`) — a reader who cannot see that `pave` and
`route` name one concept from two angles invents a third marker for the overlap.

## ⚠️ .pave is NOT a synonym of `learn`

they differ in **beneficiary and direction**, and the distinction carries real weight:

| verb | the act | the beneficiary |
|---|---|---|
| **learn** | durably retain a lesson so it is not re-derived | **the retainer** |
| **pave** | lay a reusable path | **a future traveler who never had the lesson** |

the decisive case: a lesson can be **learned and never paved** — retained by one clone,
unavailable to the next. both words stand; neither forbids the other.
