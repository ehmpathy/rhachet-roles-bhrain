# wish: every brief a seed has asked for is present in the role that needs it

## .what

**find all the seeds in radio that ask for briefs, and write them.**

the radio queue holds 41 open seeds. a large share ask for one thing: a **brief** — a lesson, a
rule, a howto, a clarification, a promotion to say-level — to be written into a role of this repo.
not one has landed.

the outcome: for every open seed that asks for a brief on an extant role, **that brief exists in the
repo, the seed is claimed, and the seed is closed.**

## .why

a brief nobody wrote is a lesson nobody has. every one of these seeds is a lesson someone paid for
in the field — a defect hit, a wrong call made, a round lost — and each has sat in a queue instead
of in the role that would have prevented the next one.

the cost compounds in a way a code defect does not:

- a **code** defect bites the next caller, once, loudly
- an **absent brief** bites every clone that boots that role, silently, forever — and each victim
  re-derives the same lesson at full price

the queue's shape is the evidence. **41 open, zero claimed.** the oldest ask dates to well before
this quarter; the newest landed this week. a growth-only queue of lessons is a repo that learns
naught while it accumulates proof that it should.

this dispatch is not about any one brief. it is about the **arrears**.

## .scope — briefs ONLY

**in scope:** briefs. lessons, rules, howtos, defines, clarifications, promotions between say-level
and ref-level, and the `boot.yml` wiring that makes them load.

**out of scope, and these are hard bounds:**

- **no new skills.** many seeds here ask for a skill, a route, a guard, an sdk export, or a code
  fix. leave every one of them open and untouched. a seed may mention a brief in passing and still
  be code work
- **no new roles.** several seeds ask for roles this repo does not have — `navigator`, `router`,
  `reflector`, `researcher` among them. **do not create any of them.** a brief written into a role
  with no `boot.yml` is a file no clone ever loads
- **no seeds owned by another repo** — leave them

## .claim what you take

**claim each seed you choose, at the moment you choose it — before you write its brief.**

an unclaimed seed you are mid-way through is indistinguishable from an untouched one, so a peer can
pick up work already underway. the claim is what makes the queue readable to anyone but you.

then close it when its brief lands, and let the close name the file that satisfies it — so the
queue's count drops by exactly what was delivered, and a reader can trace every close to an
artifact.

a seed you judge OUT of scope stays **open and unclaimed**. a wrongly closed seed is worse than an
unclosed one: it retires the question.

## .acceptance

1. every in-scope seed has its brief in the repo, in the right role, booted where it warrants
2. every seed you took is **claimed**, then **closed** against the file that satisfies it
3. every seed you judged out of scope is left open, unclaimed, and named in your yield with the
   reason
4. your yield carries **the enumeration you settled on** and the criterion you used — this is the
   artifact the next round trusts

## .the enumeration is yours, and here is what i learned failing at it

i tried twice, from outside a peer repo's queue, and both attempts were unsound. recorded so you
skip two dead ends — not as a method to adopt:

| my filter | why it failed |
|---|---|
| **title prefix** (`lesson(`, `docs(`, `teach`, `brief:`) | a seed titled `feat(<role>)` can be a pure brief ask start to finish. a title prefix is orthogonal to what a body asks for |
| **body keyword** (`brief`, `rule.`, `lesson`, `howto.`) | it catches every seed that merely *mentions* a rule, which is most of the code fixes |

the criterion that parts *asks for a brief* from *mentions a brief* is a read of the body against
what each role already carries. that read is in-repo work, and it is yours.

## .expect the arc, not the item

several clusters in this queue read as **one thought decomposed on the fly** — seeds pushed minutes
apart that share a subject and only make sense together. the learner has at least one such cluster
this week, and the driver has another.

do not write one brief per seed by reflex. where several seeds are one lesson, **one brief that
serves them all** is the better artifact, and closing each of those seeds against that one file is a
correct close. the reverse holds too: a seed carrying two separable lessons may earn two briefs.

the seed count and the brief count are not meant to match. say where they diverge, and why.

## .this repo's own vocabulary is a live hazard here

several seeds propose or refine **terms** — one asks outright to pave a term for the learner's own
verb. this repo declares the glossary discipline that governs that, and it binds your own work:

- a term this repo already declares has one canonical word. **conform, or open a dispute** in its
  `.reason` — never drift to a synonym while you write a brief about it
- a term a seed asks you to PAVE gets its full `term=<x>._.choice.*` cluster, reason file included.
  a term paved without its etymology and evidence is a guess on record dressed as a decision
- a seed that merely USES a word is not a request to pave it

you own this vocabulary. a brief that quietly introduces a second word for an extant concept costs
more than the brief was worth.

## .a seed worth reading FIRST, because it governs the rest

**"lesson-extraction must be timeless — a distilled lesson must not reference the session that
produced it."**

it is a brief ask, and it is also the standard every file you write in this behavior must meet. a
brief that says *"we decided"* or *"as discussed"* or *"not yet"* rots the moment its conversation
is forgotten. read it before you start, and hold your own output to it.

## .the HOW is yours — advisory only

per `rule.require.wish-outcome-over-proposal`, `.what` / `.why` / `.acceptance` and the scope bounds
are authoritative. all else is ground, not instruction.

itemized, so no line below reads as a spec:

- **the five-role table** — **verified fact**, read from `origin/main` on 2026-08-12. it bounds
  scope and it binds
- **the no-new-roles and no-new-skills exclusions** — **constraints that bind**, and the reason is
  stated: a brief in a role with no boot.yml is a file nobody loads, and a skill is not a brief.
  neither is taste
- **claim-then-close** — the **outcome** binds; a claimed seed is legible to a peer and an unclaimed
  one is not. the mechanism is yours, though the radio already has one
- **the two failed filters** — **evidence** of what does not work. not a method
- **"expect the arc"** — a **proposal**. the property that serves the outcome is that a reader finds
  one coherent lesson per concept rather than N restatements. how you group them is entirely yours
- **the glossary caution** — the rules it names are this repo's own and they bind. which terms are
  at stake, and what each resolves to, is yours to settle in-repo
- **"read the timeless-lesson seed first"** — a **suggestion of order**, nothing more. take a better
  sequence if you find one

if you deliver the acceptance criteria with a shape this wish did not imagine — a different
grouping, a different count, one brief that supersedes three seeds — that is a success, not a
deviation. say why in your yield.
