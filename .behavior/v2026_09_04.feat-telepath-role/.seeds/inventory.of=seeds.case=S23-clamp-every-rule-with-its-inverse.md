## .settled

**every rule in the canon carries its inverse.** a `require` is clamped by a `forbid`; a `forbid` is
clamped by a `require`.

⇒ this **generalizes `S16`**, which settled the pair for one trait — *"the require names the target;
the forbid names the smell. either alone leaves half the cue."* the wisher now applies it to the
whole canon.

### the reason is a RETRIEVAL claim, and it is new

> *"forbids are easier for clones to recognize"*

| directive | what a reader must do to fire it |
|---|---|
| a **require** | hold the **target** in mind, and compare the passage against it |
| a **forbid** | **pattern-match a named smell on the page** |

⇒ a forbid fires on **recognition**; a require fires on **recall**. and recall is what fails under
load — a reader who has read, believed, and agreed with a require will still write past it, because
no cue on the page matches.

🟡 **that is `rule.require.specialize-a-rule-its-readers-look-past`'s mechanism**, reached on a
different axis: that rule fixes a retrieval failure by **specialization**; this fixes one by
**inversion**. same defect, second lever.

### the clamp metaphor is exact

a clamp needs **two** jaws. one alone holds naught:

- the **require** alone → the author knows the target and cannot see they missed it
- the **forbid** alone → the author knows the smell and cannot see what to aim at

### the first pair was proposed, and held open

`forbid.obfuscation` as the peer to `require.elucidation` — offered by the wisher with *"what do you
think"* and *"maybe theres a better alt though"*, so **the name is open, the pairing is settled**.

## .landed

- **owed**: an inverse per unpaired rule. extant pairs: `require.bulletize` ⇄ `forbid.narration` ·
  `require.brevity` ⇄ `forbid.rambles` (ehmpathy, owed to move)
- 🟡 the **bound** the settlement does not state: a forbid earns its place when it **names smells**,
  never when it merely negates its require. a negation doubles the boot cost and adds no cue
