## .the gap, precisely

**three booted driver briefs assert the guard edit is the driver's lever. the cli's own usage
string says the privilege behind it is human-only.** both claims are in this repo, and they are
checkable in one read each:

| the claim | where |
|---|---|
| owner column: `rhx route.mutate.guard` → **driver** | `src/domain.roles/driver/briefs/rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation.md:15` |
| *"the repair is a guard edit"*, with the command, no grant mentioned | `src/domain.roles/driver/briefs/rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions.md:77-83` |
| *"the bind is a guard field, and the guard is the driver's to edit"* | `src/domain.roles/driver/briefs/rule.forbid.hand-run-reviews.md:57-58` |
| 🔴 `rhx route.mutate grant allow   # grant privilege (human only)` | `src/contract/cli/route.ts:1899` |

```sh
rhx grepsafe --pattern 'route.mutate.guard' --path src/domain.roles/driver/briefs
rhx grepsafe --pattern 'human only' --path src/contract/cli
rhx route.mutate grant get
```

**the mechanism**: `routeMutateGrant` gates on the presence of
`$route/.route/.privilege.mutate.flag` (`route.ts:1923-1927`). absent the flag, `route.mutate.guard`
refuses and prints *"the route is sealed / it reveals itself one stone at a time"*
(`route.mutate.guard.output.sh:30-31`).

⚠️ **the seal itself is correct and deliberate** — it is a focus mechanism, and
`research.importance-of-focus` is the argument for it. **this dream proposes no change to the
seal.** the defect is that three briefs describe a lever as the driver's and omit that it sits
behind a human-only flag.

## 🔴 .why this one bites harder than an ordinary doc gap

`rule.forbid.hand-run-reviews` exists **because of this exact failure, in the other direction.**
its own last section says so:

> *the recipe existed because the guard-edit lever was absent from the owner table. a driver who
> believes the bind is not theirs reaches for the only tool left. fix the ownership, and the
> hand-run loses its motive.*

and `rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation` states the general form:

> 🔴 *an absent row does not read as absent. it reads as "not mine."*

⇒ **the repair added the row, and the row over-corrected.** a driver now reads *"the guard is
yours"*, reaches for it, hits a human-only flag, and is back in the position the rule was written
to prevent — except now with **no sanctioned move at all**, because the hand-run is forbidden and
the guard edit is gated.

🔴 **the general form, and it is the transferable half:** *a row that says MINE and is gated fails
worse than an absent row.* an absent row sends a driver to hunt for the owner; a wrong row stops
the hunt before it starts.

## .the shape of the fix

**leave the seal alone, and leave the owner column's intent alone.** add the gate to the row, in
all three places, so a driver learns the precondition before they reach:

```md
| 🔴 `rhx route.mutate.guard` — the stone's own `--paths-with`, `--conversation`, `level`, `budget`
  | **driver**, ⚠️ **behind a human-granted flag** — check `rhx route.mutate grant get` first; a
  `blocked (no privilege flag)` means the ask is `rhx route.mutate grant allow`, and only a human
  may run it |
```

and in `rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions`' overflow section, one line above the command:

```md
⚠️ check `rhx route.mutate grant get` first. the route is sealed by default, so on most routes this
command refuses until a human runs `rhx route.mutate grant allow`. **that ask is the escalation** —
it is a one-command grant, and it is the only lever between an overflowed lane and its repair.
```

🟡 **and the escalation path is what the briefs really lack.** today an overflowed lane reads as
un-escalatable: the guard edit is *"yours"* so no driver surfaces it, the hand-run is forbidden,
and a commit is forbidden. ⇒ with the grant named, an overflowed lane becomes a normal, cheap
escalation with an exact command — which is what `rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation`
asks for in its own final test.

🔴 **six files, not three** — each brief has a `.md` and a published `.md.min` beside it, and a
`.min` that keeps the un-gated row would re-teach the defect to every consumer that boots the
condensed form.

## .why it is not done in this round

the SAFE/CLEAN test (`rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`):

- **SAFE — no.** these are **booted doctrine** for the driver role, published to other repos. a
  change to an owner table changes what every driver in every repo believes about who may do what.
  the drive has **one** observation on **one** route, and cannot tell from it whether the intended
  design is *"drivers get the grant routinely and this route simply lacks it"* or *"the row is
  wrong and the owner is human"*. **the two repairs read differently and the drive does not know
  which is meant**
- **CLEAN — no.** six files across three brief clusters, in a role-doctrine subsystem this wish
  never touched, added to a diff already ~240 files wide and under active review

⇒ deferred, and because the SAFE test is what fails, it owes a **fulcrum** as well as this dream
(`rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`'s table: *unsafe is a harder stop than unclean*).
that fulcrum is **`F21`**.

## .the one property that makes it worth the trip

the defect is **silent, and it hides itself**. a driver who hits the wall has no row that says a
grant exists, so the natural read is *"guard edits are not available here"* rather than *"i need to
ask for one command."* ⇒ the failure teaches the driver to stop the ask, which is the precise
behavior the three briefs were each written to prevent.
