## .the gap, precisely

### 1 · the unit under test may be a PATTERN, and the rule assumes a WORD

the rule's test reads:

> **1. "what are ALL the instances this word must cover?"**
> **2. "does the candidate cover every one — and does it EXCLUDE its neighbours?"**

both steps take the *word* as the unit, so the enumeration is the word's own use sites. `entrait`
has one, so the rule bars it.

🟡 but `entrait` is tested against a *pattern* — `en$X` produces a `$X` — with three prior members:

| verb | produces | prior? |
|---|---|---|
| `enbrief` | a brief | yes |
| `enskill` | a skill | yes |
| `entool` | a tool | yes |
| `entrait` | a trait | the first application |

⇒ **the family IS the enumeration, and it was walked before the member was named.** the rule's own
bar was already cleared, and the rule cannot see it.

### 2 · a rule's SCOPE is a set too, and the rule binds only words

`rule.forbid.subversive-prose` shipped 2026-09-04 with one test — *"could a reader disagree?"* — and
ran two days. `#444` then measured it against three passages rather than one:

| the specimen | prose | could a reader disagree? | what settles it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| the hedge | *"the review flow has some issues worth a look"* | no — **caught** | — |
| the metaphor | *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | **yes — so it PASSED** | **none** |
| the mechanism | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStone…Unforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* | yes | open the file |

⇒ the metaphor is the expensive instance and the rule passed it. the disagreement test grades **taste**.

| the artifact | the failure an enumeration catches |
|---|---|
| a word | too narrow (a row it misses) · too wide (it covers the neighbours) |
| **a rule's test** | too narrow (a defect it misses) · **too wide (it fires on the good case too)** |

> **a test that answers YES on both the good and the bad case is a filter one notch too coarse.**

🟡 the wrong-scope failure is the more expensive of the two. a wrong word costs a rename; a wrong
scope ships a gate that passes the defect it was built for, and it does so *silently* — the rule
appears to work, because the half it does catch is the half a reviewer meets most often.

## .the shape of the fix

### branch 1 — a step 0, before the extant step 1

> **0. is the unit under test the WORD, or a PATTERN it instantiates?**
>
> - a **word** → steps 1 and 2 as written. list its use sites
> - a **pattern** → the enumeration is the pattern's extant members, and a candidate passes when it
>   fits them. one use site is then no evidence at all, in either direction

🟡 **the carve-out must stay narrow, or it becomes a licence to coin by rhyme.** two properties make
a family an enumeration: **stated** (written down, over inferred from a look-alike suffix) and
**≥2 prior members** that satisfy it, verified against disk over recalled. `en-` qualifies on both.

### branch 2 — one clause, for a rule's own test

> **before a rule ships: write the defect three ways — absent, plausible-but-wrong, and correct —
> and confirm the test separates all three.**

### the when-rows both branches owe

| when… | then… |
|---|---|
| the candidate is a member of a declared family | the family is the enumeration. do not count the member's own uses |
| you author a rule's test | write the defect three ways first. a two-way check confirms only that the test is not empty |
| a test catches every instance you can think of | 🟡 you thought of the instances the test already suggests. write the plausible-but-wrong row on purpose |
| a rule reports a defect and names one repair | check whether the defect has two shapes. if the repairs differ, the rule must **sort**, over flag |

⇒ the last row is the general lesson `#444` carries past its own subject: **a grade with one label
over two opposite repairs leaves the reader unable to act on it.**
