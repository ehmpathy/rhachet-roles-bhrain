# 1.vision.experience.dimensions

## .what

the orthogonal axes the telepath experience varies over, and the product to walk.

⇒ the walk lands in `1.vision.experience.case=_.md`. the demos land in `1.vision.experience.case=N.$slug.md`.

## .the axes

### D1 · actor — who encounters it (4)

| value | who |
|---|---|
| `author` | a bhrain that composes prose — a chat reply, a yield, a brief, a code header |
| `driver` | a bhrain that drives a route and meets a stone guard |
| `adopter` | whoever wires telepath into a role or a repo |
| `reader` | the actor a telepath artifact is addressed to — human or robot |

🟡 **goal is not a separate axis.** it maps 1:1 onto actor — author→compose, driver→grade, adopter→adopt, reader→act. two axes that correlate are one axis.

#### `reader` collapses human and robot, and the collapse is a SUBSUMPTION with a stated bound

`case=6`'s bdd names the split outright — `[t0]` a human *"reads only what stands out"* and can then state the ask; `[t3]` a robot parses and can extract it, *"not merely notice it."* those are different failure modes, so by this file's own latent-dimension test the pair is a candidate axis and it is not walked.

🟡 the citation read `[t2]` until `i010`, and `[t2]` is the multi-ask criterion — *"a message carries two or more asks."* the subsumption argument was unaffected; the coordinate was wrong. it was introduced in the same round I claimed a `Grep`-based sweep had closed the mis-aimed-reference class — and that is the limit of the countermeasure, stated rather than papered over: **a `Grep` matches a string, and a citation is wrong at the level of what the cited step SAYS.** only a read of the target catches it.

⇒ it is subsumed rather than walked, and the ground is a one-way containment:

| the treatment | serves the human? | serves the robot? |
|---|---|---|
| **machine-detectable** — a token a parser can match | yes — it is also visible | yes |
| **merely visual** — bold, indentation, position | yes | ❌ **no** — the parser matches no token |

**so the robot face is the stricter of the two, and a treatment that satisfies it satisfies both.** `case=6` `[t3]` states that as its own criterion: *"the treatment is machine-detectable, never merely visual."*

🟡 the bound, and it is what makes this a subsumption rather than a dismissal: the containment holds only while the criterion stays *machine-detectable*. a rule that ever prescribes a purely visual treatment breaks it, and the axis becomes real that same day.

| the trigger that would split D1's `reader` | why |
|---|---|
| a rule prescribes a **visual-only** treatment | the human is served and the robot is not — the cells diverge |
| a robot surface acquires a failure the human's does not — a parse throw, a token budget | a second failure mode with no human twin |

⇒ the walked `reader` cells are therefore the robot's, and the human's are contained in them. that is stated here so no reviewer reads the collapse as an oversight, and so the trigger that reopens it is on record rather than re-derived.

### D2 · mechanism — the surface that carries the rule (3)

| value | when it fires | what a use costs |
|---|---|---|
| `brief` | at authorship, from the say tier | tokens on every boot, forever |
| `reviewer` | at a stone guard, on an arrived artifact | one review run per arrival |
| `subconscious` | in flight, from a cheap background brain | a continuous small-model watch |

#### .the fourth value, considered and EXCLUDED — `hook`

a `Write|Edit` PreToolUse term hook is a real fourth surface on this axis, and it is live today: `.claude/settings.json:142-155` wires `forbid-terms.gerunds` and `forbid-terms.blocklist`, and both halted writes on this very route.

| where it would sit | when it fires | what a use costs |
|---|---|---|
| between `brief` and `reviewer` | at the write, from a regex matcher | **zero brain** — a pattern match |

⇒ it fits the axis cleanly and even preserves its implicit order: authorship → write → arrival → in-flight.

🟡 **it is excluded from the walked product on a SCOPE ground, never an orthogonality one**, and the reason is stated so the exclusion is auditable rather than an oversight:

- the wish's deliverables are a role (briefs) and a reviewer. a PreToolUse hook is repo config (`.claude/settings.json`), owned by no role — telepath could not ship one as part of itself
- to admit it grows D2 from 3 to 4, which adds 48 cells to the walk (`4 × 4 × 2 × 3 × 2 = 192`) and `768` to the full product — every one of them about a mechanism outside the deliverable set
- `rule.require.experience-catalog-evolution` names this exact carve-out: *"a discovery that is genuinely out of the wish's bounded scope is flagged to the wisher, not force-fit into the contract"*

⇒ so it is flagged to the wisher in `1.vision.yield.md` §5 as a cheaper surface for any rule that reduces to a phrase — and the product below stays at `3`.

**if the wisher pulls a hook into scope, this axis becomes 4 and the walk is owed again at 192 (768 across all slices).** that is the trigger, stated now so nobody has to rediscover it.

### D4 · supply — the state of what the mechanism needs (3)

| value | means |
|---|---|
| `complete` | telepath's canon resolves, and the subject fits the context gate |
| `canon-absent` | the output-half canon is not reachable from telepath |
| `oversized` | the artifact under review exceeds the context gate |

🟡 **supply is orthogonal to mechanism, never derived from it.** `canon-absent` is today's real state for `brief` — telepath boots **four** rules (three, until `rule.forbid.subversive-prose` was wired at `i018`) and not one of them says *be terse*. it is separately the hazard that couples `reviewer` to a sibling repo — a rubric that cites a repo the consumer never enrolled.

#### .this axis is a FLATTENED 2×2, and the fourth state has no coordinate

the three values are not one enum. they are two independent binaries, collapsed:

| | subject fits the gate | subject **oversized** |
|---|---|---|
| **canon found** | `complete` | `oversized` |
| **canon absent** | `canon-absent` | ∅ **impossible** — see the verdict below |

⇒ the fourth coordinate — a rubric that cites an unenrolled repo and a 155.8k yield — carries a verdict rather than a blank. the verdict is `impossible`, and its reason is a source read, not a convenience.

**it stays at 3, and the ground is a source-read precedence, never convenience.** the two failures do not co-occur; they sequence, deterministically:

- supplies are looked up first — a zero-match rules glob throws `BadRequestError('--rules glob was ineffective')` at `stepReview.ts:328`, behavior pinned by `stepReview.integration.test.ts:116-136`
- the gate is measured after — the overflow throws `BadRequestError('validation failed')` at `stepReview.ts:712`, under a hint that names the token breakdown

⇒ 328 precedes 712 in the same operation, so the order is a property of the code rather than an inference about it. a run cannot reach the gate with an unresolved rubric.

⇒ so the fourth coordinate's verdict is `impossible`, with that citation as its reason — never a blank, and never a cell quietly absent from the walk.

🟡 the counter deserves a direct answer, since a reviewer raised it:

- *"the matrix is a state-space of the experience across a route, not a single in-process run"*
  - ⇒ so an absent canon plus a yield that will overflow is a real configuration
- true, and it still **sequences**
  - across a route the driver meets the canon error, repairs it, re-arrives, and only then meets the overflow
  - ⇒ two encounters at two extant cells, which is exactly how the matrix represents it — `case=8` walks it at `[t1]` → `[t3]`

⇒ so `canon-absent` shadows `oversized`:

- the driver meets the canon error
- the adopter repairs it
- the driver re-arrives, and *then* meets the overflow
- ⇒ the composite is two extant cells walked in order, never a fourth

`case=8` already demonstrates that order, and it declares the cell sequence in its own header — `driver · reviewer · sharp · canon-absent · file` `[t1]` → `driver · reviewer · sharp · oversized · file` `[t3]`, with the adopter's repair at `[t2]` between them. ⇒ the shadow is not asserted here; it is walked there.

🟡 corrected at `i008`: this previously assigned the throw to the adopter and the repair to the driver, which inverts `case=8`'s own seam:

- that seam is the reason the walk exists — *"the actor who hits the error and the actor who can fix it are different people"*
  - the driver often lacks write access to the config that would repair it
- ⇒ the shadow verdict is unaffected; only the actor attribution was wrong

🟡 the axis keeps 3 values; what it owed was this proof. an unexplained 3-value enum over a 2×2 reads as an oversight and cannot be audited.

#### 🟡 .the axis name borrows a term this repo declares with an OPPOSED sense

`define.review-ubiqlang.supplies-vs-subjects` settles the pair for review operations:

| term | what it means there |
|---|---|
| **supplies** | the files that **inform** the review — rules, refs, briefs |
| **subjects** | the files **under** review |

⇒ **D5 uses `subject` correctly. D4 does not use `supply` correctly**: `canon-absent` is a genuine supply state, and `oversized` is a subject state filed on a supply-named axis.

🟡 so a reader must not read D4 in the reviewer's sense. D4 asks *"which precondition fails before a verdict is possible?"*, and preconditions sit on both sides of that pair.

the name is left in place, deliberately:

- `rule.forbid.domain-term-synonyms` scopes its blocker to **contracts** — a dobj/dop name, a signature, a published interface
  - an axis label in a behavior artifact is prose
- ⇒ a rename would touch 15 call sites across two artifacts for no contract gain, and a partial rename is the worse outcome

⇒ it becomes a blocker the moment this axis is lifted into a rubric field, a cli flag, or a type. at that point it is a contract, and the correct word is `precondition`.

### .a SIXTH axis, considered and EXCLUDED — `read` (correct · misread)

telepath's own booted canon names it. `rule.require.answer-what-they-meant.md.min:20-25`:

> *"**misread-and-terse is the trap, and the output-half rules make it worse.** terseness strips the reasons an actor would have used to spot the misread."*

⇒ so `terse × correct-read` and `terse × misread` are two genuinely different experiences, and every axis above assigns them the same coordinates. that is the signature of a missed dimension.

| read | answer | what transfers |
|---|---|---|
| correct | terse | all of it — this is `case=1` |
| **misread** | **terse** | **none, and it arrives with confidence** |

🟡 **it is excluded on a SCOPE ground, and the ground is the role's own split:**

- read-correctness is telepath's input face, and the input face already shipped — `rule.require.answer-what-they-meant` boots today
- all ten of this wish's issues live in the output face
- to admit it doubles the product — `576 → 1,152` as the product now stands — and every added cell restates an input-half rule this behavior does not touch

⇒ but the interaction is real and it lands as a design constraint rather than a cell: the output rules must carve out the stated read — the one line `answer-what-they-meant` prescribes as the defense against row 3 — or they will grade it a ramble and delete the only fail-safe the misread case has.

⇒ recorded in `1.vision.yield.md` §8, and it is a constraint the wish does not name.

### .and the excluded cells carry a VERDICT, not a blank

an axis excluded with no verdict on its cells reads as coverage — a reviewer who trusts the completeness-by-construction claim would believe the walk covers the misread face. it does not.

⇒ **`misread × terse` is `impossible` within this vision's declared scope**, and that is its verdict. the walk covers the `correct-read` face only.

| cell | verdict |
|---|---|
| `correct-read × terse` | walked — it is `case=1` |
| `misread × terse` | **`impossible` for this scope** — read-correctness is the input half, and it already shipped |

the trigger that would make it real: the wisher pulls the input half into scope. the walk is then owed again at 1,152, and the misread cells become live rather than out-of-scope.

🟡 and the design constraint stands regardless of the verdict:

- the output rules must carve out the **stated read** — the one line `answer-what-they-meant` prescribes as the defense against a terse wrong answer
- ⇒ or they will grade it a ramble and delete the only fail-safe the misread case has
- a live obligation on #411 and #415, recorded in `1.vision.yield.md` §8

### D6 · attempt — is the verdict computed, or reused? (2)

| value | means |
|---|---|
| `fresh` | the mechanism runs and produces a verdict |
| `cached` | the guard reuses a prior clean verdict, keyed on the artifact hash |

added 2026-09-04, after the `dimensional-decomposition` peer reviewer graded its absence a blocker. the reviewer's case, and it holds:

> *"the re-arrival / retried-run experience is one of the sharpest paths in the whole vision … case=3's timeline ends on a cached re-arrival (t4); case=8 walks … the artifact-hash cache on re-arrival (t8) … that is a real, named, high-severity experience that has no dimension, no walked cell, and no verdict."*

⇒ **the same coordinates carry two different experiences**, which is the latent-dimension signature D5 was found by:

| at `driver · reviewer · happy · complete · file` | what happens |
|---|---|
| `fresh` | the reviewer runs, costs a brain call, returns a verdict about **this** artifact under **this** rubric |
| `cached` | no reviewer runs, no cost — and the verdict may predate a rubric change |

🟡 **`retried` is not a third value on THIS axis.** what `attempt` varies is whether a verdict is computed or reused, never how many times the driver has arrived.

but the clause that followed it was wrong, and `D7` below is the repair:

- it read *"a re-arrival on a changed artifact busts the hash and runs fresh, so its experience is the `fresh` one"* — with `fresh` treated as ONE experience
- the `dimensional-decomposition` reviewer refuted that at `i003`, from this walk's own `case=8`
  - a warm re-arrival carries the driver's `.taken` articulations in its subject
  - ⇒ and the reviewer's obligation changes with them
- ⇒ so `fresh` covered two experiences, and the split is a second axis, never a third value here

#### .the axis is heavily constrained, and the constraints are the point

`cached` requires a prior clean run, which forces four of the other five axes:

| axis | why `cached` pins it |
|---|---|
| mechanism = `reviewer` | the artifact-hash cache is a guard-reviewer mechanism. a brief and a subconscious have no verdict to cache |
| supply = `complete` | a `canon-absent` run throws and a `oversized` run overflows. **neither produces a verdict, so neither can populate a cache** |
| subject = `file` | inherited from `reviewer` |
| actor ∈ `{driver, adopter}` | **the cache is a property of a GUARD arrival**, never of a bare reviewer invocation. `route.stone.set` computes the artifact hash and consults the cache; a hand-run `rhx review` does neither |

⇒ so the `cached` slice is 144 coordinates of which 4 are real: `{driver, adopter} × reviewer × {happy, sharp} × complete × file`. the rest are ∅ by the constraints above, and the inventory states that inheritance once rather than 140 times.

🟡 **this table stated THREE pins until `i015`, while it derived a set that needs four.** the fourth was added to the inventory at `i007`, after a reviewer showed the count was otherwise unfalsifiable — three pins leave `actor (4) × feel (2) = 8` candidates, so *"140 are ∅"* justified at most 136, and the `author` and `reader` rows were dropped with no bar named.

⇒ the repair landed on the deliverable and not on the artifact that defines the axis. so for eight iterations a reader of this file alone could not derive the count the same file asserts — which is the sharper form of the defect, because `dimensions.md` is where a reader comes to check the inventory, not the other way round.

#### .and it gives F08's seam a coordinate it did not have

`driver · reviewer · sharp · complete · file · cached` **is** the stale-verdict cell — a rubric edited after a clean cache entry, invisible to the next arrival.

🟡 **that cell previously lived off the matrix**, deferred as a dream under `F08`. the reviewer's sharpest line is that a deferral is not a verdict: *"the cache seam must be walked and verdicted rather than deferred off the matrix."* it is now walked. **`F08` still defers the FIX; the EXPERIENCE is on the grid.**

### D7 · priors — does the subject carry a prior round's answers? (2)

| value | means |
|---|---|
| `none` | the subject holds no `.taken` from an earlier round. the reviewer reads **cold** |
| `taken` | the subject holds the driver's `.taken.by_self` articulations. the reviewer reads **warm** |

**added 2026-09-04, after the `dimensional-decomposition` reviewer graded its absence a blocker at `i003`.** the reviewer applied this walk's own latent-dimension test to the walk itself, and it fires:

> *"A cold first arrival and a warm convergence re-arrival are two genuinely different experiences (distinct failure modes — the cold one finds accretion, the warm one must not re-raise answered blockers), and the matrix gives them the SAME coordinates."*

⇒ correct, and the refutation came from `case=8` boundary 4, which I wrote:

| at `driver · reviewer · sharp · complete · file · fresh` | what the reviewer must do |
|---|---|
| `none` — cold | grade the artifact. every defect it finds is new |
| `taken` — warm | grade the artifact **and honor the `.taken`**. a point already answered must not be re-raised, or the driver's fix *"reads to it as no fix at all"* |

#### .why a second axis, and NOT a third `attempt` value

the reviewer offered both: *"a `round` axis (first · converged) or a third `attempt` value (`fresh-converged`)."* the third value is refused, and the ground is this file's own D4 section.

|  | `none` | `taken` |
|---|---|---|
| **`fresh`** | the cold first arrival | the warm convergence re-read — 🎬 **`case=10`** |
| **`cached`** | **4 real cells** — see D6's `cached` slice | a third arrival, unchanged, hash hit |

🟡 **`cached · none` is the COMMON corner, never an empty one.** the cache is keyed on the artifact hash, never on a convergence — and a clean prior run returns `0/0`, which writes no `.taken` at all. so `cached` implies `priors = none` by default, and `cached · taken` is the narrower of the two.

⇒ it is a 2×2 with four occupied corners, and a `fresh-converged` third value would flatten it — the exact defect D4 already carries a section about, and the one a reviewer made me justify at `i001`. to repeat it one axis later would be to take a repair I had already argued against.

🟡 `round` is refused too, and for the reason `retried` was:

- a 2nd arrival and a 5th are the same experience — what varies is whether answers are present, never how many rounds have passed
- ⇒ `priors` names the state; `round` would name a counter

#### .the slice is pinned, and the pins are what it teaches

`taken` presupposes a prior rejection, which forces three axes:

| axis | why `taken` pins it |
|---|---|
| **actor** = `driver` | only a driver converges through a guard. an author's by-hand run writes no `.taken` |
| **mechanism** = `reviewer` | a `.taken` answers a reviewer's verdict. a brief renders none |
| **subject** = `file` | inherited from `reviewer` |

⇒ so the `taken` slice is 288 coordinates of which 12 are free — `feel` (2) × `supply` (3) × `attempt` (2):

- 5 are real experiences — 1 demoed by `case=10`, 4 itemized
- 1 is forbidden — INV-7
- the other 6 are ∅, each with its bar named in the inventory's `taken` slice table

🟡 **corrected at `i007`, and the flip is left auditable rather than silently overwritten:**

- this line previously read *"6 are real … less the two `happy · oversized` cells that cannot co-occur"*
  - which counted `sharp · oversized · cached` as a real experience
- D6's own `supply = complete` pin voids it — an overflowed run renders no verdict, so it populates no cache
- ⇒ the inventory graded it ∅ at `i006` while this file kept the old count
  - the two artifacts disagreed on whether a cell existed, which is the one defect this decomposition cannot survive

#### .the axis produced a cell no artifact had, and it compounds `case=4`

**`driver · reviewer · sharp · oversized · file · fresh · taken`** — *the `.taken` files are IN the subject, so every convergence round grows the subject toward the context gate.*

| round | subject |
|---|---|
| 1 | the yield + the experience artifacts |
| 2 | ⬆ + two `.taken` files |
| 3 | ⬆ + two more, and the yield has grown to answer them |

⇒ **convergence is a ratchet on the reviewer's own subject**, and the mechanism that pushes toward the gate is the mechanism the guard requires. it appears in no `case=N`, no ledger row, and no fulcrum.

🟡 **this is measured on this very route, not hypothesized.** `i001` ran at 79,789 tokens; `i003` at 117,470 — **+47% across two convergence rounds, on an unchanged rubric and an unchanged file list.** at that slope the gate is reachable in a long convergence, and F07's prose-only scope is what keeps the slope survivable rather than what removes it.

⇒ so the axis paid for itself twice: it gave the warm re-read a coordinate, and it named the ratchet that the flat `fresh` cell had hidden.

## .the latent axis the walk surfaced — D5 · subject

D5 was not in the first cut. it forced itself out of the `reader` row, and it is the most consequential discovery in this decomposition.

- the ten issues split cleanly on it — #407 and #411 govern a message; #415, #416, #418, #419 govern a file
- a reviewer reads files. it cannot read a live message
- ⇒ **the reviewer structurally cannot enforce two of the ten issues**

that reframes the mechanism choice:

- a reviewer is not a general enforcement surface for telepath — it is a file-only one
- ⇒ the message half has exactly two reachable mechanisms
  - a `brief` at authorship
  - a `subconscious` in flight

⇒ this is why #414 is graded in the inventory as a real coverage gap rather than a luxury.
