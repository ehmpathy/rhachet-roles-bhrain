## .notes

- **care for rows 1, 6, 9, 17, 19:** every call of a work day walks them, and a false halt has no route around it but a human who strips the hook. that is why R5 ships here.
- **row 8** is an extant over-match, kept: `cp` and `mv` are graded blunt, by the presence of a memory path, not by their destination (fulcrum F10). **tee is not graded this way**: it writes its operands and naught else, so rows 59–67 grade it by where each operand lands.
- **row 62 is the read route the nudge names.** a clone that reads a memory note and tees it into a durable brief, with the cwd known, passes silent: the guard's own advice, honored.
- **row 20 is the stall, and row 24 the invariant it breaks.** both sit in the framework that runs the hook (R1–R4). the p0 rows that cure them are linked in `root-causes.md`.
- **rows 25, 26, 46 are silent, as on `main`.** the cast still names a malformed or no-tool payload (`asToolInvocation`); the guard lets the call proceed, exit 0.
- **rows 48, 49 are R6.** a payload past the ~128KB env cap faulted the `exec` (exit 126, non-block, so the guard was off for that call); it now rides node's stdin.
- **row 54 and 57 share an act.** both end in the unplaced nudge: a write the guard cannot place, beside memory, blocks rather than guesses.

## .density

row 20 is the densest: it crosses the settings file, the role link, rhx's lookup and claude code's exit contract in one call — and it is the experience that stalled two crews. its cure is at rhachet. of the rows this pr holds, 51, 52 and 54 are the densest: the redirect the clone writes by hand every day, relative to a cwd the guard must place.
