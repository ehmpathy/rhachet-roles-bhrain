# domain.term.choice.reason: itemize

## .etymology

**itemize** — from latin *item* (likewise, also), the word that headed each line of an old
account. to itemize is to break a whole into **separately addressed lines**.

the etymology carries the exact property the practice needs: an item is not merely *counted*, it
is **given its own line**. that is the difference between a tally and an inventory — and the
reason this verb, rather than a synonym, names the act.

## .the pair

**`itemize` → `inventory`**, as **`catalogize` → `catalog`**.

the verb was declared in this repo long before its noun was: `rule.require.domain-term-itemization`
has governed `domain.terms/` since the glossary began. **what was absent was a name for what
itemization produces** — so the output archetype had no word, and the librarian had no rule for
its shape. that gap is what the `inventory` cluster fills, and it is why the two were paved
together (2026-08-13).

⇒ worth the note as a glossary lesson in itself: **a verb declared without its noun is a
half-captured concept.** the act was governed; the artifact was unnamed, so nobody could state
what a good one looked like.

## .why not the rejected synonyms

- **enumerate** — the near-miss. `enumerate` means *to count off*, and a count needs no separate
  home per entry — a comma-joined sentence enumerates perfectly well. itemize demands the
  **address**, which is the whole dereference benefit (`term=inventory`). also collides with
  `enumeration`, already forbidden as a synonym of `inventory` for the same family of reasons
- **register** — implies **registration**: an actor adds entries as they arrive. itemization walks
  a generated space instead, so its entries are not registered but *discovered* — the empty cells
  among them, which no registration process would ever produce
- **inventorize** — a back-formation from the noun, and clumsy. english already supplies the verb;
  to coin one from the noun would be to invent where a word exists
  (`def.domain-discovery`: discover, do not invent)
- **tabulate** — names an arrangement into a **table**, which is a presentation choice. an
  inventory is a filetree, and its shape is one file per cell — not rows

## .disputes

none. the verb predates this capture and no contract has drifted from it.

## .evidence

- **code + contracts**: `rule.require.domain-term-itemization` (declared in both the learner role
  and `repo=.this/role=any`) governs `domain.terms/`, whose shape is exactly one addressed file
  per term — an itemization in practice since the glossary's first entry
- **the wisher named the pair (2026-08-13)**: *"inventory pairs with itemize"* — which surfaced
  that the extant verb had no noun, and set the librarian's new rules on the right axis
- **the gap check is the proof of the pair**: `rule.require.domain-term-itemization`'s enforcement
  (*a declared dobj/dop whose terms are not itemized = blocker*) is only expressible **because**
  the space of required terms is generable from the declarations. that generability is what makes
  an inventory an inventory — applied here by a rule that predates the word for it
- **invariant:** `itemize` is a verb of THIS org's practice vocabulary — it names a declared rule
  and the act that maintains this glossary — not generic english like `list`
