# razor: hasty generalization

shaves: a claim at the wrong level (a case stated as a law) · settled by: observation (count the cases, read how they were drawn)

## .the question

> **how many cases back this "all", and how were they drawn?**

the catch words: "all", "always", "any", "every", "never". a scope word that outruns the count behind it is the defect.

## .the source

- a classical informal fallacy; alias *secundum quid*
- "an informal fallacy wherein a conclusion is drawn about all or many instances of a phenomenon on the basis of one or a few instances of that phenomenon"
- the cost of a law named too early: "duplication is far cheaper than the wrong abstraction" (Sandi Metz, 2016); the habit of three cases before a law (Roberts, via Fowler, 1999)
- read via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasty_generalization, https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction

## .fails when

- the claim is existential ("some"), or logic gives the law; then one case suffices
- a large count from a biased draw is no cure: run `razor=survivorship`
- waved at every claim, it leaves none. no generalization is proved (Hume's problem of induction); this razor grades degree

## .run by

`rule.always.test-a-claim-against-the-whole-it-speaks-for`
