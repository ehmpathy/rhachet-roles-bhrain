# S08 · there is no cap on rules or briefs

## .said

verbatim, 2026-09-04, mid-drive at `1.vision`, three messages:

> this is irrelevant, we can tune that later: | cheap enough to run on every stone that emits a yield | 🟡 **conditional** — only with a prose-only declared scope (F07). the subject side is now **measured on this route**: 12 prose files at **8.0% of context, $0.0134, ~97s**. 🔴 **but the line itself carries no threshold — see below** |

> false, we can have as many rules as we want; ### 🟡 why `≤ 5 rule files` does not contradict §8's *"six new briefs"*

> as many rules and as many briefs

## .settled

**a canon is sized by what it must teach, never by what a reviewer can afford to load.**

| what the vision had claimed | the settlement |
|---|---|
| `≤ 5 rule files, ≤ 4k tokens` as a rubric cap | ❌ **false.** as many rules as the canon needs |
| six new briefs is a tension against the boot budget | ❌ **false.** as many briefs as the canon needs |
| a wall-clock / spend / context threshold on acceptance line 4 | **irrelevant here — tune later** |

⇒ so cost is **not** a design constraint on the canon. it is an operational parameter of a downstream consumer, and it gets tuned when that consumer is built.

### the inversion this corrects

the vision had let a **consumer's budget** shape the **canon's size** — it capped the rule count at what one reviewer could load, then reconciled the cap against the rule set.

**that is backwards.** the canon is the deliverable; the reviewer is one reader of it. a reviewer that cannot load the whole canon splits into several reviewers, or narrows its rubric — **it does not shrink the canon.**
