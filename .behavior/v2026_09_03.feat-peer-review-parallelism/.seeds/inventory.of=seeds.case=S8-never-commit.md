## .settled

**a driver does not commit.** the act is removed from the driver's scope outright — not gated behind
a grant, not permitted when a stone asks, not available at the close of a route.

⇒ so **an unmerged index, a dirty tree, or an unlanded branch is not a driver's wall.** it is the
human's to close at release time, and a peer reviewer that raises one raises a point the driver must
**refute as out of scope** rather than halt on.

🔴 **it hardens `rule.forbid.commits-the-route-did-not-ask-for` by a cut of that rule's own carve-out.**
the rule sanctions two commits — one a stone asks for, one at a finished route. **this seed sanctions
neither.**

## 🔴 .what it CORRECTED, in this round

the halt it answered was mine, and it was wrong in its disposition though right in its diagnosis.

i had raised `--as blocked` with the ask `git.commit.uses allow --global`, so that i could run
`rhx git.stage.add pnpm-lock.yaml` and close peer review i007's only blocker. the argument ran:

1. the reviewer's blocker is real — the index entry is genuinely unmerged
2. the repair is a one-line stage
3. the stage is globally blocked ⇒ **not permitted** ⇒ `rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close`
   prescribes a halt with the ask

🔴 **step 3 is where it went wrong, and the flaw is the same one that rule already names.** it warns
that *"the question the wisher answered and the question this paragraph asks are different
questions"*, and that a lever's owner is not the same fact as whether the act is wanted once granted:

> ⚠️ **the check is not *"who owns this lever?"* but *"and once they hand it over, may I use it here?"***

⇒ **i asked the first question and skipped the second.** the quota is human-owned, so the halt looked
correct. the act itself was never wanted, so the ask should never have been put.

⚠️ **and the rule records this exact correction already, from 2026-09-07** — a driver halted on a
commit quota, a wisher struck the ask, and the section now reads *"to escalate for the quota alone
asks a human to pay for an act that stays forbidden once paid for."* **i booted that rule, cited it by
name in my own halt artifact, and made the mistake it was written from.**

🔴 **so a rule a driver can recite is not a rule a driver obeys** — that rule's own final line, earned
a second time.
