## .the cue that fired

the wisher, verbatim, on the row this repo inherited from #360:

> this is false; lossless compression does not increase ambiguity. infact, rambled prose increases ambiguity

## .the chain — a false claim was replaced with a second false claim

| # | the claim | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| #358 | a terseness reviewer *selects FOR* subversive prose | ❌ refuted by #360 — the de-subversion ran 155 → **163** lines |
| #360 | the real opposed gradient is *terseness → ambiguity* | ❌ false too |
| — | *ramble* produces ambiguity | true, and it is accretion at sentence grain |

**lossless compression cannot add ambiguity, by construction.** it removes the words that carried no distinction. a word that carried one is not surplus, so a cut that drops it was *lossy* — a defect of that cut, never a property of density.

⇒ so #360 corrected #358's gradient and then **asserted a replacement gradient of the same shape**. both name a cost that density does not have.

## .the gap, precisely — #360's own repair refutes #360's own diagnosis

#360's evidence, quoted from its body:

> *"…whose scope sat in **a column header two cells away**. **the repair was to move the conditional into the row** and name the hypothetical outright."*

| what the repair did | what defect that diagnoses |
|---|---|
| **moved** the conditional into the row | placement |
| restored a deleted qualifier | a lossy cut |
| added words back | density |

⇒ **the qualifier was never deleted. it was displaced.** a move is the repair for a passage that sits apart from its subject, and that is `place-a-passage-by-its-subject` — not a density tradeoff.

**so the one measurement in evidence supports a placement bound and was filed under an ambiguity bound.**

## .the shape of the fix

1. **strike** — #360's *"terseness → ambiguity | real, first-party, measured"* row, and the table it sits in
2. **replace** — with *lossless compression has no cost side. a lossy cut has one, and it is a defect of the cut*
3. **re-aim** — #351's owed bound. the question a terseness reviewer must ask of itself is not *"is this ambiguous?"* — it is:

   > **"does every compressed claim carry its own scope IN ITS OWN CELL, or does the reader fetch it from another?"**

   that is a placement check, it is checkable per cell, and `rule.require.place-a-passage-by-its-subject` already states it
4. **add** — the inverse to the reviewer's rubric: ramble is where ambiguity hides. every added word is one more place an unresolved referent sits unnoticed
5. **keep** — everything else in #360: the accretion-hides / compression-exposes model, the substitutions table, the false-positive bound, and the promotion of the self-review half. all of it survives

## 🟡 .what this changes about the reviewer count

#358 argued for an amendment over a second reviewer because *"two reviewers with opposed gradients would trade blockers forever."* #360 struck that argument once, since the rhetoric gradient does not exist.

⇒ **this strikes it a second time.** with the ambiguity gradient gone too, no opposed gradient remains at all — the terseness bound, the anti-subversion bound, and the placement bound all point the same direction. so the reviewer-count decision rests purely on rubric size and context budget, and on no claim about conflict.
