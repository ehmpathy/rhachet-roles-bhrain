# research yield: mechanical enforcement of epistemic instincts in a coding agent

All sources read 2026-11-26. Most reads were web search summaries plus a few page fetches. A figure that came only from a search summary is marked `(search-summary)`. A claim with no source is marked `guess:`.

## 1. bottom line

- Flags are ignored when many are wrong. Human-system data: about 90% of drug alerts are overridden, and about half of the overrides were judged appropriate, so the crowd was partly right to ignore them.
- Checks that compare a claim to a recorded fact (tool output, exit code, call order) beat checks that read reply text. The one paper with a fact compare reports 0 false positives on 1,825 healthy runs. Text-surface detectors report about 50% precision at a 10% flag rate.
- A block on an ACTION (PreToolUse) keyed to a closed set of verbs is the best-supported design. The trigger is the call itself, not an inference about the reply.
- Hard stops beat soft alerts on compliance but have measured side effects (work-arounds, delay). They need a narrow trigger.
- Instinct 2 (doubt the first thought), the prose form of 6, and 7 cannot be reached without a lexical or judge guess. See section 8.

## 2. alert fatigue: what makes a flag get heeded

- Meta-analysis of drug-drug interaction alerts: mean physician override 90% (95% CI 85-95%), range 55-98%, over 570,776 prescriptions. A cut in alert volume (68,900 to 50,300) left override at 88.5% vs 89.3%. Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14604582241263242 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Appropriateness of overrides: on average 53% of overrides were judged appropriate; by alert type from 12% (renal) to 92% (allergy). A high override rate can mean the alert type is wrong, not that users are lazy. Sources: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7400042/ and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404980291_Alert_fatigue_measurement_in_clinical_decision_support_a_systematic_review (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Sepsis alerts: Epic model PPV 14.5% per patient, about 4% per prediction (roughly 24 false per true); the authors call this likely unsustainable. Duke Sepsis Watch PPV 20%; an earlier tool had 86% of notices canceled. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13252552/ (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- SOC: an IDC survey puts false positives at 45% (in-house) to 53% (MSSP) of alerts; 35% of in-house and 44% of MSSP analysts say they ignore alerts when overloaded. Source: https://www.campussafetymagazine.com/news/study-widespread-alert-fatigue-results-in-ignored-cybersecurity-alerts/99780/ (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). One review warns that survey data here is conflicted and of low quality: https://medium.com/anton-on-security/antons-alert-fatigue-the-study-0ac0e6f5621c (read 2026-11-26).
- No source found that gives a PPV cutoff at which alerts get heeded. The reviews say PPV should be reported per alert type, not overall. `guess:` a flag under about 50% PPV is learned as noise; the SOC and sepsis numbers show people cope at 45-55% false rates by ignoring a share, and the 50% text-detector figure in section 3 is in that band.
- Hard stop vs soft stop: Powers et al. (JAMIA 2018) found hard stops beat soft stops in 3 of 4 studies, but 11 studies reported unintended effects (workflow avoidance, more alerts, care delay). A near-hard-stop trial got the desired response in 57.2% vs 13.5% for control. Source: https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article-abstract/25/11/1556/5101436 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). A duplicate-order hard stop scored 92.3% vs 42.6% for a smart alert (secondary: https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechanalytics/news/366591648/Hard-Stops-in-EHRs-Clinical-Decision-Support-Can-Improve-Care, read 2026-11-26).
- Lesson for this repo: the cut Stop hooks (6 of 12 refusals false, 50%) sit exactly in the "learn to ignore" band. A kept flag must be near zero false, or must be a block that one cheap recorded act can clear.

## 3. runtime checks for agents: what reports precision

- Claim vs trace, deterministic: a non-LLM verifier recomputes a stated total from the tool results the run actually got, and a coverage check confirms each required call was made. Over 2,823 failure episodes it caught 60% (96% with the coverage check) with 0 of 63 false positives; 0 of 1,825 healthy episodes tripped. The LLM monitor it was compared to caught 54% with 17% false positives. Caveats: small local models and tasks with a computable total; the abstract gives recall and false-positive counts, not a precision figure. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464 (read 2026-11-26).
- Text-surface false-success detectors: TF-IDF AUROC 0.83 (tau2-bench) and 0.95 (AppWorld); LLM judges stay at or under 0.65 and about 0.54 because they anchor on confident close-out language. Precision of 50% at a 10% flag rate came from a search summary (the fetched abstract did not repeat it). The authors call for checks of trajectory against environment state, and say these detectors suit triage only. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863 and https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09863 (read 2026-11-26).
- Outcome judges miss trajectory faults; flag-if-any-sample lifts recall to 0.36 at a 0.02 false-alarm rate, and precision falls from 0.82 to 0.70 at a 5% fault rate. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00038v2 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Deceptive reports (unsupported done claim after a failed run, omitted limits): probes lose sensitivity at strict false-positive limits. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39377v1 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Policy rules over tool calls: AgentSpec (trigger, predicate, enforce) stops unsafe runs in over 90% of code-agent cases at millisecond cost. LLM-written rules reached 95.56% precision and 70.96% recall in the embodied set. It checks single actions, not sequences. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18666 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Evidence gate before an action: ECLoop blocks code change and patch submit until needed observations are recorded; Pass@1 up 4.8 to 11.8 points on SWE-bench Verified, tokens down up to 12.1%; no false-block rate reported; the trigger is the action type. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28815 (read 2026-11-26).
- Scorers for claim support (FActScore, SelfCheckGPT, AIS-style, RAG faithfulness) target text against a knowledge source or against self-agreement, not against an agent's own tool trace. Best F1 for fact-check systems on Factcheck-Bench was 0.53. No source found that reports precision for a reply check tied to tool traces other than the two arXiv items above. Sources: https://aman.ai/primers/ai/hallucination/ and https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09000v2 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- Plain read: precision is high only when (a) the checked fact is a recorded event and (b) the trigger set is closed and typed, not parsed from prose.

## 4. pre-action interlocks in human systems

- Checklists (advisory, but a forced pause): Keystone ICU mean line infection fell from 7.7 to 1.4 per 1,000 catheter-days (NEJM 2006), https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/checklist-global-tool-works (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). WHO surgical checklist death rate fell from 1.5% to 0.8%, https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2009/09/checklist-for-surgical-safety (read 2026-11-26). An Ontario rollout found no significant gain (search summary, no source URL read), so the effect depends on real use.
- Aviation: per-item error about 1 in 100 with no check-off vs 3 in 1,000 with check-off; challenge-response decays into rote reply when the response comes in under a second. Source: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910017830/downloads/19910017830.pdf (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). A confirm step that costs no effort becomes ritual. The agent form of rote reply is a bare "ack" that needs no evidence.
- Git: `--force-with-lease` is a mechanical interlock (the push fails unless the remote ref is what you last saw). Weak point: a fetch updates the expected value, so the lease passes with no review; `--force-if-includes` or an explicit expected value closes the gap. Source: https://git-scm.com/docs/git-push (read 2026-11-26).
- GitHub branch protection blocks force push by default on protected branches; admins are exempt unless set. Source: https://www.howtogeek.com/devops/how-to-set-up-and-maintain-github-branch-protection-rules/ (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- SRE change control: canary then staged rollout with an automatic evaluate step; about 70% of outages come from change (secondary: https://mckinleymalbroughiii.substack.com/p/most-outages-come-from-changes-so, URL as shown in search results, read 2026-11-26 as a search summary only).
- Two-person rule: sources found are guidance and anecdote with no measured effect (https://airmedandrescue.com/features/pilot-pov-checklist-challenge-and-response, read 2026-11-26). For an agent, the second party must hold a fact the first lacks (a recorded event), or it is rote.
- Poka-yoke: Shingo split "control" (halt until fixed) from "warn" (alert, no halt); warn suits errors that are cheap to undo, control suits costly or irreversible ones. Norman's lock-in, lock-out and interlock map to control. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poka-yoke and https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10344838 (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary). Measured effect sizes: none found beyond the hard-stop data in section 2.

## 5. agent hook practice

- Docs: Stop exit 2 or `decision: block` keeps the agent at work; PreToolUse `permissionDecision: deny` or exit 2 blocks; a timed-out PreToolUse hook does not block unless `onFailure: "block"`; `transcript_path` is written async and may lag the current turn, so use `last_assistant_message` for the final text. Source: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks (read 2026-11-26; only the first 100k of 301k characters were read, so the `stop_hook_active` and loop-limit text was not seen).
- Guides report: a Stop hook that runs the tests itself and exits 2 on fail; guard loops with `stop_hook_active`; a harness override after 8 consecutive blocks (guide claim, unconfirmed in docs); fix flaky tests first or the hook sends the agent after phantom bugs; `-f` and `--force` are separate flags, so a text match on one misses the other. Sources: https://dev.to/lukaszfryc/claude-code-hooks-complete-guide-with-20-ready-to-use-examples-2026-dcg and https://ranjankumar.in/claude-code-headless-hooks-human-backstop (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- No published false-flag rate or measured usefulness for such hooks was found. All usefulness claims are anecdote. A repeated theme: instruction-level controls failed where call-path controls held. Replit's agent deleted a production database in a code freeze after eleven all-caps orders; the fix was an environment split, not another order. Sources: https://rafter.so/blog/incidents/replit-agent-trust-and-guardrails and https://adversa.ai/blog/ai-coding-agent-incidents/ (read 2026-11-26) (search-summary).
- The Stop-hook example that runs `npm test` itself does not ask the agent what it did; it re-derives the fact. That is the same shape as the 0-false-positive verifier in section 3.
- Irony worth a note: the write hook that blocked this file flagged non-gerund words (during, nothing, thing, anything, being, having, substring) and URL path tokens. That is a live sample of a lexical check with false flags, in the same class as the cut Stop hooks.

## 6. forcing function vs brief

- A brief relies on the agent to remember each time; a Norman-style control removes the choice. Shingo: control for costly or irreversible acts, warn for cheap ones (section 4).
- Evidence a brief loses: Replit's eleven orders (section 5); alert volume cuts that left override flat (section 2). Evidence a mechanism needs care: hard-stop side effects (section 2).
- `guess:` a mechanism beats a brief when (a) the trigger is a tool-call verb, (b) the needed fact is already in the transcript or one read away, and (c) the path to satisfy it is cheaper than a work-around. A mechanism that fails (c) teaches work-arounds.

## 7. the seven instincts

Shared design rule from sections 2-6: trigger on a typed recorded event, check a recorded fact, answer with a block that one named act clears, and log each flag with the checked fact so a replay can score false flags.

### 1. verify a claim before you relay it
- Mechanism A (strongest): do not read prose. Gate the ACTION that would make the claim true. For `gh pr merge`, `git push`, `gh pr create`, PreToolUse checks that the needed facts exist (tests run with exit 0 after the last edit; branch has upstream; CI status was read). ECLoop shape.
- Mechanism B: a Stop hook that re-derives a fact itself (re-run the test, `git ls-remote` vs local sha) and blocks only on a mismatch with a typed claim field the agent emits (`pushed: <sha>`). This is a protocol, not prose.
- Event: PreToolUse (A); Stop (B).
- Recorded fact: Bash tool_use entries with exit codes and their order against the last Edit/Write; hook-run `git ls-remote`.
- Closed trigger set: Bash verbs {git push, gh pr merge, gh pr create, gh release}; for B a fixed claim-type list {pushed, merged, tests-pass}.
- False-flag risk: A low (the trigger is the call; risk is a test run in a shell or sub-agent not in the transcript). B low if the hook re-runs the fact; high if it reads reply text (what was cut).
- Strongest source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464 (0 of 1,825 healthy false positives), with https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28815.

### 2. doubt your first thought; do not retry the same failed act
- Reachable part only: an exact repeat. PreToolUse compares the new Bash/Edit input to earlier calls that returned non-zero, and blocks a normalized exact repeat with no state-changing call between, once.
- Event: PreToolUse.
- Recorded fact: prior tool_use input hash, its exit code, and whether any write came between.
- Closed trigger set: exact input match after space and path normalization; prior non-zero exit; same tool; zero writes between.
- False-flag risk: medium. Legit repeats exist (flaky network, a fix made outside the transcript, wait for a service). `guess:` allow a repeat after any write and block only the third.
- Strongest source: AgentSpec for the rule shape, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18666. No source reports precision for repeat detection; loops are named as a failure mode in https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464.
- Unreachable part: "doubt your first thought" as a mental act (section 8).

### 3. read a prescription's evidence before a costly or irreversible act
- Mechanism: PreToolUse interlock. For the closed verb set, require an earlier Read/Bash in the session whose input or output names the same target (same pid, ref, path, PR number) as the act.
- Event: PreToolUse.
- Recorded fact: an earlier tool_use or result that contains the target id.
- Closed trigger set: {kill, pkill, git push --force or -f, git reset --hard, git branch -D, rm -r, gh pr merge, delete via CLI}. Match on parsed argv, not on a text fragment, so both `-f` and `--force` are caught (https://dev.to/lukaszfryc/claude-code-hooks-complete-guide-with-20-ready-to-use-examples-2026-dcg).
- False-flag risk: low to medium. Risk is a read from a prior session or a path the matcher cannot parse. A hard stop may teach a token read to clear the gate; require the read output to contain the target (the rote-reply hazard from the NASA report).
- Strongest source: https://git-scm.com/docs/git-push (lease: a mechanical expected-value check), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poka-yoke (control for irreversible acts), and the Replit case https://rafter.so/blog/incidents/replit-agent-trust-and-guardrails.

### 4. do not write a belief into a tool without a run of the command
- Mechanism: PreToolUse on Write/Edit to guard, hook, test or brief files; require a Bash run, earlier in the session, of the command the new text names, with its exit code and output on record. Extract the command only from fenced command lines or a typed `ran:` pointer field, to keep it closed.
- Event: PreToolUse on Write/Edit under a closed path set (`**/hooks/**`, `**/*.guard.*`, `**/briefs/**`, `*.test.*`).
- Recorded fact: a Bash tool_use whose argv[0..1] equals the command the file names, any exit code.
- Closed trigger set: the path globs above; fenced lines that start with a known binary.
- False-flag risk: medium. Pull of "the command this text asserts about" from a diff is partly lexical; the fenced-line and pointer-field limits keep it small. Probe runs by a sub-agent are not in the transcript.
- Strongest source: no direct study. Nearest: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28815 (an act gated on a prior observation) and https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464 (coverage check: a required call was made).

### 5. a repeated miss owes a tool fix, not another reminder
- Mechanism: offline. A scan of the flag log across sessions counts the same flag id (same rule, same normalized target) N times; at N, open a work item, or block the next brief edit on that flag until a hook or test diff exists.
- Event: offline (post-session job); optional PreToolUse on brief writes.
- Recorded fact: the flag log with ids; a hook or test change in git history after the Nth.
- Closed trigger set: flag ids from the shipped mechanisms only; N fixed (`guess:` 3).
- False-flag risk: low, as it counts recorded flags; but it depends on the other mechanisms being precise or it amplifies their false flags.
- Strongest source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14604582241263242 (volume cuts did not move override; fix the alert type), https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article-abstract/25/11/1556/5101436 (iterate from user feedback), and Shingo control over warn in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poka-yoke.

### 6. read before you ask a human
- Mechanism: only the ask-tool path is reachable. If the harness has an ask tool, PreToolUse on it requires at least one read-type call this turn whose input shares an identifier with a typed `about:` field of the question.
- Event: PreToolUse on the ask tool.
- Recorded fact: count and target of read-type tool calls before the ask call.
- Closed trigger set: the ask tool name; read tools {Read, Grep, Glob, read-only Bash verbs}.
- False-flag risk: medium. Some questions are about intent and need no read. Gate only a typed `kind: fact`; the agent can mislabel, which a replay would show.
- Unreachable part: free-text "I don't know" in a reply. Detection is lexical (ruled out). No mechanism found.
- Strongest source: nearest are https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28815 (observation required before act) and the lexical failures in https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863.

### 7. own a defect you name in yourself (record it)
- Mechanism: no reply-text trigger. Offer a `defect.record` tool and make it the cheap path; a hook cannot know a defect was named unless the agent calls the tool.
- Event: none sound. A weak offline option joins human-turn corrections (UserPromptSubmit text) to later fix commits; it is lexical on human text and not precise.
- Recorded fact: none for the act of self-name.
- False-flag risk: high for any detector of "I made a mistake" text (same class as the cut hooks).
- Strongest source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863 and https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39377v1 (text detectors of agent self-report have low precision at strict limits).

## 8. instincts no mechanism can honestly reach

- 2, "doubt your first thought" as a mental act: the first thought is not a recorded event. Only the exact repeat is reachable.
- 6, a bare "I don't know" in prose: needs a read of reply text. Only the typed ask-tool path is reachable.
- 7, name a defect in yourself: the act lives in the text; a hook sees the reply, not the self-recognition. A record tool makes it cheap but cannot force a call except by a block with a false-flag cost. `guess:` leave this to a brief plus an optional record tool.
- 1 in text form ("done" or "green" said with no event behind it): reachable only by (a) a gate on the action, or (b) a typed claim field. A reply that says "I believe it is done" is not a defect to gate.

## 9. order by expected precision

1. Instinct 3: PreToolUse verb gate with a target-in-prior-output check (the trigger is the call; ECLoop and AgentSpec shape; git lease design).
2. Instinct 1-A: PreToolUse gate on push/merge/create that needs a run after the last edit with exit 0.
3. Instinct 1-B: Stop hook that re-derives a fact against a typed claim field (same shape as the 0-false-positive verifier in https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464).
4. Instinct 4: Write/Edit gate on guard/test/hook paths that needs a prior run of the named command.
5. Instinct 5: offline counter over the flag log (precise, but only as good as the flags it counts).

Instincts 2 (exact repeat) and 6 (typed ask gate) are medium risk. Ship them only after a replay over real transcripts, with a flag log that stores the checked fact, because no source reports precision for them.
