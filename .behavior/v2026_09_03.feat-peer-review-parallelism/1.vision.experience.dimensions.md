# 1.vision.experience.dimensions

the axes this behavior's experience varies over, and the product walked.

⇒ the inventory that verdicts every cell is `1.vision.experience.case=_.md`.

## .the axes

### A. actor — who encounters it (3)

| value | who |
|---|---|
| `guard-author` | writes the `.guard` — declares which reviewers sit at which level, and how many may run at once |
| `driver` | runs `--as arrived` — waits on the level, consumes its verdicts |
| `onlooker` | reads the terminal while the level runs — a human foreman, or the driver reading stdout |

⚠️ `onlooker` is a **separate actor from `driver` even when it is the same process**, because the
two want different things at the same moment: the driver wants the verdict array, the onlooker
wants to know what is happening *right now*. that split is what makes the renderer its own
experience rather than a detail of the run.

### B. level shape — how many reviewers, under what bound (6)

| value | shape |
|---|---|
| 🔴 `phantom` | **zero** reviewers — a bound declared at a level nobody sits at |
| `single` | one reviewer at the level; a bound is moot |
| `multi-unbounded` | N reviewers, no cap — all may fly at once |
| `multi-capped` | N reviewers, cap = K where `1 < K < N` |
| `multi-cap-1` | N reviewers, cap = 1 — genuinely serialized |
| 🔴 `multi-mixed` | N reviewers at one level, where at least one belongs to a bounded concurrency group and at least one belongs to no group — the bounded sub-set is governed; the ungrouped lane is not |

🔴 **`multi-mixed` was added after peer review r5 blocker.1**, which found the shape demoed by `[t5]` of the journey test and named in the F13 advisory carried no axis value of its own — a level that mixes a bounded concurrency group with an ungrouped lane is not `multi-capped` (the group's bound governs only the group) and not `multi-unbounded` (a bound does exist, for a subset). its own value is what makes the advisory findable on B's axis and what closes the gap the `[t5]` demo opened.

🔴 **`phantom` was added after peer review i004/r2**, which found the state mandated by case 3
`[case3]`, repeated in the yield's edge-case table, and **verdicted in no cell**: an author pastes
`4: { concurrency: 1 }` when no reviewer declares level 4.

it belongs on **B and not on E**, and the reviewer's own analysis is why neither axis held it:

| axis | why it could not absorb the state |
|---|---|
| **E** | the declaration is perfectly legible — it is `consistent`. its defect is its *target*, not its form |
| **B** | every value assumed ≥ 1 member, so a zero-member level had no shape |

⇒ B measures *how many reviewers*, and **zero is a value on that axis.** the omission was an implicit
floor of one, never a decision.

⚠️ **and it is the second omitted axis value this vision shipped past four self-reviews** — E's
`unrecognized` was the first (i002/r1). both were **attemptable, demoed, and refused at parse**, and
both were invisible to a census that only counts the cells it was handed.

🔴 **the pair of them names the pattern: I bounded each axis by the cases I had already written.**
axis E listed the declaration states c7 demonstrated; axis B listed the level shapes c1–c6 used. an
axis derived from its demos can never surface a cell those demos missed — which inverts what the
walk is for.

### C. run state — what the level's members are, at run time (3)

| value | state |
|---|---|
| `all-fresh` | every member must actually execute |
| `mixed-shortcircuit` | some members are cached-approved or exhausted — they must NOT consume a slot |
| `member-fails` | one member returns malfunction or constraint while its siblings are still in flight |

### D. feel — happy or sharp (2)

`happy` · `sharp`

### E. declaration state — the author's axis (4)

| value | state |
|---|---|
| `absent` | no concurrency declared — the default applies |
| `consistent` | declared, and unambiguous |
| `conflicted` | two declarations for one level disagree |
| 🔴 `unrecognized` | declared, but at a key the parser does not read |

🔴 **`unrecognized` was added after a peer review (i002/r1) found the axis omitted it.** it is a
**fourth state, not a shade of the other three**, and the walk was un-walked without it:

| it is not… | because… |
|---|---|
| `absent` | the author **did** write a bound. they believe the level is capped |
| `consistent` | it is not legible to the parser, so it governs no level |
| `conflicted` | there is no second value to disagree with |

⚠️ **the axis had to carry it because it owns the most consequential behavior in the whole walk.**
`parseStoneGuard.ts:47-53` builds the guard from four picked keys out of a hand-rolled
`parseSimpleYaml` and rejects **no** unknown key — so an unrecognized bound **parses clean and the
cap is silently absent.** the level then fans out uncapped while its author believes it capped, at
precisely the level a cap was written to protect.

⇒ it is verdicted **forbidden** — refused at parse, per case 7 `[case4]` — and it is the
disqualifier carried on fulcrum F1: whichever declaration site wins must be **actively validated**,
because a safety valve that fails open is worse than no valve at all.

#### 🔴 E models the `.guard` only — `RHACHET_LEVEL_CONCURRENCY` is an OPERATOR dial, declared out

⚠️ **raised as a nitpick in three peer rounds.** `driver.route.peer-concurrency-env.acceptance.test.ts`
pours a twelve-member level under cap 2 with no `reviews.levels` in the guard, so a bound is reached
by a route E's four values do not name.

**it is declared out of the walked space, and the partition is the one A already draws:**

- E's subject is the **guard-author's** written affordances — every value is a state of a `.guard`
  file, and `unrecognized` is the value that makes that subject explicit
- the env var is set by an **operator** at the shell, per invocation
  - they hold no `.guard`, so they cannot reach `consistent`, `conflicted`, or `unrecognized`
  - the guard-author cannot reach the dial, so it is no value on the author's axis
- ⇒ an operator is a **fourth actor**, and A walks three. to seat the dial honestly is to widen A,
  which is the move `.the gaps` records rather than performs

🔴 **and it adds no cell the walk does not already carry.** the dial's whole effect is to choose the
effective bound, which is B's bound-half — so an override-derived cap of 2 over twelve members
lands at `multi-capped`, the same coordinate a written `concurrency: 2` lands at, with the same
pour. what differs is **who** set it, which is A's question rather than B's or E's.

⇒ so its coverage is an **acceptance oracle rather than a cell**: the env suite is the contract,
and `asGuardPositiveInt` refuses a malformed dial at the same bar a malformed `.guard` key meets.
⚠️ if a second operator dial ever appears, that is the signal A owes its fourth actor.

## 🔴 .the hidden dimension the grid surfaced

axis **C** (run state) and axis **E** (declaration state) were one axis in the first draft. the
walk broke them apart, because a whole block of cells collapsed:

> **`guard-author × {all-fresh | mixed-shortcircuit | member-fails}` is empty.** the author is not
> present at run time. the author walks **E** — `absent / consistent / conflicted / unrecognized` —
> and never walks **C** at all.

⚠️ 🔴 **the claim is the AXIS split, never a match of cardinals.** an earlier phrasing contrasted
*"their three states"* against *"the runtime three"*, which implied the two axes are the same size.
they are not: E has held **four** values since peer review i002/r1 added `unrecognized` (itself
forbidden at parse, per c7 `[case4]`).

⇒ **a cardinal stated beside a claim it does not support is the staleness class this drive spent 24
rounds eliminating** — the count moved and the sentence did not. the durable form names *which
axis*, and lets the inventory carry *how many*.

⇒ and the split immediately yields an **invariant** the free-list would not have produced:

> **the driver never meets a conflicted declaration — it is barred by the author's `forbidden`
> cell.** a conflicted declaration must be refused where it is *written*, never where it is *run*.
> otherwise the driver inherits an ambiguity the author created, and inherits it mid-flight, with
> reviewer subprocesses already spawned.

that is the whole payoff of the walk: the rejection has to land at parse time, and no line of the
wish said so.

## ⚠️ .this claim read *"`driver × conflicted` is `impossible` by nurture"* until peer review i008/r1

🔴 **and that phrase fused the two verdicts the whole grid is parted by.** the reviewer is right that
it *"muddies exactly the checkable distinction the rule grades for"*:

| verdict | why the cell cannot occur | what an implementer owes it |
|---|---|---|
| **forbidden** | **nurture** — it is attemptable, and the behavior must reject it | 🔴 **a guard** |
| **impossible** | **nature** — no attempt can be made at all | **naught** |

⇒ *"impossible by nurture"* is a contradiction in one phrase: it names the nature verdict and the
nurture cause. a reader who took the verdict at face value would conclude **no guard is owed** —
which is the exact opposite of what this section argues.

⚠️ **and a second error sat inside it, which the reviewer also caught: `driver × conflicted` is not a
cell.** the driver's walk carries no E axis, so there is no coordinate for it to hold any verdict at
all. **the invariant is a statement about the AUTHOR's grid**, read from the driver's side — that
`conflicted` is forbidden at parse is precisely why the driver's walk needs no E.

🔴 **so the honest sentence names the cause, never a verdict**: the driver never meets it, **because
the author's cell is forbidden.** the two are one decision, and the guard is owed at exactly one
place — the parser. that is what c7's *"the two verdicts are one decision"* already says, and this
section had said it with the wrong word for six rounds.

## .orthogonality — and the correlation this vision confessed to and did not have

A, B, D are mutually independent. **C and D are independent too** — an earlier draft conceded a
correlation between them that does not survive the rule's own test.

🔴 **E and B are NOT independent, and the claim that they were survived two peer rounds inside the
same sentence that repaired C/D.** peer review i003/r1 caught it, from the walk's own footnotes. it
is answered in its own section below.

⚠️ **the sequence is the lesson, and it is worth more than either result.** i002/r1 asked me to apply
the orthogonality test to C/D. I applied it, found the confession false, rewrote the section — **and
re-asserted `E ⊥ B` in the same breath, untested.** a rubric question answered for the pair it was
asked about, and not for its neighbour.

### the rule's test, applied

> *"does any value on one axis imply a value on another? if so, they are one axis, or a latent
> dimension hides."* — `rule.require.dimensional-decomposition`

**the test is implication, not frequency.** and every value of C spans both values of D:

| C value | × happy | × sharp |
|---|---|---|
| `all-fresh` | the level clears at its slowest member | a lone lane times out; resource pressure under fan-out |
| `mixed-shortcircuit` | the cached members settle at once, the rest pour | every member short-circuits, so the pool is never entered |
| `member-fails` | 🔴 see below — **both, always, at once** | 🔴 same event |

⇒ no C value implies a D value, so **they are not one axis.** what the earlier draft called a
correlation was a *frequency* skew — `member-fails` lands sharp more often — and frequency is what
axis D's `care` companion measures, not what orthogonality asks about.

### 🔴 the latent dimension the rule warns of — named, and shown to add no cell

the rule's second branch is the sharper one, and there **is** a latent variable at
`member-fails`: the **unlock / passage** split. a malfunction is terminal-for-unlock
(`isReviewPeerVerdictTerminal.ts:18-21`) and it is **not** terminal-for-passage.

⚠️ so one malfunction produces a happy consequence and a sharp one **simultaneously**, for the same
driver, from the same event:

| the consequence | the feel |
|---|---|
| the level clears; the next level opens; the ladder advances | **happy** — "it carried on" |
| the stone still cannot pass, and a reviewer needs diagnosis | **sharp** |

⇒ **that is the opposite of a collapse.** an axis pair collapses when one value forecloses another;
here `member-fails` **forecloses neither** — it spans D completely, so both cells are occupied by
construction rather than by luck.

⚠️ and the latent variable does not earn an axis of its own, because it is **not free**: every
malfunction carries both consequences, always. an axis whose value is a constant function of another
axis's value doubles the grid and adds no distinct cell — which is the over-decomposition
`howto.experience.decompose` warns against, arrived at from the opposite side.

🔴 **the confession was the defect, not the correlation.** to concede *"C and D correlate"* and then
argue for an exception reads as candor and is worse than either honest answer: it invites a reviewer
to grade a non-orthogonal decomposition, and it left the `member-fails × happy` cell defended by
assertion rather than by the mechanism that actually guarantees it.

## 🔴 .E and B — a real dependency, kept on purpose

added after peer review i003/r1, which found the independence claim contradicted **by this walk's
own footnotes**. the reviewer is right, and the evidence is already on the page:

| the footnote | what it establishes |
|---|---|
| ¹ `absent × {multi-capped, multi-cap-1}` is impossible — *"a cap never written cannot bound a thing"* | B ∈ {multi-capped, multi-cap-1} ⟹ E = `consistent` |
| ⁶ `unrecognized` and `absent` produce the identical runtime shape | the same implication, reached by a second route — **4 cells** |
| 🔴 ⁷ `{absent, unrecognized} × phantom` is impossible — a phantom level exists only where a declaration names it | 🔴 B = `phantom` ⟹ E ∈ {`consistent`, `conflicted`} — **4 cells** |

⇒ **that is an implication, so by the rule's own test E and B are not independent.** the **12**
`impossible` cells those footnotes mark **are the dependency, itemized** — which is why the walk
never hid it and the orthogonality sentence contradicted it. (row ¹ carries the other **4**.)

🔴 **the third row was added in peer review i007/r1, and it does more than move the count from 8 to
12 — it widens the dependency's SHAPE.** rows ¹ and ⁶ run E ⟹ B's *bound*. row ⁷ runs E ⟹ B's
**member count**: a level with zero reviewers is no fact about the roster at all, since a roster that
names no such level produces no such level. **the declaration is what conjures it.**

⚠️ **so the table below's claim that "the member count is free" holds for five of B's six values and
fails for `phantom`** — stated in place rather than smoothed over, because it is the exact claim the
reviewer checked and the one an implementer would lean on.

### the structure, named precisely

**B conflates two things, and only one of them is free:**

| B's half | free? | determined by |
|---|---|---|
| the **member count** — `single` vs `multi` | ✅ yes, **for five of six B values** | the guard's roster |
| 🔴 the **member count at `phantom`** — zero | ❌ **no** | 🔴 E — a declaration conjures the level |
| the **effective bound** — unbounded / capped-K / cap-1 | ❌ **no** | 🔴 E, totally |

`absent → unbounded` · `consistent → as declared` · `conflicted → refused` ·
`unrecognized → unbounded`. so B's bound-half is **E's runtime projection**, and the dependency is
one-directional and total.

### 🔴 why they are kept apart rather than collapsed

the rule offers two exits — *"they are one axis, or a latent dimension hides."* the latent dimension
is named above. the axes stay separate for a reason the actor split forces:

> **the driver and the onlooker never see E at all.** they encounter the level's *effective shape*
> and have no access to how it was declared. only the guard-author sees both.

⇒ to collapse E into B would delete the ability to name a cap in the driver's and onlooker's walks
**without reference to how it was written** — **72 of the 120 live cells** (2 actors × 6 B × 3 C × 2 D).
and to collapse B into E would delete the member count, which is free for five of B's six values.

⚠️ **so the honest statement is not *"they are orthogonal"* and not *"they should be one axis."* it
is: B and E overlap on one half **plus the `phantom` value of the other half**, the overlap is total
and one-directional, and the walk pays for it in **12** `impossible` cells** — which is the smallest
price the actor split permits.

🔴 **both figures in this section were stale until peer review i007/r1, and by the same mechanism.**
`48 of the 80` was driver+onlooker at B=4, and `exactly 8` was footnotes ¹ and ⁶ before footnote ⁷
existed. **the argument was correct in both cases and its two checkable quantities were not** — which
is precisely the failure this section exists to make checkable.

⚠️ **so both now carry their derivation** (`2 × 6 × 3 × 2`, and `4 + 4 + 4` across three named
footnotes). a cardinal alone cannot survive an axis that widens; a product recomputes.

🔴 **and this is a stricter claim than the earlier draft made, not a looser one.** *"E is independent
of B"* was unfalsifiable prose; the statement above is checkable against the grid, and its cost is
countable.

## .the product

`3 × 6 × 3 × 2 × 4` = **432 tuples**, of which **312 are `impossible` by the actor split** above
(**an author has no run state, and a driver/onlooker has no declaration state**). the inventory
walks the **live** region — `guard-author × B × E × D` (48) plus `{driver, onlooker} × B × C × D`
(72) — and verdicts all 432.

⚠️ **the split bars a whole AXIS per actor, never one value on it.** a driver does not hold
*"`consistent` rather than `conflicted`"* — the driver's walk carries **no E axis**, so there is no
coordinate for it to hold any declaration verdict at all. the same holds mirrored for the author and
C. ⇒ a reason worded as *"a driver has no **conflicted** declaration"* names one E value and thereby
implies the other three are open to it; they are not, and that reading would put 260 cells back in
play.

⚠️ **`impossible` is a verdict, not a bucket** — the four are demoed / itemized / forbidden /
impossible, and the 260 are the fourth. **this paragraph said `void` until peer review i016/r1**,
which is a fifth label the grading rule does not carry; the inventory's census table now names the
verdict and splits the 291 into the 31 walked per cell and the 260 barred wholesale. ⇒ **the
distinction is auditability, never arithmetic** — `120 + 312 = 432` held either way, but a reader who
applies the four-verdict census could confirm it for the 120 alone.

🔴 **the product moved twice under peer review, and both moves were the same defect on two axes:**

| round | axis | the added value | live cells gained |
|---|---|---|---|
| i002/r1 | **E** | `unrecognized` | 8 — 4 forbidden, 4 impossible |
| i004/r2 | **B** | `phantom` | 20 — 4 forbidden, 16 impossible |
| r5 blocker.1 | **B** | `multi-mixed` | 24 — 2 demoed, 22 impossible |

`3×4×3×2×3` = 216 → `3×4×3×2×4` = 288 → `3×5×3×2×4` = 360 → `3×6×3×2×4` = **432**.

⇒ neither move re-scoped the walk. **both times the space itself was short a value**, which is
exactly what `rule.require.dimensional-decomposition` predicts an omitted axis value costs: every
cell along it is un-verdicted, and the census cannot report the absence, because it counts only the
cells it was handed.

⚠️ **and each omission concealed a parse-time invariant** — `unrecognized` hid a silently-absent cap;
`phantom` hid a bound that governs no level at all.
