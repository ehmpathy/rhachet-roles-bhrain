# 1.vision · the review budget becomes a bound

## .the outcome, in one line

> **`rhx route.guard.budget --for review --add N` is refused by default. it is lifted only by a
> live urgent concession — a recorded harm claim on the last round — or by a human.**

🔴 **and the rule beneath it, ruled at the council on 2026-09-18:**

> **the budget IS the allowance for `better` churn. inside the meter, taste counts. past the meter,
> only `urgent` does.**

⇒ the gate does not stop the loop. **it marks where the window the route author bought comes to an
end**, and asks for a named harm past it. `$route/.seeds/…case=S03…`.

## .the design

two lifts, and they answer two different questions:

```
permitted  ⟺  ( a live urgent concession stands       ← the warrant   (F01, F06)
              ∧ the target reviewer has run dry       ← the moment    (F10, ruled)
              ∧ --stone resolved to exactly one stone ← the scope     (F12, ruled) )
           ∨    the caller is a human                 ← the human's GRANTED path (F13)
```

🔴 **the earned path gained TWO conjuncts at the council's second session**, and both narrow it:

| conjunct | what it refuses | row |
|---|---|---|
| the **moment** | a preemptive pad — a grant sought at 3/8 with rounds still in hand | `F10`, **reversed** |
| the **scope** | a `--stone` prefix that resolves to several guards, on one stone's warrant | `F12`, ratified |

⇒ the earned path is a **conjunction of three**, never the single predicate this design opened with.
each conjunct is one read of state the command already holds.

| the lift | answers | who holds it | where it is recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **ledger predicate** | *is the round earned?* | the driver, by its own concession | `passage.jsonl` — a `conceded` row with `--severity urgent` |
| 🔴 the **actor check** | *who asks?* | a human | the extant `route.mutate grant allow`, which claims *human only* and now enforces it |

**both are needed, and neither suffices.** an actor check cannot read a concession, so it alone
loses req 2. a predicate cannot detect an actor, so it alone loses req 7. ⇒ `F05` states the
argument that forces the pair.

🔴 **the human lift changed at the council, twice.** it was drafted as a NEW `route.budget.uses`
meter on the paved three-level pattern (`F02`); `F13` put an actor check on the **extant** human
lever instead; then `F02` was ruled **against** — **the meter does not ship here at all.**

⇒ **one human gate, on a command that already exists and already documents itself as human-only.**
`#458` stays open as a refinement for scope granularity, never as a prerequisite.

🟡 **the meter's case survives the verdict and is deferred, not dismissed:** the privilege flag lifts
**every** write on the route, where a budget meter would scope to budget alone. ⇒ what weakened that
case is `F13` itself — **a coarse lever a human must pull deliberately is a different object from a
coarse lever any clone may pull.**

🟡 **and `F05`'s fork shrank with no verdict of its own.** it read *"the gate inside
`routeGuardBudget`, with the meter beside it"*; the second clause is gone, so what remains open is
the **layer** question alone — command, or guard.

**where it sits:** inside `routeGuardBudget` (`src/contract/cli/route.ts:2140-2353`), **after the
stone filter at `:2264` and before the write at `:2312`** — the exact slot the extant comment at
`:2271-2276` reserves, for the same read-before-write reason it already states.

🟡 **this read *"at the head, at `:2140`"* until `r2` self-review, and the source refuses it.** the
predicate takes `{ route, stone }`; the route is looked up at `:2242-2250` and the stone's guard set
at `:2264`, so **neither input exists at the head.** the failure-order argument `F09` makes is
untouched — a malformed `--peer` is already rejected at `:2216-2224`, above the gate either way.

**what it calls:** `getStoneLiveUrgentConcessionSlugs({ route, stone })` — **already shipped**, and
it already computes the freshness-keyed predicate req 2 demands.

✅ ~~**and that call's signature is the design's sharpest open seam.**~~ — **CLOSED at the council.**
it takes **one** `stone: string`; `--stone` matches by `startsWith` (`route.ts:1948-1956`), so one
invocation may target **several** stones. ⇒ `F12` ruled the remedy: **a multi-match is refused
outright**, and the refusal names the stones it matched.

🔴 **the refusal lands at the COMMAND, above both `startsWith` sites.** `getCurrentPeerMetersForStones.ts:25`
runs the same bare match and its own comment states why — *"the meter set and the guard set agree by
construction."* ⇒ a refusal inside either transformer would break that invariant; what is refused is
the **invocation**.

🔴 **a second seam, stated here because it is invisible at the call site:** the operation returns an
array, and **the gate reads it as a BOOLEAN — `.length > 0` — never as a count.** its last line is
`[...new Set(urgentSlugs)]`, a dedupe by **reviewer**, written for a different purpose (its own
comment: *"names the human once"*).

⇒ so `.length` counts lanes rather than harm. a size cap built on it would grant **five** rounds for
one urgent concern spread across five lanes and **one** round for five concerns in a single lane —
the inverse of the harm test. ⚠️ **the boolean read is unaffected by a dedupe**, so the gate is
sound; a future count read would not be. `F10` carries the full argument.

## .before and after

### 👎 before — the window with no end

```
lane raises a nitpick → driver edits → lane raises another nitpick
   → budget dry → rhx route.guard.budget --add 4 → lane raises another nitpick → …
```

🔴 **the churn up to the meter is CORRECT — it is the allowance the route author bought.** what is
wrong is the step past it: the meter runs dry, and `--add N` re-opens the window on no more than the
driver's wish to continue.

⇒ **the budget is the floor, and the top-up removes it.** `$route/.seeds/…case=S03…`.

🟡 **this section read *"every round is metered grove spend, not one was a blocker … a lane that
grades taste has no floor"* until the council.** that framed the whole window as waste, which the
wisher struck — see § *what is awkward* §0b.

### 👎 before — and the engine hands over the command ITSELF

worse than a lever a driver may reach for: the ack the engine renders the instant a concession lands
**sequences the purchase**, between the fix and the re-arrival.

```
   └─ fix the nitpick, buy the round, then re-arrive
      ├─ rhx route.guard.budget --for review --add 2 --peer experience-coverage --stone 1.vision
      └─ rhx route.stone.set --stone 1.vision --as passed
```

🔴 **that render is verbatim from this route's own stone**, on a lane that had **already approved
with 0 blockers**, for a nitpick graded `better`. a peer driver met the same render **three times in
one halt** across seven `better` concessions — `$route/.seeds/…case=S02…`.

⇒ its branch is **meter-driven** and ignores the `severity` input it already takes. **the command
must not appear in the sequence at all where the grade did not earn the round.**

### 👍 after — the ack falls silent where the grade earned no round

```
🗿 route.stone.set --as conceded
   ├─ with  = experience-coverage (l1), 3 rounds, 3 budget — budget spent
   ├─ about = nitpick.1 — the last concern this lane owed
   │
   └─ fix the nitpick, then re-arrive
      └─ rhx route.stone.set --stone 1.vision --as passed
```

**one line removed, and it is the whole repair.** the fix and the re-arrival stay; the purchase is
gone, because a `better` grade past a spent meter earns no round.

🟡 **inside the window the ack is unchanged** — with rounds left the top-up term already elides, and
that behavior is correct.

### 👍 after — the same reviewer, round 19

**to stderr, exit 2** — a constraint the caller must fix (`rule.require.exit-code-semantics`,
`rule.forbid.stdout-on-exit-errors`, the latter already cited in this function at `:2160-2161`):

```
🦉 budget refused

🗿 route.guard.budget
   ├─ route = .behavior/v2026_09_03.example
   ├─ add = 4
   ├─ peer = mech-rules
   ├─ refused — a grant needs a live urgent concession on the last round
   │  ├─ mech-rules = 18/18 rounds spent
   │  ├─ no live urgent concession stands on 5.1.execution
   │  └─ the route author set 18, with the whole rubric in view
   │     those 18 were the allowance for taste. past them, only harm buys a round
   │
   └─ what to do — yours to run, no human needed
      ├─ converge — a .taken per open .given, then re-arrive
      │  └─ rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as absorbed --that mech-rules
      └─ or grade a concern urgent, where one ships nameable harm
         ├─ urgent = security · safety · monetary · reputation · behavioral
         ├─ better = every other concern, and it earns no round
         └─ rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as conceded \
              --with mech-rules --about nitpick.2 --severity urgent
```

🔴 **the two tips are tree ROWS, never warn lines** — and that is the wisher's call, not a style
preference. they were drafted as a `⚠️` pair suspended beneath the command; the directive struck that
shape: **a tip that dangles off a command is spam; a tip that is a branch is part of the answer.**

⇒ three consequences, and each traces to a rule already on the books:

| what changed | why |
|---|---|
| the tips became branches | `rule.require.treestruct-output` — every other fact on this surface is a row, so a suspended line reads as a different kind of claim and costs a re-parse |
| the `⚠️` glyphs are gone | `rule.prefer.chill-nature-emojis`: *"important callouts only."* **the refusal IS the warn** — a glyph stacked on it adds no signal and dilutes the one glyph that does |
| the harm set lost its *"a harm you can NAME"* preamble | `rule.require.brevity` — the branch already sits under *where one ships nameable harm*. the preamble restated its parent |

🔴 **and the `better` row carries weight rather than decoration.** `F07` ruled the `better → urgent`
re-grade **permitted**, so this one line holds the whole of the design's discouragement — see
§ *what is awkward* §1 and `F07`'s `.what moves`.

🔴 **that second branch was worded *"grade it and this grant is yours"* until `r3` self-review, and
the phrase taught `F10`'s hole as an invitation.** the header already carries `add = 4`, so a driver
read *"one urgent grade unlocks the four rounds i asked for"* — which is **true**, and is precisely
the residual `F10` records as unbounded. ⇒ the copy now says **re-run this command** and makes no
claim about the size it will permit, so it does not advertise a width the design has not settled.

🟡 **and the `better` line is added for the same reason, from the other side.** without it the block
reads as though the concession path is the normal one; `rule.always.concede-with-a-severity` says the
opposite — *"bias to `better`"* — and a refusal that nudges toward `urgent` is a refusal that funds
the loop it exists to stop.

🔴 **the human path is deliberately ABSENT from this block, and the absence is a design call.**
`rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation` measures the cost of the adjacency it would create:
*"two remedies rendered side by side with no owner column read as two human remedies."* the driver's
own lever at this cell is **convergence**, so the block is headed `yours to run` and carries only
levers the driver owns. ⇒ to print the human lift here would hand a driver an escalation for a round
the design already priced as unnecessary — the exact cost `0.wish.md` §gain.rank names.

⚠️ **the human lift is not thereby undiscoverable.** a driver that genuinely cannot converge reaches
a human by `--as blocked` (`rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close`), which is the
sanctioned escalation and carries the ask. the meter is the **human's** lever, learned from the
meter's own help — a driver never needs its name.

🟡 **this block carried a third `or ask a human` branch until `r2` self-review**, which contradicted
`case=1`'s explicit argument in the same vision. the demos are the authority on refusal copy; they
always said so, and this render now agrees with them.

🟡 **the format is derived from the source, not invented.** `route.ts:2326-2344` emits success as an
owl line, a blank, then a `🗿 route.guard.budget` tree whose rows are `route =`, `add =`, `peer =`,
`level =`, `updates`. the refusal above **keeps that skeleton** and swaps the tail — so a driver
reads one shape whether the grant lands or not.

🔴 **it deliberately departs from the function's OTHER extant convention**, and the departure is the
design call worth a reviewer's glance:

| the extant path | its format | why the refusal cannot use it |
|---|---|---|
| every error in `routeGuardBudget` (`:2145`, `:2190`, `:2302`, `:2321`, `:2348`) | bare `error: <msg>` + exit 2, **no tree at all** | req 5 demands the refusal **name the sanctioned move**, and it is a diagnosis plus two commands whose ORDER is load-carrying. a single `error:` line cannot hold an ordered remedy block legibly |
| the drive halts (`formatRouteDriveHalts`) | a `🗿` tree with a nested remedy block | ✅ **this is the precedent the refusal follows** — it is the surface in this repo that already solves *"refuse, and name what to run instead"* |

⇒ `rule.require.errors-name-the-fix` demands what / why / **fix**, and renders the fix as a tree in
its own worked example. **the bare `error:` style satisfies what and why, and cannot satisfy fix
here.** ⚠️ so this is a real inconsistency the design introduces inside one function, argued rather
than glossed — a reviewer may fairly rule that every error in `routeGuardBudget` should move to the
tree instead.

## 🔴 .the aha

> **the refusal does not stop the driver. it makes the driver say what it asks for.**

the command the driver reaches for at round 19 is *"give me more"*. the gate substitutes *"name the
harm that ships if this is not fixed, and then take your round."*

- 🔴 **a `better` grade refuses the round** — and that is the whole mechanism. the driver cannot
  reach the lift without a harm it can name, and taste has no harm
- ⇒ **the bound holds not because the lever is locked, but because the key is a sentence the driver
  must be able to write.**

that is why the gate is a **predicate over a recorded claim** rather than a permission list. a
permission list asks *who are you?*; this asks *what did you find?* — and only the second question
bounds the nitpick loop, because the nitpick loop is run by a party who would pass any identity
check.

🟡 **the honest version:** the key is a sentence the driver writes **about itself**, so the bound is
a claim on the record rather than a lock. `F07` carries that cost in full, and it is the row a
council should read hardest.

## .mental model

### how a driver would describe it to a peer

> *"you can still get more rounds. you just have to say what the round is for — and 'this could be
> tidier' is not an answer."*

### the analogy that fits

**an expense claim, not a locked safe.** the money is available; you file a reason, and the reason
is on the record with your name on it. nobody stops you at the door, and everybody can read what
you claimed.

⇒ this is the right analogy precisely **because** it is honest about `F07`: an expense claim can be
padded. what it cannot be is anonymous or unexamined.

**the analogy that does NOT fit: a credit limit.** a credit limit is enforced by a party that does
not trust you. this bound is enforced by a record you write yourself — so a reader who carries the
credit-limit model will expect a strength the design does not have.

### their words vs ours

| a driver says | the domain says |
|---|---|
| "top up the budget" | a **grant** — `route.guard.budget --add N` |
| "it's just a nitpick" | `--severity better` — no nameable shipped harm |
| "this actually matters" | `--severity urgent` — a harm in the closed set |
| "the reviewer already agreed" | a **live** concession — keyed to the lane's LATEST `.given` |
| "I'm out of rounds" | the lane is **exhausted** — terminal for unlock, and the debt survives it |

🟡 **the term that carries the most weight and reads the most casually is `live`.** a driver says
*"I conceded that"* about a concession three generations old; the engine says it has **lapsed**.
`case=7` demos exactly that gap, and `F11` records what actually moves the key — **a reviewer run,
never a driver edit.**

### 🔴 the vocab defect this vision walked into — `lane`

`term=route.guard.review.reviewer._.choice._.md:6-7` lists **`lane` as a forbidden synonym** of
`reviewer`. **these vision artifacts use `lane` throughout**, and so does the extant code comment at
`route.ts:2271` (*"the lanes a DISPUTE has quieted"*) and the snapshot key `case1b` (*"every skipped
lane"*).

**it is not a rule violation as written** — `rule.forbid.domain-term-synonyms` binds **contracts**
(dobj/dop names, signatures, published cli/api/sdk), and permits a synonym in a **comment**. route
prose is comment-tier, and the two extant uses above are comment-tier too. ⇒ **no sweep is owed**,
and a bulk rewrite is forbidden regardless: some uses are verbatim quotes of shipped text.

🔴 **but the reason everyone reaches for `lane` is a declared, open gap**, and that same term file
names it at `:19-23`:

> *"the run-sense still has no word of its own"* — `reviewer` carries **both** the role and one run
> of it, and the ambiguity repair is **an open gap**

⇒ **this design needs the run-sense specifically.** axis C's positions (`live` · `exhausted` ·
`malfunctioned` · `forgiven`) are states of **one run**, never of the role — which is precisely why
the walk reached for `lane` and why the census calls C *lane-scoped*.

| the rule | what it binds | this vision |
|---|---|---|
| `rule.forbid.domain-term-synonyms` | contracts | ✅ clean — **the refusal's rendered stdout must say `reviewer`**, and does |
| — | prose | 🟡 uses `lane`, as the code's own comments do |

🔴 **the actionable output:** the refusal copy is **published cli**, so it is contract-tier and must
use `reviewer`. and this vision is fresh evidence for the open ambiguity gap — a design that needed
the run-sense and had no word for it.

🟡 **the surface shrank at the council.** this read *"and any new `route.budget.uses` help text"*
until `F02` was ruled against — no new skill ships, so no new help text is authored. ⇒ **the
contract-tier surface this rule binds is the refusal copy alone**, plus the one-line help at
`route.ts:1845` that `F13` makes true.
