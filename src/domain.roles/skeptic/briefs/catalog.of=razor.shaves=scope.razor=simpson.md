# razor: simpson

shaves: a claim at the wrong level (a part stated as the whole) · settled by: observation (split the data by the likeliest hidden group, and compare)

## .the question

> **does this trend hold inside each subgroup, or only in the pool?**

`razor=composition-division` is the logic of a part-whole claim; this is the count that tests it.

## .the source

- Simpson, 1951; named by Colin R. Blyth, 1972; earlier notes by Pearson and Yule
- a trend "appears in several groups of data" but "disappears or reverses when the groups are combined"
- UC Berkeley graduate admission, fall 1973: the pooled gap favored men; by department, "a small but statistically significant bias in favor of women"
- read via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson%27s_paradox

## .fails when

- the split runs through the cause itself and hides the true effect. which level is true is a causal claim (`guess:` not read at the page)
- a hunt across many splits finds a reversal by chance. name the split before the look

## .run by

`rule.always.test-a-claim-against-the-whole-it-speaks-for`
