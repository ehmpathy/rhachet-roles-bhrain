# rule.always.weigh-each-claim-with-one-burden

> **hold every claim to one burden of proof, whichever way it points. a skeptic who doubts only the claims it dislikes is a dogmatist with good tools: each flaw it learns to detect makes it more wrong.**

**how it applies the basis:** trust no one includes your own preference. the claim you want true gets a light check and the one you want false gets a heavy one, unless you set one bar and hold both to it.

## .what

the order of the reads starts with your own claim (`rule.always.trust-no-one-especially-not-yourself`); the burden of each is the same.

| uneven | even |
|---|---|
| the claim you like passes on one read; the rival owes three | each owes the read that would settle it |
| a peer's claim is checked; your own goes out on recall | both are read, yours first |
| the convenient "it is fixed" is relayed; the inconvenient "it regressed" is re-run twice | each is re-run once, or each is marked `guess:` |
| a flaw is hunted in the argument you oppose | the same hunt runs on the argument you hold |

## .the test

> **would this read have moved me as far if it had pointed the other way?**

no → the burden is uneven. one bar for both.

## .enforcement

blocker: a claim you favor accepted on less proof than its rival was asked for, and acted on by a peer
nitpick: a review that hunts flaws in one side only

cites: `define.skeptic.lessons-from-the-greats` (Yudkowsky, evenness; Truzzi 1987, the pseudoskeptic).

peer: `rule.always.weigh-a-read-by-what-it-rules-out` (what a read is worth) · `rule.always.pushback-what-you-refute` (refute the strongest form)

forbid twin: `rule.forbid.uneven-burdens`
