# case=1 · the disagreement that drives on

- **coordinates** = `dispute × answered × live`
- **feel** = happy · **care** = critipath · **verdict** = demoed

**the case the whole wish exists for.** the driver and one reviewer disagree on substance. today
that costs every round left and then a human. here it costs one command and one row.

## .the narrative

*driver, on `5.1.execution`, round 2, architect lane*

the architect says my `setStoneAsDisputed` belongs under `guard/review/peer/` rather than under
`stones/`. I put it under `stones/` because every other `setStoneAs*` lives there, and a reader who
looks for it will look there first.

I wrote the `.taken` — a `[REFUTE]`, with the seven peer paths quoted. the architect read it and
**raised the same blocker again**, with a longer argument.

**today** I have three moves, and each is worse than the last:

1. answer again, re-arrive, spend round 3 — and the architect will re-raise, because neither of us
   is wrong and neither is convinced
2. `--as blocked` — but `rule.forbid.unanswered-exits-from-a-blocker` is explicit: *"a critique you
   have not answered is not a wall"*, and I **have** answered it
3. burn the budget to `exhausted` — the coast that same rule lists as forbidden

⇒ all three roads end at the human, and roads 1 and 3 charge for every round on the way.

**now there is a fourth, and it is the honest one:**

```
rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1 \
  --why .fulcrums/inventory.of=fulcrums.case=F007-file-placement.md
```

I write the argument into a fulcrum entry a council will read, and point the stance at it. the
architect lane goes quiet **for this generation**, and **the drive proceeds**. at the close, the
human reads one row and rules in ten seconds.

**the aha:** *a review budget was never the right instrument for a disagreement. it ends one by
exhaustion. a dispute ends it by judgment — and defers the judgment to the one review that would
have happened regardless.*

🔴 **this cell is the ESCALATION path, and until 2026-09-11 it never said so.** `S11` ruled that a
driver **concedes and fixes by default**, and disputes only at a named bar. so a demo headed
*"the case the whole wish exists for"* reads as the model response to any blocker — which is the
norm inverted, in the artifact a reader reaches for first.

⇒ the narrative already **meets** the bar; it merely never named it:

| the bar (`S10` · `S11`) | what this narrative shows |
|---|---|
| **frequent** — the concern recurs, so one council read settles a class | the architect **re-raised the same blocker** after a cited `[REFUTE]` |
| a fix would not converge it | *"neither of us is wrong and neither is convinced"* — a placement convention, never a defect |

⚠️ **and the concede path was weighed, which the three-moves list omits.** a fourth today's-move
exists: **concede, move the file, re-arrive** — and it is the correct move whenever the reviewer has
named a real defect. it is rejected here because there is no defect to repair: the driver holds that
`stones/` is right, so a concede would record a judgment it does not hold (`case=5`).

⇒ **a dispute is what is left when a fix cannot converge the point.** *"a fix converges; an argument
recurs"* — and this is the second kind.

## .the bdd timeline

```gherkin
given a stone `5.1.execution` with peer reviewer `architect`, budget 4, rounds 2
  and the architect's latest .given raises 1 blocker on file placement
  and the driver has written .taken.by_self.architect with a [REFUTE] + cited evidence
  and --as contemplated --that architect has already run          # B = answered

[t0] when the driver runs --as passed
     then the guard halts
      and the halt names the architect as answered and undeclared
      and it prints exactly two commands — dispute and concede    # R4; demoed at case=2

[t1] given the .taken had NOT been written
     when the driver runs --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1
     then it is refused: "answer architect before you take a stance on it"   # R1; case=3

[t2a] given the driver has NOT yet written the fulcrum entry
      when it runs --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1 --why .fulcrums/…case=F007-file-placement.md
      then it is refused: "no fulcrum entry at <path>"            # 🔴 F018 — a require, never a mint
       and no passage report lands                                # the boundary, before any state moves

[t2] given the driver has written .fulcrums/…case=F007-file-placement.md
     when the driver runs
       rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1 \
         --why .fulcrums/inventory.of=fulcrums.case=F007-file-placement.md
     then the path is checked and it exists                       # 🔴 F018, rung 1
      and a passage report lands: status = disputed, reviewer = architect, about = blocker.1
      and the report stores that --why path VERBATIM as fulcrum   # no derivation, no drift
      and the stance keys to the architect's LATEST given         # 🔴 as the debt does
      and the lane's ONLY concern is now disputed, so its tally falls to 0   # 🔴 S07

[t3] when the driver then runs --as passed
     then the architect no longer gates                          # the hold is LIFTED
      and the stone proceeds to the next level, or to the judge
      and the architect's meter still reads rounds 2, budget 4    # 🔴 acceptance #2

[t4] given the driver ALSO wants the mechanic to re-check a fix
     when it runs --add 2 --peer mechanic --stone 5.1.execution
     then ONLY the mechanic's budget rises                        # route.ts:1853 — the scoped form
      and the architect's meter is untouched
      and no human was asked                                      # acceptance #5

[t4b] given the driver instead tops up the DISPUTED lane on purpose
      when it runs --add 2 --peer architect --stone 5.1.execution
      then the architect's budget rises 4 → 6
       and the architect does not run THIS generation             # 🔴 F004
       and the emit says so, rather than a silent no-op           # rule.forbid.surprises
       and it says the rounds are deferred, never lost            # 🟡 S03 — they buy the next generation

[t5] given instead the architect were exhausted rather than live
     when the driver runs --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1 --why <path>
     then it is admitted, identically                             # the stance is orthogonal to the meter

[t6] given the driver then EDITS the artifact and re-arrives      # 🔴 S03 — the lapse
     when the guard runs the architect again
     then it runs                                                 # the lens returns
      and it mints a NEW .given                                   # so the old stance no longer covers
      and the lane's B state falls back to unanswered
      and the driver must answer, then declare again              # one command, and the SAME --why path
```

🔴 **`[t2a]` is the shape `F018` inverted, and it is easy to skip past.** the design once **minted**
the entry from the command; the wisher ruled the driver authors it and the command validates the
reference (`S05`). ⇒ **the acceptance-#4 guarantee survives by a different mechanism** — an unwritten
fulcrum is an unresolvable path — and the entry a council reads is far heavier than a stub.

🔴 **`[t2]`'s key line is the one a builder is most likely to misread.**
`getLatestPeerGivensPerSlug.ts:44-50` keys the debt by `iteration`, then `pathGiven`, *"across all
hashes"*. ⇒ a **hash**-keyed stance looks equivalent and is not: the hash moves on any edit the
stone's glob sees, the given moves only when **that lane speaks again**. a hash key would void a
dispute the reviewer never re-raised.

🔴 **`[t3]`, `[t4b]` and `[t6]` are the acceptance-#2 trio, and `[t6]` is the one this demo lacked
until 2026-09-09.** the dispute does not merely stop the *charge* for a round — it stops the *round*,
**for one generation**. `[t4b]` is the proof of the stop; **`[t6]` is the proof that it ENDS**, and
without it a reader takes the skip for the stone.

⚠️ **`[t4b]` is where `F004` costs the most, so it must be loud** — a budget spent on a lane that
will not run this round is the one surprise this cell can cause (`rule.require.status-feedback`).
✅ **and the emit's message improved with `S03`**: the rounds are **deferred**, never wasted, which
is a smaller surprise to report than the one this line first named.

🔴 **that "loud" clause is not copy — it is a build.** the budget emit is `asGuardBudgetUpdateLines`
(`route.ts:1896-1910`), rendering one line per update out of three fields —
`{ peer, budgetBefore, budgetAfter }`. **not one of them can say a lane is disputed.** and its
producer cannot learn it either: `processGuardFileBudgets` (`:1916+`) builds those records from a
**text scan of the guard YAML** and never opens `passage.jsonl`, where a stance lives. ⇒ a new field
**and** a new data source, filed against `F04`'s bill.

🟡 **the five artifacts citing `route.ts:1853` all cite the FILTER; the emitter sits 43 lines below.**
⇒ **a line number is a claim about a boundary.**

⚠️ **and `[t4b]` is the ONE-lane form of this ask.** `case=9` §2 states the two-lane form — a top-up
that must name a re-armed lane **and** a lane left dark, in one block — and that is strictly stronger:
a filtered-out lane produces **no update record at all**, so no render can add it. ⇒ **read `case=9`
§2 for the full price**; this cell states the half that a single-lane demo can hold.

⚠️ **`[t4b]` is where F04's cost is visible in the happy path.** the driver has budget, has a live
guard, aims it at the architect, and still cannot make that lane re-read. **that is deliberate and it
is the sharpest call in this design** — read `F04` before you accept it.

## .why it is a happy path and not a sharp one

the driver is not surprised, not blocked, and not escalated. every step reports what it did, the
record is written for it rather than by it, and the disagreement ends where a disagreement should —
in front of the person who owns the call.

⚠️ **the reviewer is not overruled and is not wrong.** its blocker stays on record, in its `.given`,
cited by the fulcrum. *"disputed"* is a verdict on the **disagreement**, never on the reviewer.

`dispute × answered × live` ✅ · `dispute × answered × exhausted` ✅ `[t5]` ·

🔴 **`top-up × disputed × live` rests on `[t4b]`, never `[t4]`.** `[t4]` tops up the **mechanic**
(`--add 2 --peer mechanic`) — it demos the scoped form of the top-up, and a scoped form is not a
coordinate. `[t4b]` aims the budget at the **architect**, the disputed lane, and watches it stay
dark. ⇒ that is the only step that can prove acceptance #2, *"the disagreement consumes no budget."*
