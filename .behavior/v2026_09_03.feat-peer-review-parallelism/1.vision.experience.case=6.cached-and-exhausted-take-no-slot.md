unbounded, the freed slots buy no time (every member could run at once anyway); capped, they buy the
fresh members an earlier start. ⇒ **the capped case is the one worth a demo, and the unbounded case
is the one worth a claim** — this file needed to say it covers both.

## .the narrative — the driver's pov

round three of a convergence loop. level 1 holds five reviewers, and by now two of them approved
me at this hash and one has burned its budget. only two actually need to run.

what i must not get: a pool that admits five, three of which are no-ops. under a cap of 2 that
would be a real loss — two of my slots would go to reviewers that were never going to run, and my
two live reviewers would queue behind them. the cap would be spent on emptiness.

what i get: the short-circuits are decided **before** the pool is entered. the two cached-approved
lanes render at once. the exhausted lane renders at once. then the pool takes the two fresh ones,
and my cap of 2 admits exactly the two that matter.

the aha: **a slot is for work, and a skip is not work.**

## 🔴 .why this ordering is load-bearing

the extant code decides the short-circuits inside the same loop that runs the reviews — the
cached-approved check at `:399`, the exhausted skip at `:438`, both `continue` before execution.
under concurrency those checks must move **out of the concurrent body and into the group's
setup**, so a skip never occupies a slot.

and the order between the two is itself load-bearing and already settled by the extant code:

> `.note = cache check MUST come before exhaustion check to honor this contract` — `:398`

⇒ a reviewer that already approved at this hash is cheap even when its budget is spent. the
concurrent port must preserve that order, or an exhausted-but-approving reviewer would be reported
as exhausted and its approval lost.
