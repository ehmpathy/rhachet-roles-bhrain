## .the one-line vision

> a peer rejection stops to be a wall the driver can only wait out, and becomes a **question the
> driver must answer**: *do you think this is a defect?* — and its answer, on record, decides whether
> the road holds or advances.

## .the outcome world

### a day in the life

*driver, stone `5.1.execution`, eleven peer lanes*

round 2. ten lanes approve. the architect says my file placement is wrong; I say the convention says
otherwise. I write the `[REFUTE]` with seven peer paths quoted. the architect re-raises, longer.

so I write the argument down where a council will find it — `.fulcrums/…case=F007-file-placement.md`
— and point the stance at it:

```
rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1 \
  --why .fulcrums/inventory.of=fulcrums.case=F007-file-placement.md
```

the architect lane goes quiet **for this generation**, and the drive proceeds. round 3 costs ten
lanes instead of eleven.

then I change the artifact, and round 4 brings the architect back — it reads the new state and
raises two points I had not heard. one is real; I fix it. the other is the same placement argument,
so I re-declare against **the same path** — the argument still holds, and the wisher named that
reuse. the stone passes.

at the close, the human opens `.fulcrums/inventory.of=fulcrums._.md`, sorts on `rework`, reads one
`dirty` row, and rules. total human attention: under a minute.

### before / after

| | today | after |
|---|---|---|
| a driver answers and still disagrees | re-arrive, re-raise, repeat | 🔴 **declare a stance; the road advances or holds on it** |
| the halt asks the driver | *"more budget, or a human?"* | *"do you think this is a defect?"* |
| what ends a disagreement | **exhaustion** — the budget runs out | **judgment** — the driver's, then the council's |
| what a churned round leaves | a budget number that shrank, no reason | a `conceded` row per round, and a count |
| where a disagreement is settled | mid-route, by a human, one at a time, at full price | at the close, by the same human, in a batch, off one index |
| a lane the driver disputes | re-raises the same point every round, at full cost | 🔴 quiet until the artifact moves — then live again, on what is **new** |

### 🔴 the wish's third claim understates its own best argument

> *"a driver that silently proceeds and a driver that declares `<dispute>` do the same work."*
> — `0.wish.md:43-44`

**a driver cannot silently proceed today.** `setStoneAsPassed.ts:870` gates every later check behind
`allJudgesPassed`, and a judge tallies its lane's verdict; `rejected` is not terminal
(`rule.always.converge-to-terminal`). so the comparison is against an act the engine forbids.

⇒ **the real baseline is worse than "silent", and that is the sharper case.** today the one exit
from a disagreement is to run the lane **to exhaustion** — and an exhausted lane is *ambiguous by
construction*. `rule.always.converge-to-terminal` names the ambiguity outright:

> *"budget burned without genuine engagement is a coast, and a defect … an exhausted level with no
> record is indistinguishable from a coast."*

| the driver who… | leaves today | leaves after |
|---|---|---|
| genuinely disagreed, and argued it | `exhausted`, 0 rounds left | `disputed`, a fulcrum, budget intact |
| coasted, and let the meter run out | `exhausted`, 0 rounds left | `exhausted`, 0 rounds left |

🔴 **today those two rows are the same row.** the artifact cannot tell an argued disagreement from a
driver that stopped trying — which is precisely the distinction a council needs and precisely what
`converge-to-terminal` asks a driver to supply by hand, in prose, in `.taken.by_self`.

⇒ **the dispute is not a record where there was none. it is an unambiguous record where the only
extant one is ambiguous** — and it separates the two rows *mechanically*, rather than ask a driver to
self-certify which one they are.

### the aha

> **a review budget was never the right instrument for a disagreement. it ends one by exhaustion.**
> a dispute ends it by judgment — and defers the judgment into the fulcrum council, **which was
> already due to happen.**

⇒ that is the wish's own argument, and its first half is **verified**: the disputed verdict is
**already** bound for human review (`rule.always.itemize-the-fulcrums-you-best-guess` requires every
judgment call be itemized; `rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last` gathers them into one review at the
close). **a mid-route halt buys a review that would have happened regardless.**

🔴 **its second half — *"at the highest possible price"* — this vision does NOT adopt whole.** that
clause prices the human's attention and prices the driver's rework at zero, and the two reviews are
the same review only where the council agrees. the trade is real rather than free, and the design
holds anyway because **today's world offers no cheap early verdict either** — it re-argues to
exhaustion and *then* summons the human. ⇒ the full weighing is `.what is awkward` § 7, and the
alternative it surfaces is `F13`.

## .the contract

**two new `PassageReport.status` values**, declared per reviewer, on the extant verb:

```sh
rhx route.stone.set --stone <stone> --as disputed --with <reviewer> --about <concern> \
  --why .fulcrums/inventory.of=fulcrums.case=F00N-<slug>.md
rhx route.stone.set --stone <stone> --as conceded --with <reviewer> --about <concern>
```

#### 🔴 the canonical signature — every flag, and which are required

| flag | takes | `disputed` | `conceded` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--stone` | the stone name | **required** | **required** |
| 🔴 `--with` | a **reviewer** slug, sanitized form (`setStoneAsContemplated.ts:77-92`) | **required** | **required** |
| 🔴 `--about` | a **concern**, by ordinal within that reviewer's report — `nitpick.4`, `blocker.3` | **required** (`S07`) | **required** |
| 🔴 `--why` | a **path** to a fulcrum entry the driver authored; the file must exist (`F018`) | **required** | ❌ **forbidden** — a concede claims no judgment |

🔴 **the object flags differ from `--as contemplated`'s `--that` on purpose, and the parser dispatches
on `--as`, so nothing collides.** the grammar reads as English at the call site:

| the act | reads |
|---|---|
| `--as contemplated --that <reviewer>` | you answer **that** reviewer's points |
| `--as disputed --with <reviewer> --about <concern>` | you take a stance **with** a party, **about** a thing |

⇒ **`--with` names the PARTY and `--about` names the SUBJECT**, which is the split `case=11` proves
the design needs: a stance keyed only to a party sheds every concern that party raised.

✅ **every demo carries this signature** — swept 2026-09-13. and the sweep was not cosmetic: it
changed **step counts**, **arithmetic**, and **one invariant's key**.

| what the sweep moved | where |
|---|---|
| 3 blockers → **3 declarations**, one conceded and two disputed | `case=3` `[t2b]` |
| 🔴 R3's key narrowed to `(reviewer, concern, given)` — a contrary stance on a **different** concern is no flip-flop | `case=8` `[t2b]` |
| 🔴 the nitpick tally: **2 of 8** leave the sum, never all 8 | `case=10` `[t1]` `[t1b]` |
| a `given` pinned to ONE concern, so lane and concern coincide | `case=4`, `case=9` |

🔴 **and it surfaced a fork the board did not carry — `F024`, at 70%, owed before 2.1.criteria.**
`F04`'s skip and the stance gate's exit condition were both argued under the per-LANE grain, and
neither has a stated answer at two concerns. ⇒ **a verdict that lands late leaves its own blast
radius unwalked**, and the walk is what finds the rows the verdict implied.

| | `disputed` | `conceded` |
|---|---|---|
| it means | *"this ONE concern is fine to continue"* | 🔴 *"the reviewer is right on this ONE — I will FIX it, then run again"* |
| the tally | 🔴 **that ONE concern leaves the sum** — see below | **kept** in the sum |
| the hold | lifts **iff what remains clears the threshold** | **kept** — the extant hold stands |
| the lane's next round | 🔴 **skipped for ONE artifact generation**, then live again | runs, as today |
| budget | untouched | untouched by the stance itself |
| the record | 🔴 a **fulcrum the driver authored**, referenced by `--why` | a row in `passage.jsonl` |
| the human | reads it at the council — 🔴 **guaranteed** | is not summoned |

### 🔴 *"the hold is lifted"* is an OUTCOME — the mechanism is a TALLY EXCLUSION

`S06` states the mechanism in the wisher's own words, and it is narrower than *"lifted"* reads:

> *"they only need to dispute if they want that review excluded from the judges counts, right?"*

⇒ **a dispute subtracts from the set the judge sums.** the judge does not read a stance; it counts
and compares to allowances:

```ts
const { totalBlockers, totalNitpicks } = await computeReviewTotalsFromFiles({
  reviewFiles: reviewFilesToCount,          // route.ts:1404
});
```

and `reviewFilesToCount` is **already a filtered set** (`:1397`) — so the design reuses a seam rather
than cuts one:

| | the **forgive** (extant) | the **dispute** (new) |
|---|---|---|
| who declares it | a **human** | the **driver** |
| its grain | a **level** | 🔴 **one CONCERN** — see below |
| what it does to the tally | drops those files whole | 🔴 subtracts **exactly one** |

> *"keeps only the review files whose level was NOT overruled by a human … a level the human
> overruled is forgiven — **its blockers must not gate passage**"*
> — `getNonOverruledReviewFiles.ts:7-12`

#### 🔴 the analogy holds on the AXIS and breaks on the GRAIN — `S07`

`S06` named a dispute *"the driver's forgive, at lane grain"*, and that phrase carried the extant
implementation in with it: `getNonOverruledReviewFiles` drops a whole **review FILE**. so a lane at 4
nitpicks, disputed on 1, shed all 4 — **the driver's own three concessions among them.**

| the grain | a lane at 4 nitpicks, 1 disputed | what the judge sees |
|---|---|---|
| per-**file** (as `S06` landed) | all 4 leave | 🔴 a clean lane. **the concessions are erased** |
| per-**concern** (`S07`) | 1 leaves | ✅ 3 still counted, still gate |

⚠️ **a forgive is legitimately coarse BECAUSE A HUMAN DECLARES IT. the breadth is the authority.**
copy the grain onto a driver's move and you copy the grant along with the code.

🔴 **and `S05` had already ruled it, one day earlier** — *"a stance is declared per lane; a
disagreement is about one point."* the prose said *per point* while the mechanism read a file. ⇒ **a
settlement reached the row it was written on and no mechanism.**

⇒ the law that binds it: **a declaration discharges the concerns it NAMES; concerns it did not name
survive it** (`rule.forbid.suppression-of-undeclared-concerns`), and the twelve-row walk of every
declaration the engine accepts is at `case=11`.

🟡 **the atom now has a word.** a `concern` is one blocker or one nitpick
(`term=route.guard.review.given.concern`); the driver names one by ordinal within that reviewer's own
report, and **no reviewer is asked to mint an id** — `contract.reviewer-output` is untouched.
⇒ ✅ **both calls are wisher-ruled and both UPHELD** (`S08`, 2026-09-10):

| | the verdict |
|---|---|
| `F020` — what the ordinal indexes into | **one given.** *"stances do not survive rounds; only live in latest round"* ⇒ a fresh round renumbers, and the driver re-declares |
| `F021` — is there a bulk form | **no `--about all`.** *"a driver who types `all` has not read all"* ⇒ a declaration is **evidence of a read**, never merely a record |

🔴 **and the second verdict is a claim the whole per-concern grain rests on.** it settles that the
per-concern cost is not friction to be optimized away — it is the mechanism's **purpose**. ⇒ an
enumeration (`--about nitpick.1,2,3`) stays reachable because it names each concern; a **range**
(`nitpick.1-6`) does not, since it is `all` with a hyphen.

✅ **and this is the plainest read of the wish's boundary anywhere in the vision.** *"do not remove
the peer review. the reviewer still runs and still renders a verdict … never whether one happens."*
⇒ under a tally exclusion the review **runs**, its file is **written**, its verdict is **rendered**,
and the artifact **persists for the council**. only the **count** is dropped. ⇒ demoed at `case=10`.

#### 🔴 the tally is a sum ACROSS LANES — so a per-lane command has a global effect

> *"aggregates counts **across multiple review files** for threshold check"*
> — `computeReviewTotalsFromFiles.ts:6-7`, a `+=` over every file (`:20-21`)

**one `totalBlockers` and one `totalNitpicks` for the whole stone**, compared to one allowance
(`route.ts:1404-1414`). ⇒ **a dispute lifts the hold only if what REMAINS falls under the threshold.**

| the stone | `--allow-nitpicks 7` | dispute one lane |
|---|---|---|
| two lanes at 5 | total 10 — **held** | leaves 5 — ✅ **passes** |
| three lanes at 5 | total 15 — **held** | leaves 10 — 🔴 **still held** |

⚠️ **`--as disputed --with <reviewer> --about <concern>` is a per-CONCERN command whose effect is a GLOBAL arithmetic**, and
*"the hold lifts for that lane"* — the phrase this contract's own table used — states the grain of the
command rather than the grain of the effect. ⇒ **a driver who reads it that way disputes a lane, sees
the stone still held, and has no way to tell whether the dispute worked.**

⇒ **the halt owes an arithmetic line the extant one does not carry**: the **residual total** against
the threshold.

🟡 **`S07` struck the fork over what to print** (`F019`): under per-concern arithmetic every dispute
is worth exactly 1, so the *"which lane next?"* search cannot be performed, and the residual total is
the only quantity a driver cannot derive at a glance.

⚠️ **the `.tell` survives the strike.** the mechanism implied **that** a line was owed; it implied
**which** line not at all. ⇒ **a consequence of a verdict does not feel like a choice**, which is how
a fork gets recorded as prose.

🔴 **and it was findable only after `S06`.** under `F015` fork A a nitpick-only lane could not be
disputed at all, so **no case exercised a dispute whose only effect was a subtraction** — the grain
mismatch had nowhere to show itself. ⇒ **a reversal re-opens the questions the old answer made
unaskable.**

**1. the skip is per GENERATION, never per stone** (`S03` → `F004`, reversed 2026-09-09):

> *"a reviewer may have many blockers, and only 1 of them could be disputed; then artifact changes,

⇒ **a stance is declared per lane; a disagreement is about one point.** to silence a lane for the

**2. `--why` takes a PATH to a fulcrum entry** (`S05` → `F018`) — 🟡 and its *"one fulcrum is one

> *"the `--as disputed --why $path` shoould reference fulcrums"*
> *"each fulcrum is a single dispute; and they can reuse fulcrums from past disputes"*
> *"they can open multiple disputes w/ multiple fulcrums for the same reviewer if they want too"*

| rule | |
|---|---|
| `--why` is a **path**, never prose | required, and it must resolve |
| **one fulcrum = one dispute** | a lane with two disputed points owes **two** entries |
| **N per reviewer** | the relation is many-to-one; a lane may carry several live disputes |
| **reusable** | argument still holds ⇒ same path, across generations and across routes |

⇒ **the reason and the fulcrum are ONE artifact**, and the design had modelled two. every question

### the five invariants

1. **a stance is owed** iff the reviewer's latest given is **`rejected`** and its level is
   unforgiven. ⚠️ *not* the raw `blockers > 0` that
   `getAllRouteGuardReviewPeersUncontemplated.ts:31` uses today — **the two part on two separate
   axes**, and both are below

   ✅ **WISHER-RULED 2026-09-10, and the ruling is the design's cleanest simplification** (`S06` →
   `F015` fork B):

   > *"nah, they only need to dispute if they want that review excluded from the judges counts,
   > right?"*

   ⇒ **the predicate is not a judgment about which critiques deserve a formal answer. it is whatever
   the JUDGE COUNTS**, and the judge counts both axes:
   `computeReviewThresholdVerdict.ts:15-27` fails on `totalBlockers > allowBlockers` **or**
   `totalNitpicks > allowNitpicks`. **so a nitpick-only rejection owes a stance**, and the invariant
   above stands unmodified — one predicate, `F012`'s, with no second clause. ⇒ demoed at `case=10`

   🔴 **the extant predicate is under-specified, in the code as much as in a first read of it.** the gate
   hard-codes `blockers > 0` and takes no thresholds; the verdict uses `blockers <= allowBlockers`
   (`computeReviewPeerVerdict.ts:65`), which a guard sets via `allow-blockers`
   (`getReviewedJudgeThresholds.ts:18`; its tests pin 2, 3, 5). ⇒ at `allow-blockers 3` a lane with
   2 blockers is **approved** and **still owes a taken** — and, read literally, would owe a stance
   too. **you would be asked to dispute a reviewer that approved you.**

   ⇒ **the stance predicate keys on the verdict, never on the raw count:** a stance is owed iff the
   lane's verdict is `rejected`. that reuses `computeReviewPeerVerdict` as the one source of truth.
   ⚠️ the extant contemplation gate keeps its own predicate; whether **it** should key on the
   verdict is a live defect this vision surfaces but does not own — see `.dream/` at 5.1

   🔴 **and the verdict has a SECOND axis the debt predicate does not — nitpicks.** the check spans
   `:64-65` — `blockers <= allowBlockers && nitpicks <= allowNitpicks` — and **this route's own guard sets
   `--allow-blockers 0 --allow-nitpicks 7`** (`1.vision.guard:13`). so an 8-nitpick, 0-blocker lane
   computes **`rejected`** while `blockers > 0` reads it **clean**.

   ⇒ **the two diverge on this very route**, never at some hypothetical threshold. so a nitpick-only
   rejection **does** owe a stance (`S06`), and the predicate stays **one clause** — `F012`'s,
   unmodified. the reversal that got here is `F015`.

   🟡 **`runStoneGuardReviews.ts:430` is what makes that case reachable**: it skips on raw
   `blockers === 0` **before** the exhaustion check, so a nitpick-only lane never spends a round and
   cannot exhaust. ⇒ `case=10`'s *"let it exhaust"* exit is shut by that line, which is why the stance
   is the road out.
2. **answer first.** a stance at `unanswered` is refused. the wish's boundary, mechanized
3. **exactly one, and it is final for its given.** neither is refused (`enter × answered` halts);
   a contrary second is refused; an identical repeat is idempotent

   🔴 **"its given" is keyed the way the DEBT is keyed — never by hash.**
   `getLatestPeerGivensPerSlug.ts:44-50` orders by **`iteration`, then `pathGiven`**, and its `.why`
   says the point outright: *"of every peer .given **across all hashes** … the read that makes the
   debt survive an edit to the artifact under review."*

   ⇒ **the given key is what makes the wisher's per-generation lapse fall out by construction.**
   when the artifact moves and the lane re-runs, it mints a **new** given — so a stance declared
   against the old one no longer covers it, and the driver re-declares. that is `F004`'s ruled
   behavior, obtained from the key rather than from a second mechanism.

   ⚠️ **a HASH key is the near-miss, and it lapses on the wrong event.** the hash moves on any edit
   to any file the stone's glob matches; the given moves only when **that lane speaks again**. ⇒ a
   hash-keyed stance voids a dispute the reviewer never re-raised — the driver re-declares against
   a lane that had nothing new to say. the hash is **provenance the fulcrum records**, never the key
   (`F004` § *what the review hash actually is*).

   🔴 **and a SECOND, independent reason: a hash key is architecturally unaffordable.** the entrance
   gate's own placement note (`setStoneAsPassed.ts:321-331`) argues its seam in three clauses, and the
   third is that it sits **before `computeStoneReviewInputHash`**, so *"a halt costs a directory read
   rather than a hash + a round of subprocesses."* `:330-331` names what buys that: *"it needs NO
   hash. under P2 the debt is keyed to the reviewer."*

   ⇒ **a hash-keyed stance gate could not sit in that seam.** it would have to move below the hash —
   past `stampGuardReport`, which the same note says would let a halt *"stamp a guard report for
   reviews that never ran."* ⇒ **the slug key is what lets the stance gate sit beside the
   contemplation gate at all**, and the two arguments are independent: one is about **when a stance
   lapses**, this one is about **where the gate can physically live**.

   🟡 **and *"free across givens"* is live for BOTH stances**, which is the wisher's ruling read at
   this grain: a conceded lane re-runs and a disputed one returns after one generation, so each can
   mint a new given and open a fresh stance slot (`case=8` `[t5]`). ⇒ *"final for its given"* is
   **final for one generation**, never final for the stone — and the driver re-declares, at the cost

4. **a dispute cannot exist without its record.** `--why` takes a **path** and the path **must
   resolve** — so a dispute with no readable fulcrum entry is refused at the boundary, before any

   council reads is far heavier than a minted stub: the fork, the counter, the `rework` grade, and

   🟡 **a record cannot outlive its dispute in any way that MISLEADS** — there is no findsert and no

5. 🔴 **budget adds ONLY to the LATEST LEVEL by default — this vision OVERRIDES the wish here, ON
   PURPOSE.** the wish treats budget as a stone-level knob; it is not. today
   `route.guard.budget --add N` sprays **every peer at every level** (`route.ts:1851-1872`;
   `--peer <slug>` is the only filter, and there is no `--level`), so a top-up meant for the level in
   play silently resurrects lower **exhausted** levels. the deliberate override: **a default add
   lands on the LATEST (current) level alone.** a lower level — exhausted or not — is extended ONLY
   when it is **explicitly named** (an explicit rewind, or an explicit budget against it), and an
   exhausted lower level is a state to accept, never a defect to heal (`S09` · `S13` → `F022`).

   ⚠️ **this is a deliberate correction of the wish's *"a multi-level ladder: untouched"*, and it is
   WORK this vision owns — not a note.** the concede emit at `case=6` `[t1]` prints the **scoped**
   command (the latest level, or the named lane), never the blanket one;
   `formatBlockRemedyGroups` already computes the exact exhausted set (`:79-85`) and discards it — the
   repair is to print that set and let the driver pick. ⇒ budget is scarce, and the scarcity is the

### 🔴 the fields the ledger does not have, and why `reason` must not carry them

a stance is **per-reviewer**, so the ledger must record *which*. `PassageReport` today offers two
precedents, and **the nearer one is the wrong one:**

| the precedent | how it scopes | who reads it back |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 `level?: number` — *"only for status='overruled'"* (`PassageReport.ts:55-62`) | a **structured** field, with a `.why` and a legacy-absent rule | the engine, for a **behavioral** decision |
| `contemplated` / `promised` | the slug is interpolated into free-text `reason` (`setStoneAsContemplated.ts:108`, `setStoneAsPromised.ts:53`) | 🔴 **nobody.** their debt is derived from **files on disk**, never from this string |

⇒ **`--as disputed` must follow `level?`, and add `reviewer?: string`.** the skip predicate has to
answer *"is the architect disputed on this stone?"* from the ledger — and that is a **behavior**
decision, which no extant `reason` consumer makes.

⚠️ **the trap is that the wrong precedent is the one this design tells you to copy.** `--with
<reviewer>` reuses `--as contemplated`'s slug check, so the nearest model puts the slug in `reason`
— and it works there, because `contemplated` never reads its own string back. a stance does.

🟡 **and free-text parse is not hypothetical here.** `formatBlockRemedyGroups.ts:64` runs
`reason.includes('budget exhausted')` and `:79` regexes it; `formatRouteDriveMixedHalt.ts:44` runs
`reason.includes('malfunction')`. **the engine already does this — for DISPLAY.** ⇒ a skip predicate
built the same way fails **silently** the first time a reason string is reworded, and a silent
mis-skip runs a lane the driver disputed, or quiets one it did not.

⇒ `level?`'s own doc supplies the migration: *"absent on legacy … rows; absent treated as 'all
levels' for backward compatibility."* a new `reviewer?` reads the same way — **absent means the row
predates the field**, never *"every reviewer"*.

#### ✅ and one field is not enough — but `F018` makes the second one FREE

`case=8:35` prints, on the R3 refusal — *"you already declared the contrary; here is what stands"*:

```
├─ declared against .reviews/peer/…given…, fulcrum F007
```

| what the refusal prints | derivable? |
|---|---|
| **which** reviewer holds the stance | ✅ `reviewer?`, the field above |
| **which generation** it was declared against | ✅ the `.given` path — the same value invariant 3 keys on, so it is on hand before the row is written |
| ✅ its **fulcrum** | ✅ **it IS the `--why` argument.** stored verbatim, no derivation |

🔴 **row 3 is where `F018` pays.** the driver names the path, so `fulcrum?: string` is a **copy of an
argument** — the one shape that cannot drift from itself. under a mint it would be a pointer to a
file the command chose, and pointer-and-file drift would owe its own argument.

⚠️ **and without a stored path at all, an R3 refusal must open `.fulcrums/` and scan every entry for
a reviewer match** — a directory read plus N file reads, **on a refusal path**, to render one line.

🟡 **no HASH is stored.** a driver-authored entry records none — the driver does not compute the
stone hash — so the refusal names the **given**, which is both derivable and the truer referent: the
given is what the stance answers, where a hash is the artifact set the guard happened to read.

🟡 **a CONCEDE has no fulcrum** (`case=5` `[t0]`), so `fulcrum?` is absent there by design — the
same legacy-absent discipline `level?` uses, one sense further: **absent means either a legacy row or
a concede**, and `status` tells the two apart.

## 🔴 .the severity split — a concede carries `better` or `urgent` (`S14`)

**the wisher added a taxonomy after the contract was drawn**, and it lands on the concede alone:

> *"maybe we need to split --concede into --concede urgent vs --concede better … urgent = security |
> safety | monetary | reputation | behavioral defects … code idealism is never urgent … thats
> maintenance that buildsup as techdebt if we dont spend atleast the budget … but should never
> require an increased budget - shouldnt weight us down"* — `S14`

### the WHY it serves — a budget balances perfection with pragmatism

the enbriefed philosophy (`philosophy.a-review-budget-balances-perfection-with-pragmatism`) states
the ground: **the budget is a maintenance floor.** perfection has no natural stop, so a review budget
draws the line at *good enough* — spend at least the budget, and what remains evolves later. the one
exception is a defect that **ships harm**, and that is what `urgent` names.

### the two severities, by the same harm test a blocker uses

| the concession | the harm test | the severity |
|---|---|---|
| a defect in the closed set — security · safety · monetary · reputation · behavioral, harm nameable | you can name who suffers, and how | **`urgent`** |
| code idealism, maintenance, polish — a tidier shape, a clearer name, an absent guard on a case that cannot arise | you cannot name a shipped harm | **`better`** |

⇒ **the same test `rule.forbid.overzealous-blockers` gives a blocker** — *name the harm that ships* —
grades a concession's severity, so a driver and a reviewer cannot reach opposite grades on one point
(`rule.always.concede-with-a-severity` driver · `rule.require.grade-a-concession-by-its-harm`
reviewer).

### what the severity decides — the needs-a-human verdict at a budget hit

| the budget hit | the verdict |
|---|---|
| every concession `better`, or none | 🔴 **good enough — no human, the stone proceeds.** the budget is the floor, and it was met |
| ≥1 live `urgent` | needs increased budget, **warn the human this PR** — they grant more |

🔴 **`better` must NEVER earn increased budget** — *"shouldnt weight us down"*. a `better` concession
cited as a reason to top up is the mis-grade this rule catches; it evolves after the budget, by design.

### the shape it takes — `--severity better|urgent`, default `better` (F028, 80%)

```sh
rhx route.stone.set --stone <stone> --as conceded --with <reviewer> --about <concern> \
  --severity urgent
```

- **a severity is an ATTRIBUTE of a concede, never a kind of stance** — so it is one optional flag on
  the extant `conceded` status, beside `--with`/`--about`, never a fork into two statuses (F028)
- **default `better`** — the taught norm is concede-and-fix (`S11`), and a `better` concession is the
  common case. an ungraded concede is a `better` one; the harm test is owed only when a driver reaches
  for `urgent`
- **the ledger gains one optional `severity?`** on the `level?` precedent; the exhaustion disposition
  reads it (`isRouteGuardConcessionExhaustion`, refined by severity)

## .mental model

### how a driver would describe it to a friend

> "usually i just fix what the reviewer flagged and run again — that's the normal move. but when i
> think it's genuinely wrong, or it keeps recurring, i can file a formal objection instead. i say
> *i object, and here's why* — on the record — and the road keeps on. the judge rules at the end."

🟡 **the fix leads, and the objection follows it** — a mental model that names only the exception
teaches the exception as the rule.

### how a human would describe it

> "before, when the bot and a reviewer disagreed, i found out because the stone had taken eleven

**the dissent.** a judge who disagrees does not halt the court. they file a dissent, it goes on the
record, and the case proceeds. the dissent's value is that it is **written down and attributable** —
precisely the wish's *"the signal is the value, never the permission."*

## .evaluation

### 🔴 first: the wish states per-LANE variables as stone-level constants

**three of the six criteria mislead a grader who checks them word-for-word.** the wish's unit is the
stone; every mechanism this design touches is keyed to a **reviewer** — its meter, its debt, its
verdict. read the acceptance table below with this in view, or it will read as three unrelated
deviations from the wish.

| the wish's words | a literal grade | what the criterion is actually about | the variable it fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1 *"neither, or **both**, is refused"* | 🔴 **vacuous.** the stance is one `--as` value, so *both at once* is structurally impossible and no shape can fail it | a driver who declares one stance and **later** declares its contrary — `R3`/`case=8`. a stance flipped to suit the moment | **unit** — one *"response"* per halt, where there is one per lane |
| #2 *"the **route** drives on"* | 🔴 **false.** with 3 lanes and 1 dispute the stone still holds — the other two are unsettled | **that lane's** hold lifts, and its rounds cost zero thereafter (F01, case=1 `[t3]`, case=4) | **unit** — the stone, where the design moves a lane |
| #3 *"surfaces a **budget** ask"* | 🔴 **wrong at `live`**, where rounds remain and the remedy that suits is a re-arrival | the concede **halts**, and the emit prints the remedy the meter warrants (F05) | **state** — assumes `exhausted`, where the meter also has `live` |

⇒ **the outcome is honored in all three; the words are not** — `rule.require.wish-outcome-over-proposal`.

⚠️ **the cause is one family, not one mechanism, and the distinction is worth the extra column.**
#1 and #2 collapse the per-lane **unit**; #3 collapses the per-lane **state**. to call all three
*"a grain mismatch"* would be tidier and would be an invented taxonomy — the honest generalization
is the weaker one: **the wish fixes, as a stone-level constant, a quantity that varies per lane.**

🟡 **why this is stated once, above the table, rather than three times inside it.** the two failure
modes point opposite ways and neither cancels the other: a reviewer who grades #2 by *"did the stone
pass?"* reads **a correct design as a failure**, and one who grades #1 literally **passes an
untested design as correct** — the flipped stance never gets a test. one wastes a round; the other
ships a gap.

- 🔴 **and the lens is not lost, which is the per-generation skip's whole gain over a per-stone one.**
  the lane returns on the next generation and grades what it has never seen — *"it might have some

## 🔴 .what is awkward

### 1. the driver grades its own escape hatch — and I am the party it favours

no gate stops a driver from a dispute on every lane, at every generation. it must answer first and
it must point at a fulcrum it wrote, and then it may pass any stone it likes.

⚠️ **and this vision was written by the party that benefits.** `F004` carries that admission at the
lowest confidence on the board.

✅ **the second opinion `rule.always.get-a-second-opinion-before-foreman` demands is DISCHARGED, by
the strongest reader available** — the wisher read `F004`, `F013` and `F014` together and reversed
two. that is not a peer read; it is the read a peer read exists to approximate.

**the hazard is unchanged in KIND and smaller in DEGREE, on two axes:**

| what shrank it | how |
|---|---|
| `S03` — the lane **returns** next generation | an escape taken once is corrected by the next round, over by the council |
| `S11` — the taught norm is **concede-and-fix** | a social restraint is near-worthless where the escape is the default; it works on a rare, named, recorded act |

⚠️ **neither retires it.** a driver may still dispute every lane and no gate stops them — they would
now do so **against** the taught norm rather than along it.

the honest defense: the alternative today is *"answer forever, then a human anyway"*, which is not a
restraint — it is a delay with a bill attached.

### 2. `--why`'s content is read by no gate — ✅ and the wisher ruled that acceptable

`rule.forbid.unanswered-exits-from-a-blocker` grades this exactly: *"an empty or token `.taken` … ⛔
open — existence is asserted, never content."* the dispute inherits it whole.

**three things bound it:**

| the bound | why |
|---|---|
| the lane **returns** next generation (`S03`) | the loop is restored as the correction mechanism, exactly as for a `.taken` |
| a token `--why` is **hard to author** (`S05`) | it is a path to a fulcrum entry, whose contract demands the fork, the counter, the `rework` grade, and what would settle it — you cannot fill that without you state the counter-argument |
| an unread `--why` is **ruled acceptable** (`S04`) | the field's beneficiary is the **author**; a gate cannot improve a mechanism whose action completes before any reader arrives |

⇒ **what survives is narrower than "empty"**: a fulcrum entry whose sections are filled and argue
naught. ✅ and a blank is refused at the boundary — **`--why` names a path, and the file must
EXIST** — so the refusal fires before any state moves.

⇒ **issue #458 §3a's remedy list still applies**, and the wisher's ruling picks from it: a content
check (rejected — it is a judgment, and the author is the beneficiary) · a **final-round reviewer
pass over the takens alone** (still open, still cheap) · or an explicit, recorded acceptance.
**this vision takes the recorded acceptance, and it is now the wisher's acceptance rather than
mine.**

### 3. the wisher has reversed on this ground three times

08-04 driver's lever → 09-07 robot blocked → 09-08 driver's lever. whatever ships should survive a
fourth reversal. ⇒ that is the argument for F11 (leave #458 open) and against any change that

### 4. a concede looks like a no-op, and its value is invisible on the first read

it keeps a hold that already existed. a reader who meets `--as conceded` will ask what it *does* and
the honest answer is *"it records"*. the value only appears at the tenth round, when a human scans
the ledger and can tell churn from agreement. ⇒ **an ergonomic risk: a driver may skip it as
ceremony**, which is why `enter × conceded` halts rather than merely notes.

### 🔴 4b. the command's grain and its EFFECT's grain differ, and the surface says the command's

`--as disputed --with <reviewer> --about nitpick.4` names **one concern**; its effect is a subtraction
from a **stone-wide** sum (§*the tally is a sum ACROSS LANES*). ⇒ **a driver can perform a correct
dispute and see no change**, because other concerns still carry the stone over its allowance.

⚠️ **`rule.forbid.ambiguous-labels` grades this exactly** — *"a value reads one way; label units and
states so `3` is never '3 what?'"* — and here the ambiguous value is the **hold** itself: lifted for
that concern, or lifted for the stone? the extant halt prints neither total, so a driver cannot tell.

⇒ the fix is cheap and it is **not** this stage's: print the **residual total** against the
threshold. `2.1.criteria` owes the case; the halt formatter already has the numbers.

🟡 **what to print is no fork** (`F019`, struck by `S07`): under per-concern arithmetic every dispute
sheds exactly 1, so a per-lane breakdown answers a question nobody has.

🔴 **and the gap runs deeper than the render.** `S06` landed a per-FILE exclusion under `S05`'s
per-POINT ruling, so one dispute shed every concern its lane raised — **the driver's own concessions
among them.** the mismatch was invisible because the design had no word for the atom: the experience
walk's set-aside table weighed `reviewer-count` and **never asked `concern-count`**
(`dimensions.md` § 5). ⇒ **a domain with no word for a grain cannot have an axis on it**, and a walk
proves every cell of the *declared* product while it says naught about a dimension the vocabulary
could not name.

⇒ `S07` names the atom, `case=11` demos it, and
`rule.forbid.suppression-of-undeclared-concerns` states the law over every declaration the engine
accepts — of which **nine of twelve already obey it**, and the three that do not are the three at
LANE or SLUG grain.

*"surfaces a budget ask"* is wrong at `live`, where rounds remain. this vision prints the remedy

### 6. the halt is now three questions deep

*is there a debt? → is it answered? → is it declared?* three gates before a driver reaches the
budget question that used to be first. **each is cheap and each is a real question** — but the
cognitive load on a halt read is up, and `rule.prefer.defaults-match-common-case` says a common path
should be effortless. ⇒ **mitigated by the emit**: only the *live* question is ever printed, never
all three.

🟡 **and the same rule aims at a second target here, where the answer is different.** it grades *"a
required input the surface could infer or sensibly default"* a **nitpick** — so an ergonomist read
will ask why the stance is required rather than defaulted. **the answer is that no default is
correct**, and it is worth stating before the question is asked:

| candidate default | why it fails |
|---|---|
| default `disputed` | the wish forbids it outright (`0.wish.md:89-91`) — a driver drifts past a blocker by omission |
| default `conceded` | ⚠️ **the tempting one under `S11`, and still wrong.** a concede is a **commitment to fix**; defaulting it manufactures work the driver never agreed to do, and the council reads a concede they never meant |

⇒ **the rule presumes a common case exists to default to. `S11` names one — concede — and a default
still fabricates the SIGNAL**, which is the whole value of the change. ⇒ the norm is taught by the
emit's shape (`case=2`), never enforced by a default. **a deliberate, reasoned refusal of a
nitpick-grade rule**, per its own `.the test`:
*"for the common case, does the bare invocation just work?"* — there is no common case.

### 7. 🔴 the wish's central economic claim is true about WHETHER and false about WHEN

> *"a halt mid-route to settle a dispute buys a review that would have happened regardless. it buys
> it early, one at a time, at the highest possible price."* — `0.wish.md:38-39`

the first sentence is **verified**: `rule.always.itemize-the-fulcrums-you-best-guess` +
`rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last` do route every judgment call to a council. so a disputed verdict
really is destined for human review either way.

🔴 **the second sentence is where it slips.** *"the highest possible price"* prices the **human's
attention** and prices naught else — and attention is not the only cost in the ledger:

| when the verdict lands | the human pays | the driver pays if overturned |
|---|---|---|
| a **mid-route halt** | one interrupt, at once | naught. no work rests on it yet |
| the **end-of-route council** | one batched read | 🔴 every stone built on the disputed call since |

⇒ **the two reviews are the same review only in the case where the council AGREES.** where it
overturns, the late one buys a verdict the driver can no longer act on cheaply — `--as rewound`,
and the work between is rework.

**so the wish's economics net out under one unstated assumption: that disputes are rarely
overturned.**

#### 🔴 that assumption is MEASURABLE, and the only data available contradicts it

⇒ **28% to 83% of the driver's best-guesses that reached a verdict were overturned. the wish's
economics assume a rate near zero.** the measurement trail is `appendix/the-dispute-overturn-rate.md`;
the counts it rests on:

- the prior route: **5 of 6** adjudicated fulcrums reversed; **confidence protected none** (F9 at
  90%, F12 at 95% — reversed). the conservative floor over all 18 is **5/18 ≈ 28%**, still not *"rare"*
- **this route: 2 of 4** best-guesses reversed at the vision gate, and the numbers ranked them
  **backwards** — `F014` at 68% held; `F003`, filed as obviously clean, was reversed outright
- a seventh point of a different kind: **the code itself** falsifies a fulcrum before any council —
  the entries most exposed are those that price a change's blast radius (my own `F02`, `F05`)

✅ **the reversals at the vision gate cost naught** — no stone built, no code written. **that is the
mid-route halt at work, at the one place this design leaves it: the vision gate.** ⇒ the wish's
economics are strongest exactly where the correction is cheapest, and `F013` is about the stones where
it is not.

⇒ **`F013`'s fork B — halt on a `dirty` dispute — sits at 75%, on the council's table**: this
evidence strengthens it, and `S03` weakens it again, since a lane that returns each generation
supplies the second look fork B was the only remaining source of.

⚠️ **the honest weighing.** the extant world does not offer the cheap early verdict either: today a
disputed lane re-argues until its budget dies, and *then* a human reads it anyway — late, with the
same rework exposure, plus every round's cost. ⇒ **the wish's direction holds; only its
superlative does not.** the trade is *fewer rounds now, in exchange for a correction that arrives
after work has settled on it*, and that is a real trade rather than a free one.

⇒ **the mitigation is already in the design and should be read as load-bearing rather than
cosmetic**: the fulcrum's `rework` column. a council that sorts on `rework` reads the **dirty** rows
first, which is precisely the set where a late verdict costs most
(`rule.always.itemize-the-fulcrums-you-best-guess`). ⇒ **a dispute whose rework is `dirty` is the
one that most wants an early verdict**, and the column is what lets a human find it.

🔴 **and `S03` added a SECOND mitigation the design did not have on 09-08: the reviewer itself.** a
disputed point is re-raisable by the lane at the next generation, so a wrong dispute has a corrector
that costs no human attention at all. ⇒ **that is what moved `F013` off the pre-5.1 ask** — it was
the only early second look, and it no longer is.

🟡 a cheaper hedge exists and is **not** taken here, deliberately: halt on a `dirty` dispute and
drive on for a `clean` one. it is left out because it re-introduces the mid-route halt this wish
exists to remove, on a predicate the driver itself grades. **recorded as a weighed alternative, not
an oversight** — `F013`, re-scored twice: down on the measured overturn rate, then up on `S03`.

🔴 **read F13 before you raise the rule that seems to forbid this.**
`rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last` maps *"open, rework **dirty**"* → `--as blocked`, which reads
as a mandate for the hedge. it is not one: four lines below, that rule requires **all three** of
*"no defensible guess · a dirty rework · every other question already addressed"*, and a dispute
fails the first by construction — a `.taken` with a cited refutation **is** a defensible guess.
⇒ `defer-fulcrums-to-last` therefore stays off the *"rules that must move"* list, and F13 carries
the argument.
