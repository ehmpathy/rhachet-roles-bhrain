# S12 · `lane` is undeclared jargon — the wisher could not parse it

## .said

verbatim, 2026-09-04, mid-drive at `1.vision`, on the deliverable-priority table row *"a **lane** wired onto a guard | a downstream **usecase**, and separate"*:

> do you mean a peer-reviewer? wtf is a lane

## .settled

**`lane` and `reviewer` name one concept, and only one of the two is declared.**

| word | status |
|---|---|
| `reviewer` | **declared** — a whole role, and it owns `contract.reviewer-output` |
| `lane` | **undeclared** — the glossary already lists it under *"spoken in a booted brief with no cluster behind it"* |

⇒ so this is **`rule.forbid.domain-term-inconsistency`**, not a glossary gap. its repair is *choose the canonical word and conform the rest*, never *declare the second one*.

### 🟡 the word spread through prose, from a brief whose own name contradicts it

`rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions` is named for **reviewer** and its body reaches for **lane** — *"the lane's prompt blew the window"*, *"an overflowed lane returns terminal"*.

⇒ **that is the inconsistency mechanism exactly**: a second word enters through prose, spreads, and nobody declares either.

### the failure is the criterion the canon itself teaches, failed live

`case=1` `[t0]` bars *"internal jargon in the ANSWER or the ASK — the part the reader acts on."*

| where the jargon sat | what it cost |
|---|---|
| an example ask, in a demo | one lookup, by a hypothetical human |
| **a priority table, in the deliverable** | **the wisher stopped on it** |

⇒ **so the criterion was not merely violated — it was falsified in the one test that counts**, by the reader the artifact was written for. *"could the human have stopped after the first line and been correct?"* — no.

## .the second answer is better than the first

*"yes, a peer reviewer"* is true and it is not the whole answer. **`reviewer` is itself overloaded:**

| the sense | what it is |
|---|---|
| the **role** | briefs, skills, a rubric contract |
| **one run of one rubric on one guard** | one of several, on one stone, at one iteration |

⇒ `rule.forbid.domain-term-ambiguity` says an overload *"hides an ABSENT DISTINCTION"*, and its repair is a distinct word for the second sense. **`lane` is a candidate for exactly that.**

🟡 **so the fork is: rename to the declared word, or declare the second sense.** the status quo — two words, neither declared — is the one option that is wrong under either rule.
