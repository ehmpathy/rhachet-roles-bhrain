# seed S04 — eliminate friction, still encourage thorough reviews

## .said

> we want to eliminate friction but still encourage thorough self reviews

## .settled

**the north star is a TWO-SIDED objective, and the two sides pull against each other.**

this is the criterion every option in the timer fulcrum is graded against, and it is why a
one-sided answer fails:

| the one-sided answer | which side it drops |
|---|---|
| drop every gate — a promise always clears | thoroughness. the gate reduces to a driver's word |
| hold every promise until the guard is satisfied it was thorough | frictionlessness, and it is unachievable — no gate can read a review |

⇒ so the target is the **frontier**, never either pole: the **most encouragement per unit of
friction**. an option that buys a little thoroughness for a lot of friction loses to one that buys
the same thoroughness for less.

🔴 **the two words are not symmetric, and that asymmetry carries the weight.** *eliminate* is
absolute; *encourage* is not. the ask is to remove friction outright and to **encourage**
thoroughness — never to enforce it. ⇒ a gate that **refuses** a promise to make a driver more
thorough has read the second clause as *"ensure"*, and that is the misread this seed exists to
forbid.

⚠️ **the extant research already grades on this frontier.** `research.selfreview-effectiveness`
names two levers with measured effect — forced articulation (`d = 0.55`) and situational cues
(`d = 0.65`) — and both are **encouragement at near-zero friction**. friction itself appears there
with a design principle and no effect size, bounded by two rows of its own table:
*"target interventions — only confront on repeated rapid attempts"* and *"preserve autonomy —
guide but don't block."*

⇒ **the cheap side of the frontier is already built and is untouched by this wish.** what is under
review is the expensive side.
