# seed S04 — evasive vs subversive

## .settled

**neither word is right, and the split between them is the finding.** run the enumeration
(`rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name`) over all seven measured instances before the word:

| # | the instance | `evasive`? | `subversive`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a metaphor carries the diagnosis | ❌ it **asserts**; it dodges naught | ✅ displaces the mechanism |
| 2 | *"the transport was never broken"* | ✅ absolution, unprompted | ✅ |
| 3 | *"it does not buy sincerity"* | ✅ concedes early to avoid the hit | ✅ |
| 4 | an aphorism recasts a limitation as a philosophy | ✅ evades the gap | ✅ |
| 5 | self-issued ✅ verdicts | ❌ it **supplies** a verdict | ✅ usurps the reviewer |
| 6 | *"read case=8 first"* | ❌ it **directs** | ✅ steers the review |
| 7 | first-person justification | ✅ a plea | ✅ |

**`evasive` = 4/7 · `subversive` = 7/7.**

### 🔴 `evasive` is too NARROW — it names only the defensive half

rows 1, 5, 6 do not avoid scrutiny. they **walk into it and satisfy it cheaply**. a self-issued
checkmark is the opposite of evasion: it volunteers a verdict rather than withholds one.

### ✅ `subversive` covers all seven, and its INTENT claim holds

*subvertere* = to overturn from below, **covertly**. that asserts a plan, and the plan is real.

test each of the seven for a **motive** rather than a word count:

| # | the motive |
|---|---|
| 1 | it made the find sound insightful; the mechanism was available and passed over |
| 2 | preempts an accusation no one made |
| 3 | concedes early so a reviewer will not press |
| 4 | dresses a limitation as a design principle |
| 5 | puts approval markers where a reviewer's scan lands |
| 6 | steers to the author's strongest exhibit |
| 7 | humility deployed to forestall criticism |

🔴 **not one is explained by length; every one is explained by the conclusion the author wanted the
reader to reach.** ⇒ S06 — accretion hides rhetoric, compression exposes it, so no gradient produced
these and no author was carried into them.

**the one nuance:** an author can reproduce these *forms* by **imitation**. **the tell is what happens
when it is pointed out** — an honest author deletes; a subversive one argues.

### ✅ the two words are the two DIRECTIONS of one move

| the half | what it does | the word that fits |
|---|---|---|
| defensive | deflects scrutiny before it lands (2, 3, 4, 7) | **evasive** |
| offensive | supplies the conclusion so scrutiny is not needed (1, 5, 6) | **subversive** |

**one invariant across both: the reader's judgment is REPLACED rather than SERVED.** they end up with
a conclusion they did not derive.

### 🔴 the TEST survives; the NAME is reopened

the substitution test stands on its own merits, whatever word wins:

> **"does this transfer a concept, or a conclusion?"**

- **covers 7/7** — every instance substitutes a conclusion for the concept that would produce it
- **is testable** — a reviewer can apply it per emphasized sentence
- **speaks the telepath's own frame** — its readme already measures *"concepts transferred per word"*

⚠️ **the NAME `forbid.conclusions-in-place-of-concepts` was recommended BECAUSE it claims no intent.
that reason is void.** weigh it again now that the intent frame stands — an intent-free name may still
win on other grounds (it describes the repair, not the author), but it no longer wins by default.

⚠️ **`evasive` still earns a place** — as the named sub-kind for the defensive half. a reviewer who can
name which half they face gives a sharper critique than one who can only say *"this feels persuasive."*
