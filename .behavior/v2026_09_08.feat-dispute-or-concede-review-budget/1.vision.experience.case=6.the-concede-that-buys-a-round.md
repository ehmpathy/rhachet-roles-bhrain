## .the narrative

*driver, on `5.1.execution`, round 4, ergonomist lane, budget spent*

   ├─ with  = ergonomist (l1), 4 rounds, 4 budget — budget spent

🔴 **the SEQUENCE is `concede → fix → budget → re-arrive`, and the ack must render it in that
order** (`S11`: *"concede is to fix and run again … they want to ask for more budget **after** hteir
fixes"*).

| order | what it does |
|---|---|
| 👎 budget, then fix | the grant sits **unspent** through the repair, and the meter reads a round that may never be taken |
| ✅ fix, then budget | the ask lands when it will be **consumed**. a concede the driver abandons costs the ladder naught |

⚠️ **the commands are the same either way; the frame is not.** so `2.1.criteria` owes a case on the
**sequence**, never merely on the command's presence — under `S09` an unspent grant is a level that
reads budgeted while no work was done against it.

🟡 **that order is what a concede MEANS**: a declaration **with work attached**, over a passive
acceptance. ⇒ it keeps the hold because the driver intends to spend a round on the repair.

🔴 **`--peer ergonomist`, never the bare form.** the extant halt already scopes a top-up to the one
lane when exactly one is exhausted — `formatBlockRemedyGroups.ts:83-88` reads the exhausted slugs and
appends `--peer <slug>` at length 1. a concede names its lane in the command, so this cell takes that
same scope rather than re-widens it.

✅ **and `S09` makes that scope the CONTRACT.** a top-up reaches **naught** by default: *"unless a
level is explicitly rewound or budgetted, it should stay|become exhausted."* ⇒ **the bare form is
forbidden here**, not merely wide.

⚠️ **the rule bites hardest one cell over, where this one is silent.** the extant halt appends
`--peer` **only at length 1** and omits it at length ≥ 2 — its own comment says *"several → omit,
since the top-up affects them all"* (`:83-85`). ⇒ under `S09` the multi-lane emit must **enumerate
the exhausted lanes and let the driver pick**, never widen. that is `case=9` §2's bill, and the
verdict makes it a blocker rather than a nicety.

🟡 **and a lane this emit does NOT name stays exhausted, which is the intended outcome** — *"its fine
if l1 becomes exhausted after a conceded on l3."* an l1 lane starved by a top-up aimed at l3 is not
a harm this design owes a remedy for; the halt names it when it exhausts, and the driver may budget
it by name.

🔴 **but it does not take that scope by a CALL.** this ack is `formatRouteStoneEmit`'s action branch,
and `computeBlockRemedyGroups` cannot serve it as written:

| the gate | what a conceded ack meets |
|---|---|
| `:64-66` — `if (!hasBudget && !hasOverrule) return []` | 🔴 `hasBudget` is `reason.includes('budget exhausted')`, and this reason is `conceded review.peer: ergonomist`. `hasOverrule` takes a four-value `passage` union a stance is not in. ⇒ **`[]`** |
| `:79-82` — where the slug list comes from | 🔴 a **regex** over `/budget exhausted:\s*(.+)/`. no match, so `--peer` drops even past the gate |

⇒ **the same three lines that give the stance halt free SUPPRESSION are a WALL for this ack.** the
shape above is therefore a **conformance** claim — this cell renders the halt's scope by its own
construction, never by a call that returns `[]`. the repair is argued at `1.vision.yield.md` § *the
halt formatter this design does NOT reuse*.

**no human is named anywhere in that block.** the extant halt would have rendered *"increase budget
— yours to spend"* beside *"approve as-is — a human must grant"*, and
`rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation` measures exactly what that adjacency costs:

> *"two remedies rendered side by side with no owner column read as two human remedies, and the
> driver's own lever is the one that gets surfaced upward."*

⇒ after a concede the driver has **already declared** it agrees, so the human remedy has no subject.
the block narrows to the one lever the driver owns.

**the aha:** *the top-up was never a favour asked of a human. it is the price of a round the driver
decided it needs — and now the ledger says which rounds those were.*

```gherkin

      and the halt prints dispute and concede
      and it does NOT print "peer reviewer budget exhausted"       # the old halt is superseded

      and the emit prints the TOP-UP as the driver's own lever     # 🔴 F05, acceptance #3 + #5

     then ONLY the ergonomist's budget rises 4 → 6                 # 🔴 route.ts:1853 skips the rest
      and the ergonomist is live again                             # C moves to `live`
      and no human was asked                                       # 🔴 acceptance #5, unchanged from today

[t2b] given the driver drops --peer and runs the bare --add 2
      then every lane on the stone rises 4 → 6                     # ⚠️ the wide form still exists
       and the emit says 4 → 6 for each, so it is loud             # rule.forbid.surprises

[t3] when the driver fixes the copy and runs --as passed
     then the ergonomist EXECUTES                                  # ✅ a conceded lane is not skipped
      and it can CONFIRM the fix rather than leave a stale blocker
      and its new .given is clean, so no stance is owed

[t4] given instead the driver had DISPUTED at [t1]
     when it tops up and re-arrives, with the ARTIFACT UNCHANGED
     then the ergonomist does NOT execute                          # 🔴 case=4 [t2] — same generation
      and the stone proceeds with the blocker on record + a fulcrum
      and the bought rounds are NOT lost — they wait for the next generation
```

🔴 **`[t3]` versus `[t4]` is the whole difference between the two stances, at one coordinate.** a
concede keeps the lane live **this round** and a top-up restores its voice at once; a dispute defers
the lane to the **next generation**, so a top-up buys it no round *now*.

🔴 **`[t4]`'s `given` turns on *"with the ARTIFACT UNCHANGED"*.** a top-up moves no hash, so the
generation is unchanged and the lane stays quiet — **but a driver who tops up *and* edits gets
both**: a new generation, and the rounds to spend on it. ⇒ what parts `[t3]` from `[t4]` is **when**
the lane speaks, never **whether**.

⚠️ **`[t3]` also settles the case `rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation` names as the one
a hard cap strands** — *"a reviewer that spent its round to RAISE a blocker has none left to CONFIRM
your fix."* the concede plus the top-up is exactly the pair that resolves it, and now the ledger
records that the driver chose it deliberately.

## .why it fails safe

**fast** — the emit lands with the declaration; no subprocess · **loud** — it names the lane, the
meter, and the exact command · **teaches the fix** — the top-up command is printed complete, with
the stone bound, so a copy cannot land on the wrong stone.

⚠️ **the one surprise this cell must not cause, and it is narrower than it first reads.** the *bare*
`--add N` extends every lane; `--peer <slug>` does not (`route.ts:1853`). so the emit above is safe
by construction, and the surprise only lands if a driver drops the flag.

🔴 **the level grain is the part that stays open.** the filter is per-**lane**, so a stone whose
ladder has several lanes at l1 still has no way to say *"the latest level only"* —
`updateGuardPeerBudgets` never reads `level:`. ⇒ that is exactly what
`.dream/v2026_09_07.fix.a-budget-top-up-re-arms-every-level-at-once.md` asks for, and it is
untouched by this behavior.
