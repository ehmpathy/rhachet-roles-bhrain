## .the two boundaries only this walk reaches

### boundary 4 · the `.taken` re-read seam — 🎬 **owned by `case=10`**

a reviewer that re-runs stateless re-raises every blocker it raised before, and the driver's fix reads to it as no fix at all.

⇒ `rule.always.converge-with-reviewers.via-a-taken-per-point` is explicit: the reviewer *"re-reads with your articulation AND your diff in context."* so telepath's reviewer inherits a contract it did not author — **its rubric must not assume a cold read.**

🟡 and it inherits the trap `case=4` names: a blanket `--paths-wout '.behavior/**'` would repair boundary 2 and break this one in the same stroke, since the `.taken` trail lives under `.behavior/`. telepath's prose-only scope escapes it only because it keeps `.behavior/` and drops `src/`.

### boundary 6 · the artifact-hash cache seam

the guard caches a reviewer that returned zero blockers, keyed on the artifact hash (`howdoes.the-guard-caches-a-clean-reviewer-by-artifact-hash`).

⇒ that cache is what makes the per-arrival brain cost bearable — the con the yield names in §4. **an unchanged yield pays once, never once per round.**

🟡 but it keys on the **artifact**, never on the **rubric**:

- so a rubric edited after a clean cache entry never re-grades the artifact it already approved
- ⇒ a real seam, caught here rather than settled — see the dream below

## .the fail-safe proof

per `define.experience._.axis=feel.path=happy-vs-sharp`, a sharp critipath owes fast · loud · teaches-the-fix · holds-the-limit · drops-no-data:

| obligation | met at | how |
|---|---|---|
| fast | `[t1]` `[t3]` | both throw at the boundary, before any file is graded |
| loud | `[t1]` `[t3]` | exit non-zero → `malfunction` → the stone blocks (`review.by.ts:336-338`) |
| teaches the fix | `[t1]` `[t3]` | `[t1]` names the enroll command; `[t3]` names the composition and the flag that narrows it |
| holds the limit | `[t7]` | the last valid state — a fitted subject — still renders a real verdict |
| drops no data | `[t4]` | the `.taken` trail survives the **scope narrow**, because the narrowed scope keeps `.behavior/` |
| drops no data | `[t6]` | the **articulated fix** is not dropped — the reviewer reads the `.taken` and treats an answered point as answered |

🟡 the `[t4]` and `[t6]` rows were one row until `i008`, and it carried `[t4]`'s justification under `[t6]`'s label. they are two distinct guarantees about two different things that could be dropped:

| at | what could be dropped | what preserves it |
|---|---|---|
| `[t4]` | the **trail** — the `.taken` files themselves | the narrowed scope keeps `.behavior/`, so the files stay in the subject |
| `[t6]` | the **answer** — the driver's articulation of a fix | the reviewer reads what is in the subject and does not re-raise a point it answers — proven in `case=10` against **10 verified warm reviewer-rounds** (`i006`–`i010`) |

⇒ **`[t4]` without `[t6]` is a trail nobody reads; `[t6]` without `[t4]` is a reader with no trail.** both are needed, and the merged row asserted only the first while it claimed the second's coordinate.

🟡 `[t8]`'s seam is the one obligation this walk does not discharge:

- a stale cache drops no data and fails no test — it silently withholds a re-grade
- ⇒ a `drops-no-data` violation in the weakest sense, and it has no fail-safe today

⇒ caught as a dream rather than settled here: the cache keys on the artifact, and a rubric change is invisible to it.
