# 🌙 dream: one strict argv grammar for the review clis

- kind: followup
- owner: rhachet-roles-bhrain
- caught 2026-10-10

## .the cue that fired

a peer lane (i039 r030 nitpick.2, `enroll-impl-arch-defects`) read `src/contract/cli/review._.cost.ts`
and found a hand-rolled strict argv reader, while `review.ts` and `review.by.ts` each keep their own
unknown-flag refusals and `route.ts` uses the lenient `asCliOptionsOfArgs`.

## .the gap, precisely

- `review._.cost.ts` refuses unknown, stray, repeated, and valueless flags via a three-state
  `FlagRead` (`getOneFlagRead`, `getAllArgsStray`, `getAllFlagsRepeated`, `getAllFlagsUnknown`,
  `isFlagSet`, `FLAGS_VALUED`, `isArgValueOfFlag`)
- `review.ts` and `review.by.ts` refuse unknown flags by their own code, and neither refuses a
  repeated or a valueless flag the same way
- ⇒ a parse rule fixed in one cli reaches no other

## .the shape of the fix

1. lift the reader into `src/domain.operations/cli/` as a pure transformer:
   `(argv, spec: { valued: string[]; bare: string[] }) => { flags } | { faults: FlagFault[] }`,
   plus one renderer of the fault lines
2. `review._.cost.ts` declares its flag spec and renders the faults — no grammar of its own
3. `review.ts` and `review.by.ts` adopt it one at a time; each adoption resnaps that cli's
   acceptance refusal snapshots, and the diff is reviewed as a published contract change

## .why it is not done in this round

SAFE — yes. CLEAN — no: the lift alone has one consumer and shares naught, and the adoption half
rewrites the refusal text of two published clis that every guard lane spawns. that is a contract
change outside the cost-meter route. fulcrum `F213` records the judgment.
