# 🌙 dream: `--as contemplated` clears a halt it knows naught about

- **kind** = fix
- **owner** = `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain` — **both sides of the conflict live here**:
  `.agent/repo=.this/role=any/briefs/rule.require.forward-motion-clears-blocker.md` and
  `src/domain.roles/driver/briefs/rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close.md`
- **caught** 2026-09-07, `5.3.verification` — **reproduced first-hand, by accident**

## .the cue that fired

the stone sat `blocked` by wisher directive. i ran `--as contemplated --that <slug>` five times as
pure record-keeping — the five `.taken` files were already on disk, and the operation only
**validates** them (`setStoneAsContemplated.ts`'s docblock: *"the `.taken` IS the record"*).

then `rhx route.drive` printed no halt at all. it read as a clean stone, ready to arrive.

```
{"stone":"5.3.verification","status":"blocked"}
{"stone":"5.3.verification","status":"contemplated","reason":"contemplated review.peer: repo-rules"}
… ×4 more
```

⇒ **five acknowledgements of five reviewers silently retired a halt that was never about reviewers.**

## .the gap, precisely — it is a rule-versus-rule conflict, not a code slip

the behavior is **deliberate**, and its rule says so outright:

> `rule.require.forward-motion-clears-blocker`: *"there is no `--as` verb that leaves a stale blocker
> in place. every one of them is forward motion, and forward motion supersedes a halt."*

and the driver brief this repo publishes says the opposite, of this exact verb:

> `rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close`: *"a `.taken` does not lift the halt.
> `--as contemplated` discharges a debt to a reviewer; `--as blocked` reports an absent grant.
> **neither is the other's inverse** — so answer points while halted, and do not read that progress
> as permission to re-arrive."*

| | `forward-motion-clears-blocker` says | `raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close` says |
|---|---|---|
| a `contemplated` after a `blocked` | ✅ **clears it** — the driver has moved on | 🔴 **does not clear it** — different subjects |
| what the driver should then do | self-drive; the halt was stale | stay halted; close points **while** halted |

⇒ **a driver who obeys the second rule perfectly triggers the first one.** *"answer points while
halted"* is the instruction, and to follow it un-halts the route.

### the false premise

the clear-on-motion rule rests on one line: *"once the driver takes any next action … the driver is
no longer at that wall."*

**that holds only when the halt is about the driver's own work.** it is false for every halt whose
subject the driver does not own:

| the halt's subject | does a `contemplated` mean the wall fell? |
|---|---|
| a stale escalation the driver has moved past | ✅ **yes** — the rule's intended case |
| an absent credential | ❌ no |
| a wisher directive, or an open wisher call | ❌ no |
| a scope decision the driver may not make | ❌ no |

⇒ **the verb reports progress on ONE axis and the ledger reads it as progress on ALL of them.**

### the sharpest part — the driver brief cites this ledger as its own evidence

`raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close` was written from a measured incident on **this stone**, and
quotes the sequence: `blocked → arrived → blocked → contemplated`.

🔴 **the final `contemplated` in its own worked case is this defect.** the brief read it as the
driver's error and it is also the engine's — and the brief that names the distinction cannot enforce
it, because the ledger has no field for *what the halt was about*.

## .the shape of the fix

the two rules are each right about their own case, so **the repair is a subject, not a winner**:

1. **give a halt a subject.** the `blocked` passage entry carries a `reason` path already; it needs a
   machine-readable owner — `driver` (the halt is mine to clear) vs `human` (a credential, a
   directive, a scope call)
2. **scope the clear.** forward motion supersedes a `driver`-owned halt exactly as today. a
   `human`-owned halt survives every `--as` verb but `--as approved`, `--as overruled`, and a fresh
   `--as blocked`
3. **make `contemplated` and `promised` the narrow case.** both are **sub-step** verbs — neither
   claims the stone advanced, only that one reviewer was answered. ⚠️ they are the two most likely to
   fire while a human-owned wall still stands, since they are exactly what the driver brief tells a
   halted driver to do
4. **reconcile the two briefs in the same change.** `forward-motion-clears-blocker`'s *"there is no
   `--as` verb that leaves a stale blocker in place"* must gain its bound, and
   `raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close` must gain a pointer to the mechanism that now backs it

⚠️ **do not simply exempt `contemplated`.** that trades this defect for the one the other rule was
written against — a stale halt that outlives its own subject, with the statusline waved at a wall
nobody stands at. **the axis is the halt's owner, never the verb.**

## .why it is not done in this round

**out of the wish's fence**, which names the contemplation **gate** and carries an explicit
`.scope — do NOT let these expand it` block. a new field on `PassageReport` is a data-format change
to a file every route on this engine holds on disk — the same migration class `F14` already deferred.

⚠️ and it would be a **second** unilateral act on a route already halted for four wisher calls, one
of which (`F16`) is open precisely because the driver acted on a reserved decision.

⇒ **the state is restored, not left wrong**: `--as blocked` was re-run, so the ledger tells the truth
again. what is deferred is the mechanism that would have stopped the slip.
