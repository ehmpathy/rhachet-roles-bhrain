# research: razors as tactics of skepticism

> scope: verify the 16 held razors, widen the catalog, phrase each as a question put to a claim, propose a structure. method: headful bhrowser session `razors`, plays under `.temp/research.razors/`. `guess:` marks a claim not read at a source.

## .the frame (question-first)

a razor is a question put to a claim. what settles the question is one of three modes:

| mode | what settles it | answer space |
|---|---|---|
| observation | a read of the world, the code, the record, or a count | one answer, once read |
| logic | inspection of the claim itself: its form, its premises, its entailments | one answer, once inspected |
| rivals | a set of named alternatives weighed against the same reads | many answers; the rivals must be named before the shave |

- alder's own text is question-first and is settled by logic, not by a read: "What measurements or observations would, in your view, settle the matter?" the claimant derives the observable consequences; no read is run yet. https://philosophynow.org/issues/46/Newtons_Flaming_Laser_Sword — read: "The first question the Newtonian philosopher asks is: What set of observations do you consider would establish the truth of your claim?"
- popper is two questions: (1) is the claim refutable, which logic settles; (2) was the refuter run, which observation settles. the held row fuses them.
- mode is a property of the question, not of the razor name. a razor can carry two questions of two modes (popper, occam).

| occam | Ockham, c. 1317-23 (Sentences). the razor name comes later: Froidmont 1649. [S5] | which parts of this explanation does the claim assume that a rival would not need? | rivals (needs two or more explanations that fit the same reads) | tie-break only among rivals that fit equally, never between rivals that fit unequally. counter: hickam, chatton, menger, crabtree's bludgeon (R. | hitchens | Hitchens, God Is Not Great, 2007, pp. 150, 258: "What can be asserted without evidence can also be dismissed without evidence." Named a razor first in an Oct 2007 forum. [S4] | what evidence came with this claim? | observation (of what was offered, not of the world) | dismissed is not refuted: it earns no credence and states no counter-claim. fails where the claim has prima facie evidence; shuts talk if both sides invoke it (Rauser); applies to one's own claim too (Antony: atheism). | held-correct (add the dismissed-not-refuted note) |

[S2][S16] | how far does this claim sit from what is already settled, and does the evidence at hand match that distance? | rivals (the settled base is the rival; weigh evidence against it) | "extraordinary" is undefined (Deming); can suppress real discovery (Cardena); a Bayesian view puts a stronger prior on the null. | popper | Popper, Logik der Forschung, 1934 (title page 1935; SEP gives 1935); English 1959. [S15] | (1) what observation, if it came, would show this claim wrong? | alder | Mike Alder, "Newton's Flaming Laser Sword," Philosophy Now 46, May/June 2004, pp. 29-33. | hume, is and ought | Hume, Treatise 3.1.1, 1739-40. [S10] | which value joins this "is" to this "ought"? | logic (inspect the argument for a premise that is absent; the value itself needs an owner) | hypothetical oughts are factual: "to reach B, A ought to do C" can be checked (ethical naturalists). in software work most oughts are goal-relative; name the goal and the gap is closed. | held-correct (add the goal-relative note) |
| chesterton | Chesterton, 1929 book, chapter "The Drift from Domesticity" (the book title trips the gerund hook; see the Wikipedia page). [S6]. read: "If you don't see the use of it, I certainly won't let you clear it away. Go away and think." | do I know why this fence was built, and what would break if it went? | observation (history, blame, tests) plus rivals (candidate purposes) | not a veto: after the use is known, removal is fine. misused as status-quo bias. the fence may have outlived its purpose. | held-correct |
| russell | Russell, "Is There a God?", written 1952 for Illustrated magazine, published only after his death. [S7] | am I the one who asserts? then the proof is mine | logic (assignment of burden by who asserts) | burden is not a fixed side: critics say positive and negative claims split unevenly; the same page has Russell: nobody can prove the teapot absent "but nobody thinks this sufficiently likely to be taken into account in practice", a base-rate step. twin of hitchens. | held-correct (year: add "posthumous") |

[S8] | now that someone aims at this number, does it still track the outcome? | observation (compare the number against an outcome read apart from it) | rivals: real gain vs gamed gain. | base rate | Kahneman and Tversky, "On the psychology of prediction," Psychological Review 80(4): 237-251, 1973. [S12][S13] | how often does this kind of case hold across the whole class? | observation (of the reference class, never of this one case) | reference-class problem: which class? zebras occur; strong case-specific evidence beats a weak prior (Bayes). counter: hickam. | held-correct |
| law of the instrument | Kaplan, The Conduct of Inquiry, 1964: "Give a small boy a hammer, and he will find that ..." (rest read on the page). [S9] | did the tool I hold pick the shape of the problem I see, and what would a cure from another layer look like? | rivals (cures from other layers, named) | the tool at hand is often right. the bias is in the first reach, not the tool. | held-fix: split Kaplan (law) from Maslow (hammer line) |
| feynman | Feynman, "Cargo Cult Science," Caltech commencement 1974. [S11] read: "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool." | where am I easiest to fool, and what test could show me wrong? | observation (a test whose result was set before the look) | self-applied razors cannot audit themselves: needs an outside check (segal's law). | held-correct |

## .proposed structure

sort by what the blade shaves (the shape of the claim), since the role's test is "which razor does this claim's shape invite." tag each razor by mode as a second axis.

| group | what it shaves | razors (held; added marked +) | modes in the group |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. a claim that cannot be tested | claims no read or entailment could reach | popper, alder, hume is/ought, duhem-quine+ | logic first, observation second |
| 2. a claim without owner or weight | proof owed, and how much | russell, hitchens, sagan, cromwell+ | logic (who owes), rivals (how much) |
| 3. a cause among rivals | too many parts, too few, one cause where several hold, a motive where a slip fits | occam, anti-razor, hume proportion, hickam+, hanlon, morgan+, rapoport+ | rivals |
| 4. a choice or frame | options and tools left off the table | false dilemma, law of the instrument, chesterton | logic (false dilemma), rivals (instrument), observation plus rivals (chesterton) |
| 5. a number or a sample | measure, class, filter, source count | goodhart, base rate, survivorship+, segal+ | observation |
| 6. the self | where the seat is easiest to fool | feynman (cromwell and segal point here too) | observation of a pre-set test |

- rationale: the seat meets a claim with a shape (a cause, a number, a choice, an untestable claim, an assertion). a sort by shape matches the lookup the seat runs; a sort by mode does not, because mode splits inside each shape and the seat cannot know the mode before it picks the razor.
- the mode tag still earns its place: it tells the seat what to do next. logic: inspect the claim, no read needed. observation: name the read. rivals: write the rival set first.
- the seat asks three questions in order: which shape? which razor? which mode, so what work?
- groups 1 and 2 fire before the explanation work of group 3: a claim that is untestable or owns no proof needs no cause weighed.
- cross-links: popper and duhem-quine (refute, then ask what failed); occam, anti-razor, hickam, crabtree (a four-way dial of "how many causes"); hitchens and russell (the burden pair); feynman, cromwell, segal (the self pair: no seat audits itself without an outside check).
