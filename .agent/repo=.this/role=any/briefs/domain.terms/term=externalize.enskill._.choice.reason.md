# domain.term.choice.reason: enskill

## .etymology

**`en-` + skill** — same productive prefix as `enbrief` and `entool`: a verb of transformation
into a target form. to enskill is to make a skill of a sequence that lived only in a head, a
scrollback, or a private note.

`skill` is already this org's declared artifact kind (`.agent/repo=*/role=*/skills/`), so the
target form is not a metaphor — it names a real directory, a real boot manifest entry, and a real
invocation path.

## .why not the rejected synonyms

- **document** — the sharpest rejection, and the reason this word exists. to document a procedure
  is to write *prose about* it; to enskill it is to produce a **runnable artifact**. the gap is
  the whole point: a documented procedure still costs a brain a read, an interpretation, and a
  translation into commands. `document` would erase the distinction between a brief and a skill,
  which is the distinction the entoolment ladder is built on
- **write-an-sop** — SOP is **one** of several human aliases for the product (beside *tactic*,
  *guide*, *instructions*), and the say file records all four. but as a verb form it fails twice:
  it is a phrase rather than a word, and it carries corporate-process baggage (sign-offs, revision
  tables) that this act does not. 🟡 **it is also the narrowest of the four** — to name the verb
  after one alias would imply that alias is canonical, which is exactly the over-privilege the say
  file corrects
- **proceduralize** — reads as bureaucratic, and names the *formalization* rather than the
  artifact. it is also silent on the target form, which is exactly what the `en-` family supplies
- **codify** — overloaded to exhaustion, and ambiguous between "write into code" and "write into
  law". a term that reads two ways cannot mark one act (`rule.forbid.ambiguous-labels`)

## .the pair

`enskill` and `entool` are two concepts, not one. the case is settled in
`term=externalize.entool._.choice.reason.md` under *"the pair"* — recorded once, cited here, rather than
restated (`rule.require.timeless-comments`, and seed 375's own lesson about the retell).

the short form: **enskill creates the artifact; entool raises its rung.** a tactic can be
enskilled and never entooled; an unenskilled tactic has no rung to raise.

## .the trigger is the SECOND run, not the first

worth the record because it is the part most likely to drift.

- the rule fires on a **repeat**, never on a premonition
- `rule.prefer.wet-over-dry` applies directly — a sequence run once, enskilled from a guess about
  the future, is a premature abstraction
- ⇒ the second run is **evidence**, and evidence is what the trigger waits for

## .disputes

none. coined 2026-08-13 at the wisher's ask.

## .evidence

- **the wisher's ask (2026-08-13)**: *"entool the skills you touch should be paired with an
  enskill the tactics you discover; i.e., if you have to repeat some common sequence of
  operations, then you should enskill that for future travelers. (e.g., standard operational
  procedure is a common alias for this concept for humans)"* — the repeat-as-trigger comes straight
  from this, and so does the **plural** read of the aliases: the wisher wrote *"**a** common
  alias"*, never *"the"* alias
- **the wisher's correction (2026-08-14)**: *"sop is just one example; a tactic, guide, instructions
  is another"* — SOP had been recorded as though it were the human-side name. it is one of four,
  and the say file now carries the set
- **the parent rule it sharpens:** `rule.always.externalize.tactics.into_skills` already said
  *write the skill*. what was absent was the **cue** (what tells you a tactic exists) and the
  **upper bound** (a written skill is rung 2, not done). enskill supplies the first; entool the
  second
- **invariant:** `enskill` is a verb of THIS org's practice vocabulary, published via `dist/` in
  the learner's booted rules — not generic english
