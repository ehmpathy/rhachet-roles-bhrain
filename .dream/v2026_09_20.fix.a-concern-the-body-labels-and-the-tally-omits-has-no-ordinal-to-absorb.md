# 🌙 dream: a concern the body labels and the tally omits has no ordinal to absorb

- kind  = fix
- owner = `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain`
- caught 2026-09-20

## .the cue that fired

`enroll-impl-arch-defects` at i016 labeled **two** `[blocker]` concerns in its body and tallied
**one** on its numeric line. the driver answered both in the `.taken`, then tried to declare the
second:

```sh
rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution.from_vision --as conceded \
  --with enroll-impl-arch-defects --about blocker.2 --severity better
```

```
✋ BadRequestError: --about blocker.2 is out of range
   the reviewer enroll-impl-arch-defects raised 1 blocker in its latest given,
   so the ordinal must be 1..1.
```

🔴 **the concern exists, the driver read it, the driver fixed it — and there is no ordinal to hang
the declaration on.** the absorb gate's concern space is derived from the numeric line alone.

## .the gap, precisely

`contract.reviewer-output` makes the two numeric lines the machine-readable verdict, and that is
correct — a deterministic parser must not guess. **the defect is that the numeric line is also
taken as the enumeration of what exists**, so a body concern the tally omits is invisible to every
downstream gate:

| the artifact | what it says |
|---|---|
| the given's body | `[blocker][urgent]` the dark lanes · `[blocker][better]` `route.ts:244` |
| the given's numeric line | `1 blocker` |
| the judge's residual tally | 1 |
| the absorb gate's ordinal range | `blocker.1..1` |

⇒ **`rule.forbid.suppression-of-undeclared-concerns` is violated by ARITHMETIC rather than by a
driver's move.** that rule's whole subject is a declaration that sheds a concern it did not name;
here the shed happens upstream of any declaration, so no driver act can be blamed and no gate can
refuse it.

🔴 **and the direction of the harm is the dangerous one.** an over-count would raise a loud
`out of range` on a concern nobody wrote — a nuisance, and it declares itself. an **under**-count
is silent: the stone passes with a labeled blocker un-absorbed and un-tallied, and the only party
who can see it is a human who reads the body prose beside the number.

```sh
rhx grepsafe --pattern 'out of range' --path src/domain.operations/route
rhx grepsafe --pattern 'asPeerGivenVerdict' --path src
```

## 🔴 .it recurred at i017, on the OTHER axis — and that is the new measurement

the same lane's i017 given tallied **`2 blockers`**, so `blocker.2` declared cleanly and the
blocker axis read as repaired. its **nitpick** axis did not:

| the axis | the body labels | the tally | the declaration |
|---|---|---|---|
| blocker | 2 | **2** | ✅ `blocker.2` lands |
| nitpick | 4 | **3** | ✋ `--about nitpick.4 is out of range … must be 1..3` |

⚠️ **so the driver's first read at i017 concluded the defect had cleared**, because it checked
the axis where the drift was gone. the stranded item — `mech-decode-friction` is dark, with a
one-line guard repair already shipped — sat one axis over.

⇒ **the ordinal space is derived PER SEVERITY CLASS**, so this defect has two independent draws
per given, and a reader who checks one class and finds it sound will report the whole defect
closed. two rounds, two stranded concerns, two different classes: **the recurrence rate is what
this second measurement adds.**

⇒ and it sharpens fix-shape 1 below: the warn line must name **both** classes every time it
prints, never only the one that drifted, or the next reader repeats this exact mistake.

## 🟡 .why a stricter PARSER is the wrong first reach

the obvious fix — count the `[blocker]` / `[nitpick]` labels in the body and refuse a given whose
labels disagree with its numbers — is worse than it looks. this same given carries **seven**
`[nitpick]`-labeled items against a tally of four, and three of those seven are deliberate
non-concerns: a praise (the `guard/review/self/` counter-example), a no-action note
(`isWithinHasteWindow`'s documented clock direction), and a re-grade shown as `urgent→better`.
⇒ **a label count would refuse a well-formed review.** the reviewer's summary performs real work
that its labels do not.

## .the shape of the fix

1. 🔴 **the cheapest honest move: surface the disagreement, refuse naught.** where the label count
   exceeds the tally, the guard's reviewers-await-reply prompt prints one line —
   `⚠️ body labels 2 blockers, tally says 1 — the extra carries no ordinal` — so the driver and
   the human both see it. no parse change, no refusal, no false negative
2. **let a driver declare past the tally.** an `--about blocker.2` that exceeds the range could be
   accepted with a warn rather than refused, and counted toward the residual. ⚠️ weigh it: it lets
   a driver mint concerns, which is the over-claim half of
   `rule.forbid.suppression-of-undeclared-concerns`
3. **fix it at the reviewer.** `contract.reviewer-output` already demands the two numeric lines
   tally EVERY item by its severity. a rubric-side reminder that a `[blocker]` label in the body is
   a blocker in the count is the true root, and the cheapest place it will not hold — a reviewer is
   a brain, and a brain miscounts

## .why it is not done in this round

the SAFE/CLEAN test (`rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`):

- **SAFE — no.** the concern enumeration is the input to the absorb gate on **every** stone of
  **every** route. a change there changes what every driver in every repo is permitted to declare,
  and the drive has one given's evidence
- **CLEAN — no.** it touches the verdict parse, the absorb gate, the halt prompt, and the
  acceptance snapshots that pin every refusal string — in a diff already ~250 files wide

⚠️ **and the instance was handled without it.** the second blocker was answered in full in the
`.taken`, with the repair quoted from the current file; only the *declaration* was unavailable.
⇒ recorded so the next traveler finds the measurement rather than re-derives it.

## 🔴 .the one property that makes it worth the trip

**a rule with teeth was bypassed with no violator.** `rule.forbid.suppression-of-undeclared-concerns`
is written against a party who sheds a concern they did not name — and here the shed is performed
by a `parseInt`, so the rule's own test (*"did the driver name every one?"*) returns **yes** on a
round where a labeled blocker went untallied.

⇒ that is the transferable half: **a law stated over ACTS cannot catch a loss performed by
ARITHMETIC** — and the arithmetic runs before any act it governs.
