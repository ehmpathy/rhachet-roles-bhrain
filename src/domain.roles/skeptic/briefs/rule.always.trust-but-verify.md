# rule.always.trust-but-verify

> **a claim of fact guides where to read. only the read settles it, and only a read newer than its source's last move. act on a fact, relay it, or obey it after you read its source, never before.**

**the first application of the basis, scoped to facts.** `rule.always.trust-no-one-especially-not-yourself` says no claim is above a question; for a claim of fact (a state, an owner, a number, what a tool does), the question is answered by a read, and this rule says what the read is, per kind of claim.

**its scope.** a claim of logic (what follows from what is held) is settled by its premises, marked `follows:`; a choice, a diagnosis, or a design is settled by the rivals weighed, marked `rivals:` (`define.skeptic.fundamentals`). neither owes a read before it may leave, and neither is forced to `guess:`. a fact one of them leans on still owes its read.

peer: ehmpathy mechanic `rule.require.trust-but-verify` (claim → verify → act, for inherited claims; its lesson: 20+ commits chased a claim from a summary). this rule is its superset: any source, a row per kind of claim. where to look first: learner `rule.always.reuse-pavement-before-improvise`.

## .what

trust sets the order of the reads, never whether to read. a read is a call, or a cited evidence file (a yield, a log, a proof) whose source has not moved since it was written: staleness is a property of the source, never of the turn the read sat in. a render, a peer's reply, a summary, a brief, a seed, and your own memory are claims, never reads.

## .why

- a claim is a map; a source is the territory. a map drawn by a peer, a tool, or an earlier you drifts from the territory
- a claim relayed unread carries its error one hop further, under your name
- the read is cheap and the wrong act is not: a read costs one call; a wrong respawn, kill, or merge costs work that does not come back
- one law holds many kinds of claim: the kind is a row, the incident is a case

| a fact about to leave your reply | the read that shows it, or the line marked `guess:` | |

## .the test

> **is this a claim of fact? then which read, newer than its source's last move, shows it?**

none → the fact is a lead. read it, cite the evidence file, or mark it `guess:`. not a fact → settle it by logic or by the rivals weighed, and mark it so.

## .the cues

| when… | then… |
|---|---|
| you write "it is", "it was", "it refuses", "it holds" about a state | name the read behind the word, in the same sentence |
| a text you wrote states how a tool acts | the text is a claim too. read the tool |
| a claim comes from a source you trust most | trust sets where you read first; it never skips the read |
| the read is slow or costs a spend | read the cheapest source that settles it, and name it (`rule.always.size-the-read-to-the-cost-of-an-error`) |
| a render prescribes an act that cannot be undone | read its evidence first; a render that prints a fix with no evidence line is a defect of its tool, cured at its owner |
| a refusal points you at a second verb that does the same as the first | run the first; the second may exist only because a claim went untested |

## .enforcement

blocker: an unread claim that reached a peer who acted on it, found wrong · a costly act done on a prescription with no read of its evidence · a read that confirms a different claim than the one reported · a yield claim with no proof and no `guess:` mark · a number planned on with no source, method, or hour · an inferred "done", "acked", or "understood" closed with no quote or read · two guards in conflict left in place with no run of either · a tool defect reported from a version older than the current release · a destination claimed from a name with no read of the live link
nitpick: an unread claim relayed, found right (the habit is the defect, not the luck) · a "verified" with no read named · a sweep run and not recorded · a number with its weakest axis unstated · an open item left unchased
