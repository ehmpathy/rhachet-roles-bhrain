## .the gap, precisely

`rhx route.guard.budget --for review --add N` walks every `budget:` line under `peer:` and adds `N` to
each. the filter it offers is `--peer <slug>` — one lane at a time — so there is **no level grain at
all**: the choice is one lane, or the whole stone.

`updateGuardPeerBudgets` (in `src/contract/cli/route.ts`) tracks `slug:` and `budget:` and
**never reads `level:`**, so the level is invisible to the operation that most needs it.

⚠️ **the line range `:1784-1880` was struck 2026-09-08** — `route.ts` is a file this branch edits, so
the range was fused; the operation name is not, and it is what a reader searches for anyway.

| the harm | how it lands |
|---|---|
| **cost** | the add's price is `N × lanes`, never `N`. every re-armed lane may spend a brain call |
| 🔴 **it re-opens a settled level** | a top-up meant to let l3 confirm a fix hands fresh rounds to five l1 lanes that already reached a verdict |
| **the meter drifts from its subject** | a level's residual rounds stop to track that level's own convergence |

⇒ **a budget is per-lane by construction. the add is the one operation that ignores that.**

## .the shape of the fix

> `--add N` binds the **latest** level by default. any earlier level is prescribed explicitly.

⚠️ **"latest" must mean the highest READY level, never the highest DECLARED one.** if l1 still rejects
and l3 has not been reached, a top-up aimed at l3 helps nobody. readiness lives in
`getAllReviewPeerMeterStatuses`, so the operation must consult the meter rather than the guard text
alone — that is the substance of the change, and the reason it is not a one-line edit.

the ergonomics decide which side gets the bare invocation. `rule.prefer.defaults-match-common-case`:
the common case is **a driver tops up because the level under review needs another round**; a top-up
aimed at an earlier level is the rare, deliberate act, so it is the one that earns a flag. and
`rule.require.safe-by-default`: the blanket form spends more and re-opens settled work, the scoped
form does neither — **the bare call should not be the wide, expensive one.**

## 🔴 .a booted brief states the old behavior and moves with the code

`rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation`, in the driver's `say` tier, says:

> *"budget is not scarce, and to treat it as scarce is the error. `--add N` extends every reviewer on
> the stone at once."*

⇒ **that sentence becomes false the moment this ships.** a brief that describes a lever's blast radius
governs how a driver reasons about cost, so the brief edit (`.md` + `.md.min`) is part of the change,
not a follow-up to it.

## .the coupled change

`.dream/v2026_09_07.fix.a-passed-level-is-re-run-after-the-ladder-moved-past-it.md` would settle a
level once every lane approves. **a blanket add re-arms exactly the levels that dream would settle**,
so to ship either alone leaves a contradiction on disk. the wisher said both in one breath for that
reason.

## .why it is not done in this round

**in scope? no.** this behavior fixes the contemplation gate. the budget CLI and the meter are
untouched by the diff, and a change to either is a wholly separate concern
(`rule.require.review-test-changes`).

**clean? no.** the ripple is measured, not estimated: `updateGuardPeerBudgets` gains a meter
dependency it has never had · the CLI arg parse and the `--help` at `:1984` · the acceptance snapshot
at `routeStoneSetContemplation.acceptance.test.ts.snap:370`, which quotes the command verbatim · the
booted brief in two files.

**safe? no.** every suite that covers this surface is acceptance or integration, and both lanes
strict-gate on four credentials this drive does not hold (the open gate 2 halt). ⇒ an unverifiable
change to the lever a driver reaches for **when they are already stuck** is the worst place to ship
one blind.
