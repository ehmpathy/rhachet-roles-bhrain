# case=1 — the level runs at the speed of its slowest member

**cell** — `driver × multi-unbounded × all-fresh × happy`
**feel · care** — happy · **critipath**

⚠️ these `[tn]` sketches are illustrative, not proof. no code exists yet.

## .the narrative — the driver's pov

i finish the vision artifact. i run `--as arrived`. level 1 holds four reviewers, and today i
watch them file past one at a time: the mechanic rubric takes 35 seconds, then the architect
rubric takes 52, then the ergonomist 28, then the librarian 20. i sit there for two minutes and
fifteen seconds, and for most of it three of the four brains are idle.

then i fix the blockers and run `--as arrived` again. another two fifteen. and again. the wait is
not one wait — it is one wait per round of a convergence loop, and a real stone takes four or five
rounds.

**after**: i run `--as arrived`. all four go at once. the librarian lands at 20s, the ergonomist at
28, the mechanic at 35, and at 52 seconds the architect lands and the level is done. fifty-two
seconds, not one thirty-five. the level costs what its slowest member costs, and that is the
floor — there is no arrangement of four reviewers that could be faster.

the aha is small and it repeats: **the level no longer costs the sum of its parts.** and because
it repeats on every round of every stone, i do not save fifty seconds once — i save fifty seconds
times every round i will ever drive.

## .the bdd sketch

```gherkin
given [case1] a stone whose guard declares four peer reviewers, all at level 1, no cap declared
  and each reviewer sleeps a known duration: 35s, 52s, 28s, 20s
  and no reviewer is cached-approved and no reviewer is exhausted

when [t0] the driver runs `rhx route.stone.set --stone 1.vision --as arrived`

then all four reviewers are in flight at the same moment
  and the wall clock for the level is ~52s — the slowest member
  and the wall clock is NOT ~135s — the sum of the members
  and all four verdicts are returned
  and the returned artifact array is ordered by declared index, not by completion order
```

🔴 **that last `then` is not decoration.** completion order under concurrency is
nondeterministic; the extant suite carries 36 peer blackbox snapshot tests that read the artifact
array. a deterministic merge — collect the level's results, then sort by the reviewer's declared
index — is what keeps those snapshots stable. **a naive collector that appends each result as it
settles** would churn every one of them.

⚠️ **this read *"a naive `Promise.all` that pushes in settle order"* until peer review i018/r2.**
`Promise.all` does no such thing — it resolves its array in **input** order regardless of settle
order, so it is in fact one of the collectors that would **not** churn the snapshots. the defect
belongs to a hand-rolled collector that pushes on completion.

⇒ 🔴 **second instance of this class in three rounds** — i016/r2 caught *"the array holds 1"* in c5,
also impossible under `Promise.all`. both are one habit: **a language primitive named as shorthand
for "the naive way", with no check of what it actually does.** the requirement each was attached to
was correct both times; the mechanism cited was wrong both times, and **a wrong mechanism in a demo
is what an implementer builds against.**

## .the clamp this owes (acceptance 7)

```gherkin
given [case1] the four-reviewer level above
when  [t1] the level executes
then  the observed max-in-flight count is 4
```

⇒ **prove it bites**: revert to the serial loop, re-run — max-in-flight must read 1 and the test
must go red. restore, re-run — 4, green. a timer-only assertion is not enough, since a fast
machine can make a serial run look concurrent; the in-flight *count* is what discriminates.

## 🔴 .the fixture this clamp requires — found in self-review r3

⚠️ **an in-flight count is only observable if a lane is still aloft when the next one starts.** the
extant mock (`blackbox/.test/assets/route-peer-budget-parallel-l1/.test/mock-review-alpha.sh`,
verified) is `echo`-only
and returns in milliseconds — so lane 1 can finish before lane 2 is spawned, and **max-in-flight
reads 1 even under a correct concurrent implementation.**

⇒ that is a flake in the **red** direction here, and in case 2 the identical fact is a flake in the
**green** direction — a broken cap reads clean. **one fixture defect, two opposite symptoms**, which
is why it survived until the clamps were checked against the asset rather than assumed.

**repaired: the mock must DELAY**, per the extant `sleep`-based precedent at
`blackbox/.test/assets/route-peer-review-timeout/.test/mock-review-timeout.sh:14`. ~200ms makes the window real, and stays
cheap enough that case 9's 20-run clamp is seconds.

⇒ case 2 carries the full argument and the `rule.require.clamp-edge-cases` citation.
