## .settled

> **a level that is down is a level to drive PAST, never a wall to halt at.**

the latch already says so as a law — *"if its terminal, l3 is up regardless of what happens to the
l1 reviews"* (`define.invariant.review.peer.level-unlock-is-a-latch`). the directive adds the half
the law never carried: **the driver must be TOLD so, at the moment it meets the malfunction.**

| the law says | the directive adds |
|---|---|
| a malfunctioned level is terminal-for-unlock, so the level above pours | ⇒ so **drive on** — converge the level that poured |
| the latch changes *what runs*, never *what passes* | ⇒ and a stone that cannot pass **yet** is not a stone that is blocked |

### 🔴 what this REVERSES on this route

this stone was marked `--as blocked` at i004 on the ground that three lanes had malfunctioned on one
root cause, and that a re-arrival would mint three fresh malfunctioned givens for an identical
cause. **the arithmetic held and the conclusion did not.**

two of the three were a **newly poured level** — r010 and r011 exist at i004 and at no earlier
iteration. ⇒ the ladder had **advanced**, which is the latch at exactly its job, and the halt read
that advance as a stall.

⇒ **the tell was on the record the whole time and no instrument read it**: a reviewer index that
appears for the first time at this iteration IS a pour.

### 🔴 the defect this exposes in the drive stdout

the halt named the malfunction and named no forward move. a driver that meets *"three lanes
malfunctioned"* with no line that says **the level above is up, go converge it** will read a wall,
because a malfunction looks like a wall from every angle a single-lane view affords.

⇒ so the remedy is not a sharper driver. it is a **render** that states the pour beside the
malfunction, and names convergence as the move.

### 🟡 what it does NOT settle

it says naught about a malfunction at the **top** level, where no pour follows. there the
malfunction still gates passage with no further lane to converge, and a halt may genuinely be
correct. the directive governs the case where a level **did** pour.
