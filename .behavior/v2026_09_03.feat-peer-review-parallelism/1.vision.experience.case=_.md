#### 🔴 the LATCH — a poured level is not re-gated when its predecessor regresses

⚠️ **raised as a nitpick in three peer rounds, and the reviewer was right that the walk named no row
for it.** it is itemized here rather than given an axis value, and the reason is the second bullet
below.

`driver.route.peer-level-unlock-latch.acceptance.test.ts` demonstrates the sequence:

- `[t0]` — l1 **malfunctions**. a malfunction is terminal-for-unlock
  (`isReviewPeerVerdictTerminal.ts`), so l3 opens and pours
- `[t1]` — l1 is repaired and now **rejects**, which is non-terminal again
  - l3 still pours, at `2/5`, beside a predecessor that no longer clears it
  - ⇒ **the unlock is a latch, never a live predicate re-evaluated each round**
- `[t2]` — a **rewind** returns l3 to `awaits l1 terminal`, so the latch has exactly one release

🔴 **it is a property of the RUN, so it would be a C value — and it is not free.** C is *what the
level's members are at run time*; the latch is *what a level's predecessor did on a prior round*, so
it is a fact about the ladder's history rather than about this level's members. an axis for it would
double the grid and add no cell whose behavior differs from its unlatched twin: **the pour is
identical either way**, which is the over-decomposition `howto.experience.decompose` warns of.

⇒ so the honest placement is a **note on the whole driver region**, not a coordinate: at every
`{multi-unbounded, multi-capped, multi-cap-1, multi-mixed} × {all-fresh, mixed-shortcircuit,
member-fails}` cell, the level pours the same way whether its predecessor is terminal **now** or
merely **was** when the gate opened.

⚠️ **what an implementer owes it is a clamp, never a design choice** — the behavior is settled in
`define.invariant.review.peer.level-unlock-is-a-latch`, and the snapshot above is its oracle.

### 🔴 why the 260 carry a VERDICT rather than a bucket label

⚠️ **caught in peer review i016/r1.** this row read `void by the actor split | 260` — and `void` is

**and the four kinds fail differently, which is the whole reason to part them:**

| kind | who can catch it | why it is dangerous |
|---|---|---|

reports full coverage of whatever space it is handed, with whatever verdicts it is handed, against
whatever demos it is told cover them — and reports all three with the same confidence.

axes the walk was given.

## 🔴 .density — which demos are centered, and why

added in self-review r2. the grid above verdicts every cell by **feel × care**, which sets
*severity*. it is silent on **boundary-density**, which sets *priority and scrutiny*
(`define.experience._.metric=boundary-density`). the two are orthogonal, and the earlier draft
applied only the first.

| demo | boundaries crossed in one walk | density |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **c9** — a real convergence loop | **9** — fan-out, cached short-circuit, mid-flight failure, malfunction-unlocks, the gate, the cap, index-across-a-pool-change, 🔴 index-across-a-roster-append, 🔴 mixed-group-valve-leak (`[t5]`) | **highest — centered** |
| c6 — cached and exhausted take no slot | 2 — short-circuit + slot accounting | medium |
| c4 — the gate holds between levels | 2 — the gate + budget untouched | medium |
| 🔴 c10 — a corrupted log trail | 2 — concurrent fs write + the failure-diagnosis path | medium |
| c1 · c2 · c3 · c5 · c7 · c8 | 1 each | spotlights |

⇒ **c9 is the centered demo.** it is the only one that tests how boundaries *interact*, and per the
metric that is both the highest-value walk (it discharges the breadth of every spotlight its map
cites — c1, c2, c4, c5, c6 — and the `multi-mixed` shape in `[t5]`) and the highest-risk one
(defects cluster where boundaries pile up).

⚠️ 🔴 **but c9 does NOT subsume c10, and the reason is worth a line.** **every boundary on c9's map**
is **control-flow** — what runs, what waits, what unlocks, what is indexed. c10's is a **side-effect
on the filesystem**, which a journey through the control flow crosses without ever observation of it:
c9's l1 carries a failed member and cached ones concurrently, so it *causes* the log collision and
its assertions never look at `.log/`.

⇒ **a dense journey concentrates the boundaries it asserts on, not the boundaries it touches.** that
is the honest bound on the density metric, and it is why the reviewer found this hazard rather than
c9 doing so.

⚠️ **the spotlights are not pruned, and must not be.** density *ranks*; it does not *filter*. each
sharp critipath keeps its focused case so its fail-safe stays legible — which is the density
brief's own guardrail: *"dense journeys for breadth, focused spotlights for each sharp critipath's
fail-safe."*

🔴 **the boundaries c9's map marks `(none — new)` are crossed by no spotlight**, because they exist
only at an intersection. that is rows **4, 7, and 7b** — one per line below:

| boundary | why no spotlight reaches it |
|---|---|
| **4** — a malfunction and its co-members' approvals settle **in the same instant**, so the gate can read a torn snapshot | needs failure **and** success concurrent at one level |
| **7** — the reviewer **index** survives a change of **pool** membership between rounds | needs two rounds whose pools differ, which is what a cached member makes |
| 🔴 **7b** — the index survives a **roster append** between rounds | needs two rounds whose **rosters** differ — a strictly different condition, and the only one that catches an index re-derived by a run-time roster scan |

## 🔴 .the gaps

- **no `system` / unattended actor is walked.** peer reviews run only under a driver's
  `--as arrived`; there is no cron that fires them. if that changes, this grid owes a row.
- **cross-level concurrency is not an axis.** the wish scopes the bound to *within* a level, and
  the level gate keeps levels apart. if levels were ever allowed to overlap, `B` would need a
  cross-level partner axis.
- ~~**the `mixed-shortcircuit` + `member-fails` combination is not walked**~~ ✅ **closed in
  self-review r2 by `c9`**, whose l1 carries a cached member (`r5`) *and* a failed member (`r2`) in
  the same round. the axis is still modeled as single-valued, so the combination has no cell of its
  own — but it is now **demonstrated** rather than merely absorbed under `member-fails`.
- ~~**the review-log-directory collision is argued in the yield and verdicted in no cell**~~ ✅
  🔴 **closed by peer review i002/r2, via `c10`.** the reviewer named the exact defect: a path argued
  at length in prose, outside the walked matrix, so the coverage contract neither demoed it nor
  declared it out of scope. it is now demoed, and it sits at c5's coordinate.
- 🔴 **the grid has no axis for a concurrent SIDE EFFECT.** every axis here describes control
  flow — who acts, how many run, what state they are in, whether a bound was declared. a shared
  filesystem path is orthogonal to all five, which is why the log collision had no natural cell and
  had to be filed at the coordinate where its *harm* lands rather than where its *cause* sits. ⚠️ if
  a second such write is ever found, that is the signal this owes a real axis rather than a second
  borrowed coordinate.
- ~~**a bound declared at a level nobody sits at is demoed and verdicted nowhere**~~ ✅ 🔴 **closed by
  peer review i004/r2**, which found the state mandated by c3 `[case3]`, repeated in the yield's
  edge-case table, and absent from every axis. axis B gained `phantom`; the state is now
  **forbidden** at `phantom × {consistent, conflicted}`, the **third** parse-time invariant.
- ~~**a level that mixes a bounded group with an ungrouped lane has no axis value**~~ ✅ 🔴 **closed by
  peer review r5 blocker.1**, which found `[t5]` of the journey test demoed a shape (`multi-mixed`)
  that no B value described. c9 now bears two demoed cells at that value (author and driver), and
  the F13 advisory is the sharp edge that the `consistent × multi-mixed × sharp` cell names.
- ~~**the never-re-gated latch is exercised by a snapshot and named by no row**~~ ✅ 🔴 **closed by
  itemization, under the driver table** — `#### the LATCH` above. it is a fact about the ladder's
  **history** rather than about a level's members, so it is a note on the whole driver region rather
  than a C value: an axis for it would double the grid and add no cell whose pour differs.
- ~~**an operator dial reaches a bound axis E does not model**~~ ✅ 🔴 **closed by an explicit
  out-of-space declaration**, in `dimensions.md` under `#### E models the .guard only`.
  `RHACHET_LEVEL_CONCURRENCY` is set by an **operator**, and A walks three actors — author, driver,
  onlooker. ⚠️ **so the honest close is a declared partition, never a fifth E value**: the dial's
  effect lands at B's `multi-capped`, the same coordinate a written bound lands at, and what differs
  is *who set it* — which is A's question. **if a second dial appears, A owes its fourth actor.**

🔴 **three of these gaps were closed by a new AXIS VALUE, and all three were the SAME defect: an axis
bounded by the demos I had already written.** axis E listed the declaration states c7 demonstrated;
axis B listed the level shapes c1–c6 used; and `multi-mixed` was demoed by `[t5]` before it held an
axis value. ⚠️ **an axis derived from its own demos cannot surface a cell those demos missed** —
which inverts what the walk is for, and is why all three omissions survived self-reviews and were
found from outside.

🔴 **two more were closed WITHOUT one — the latch and the operator dial — and that asymmetry is the
useful half.** all five arrived the same way (a committed snapshot exercising a behavior no row
named), so the backward check of `rule.require.experience-catalog-evolution` found every one. what
parts them is the answer to *"does this add a cell whose behavior differs?"*:

| the gap | a new cell? | so the close is |
|---|---|---|
| `unrecognized` · `phantom` · `multi-mixed` | ✅ each has a pour or a refusal its neighbours do not | an **axis value** |
| the latch · the operator dial | ❌ each pours identically to a cell already walked | a **declared itemization** |

⚠️ **the drift the rule names is an unnamed experience, never an unwalked axis** — so a declaration
that says *where the behavior sits and why it needs no coordinate* discharges it as fully as a new
value does. to have added an axis for either would have doubled the grid to buy no distinction, which
is the over-decomposition the method warns of on the other side.

⇒ the standing check this leaves for later stages: **when a new demo is written, ask what axis value
it needed that no extant demo used.** that question is answerable at the time of writing, and it is
the one the walk cannot ask on its own behalf.

⚠️ **that closure is itself an argument for the density lens.** the gap was declared, accurate, and
sat unaddressed through the whole first pass — and the dense journey closed it as a side effect of
many boundaries crossed at once, which is precisely what the metric predicts a dense walk does.
