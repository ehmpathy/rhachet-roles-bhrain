## 🔴 .why this case exists — the other eight are spotlights

cases 1–8 each exercise **one** boundary in isolation. that is what a spotlight is for, and each
sharp critipath needs one so its fail-safe stays legible.

but per `define.experience._.metric=boundary-density`, a vision of only spotlights is **thin**:

> *"defects cluster where boundaries pile up. the sparse single-boundary path rarely surprises; the
> dense journey is where the vision's vague prose gets exposed."*

⇒ this case is the dense journey. it crosses **every boundary in the map below** in one realistic two-round
convergence loop, and it is the only artifact here that tests how those boundaries **interact**.
the guardrail is honored: it does not replace the spotlights, it sits beside them.

## ⚠️ .boundary 7 said "membership change" and meant POOL, never ROSTER

🔴 **peer review i008/r1 caught the imprecision, and it is a real one.** the five declared reviewers
are identical across both rounds; what flips is r5's cache state. so the reviewer's objection lands:
*"the roster is unchanged and the declared-array positions cannot shift."*

the two are different quantities and only one of them moved:

| what | round 1 | round 2 | changes? |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **roster** — the declared `reviews.peer` array | 5 | 5 | ❌ **no** |
| the **pool** — the reviewers that actually enter and run | 4 (one cached) | 5 | ✅ **yes** |

⇒ **the demo is still a real drift condition, and a narrower one than the label claimed.** an index
re-derived from **pool position or settle order** runs `1..4` in round 1 and `1..5` in round 2, so
r5 lands on a different number and r2 may too. that is exactly the silent failure this boundary
exists to refuse. what the demo does **not** prove is stability under a roster change, because the
roster never moves.

⚠️ **so the boundary is renamed to what it proves, and `[t7]` is added for the roster case** — the
reviewer offered those as alternatives; both are taken, because they are different conditions and
each catches a different wrong implementation.

| a wrong index derived from… | caught by |
|---|---|
| **settle order** | `[t6]` — the pool changes size, so the ordinals shift |
| **pool position** | `[t6]` — same reason |
| **a re-scan of the roster at run time** | 🔴 `[t7]` — only a roster change moves this one |

🔴 **and the roster case has a bound this file must state.** `index: i + 1` is the declared array
position, so an **append** is safe and an **insert** is not: to add a reviewer at the head shifts
every subsequent index, which would misroute cached reviews and the exhausted-reviewer artifact glob.
⚠️ **that hazard is extant today and is untouched by this wish** — it is a property of the serial
loop's own index assignment, and a driver hits it the moment they reorder a guard's `peer` list.
it is named here so a reader knows the boundary of the claim rather than infers a guarantee this
vision does not make.

## .the narrative — the driver's pov

i arrive on a stone with five reviewers at l1 (unbounded) and two at l3 (`concurrency: 1`).

l1 goes wide. four of the five actually launch — the fifth was approved on this exact artifact
hash last round, so it is cached and never takes a slot. that already feels right: **the pool holds
work, not roster entries.**

then one of the four dies. a credential expired; its process exits non-zero with no parseable
verdict. the other three stay aloft and land normally — nobody is cancelled for a co-member's
failure.

⚠️ **an expired credential is the demo's cause; contention is the likelier real one.** per
**fulcrum F7**, `DEFAULT_REVIEW_TIMEOUT` is a **wall-clock** `PT21M` calibrated while a lane ran
alone. four lanes that share a machine and a provider quota each run slower, so a lane can trip
that bound and exit with no verdict — which is the same `malfunction` this walk demonstrates.

⇒ **that matters for how often `[t3]` fires.** with only genuine breakage as the cause, boundary 4
is rare. with contention as a cause, **the feature's own fan-out makes it routine** — and the
consequence is a spend at l3. the demo is written against the credential because it is easier to
rig; the hazard it proves is the contention one.

now the part i did not expect, and the reason this walk earns a file. **l1 is `terminal`** — three
approved, one cached-approved, one `malfunction` — and malfunction is terminal for unlock. so
**l3 opens and spends opus budget**, on an artifact one of whose l1 lenses never actually looked at
it. the stone still cannot pass, because a malfunction blocks passage. so i have paid for the
expensive level and gained a stone that is still blocked.

i fix the credential and arrive again. the fifth reviewer is now **fresh**, not cached — so **the
POOL took a member it did not take last round**. the **ROSTER** is untouched: the declared
`reviews.peer` array held five in both rounds, and what must not change is which reviewer is `r5`.

⚠️ **the two words are not interchangeable here, and this line fused them until peer review
i023/r1** — it read *"the roster membership that entered the pool changed"*, which tells a reader
the roster changed. it did not; only the pool did. ⇒ `## ⚠️ .boundary 7 said "membership change" and
meant POOL, never ROSTER` **above** parts the two deliberately, and this narrative re-merged them a
few paragraphs later — which undid the very section that precedes it.

## 🔴 .the interaction the spotlights cannot see — `[t3]`

case 4 proves the gate holds. case 5 proves a malfunction does not cancel co-members. **neither
asks what the gate does when a malfunction and three approvals settle in the same instant.**

verified in code — `isReviewPeerVerdictTerminal.ts:18-21`:

```ts
verdict === 'approved' || verdict === 'malfunction' ||
verdict === 'constraint' || isReviewPeerVerdictExhausted(verdict);
```

with the `.note`: *"broken reviewers should not block tier escalation (l2/l3 can run)."*

⇒ **so the behavior is declared and correct: l3 opens.** this case does not propose to change it.
what it demands is that the gate reads a **consistent snapshot** — that it evaluates once, after
the whole group has settled, never on partial state mid-flight.

⚠️ **the concurrency-specific hazard is a torn read.** under the serial loop the gate could only
ever see fully-settled members. under a naive concurrent port it can be evaluated while the
malfunction has landed and an approval has not — and `l1 = {malfunction, queued, queued}` is
**not** terminal, so l3 would *not* open. the same guard, same inputs, a different answer per the
scheduler's mood.

🔴 **that is the worst class of defect this wish can ship: a nondeterministic gate on a
money-spending decision.** it would reproduce roughly one run in n, and the symptom — *"l3
sometimes runs and sometimes does not"* — reads as flake rather than as a race.

⇒ this is the observable that makes the yield's *"gate between groups, never inside"* structural
answer **checkable** rather than merely asserted.

## 🔴 .the second interaction — `[t6]`, identity across a change in POOL membership

round 1's pool holds 4 reviewers (one was cached). round 2's pool holds 5. ⚠️ **the roster holds 5
throughout** — see the boundary note above for why that distinction matters and what `[t7]` adds.

self-review r1 established that `index` is a **durable identity key**, not a display ordinal —
it is the `r$index` in the review artifact filename, and the cache key inside
`getCacheSafePeerReviewArtifact`.

⇒ **so a change in POOL membership between rounds is exactly the condition under which a settle-order
index would drift**, and its failure is silent: a clean cached review credited to the wrong reviewer
means **a review that never ran reads as approved** (`rule.forbid.failhide`).

## 🔴 .re-verified after the #466 rebase — the claim holds, and upstream added a backstop

the rebase renamed the recovery path (`…ForIndex` → `…ForSlug`) and routed every cache read through
`getCacheSafePeerReviewArtifact`, which **still keys on index** and now checks the cached artifact's
slug agrees before it reuses one.

| what changed | what it means for this case |
|---|---|
| the recovery glob is now **slug**-keyed | the filename still carries `r$index`; the *lookup* no longer depends on it |
| the cache is **still index**-keyed | ✅ **`[t7]`'s requirement is unchanged** — an index that drifts still mis-keys |
| a slug/index **mismatch is now discarded** | 🔴 the silent failure becomes a **needless re-run** rather than a false approval |

⇒ **the requirement stands and its stake falls.** `[t7]` still asserts index stability across a pool
change — but a breach now costs a budget round rather than a review that never ran. that distinction
is stated plainly here, because a clamp written against the old stake would over-claim what it
protects.

no spotlight covers this, because it needs two rounds with different pool membership — which is
what a convergence loop is and a single demo is not.

then  [t6] 5 reviewers enter the l1 pool where 4 entered before      # boundary 7
      — the POOL changed size; the ROSTER did not
  and r5 is still index 5, and r2 is still index 2
  and r2's artifact lands at `…r002._.given.by_peer.…`
  and no reviewer is handed another reviewer's cached review
  and 🔴 an index derived from settle order or pool position would
      have run 1..4 then 1..5, so r5 would move

given [case2] the same stone, with a SIXTH reviewer appended to `reviews.peer` at l1
when  [t7] the driver arrives again                                  # boundary 7b

  and r1..r5 keep the indexes they held before the append
  and each of r1..r5 still resolves its own prior artifact by `r$index`

## 🔴 .the clamp this owes — and it is the sharpest one in the vision

```gherkin
given [case1] the guard above, with r2 rigged to fail and r5 pre-cached
when  [t0] the pass runs 20 times
then  l3's meter reads the SAME round count all 20 times
  and every run lands r2's artifact at the r002 path
```

⇒ **prove it bites**: move the gate evaluation inside the concurrent region (the naive port) and
re-run. the l3 round count varies across the 20 runs — red. restore the between-groups gate —
20 identical — green.

⚠️ **the repeat count is the point.** a single-run assertion cannot catch a race; it catches the
scheduler's mood on one afternoon. this repo already holds the mechanism for exactly this —
`rule.require.repeatable-for-llm-tests`' `when.repeatably({ attempts, criteria: 'EVERY' })` — and
`EVERY` is the right criterion here, since **one divergent run out of twenty is the defect**, not
noise to be averaged away.

### 🔴 the cost, verified — and the fixture defect the verification exposed

**the cost claim holds, and it is now checked rather than assumed.**
`blackbox/.test/assets/route-peer-budget-parallel-l1/.test/mock-review-alpha.sh` was read: it is
`echo`-only, no sleep,
milliseconds per lane. so 20 runs of a mocked guard is seconds. **no real brain is spent.**

⚠️ **but the same fact broke `[t1]`'s overlap assertion.** a lane that returns in milliseconds can
finish before its co-member is spawned, so *"all 4 in-flight windows overlap"* can read false under
a correct implementation — and `[t4]`'s *"the two windows do not overlap"* can read true under a
broken cap. per `rule.require.clamp-edge-cases`, the second is the worse of the two: a clamp that
passes under the un-fixed defect **has no teeth**.

**repaired: the mock delays ~200ms**, per the extant `sleep` precedent at
`blackbox/.test/assets/route-peer-review-timeout/.test/mock-review-timeout.sh:14`. ⇒ 20 runs × ~1s is still **seconds, not
hours**, so the cost argument survives the repair — which is why the tension was resolvable rather
than a reason to cut the repeat count.

⇒ cases 1 and 2 carry the full argument; this walk inherits it, and is the case that pays for it 20
times over.

## .what this case does NOT do

- it does **not** replace cases 1–8. per the density brief's guardrail — *"dense journeys for
  breadth, focused spotlights for each sharp critipath's fail-safe"* — a boundary this journey
  merely *brushes* still owes its spotlight, and each of those already exists
- it does **not** propose to change that a malfunction unlocks the next level. that is declared,
  and correct. it demands only that the decision be deterministic
- it does **not** demonstrate the render. case 8 owns that, and to fold it in here would bury the
  load-critical moments this walk exists to expose
