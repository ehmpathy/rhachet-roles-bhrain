# domain.term.choice.reason: shallow

## .etymology

plain english, and chosen for it. `shallow` names a **depth** — a surface, with naught to reach
beneath it. that is exactly the defect:

- the sentence exists
- it is grammatical
- ⇒ and no substance sits under it

the wisher named the word directly, 2026-09-06, over the alternatives on the table:

> i preffer shallow and squishy

⇒ so it is a coinage by ruling, and its etymology is the ruling. the candidates it beat are tabled
below with the case each lost on.

## .the candidates, and why `shallow` won

| candidate | the case for it | why it lost |
|---|---|---|
| `empty` | states the absence outright | reads as *"few words"*. a shallow passage is frequently long, so the word would send readers on a hunt for a cut |
| `vacuous` | precise, and a term of art in logic | the same volume misread, plus a register that reads as an insult |
| `filler` | vivid, and immediately grasped | names a **role in the document** — text placed to occupy space. shallow prose is usually written sincerely, so the word imputes a motive the grade must not |
| `fluff` | short, and in common speech | same defect as `filler`, plus a volume claim |
| `insubstantial` | the most literal gloss | four syllables against two, and it composes badly: `subversive.insubstantial` reads as a sentence rather than a coordinate |

⇒ **`shallow` is the only candidate that names the axis (depth) with no claim about volume or
motive**, and both of those are claims this grade must not make.

## .the evidence — why the half the old rule already caught still earns a word

the coarse test (*"could a reader disagree with this?"*) already caught every shallow shape, and
that is exactly what made the gap invisible: the rule appeared to work.

| prose | the coarse test | the observation test |
|---|---|---|
| *"the review flow has some issues worth a look"* | **catches it** | catches it — shallow |
| *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | **passes it** | catches it — squishy |

⇒ so `shallow` is the half the extant rule already covered, and it earns its own word only because
its **repair differs** from its peer's. that is stated here rather than assumed, because a term with
no repair distinction behind it would be a synonym of its root and forbidden.

## .disputes

none open.

## .the invariant

> **a shallow grade must be accompanied by a cut, never by a rewrite request.**

if a reviewer grades a line `shallow` and then asks the author to *state the mechanism*, one of two
things holds: the line was `squishy` and the sort was wrong, or the reviewer knows a mechanism the
line never carried. either way the grade is not yet correct.
