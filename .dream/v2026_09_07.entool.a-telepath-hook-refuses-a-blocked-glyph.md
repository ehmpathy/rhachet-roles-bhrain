## .the shape of the fix

add a glyph class to the blocklist, beside the word class:

| the term | the alt to print |
|---|---|
| `⚠️` | `🟡` — the declared caveat mark, and Wide |
| `·` | a real separator — a period, a semicolon, a bullet, or a table column |

and the `why` line per entry, which is the part a word-shaped blocklist entry cannot carry:

> **`⚠️`** — its base is `U+26A0`, `East_Asian_Width=Neutral`, plus a `U+FE0F` selector. a terminal
> reserves **one** cell and an emoji font paints **two**, so every column to the right of it shifts.

> **`·`** — `U+00B7`, and it carries no grammar. it reads as a list separator, so a reader who meets
> it between two full clauses applies the wrong parse and must re-read to learn which one held.

⇒ **one hook, one entry class, two glyphs.** they arrive together because the failure is identical:
a rule-tier forbid is rung 1 and fires only for an author who recalls it, and in both cases the

🟡 **the two need different predicates, and only one is mechanical.**

- `⚠️` is settled by a UAX #11 class lookup — no list is owed, and the hook could refuse **any**
  width-ambiguous glyph
- `·` is a literal, and a blanket refusal is the honest read of *"a hook that blocks that character"*
  - it costs the extant list-separator use across this corpus
  - ⇒ that conform is **not priced here and not in scope**; the hook binds new writes
