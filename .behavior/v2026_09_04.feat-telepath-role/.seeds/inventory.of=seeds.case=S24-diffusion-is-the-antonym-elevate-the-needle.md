# S24 · `diffusion` is elucidation's antonym — and `obfuscation` STRADDLES, so keep all three

## .said

verbatim, 2026-09-04, mid-drive at `1.vision`, across three utterances:

> ok, so maybe instead of obfuscation, what we really mean as the antonym of elucidation is diffusion? i.e., forbid the diffusion of purpose among prose. dont loose the needle in a haystack. instead, elucidate the needle. elevate it.

> and we should ALSO have a separate rule to forbid obfuscation which straddles the subvsersive and diffusive boundary.

> the more the better here. as many angles as it takes to forbid this nasty speech pattern

## .settled

**three rules, not one.** `forbid.diffusion` clamps `require.elucidation`; `forbid.obfuscation`
stays as a **third** rule that straddles the seam between it and `forbid.subversive-prose`.

⇒ **the wisher reversed my counter.** I argued `obfuscation` collided with `subversive-prose` and
should be dropped. the settlement is that the collision is **not a defect — it is the point**: a
straddling rule catches what falls between two clean ones.

### the mandate: OVER-cover, on purpose

> *"the more the better here. as many angles as it takes to forbid this nasty speech pattern"*

⇒ **redundancy across rules is DESIRED here**, which inverts the default the canon carries
elsewhere (`declare-once`, `forbid.domain-term-synonyms`).

🟡 **the reconciliation, and it is a real distinction:**

| the artifact | its law |
|---|---|
| a **claim** in a document | declared once. a second copy drifts |
| a **cue** in a rule | as many as it takes. a missed cue catches naught |

⇒ `declare-once` governs **facts**; this governs **detectors**. a defect this costly earns
overlapped detectors, and an overlap is a second chance rather than a duplicate.

### the three, and the seam each holds

| rule | what it grades |
|---|---|
| `forbid.subversive-prose` | a claim a reader **cannot dispute** — falsifiability |
| `forbid.diffusion` | a point that **cannot be found** — findability |
| `forbid.obfuscation` | the **straddle**: prose that is disputable and findable, yet still conceals |

### diffusion's subject is the diffusion of PURPOSE, not of words

> *"forbid the **diffusion of purpose** among prose"*

⇒ that qualifier is the whole term. it is **not** *"too many words"* — a three-line passage diffuses
if it spreads its purpose across three rival points.

### the needle is PRESENT — that is what parts it from every peer

> *"dont loose the needle in a haystack"*

| the failure | the reader's state |
|---|---|
| the needle is **absent** | the point was never made |
| **diffusion** | the point **is there** and cannot be found |

⇒ so it grades **findability**, never presence. an author who states the point once, truly, and
surrounds it with six equal-rank facts has diffused it — and every per-sentence rule passes.

### the repair is a verb, and the wisher named it: ELEVATE

> *"instead, elucidate the needle. **elevate it.**"*

the needle is **raised out of** the haystack — promoted to a header, a lead line, a bolded claim, a
parent bullet with the rest as its children.

🟡 **that is not a cut.** the haystack may stay; the needle must rise above it. so the repair is
distinct from `condensation`'s, which cuts the haystack down.

### why `diffusion` is the better CLAMP, even though `obfuscation` survives

| | `obfuscation` | `diffusion` |
|---|---|---|
| claims **intent**? | yes — deliberate | ❌ **no. it is passive** — prose diffuses by default |
| easy for a clone to recognize? | 🟡 demands an intent judgment | *"where is the point?"* — a page scan |

⇒ so `diffusion` takes the clamp slot on the wisher's own S23 criterion, and `obfuscation` earns a
slot of its own for the deliberate case the other two miss.

## .landed

- `rule.forbid.diffusion` (say) — the clamp on `require.elucidation`
- `rule.forbid.obfuscation` (say) — the straddle, third
- `term=prose.diffusion` — owed. adopted, never coined: *diffuse prose* is an extant term of
  art in prose criticism
- `term=prose.obfuscation` — owed
- diffusion completes a physics pair the glossary already holds: `con- + densāre` (a
  gather-together) against `dis- + fundere` (a pour-apart)
