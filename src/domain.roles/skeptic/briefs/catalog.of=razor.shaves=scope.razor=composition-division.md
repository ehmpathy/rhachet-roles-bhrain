# razor: composition and division

shaves: a claim at the wrong level (a part stated as the whole, or a whole stated of a part) · settled by: logic (name the trait; is it additive, or does it emerge?)

## .the question

> **is a trait of the parts stated of the whole, or a trait of the whole stated of a part, and does that trait transfer?**

two directions, one blade. the plain form of "the forest from the trees": each test passes ≠ the suite passes; the team ships fast ≠ each member ships fast.

## .the source

- classical; "the fallacy of composition is the converse of the fallacy of division"
- division's statistical case is the ecological fallacy: W. S. Robinson, "Ecological Correlations and the Behavior of Individuals", *American Sociological Review* 15(3): 351-357, 1950. by state, illiteracy and the foreign-born share correlated at -0.53; by person, at +0.12
- read via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacy_of_composition, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_fallacy

## .fails when

- the trait does transfer (mass adds; every part steel → the whole steel). the test is per trait, never per form
