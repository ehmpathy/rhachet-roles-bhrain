# domain.term: squishy

term.chosen   = squishy
term.kind     = adj
term.boundary = prose.subversive
term.synonyms.forbidden:
- unjustifiable
- unfalsifiable
- slippery
- rhetorical

## .what
`squishy` is **subversive prose that asserts a position no observation reaches** — it conforms to
whatever the reader already wants, so every reader finds their own view in it and none can check it.

> **the repair is a REPLACEMENT.** state the mechanism the sentence stood in front of. a cut alone
> would delete a slot a settleable claim was owed.

## 🟡 .`unjustifiable` is a GLOSS, never a contract synonym

it states the defect precisely — no justification reaches the sentence. it also reads, in plain
english, as **morally indefensible**, so a grade that used it would charge the author rather than
grade the prose.

⇒ `squishy` is canonical. `unjustifiable` may appear in an explanation and never in a contract.

## .the sub-test

> **substitute the sentence for the mechanism it describes. if the reader can no longer act on it,
> the sentence displaced rather than explained.**

## .the demo

| 👎 squishy | 👍 the mechanism it stood in front of |
|---|---|
| *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStoneGuardReviewPeerUncontemplatedUnforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* |
| *"the engine guarantees it happens; the reviewer judges if it was real"* | *"the engine does not check whether the review was read. that gap is unfixed"* |
| *"I judged it sufficient and took it"* | *"I took it at 70%. the counter is that `reviewer` is itself overloaded, and it is real"* |

## .the shapes that sort here

invented metaphor · aphorism · humility-as-advocacy · retreat clause — enumerated in
`rule.forbid.subversive-prose`'s table, which owns the sort.

## 🟡 .squishy is the DANGEROUS shape, and the reason the sort exists

shallow prose reads as filler, so a reviewer cuts it on sight. **squishy prose reads as insight** —
the author writes it and feels lucid, the reader nods, and the mechanism is never stated.

⇒ and the coarse test *"could a reader disagree?"* passes it whole, because two readers can
disagree over a metaphor forever and no observation settles them.

## .why each synonym is forbidden

| word | why not |
|---|---|
| `unjustifiable` | a second sense in plain english — *morally indefensible* — so it charges the author |
| `unfalsifiable` | a term of art from philosophy of science, and it excludes the case where an observation exists and the author simply did not name it |
| `slippery` | grades the author's intent |
| `rhetorical` | names a **register**, and the bound is ambiguity — a metaphor beside a stated mechanism is legal |

## .the bound

the defect is **ambiguity**, never a rhetorical register.

| a violation | not a violation |
|---|---|
| a metaphor that **replaces** the mechanism | a metaphor that **accompanies** a stated mechanism |
| a claim with no observation that would settle it | a claim that runs long — terse is not thin |
