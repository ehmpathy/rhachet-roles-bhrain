# domain.term.choice.reason: obfuscation

## .etymology

latin *obfuscare* — *ob-* (over) + *fuscare* (to darken), from *fuscus*, dark. **to darken over: to
make an object hard to perceive, with the object itself untouched.**

that last clause is the whole reason the word was taken:

| the latin sense | the prose sense |
|---|---|
| the object is unaltered — only its perception is darkened | every sentence stays true and checkable |
| the dark is applied, by an agent | a selection is chosen; it does not arise |
| you cannot repair it by a change to the object | you cannot repair it by an edit to what is written |
| the repair is to let the object be seen | the repair is to **supply what was withheld** |

⇒ **it is the only word in the boundary whose repair is an ADDITION**, and the etymology is why.

## .the rejected synonyms

| word | why rejected |
|---|---|
| `omission` | grades the absence alone, and most absences are correct — a cut below the 95% bar is an omission and is right. the word would fire on `condensation`'s every legitimate act |
| `concealment` | close, and it requires a concealer. the rule fires on the artifact, never on a verdict about the author: shown what was omitted, an honest author adds it and a concealed one argues each word was true |
| `spin` | imports a distortion that is not there. spin implies the stated words were bent; here they are exact, and that exactness is the mechanism |
| `misdirection` | names the effect on the reader rather than the act on the page, so it cannot be checked against an artifact. and it imports intent, as `concealment` does |

🟡 `omission` was the near miss, and it is the one to record — it is the word a reader reaches for first.

- it fails on a single test: *did the absence change what the reader would DO?*
  - no → condensation, and correct
  - yes → this
- **`omission` cannot ask that question**
  - ⇒ so it would grade the 95% bar a defect, and delete the rule that earns the canon its brevity

## .disputes

### dispute: `concealment` — raised 2026-09-04 — status: RESOLVED (keep `obfuscation`)
- raised.by  = the author, mid-draft
- claim      = `concealment` is plainer english and states the act more directly
- counter    = it makes **intent** part of the definition, so a reviewer must judge the author to
               grade the artifact. `obfuscation` also claims intent by common use, but the rule can
               and does bound it to the artifact — and a reviewer who cannot read intent still has a
               checkable test. `concealment` offers no such bound
- resolution = keep `obfuscation`; record `concealment` as a forbidden synonym

## .evidence

### the wisher forced a THIRD rule where two already covered the family

> `S24` — *"the more the better here. as many angles as it takes to forbid this nasty speech pattern"*

the case to drop this rule — that it collides with `forbid.subversive-prose` — was argued and
refused. the settlement is the demo of `rule.require.a-cue-is-not-a-claim`:
**two cues cannot disagree with each other.** they can only both miss, or one catch — so a redundant
detector costs one line and a missed one costs whatever the defect costs.

### the seam that makes it a third word rather than a stricter second

| the rule | why it does not fire on this defect |
|---|---|
| `forbid.subversive-prose` | grades the sentence. each shape here is fully disputable, so not one of its ten shapes fires |
| `forbid.diffusion` | grades the gradient. each shape here has one — the decoy needle is *elevated*; that is what makes it work |

⇒ **so the two clean rules pass this prose, and that is what makes it the most dangerous of the
three** — it survives a careful review by an author who applied both.

### the shape that proves the repair inverts

the *decoy needle*: a real result elevated so a larger one stays below.

> 👎 *"the reviewer costs $0.013 per run"*
> 👍 *"the reviewer cannot enforce 2 of the 10 issues. it costs $0.013"*

the first sentence is true, checkable, elevated, and disputable. **every peer rule passes it**, and
the repair adds a clause rather than cuts one.
