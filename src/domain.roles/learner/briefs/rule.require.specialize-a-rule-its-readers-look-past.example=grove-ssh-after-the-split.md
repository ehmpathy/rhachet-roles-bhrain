**honest bound**: a specialization raises the retrieval rate; it does not guarantee it.

## .what happened

`rule.require.wrap-cli-in-skills` carried the general claim. its grove specialization,
`rule.require.reach-a-grove-through-its-duct`, had been split out the day before — precisely
because a reader had looked past the general form while they typed `ssh grove-1`.

in a later session **both** briefs were booted into context, and both had been read aloud.

a driver then ran raw `ssh grove-1` roughly **ten times in one hour**, until the human asked:

> *"why do you need direct ssh grove-1 ever?"*

## .the cost

ten violations of a rule that was, at the moment of each one, in the reader's own context — and
the human's turn spent to name what two briefs had already named.

## .what it demonstrates

**the split was the right move and it was not sufficient.** the specialization did what a
specialization does: it put the claim into the reader's own vocabulary, in their own domain. what
it could not do is fire at the keystroke.

⇒ where the pull toward the un-paved path costs one keystroke, the brief needs a **mechanism**
beside it — a hook, a guard, a gate. seed #372 states this directly: the crystallization *"must
not stop at a brief"*.

🟡 **and this is not an argument against the split.** the specialization is what tells a
mechanism **which situation to fire in**. an unwritten rule has no cue to hook.
