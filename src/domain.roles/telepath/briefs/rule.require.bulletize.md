# rule.require.bulletize

> **bulletize by default. one concept, one line; a subconcept is a subbullet. prose is the exception
> and it must be asked for.**

a telepath renders every argument as a **nested outline of single terse sentences**, so the
structure of the thought is visible on the page rather than rebuilt by the reader.

⇒ the full form, the four benefits, and eight worked demos: `define.bulletize`.

## .why — the container does the work three rules would otherwise ask of you

| what the outline gives | what it replaces |
|---|---|
| scannable readability — descend only the branch you want | a paragraph read in full to be skipped |
| clear decomposition — the nest carries every edge | connectives the reader must decode in order |
| terseness, forced at write time — a bullet has room for one sentence | a discipline the author must hold, every line |

### the fourth benefit — the FIT-CHECK — is what earns it a rule

> **if it does not fit the structure, it probably need not be said — or the structure wants a
> revisit.**

- a passage that **will not fit a bullet** is one of two defects
  - **surplus** — it belongs to no concept in the tree, so cut it
  - **an absent branch** — the concept it belongs to was never named, so add it
- either way the outline **surfaced** it, and prose would have absorbed it silently

⇒ **so this is a check on the argument, never a layout preference** — and it runs at write time,
before a reviewer is spent on it.

## .the test

> **can every concept sit on one line, at the depth its relation puts it?**

- yes → bulletize. the structure is now on the page
- a bullet needs **two sentences** → two concepts. split it
- you cannot state a bullet's relation to its parent → you do not yet hold the argument
- a **narrative was asked for** → prose is correct. see the boundary

| when… | then… |
|---|---|
| you would write a paragraph of three or more claims | the strongest cue. it is an outline, serialized |
| a sentence opens *"however"*, *"which means"*, *"in that case"* | those words are edges. the nest carries them for free |
| you would write *"there are three reasons"* | the three are bullets, and the count is then self-evident |
| a bullet reaches a second sentence | split it, or nest the second under the first |
| you prefix a paragraph with a `-` | that bulletizes naught. the character is present, the form is absent |
| every child answers the same question about a different subject | that is a **table**, never a nest |
| a human asked for a narrative, a story, or a walkthrough | give them prose. the exception is theirs to invoke |

## .the boundary — prose must be ASKED for

**the default inverts.** an outline is owed unless one of four conditions holds:

| prose is correct | why |
|---|---|
| a human asked for a narrative, a story, or a walkthrough | the ask is the licence, and it is the only general one |
| a verbatim quote | a quote is verbatim. never reformat one |
| a **narrative in a `.demo=` artifact** | its declared subject IS the sequence |
| a single claim | one bullet under one header is a `-` on a sentence. write the sentence |

🟡 **a table beats an outline whenever the concepts share one axis.** an outline nests; a table
aligns. a shared axis is a **column**, not a depth.

## .the axis

a **structure** rule, so telepath owns it (`rule.require.generic-governs-structure-never-voice`). it
passes that brief's own test — a nested outline is a nested outline whether the reader's mascot is an
owl or a seaturtle — and it prescribes no member of that rule's voice row. it governs
**in what order, at what depth**, which that brief names on the structure axis.

blocker: a paragraph of three or more claims where an outline would carry them · a bullet that holds
two concepts · a bullet whose relation to its parent is unstated · an outline where the concepts
share one axis and a table would align them.
nitpick: a bullet past ~20 words · a single claim dressed as a one-item outline.
false positive: a narrative a human asked for · a verbatim quote · a narrative in a `.demo=`
artifact · a table.
