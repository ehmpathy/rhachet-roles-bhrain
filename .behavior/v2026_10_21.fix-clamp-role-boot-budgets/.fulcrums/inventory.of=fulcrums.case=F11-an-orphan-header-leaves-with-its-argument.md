# F11 — an orphan header leaves the min with its argument

## the fork

eighteen sections of the old mins held only argument under their header (a `## .why`, a
`## .the measured case`, a `## .the precedent`). the census classifier counts every `#` line as a
cue, so the move of those sections reads as 18 cue lines lost.

- **A** — keep each header in the min, with no body beneath it
- **B** — keep each header, and keep a one-line stub of its argument beneath it
- **C** — the header leaves the min with its section. it stays in the `.md`, above the argument it
  titles

## taken: C, and why

a header is a cue only when it titles a cue. the wish defines the cue as the claim, the test, the
when/then rows, and the enforcement lines; a header is none of these. it is a title, and its sense
is the body under it.

- **A** leaves a title over naught. a reader who meets `## .why` with no body learns no rule, and
  meets a hint that the min was cut
- **B** keeps argument at say, which is the cost the wish moves out
- **C** keeps the header beside its body, in the `.md` the min's readme line points at

the classifier's count is a markup heuristic (a `#` is a cue by shape). a header with no cue under
it is the one case where the shape and the sense disagree. so the 18 are listed by name in the
5.1 yield, and every other line lost from a min appears verbatim in its `.md`.

## rework: clean

to restore any one header is a one-line edit to its min. none has a caller.

## confidence: 80%, and why it is not higher

- a reader may scan a min's headers as its table of contents. a header gone from the min is a
  section the reader will not know to fetch. the readme convention line ("read the `.md` for the
  argument") answers that in general, never per section
- the classifier will report these 18 as a cue loss in every later census of these mins, until the
  baseline is re-taken

## the convention, clamped

one rule, applied everywhere: a header whose whole section moved out leaves with it. the clamp
(`roleBootClamps.integration.test.ts`, "no booted min keeps a header whose section moved out")
holds it per budgeted role, so a header over an emptied section is a red test, not a judgment.
at i003 it found one, in `im_an.obsessive_learner`, which was misplaced rather than orphaned and
was moved over its list. a header that reads bare in the cue-only snapshot is a header over prose,
which the snapshot does not show.
