# 🌙 dream: a `--as contemplated` must not lift a `--as blocked`

- kind  = fix
- owner = `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain`
- caught 2026-09-13

## .the cue that fired

nine `--as contemplated` calls landed while the stone sat `blocked`. `route.drive` then reported
this:

```
🗿 route.drive
   ├─ where do we go?
   │  ├─ route = .behavior/v2026_09_03.feat-peer-review-parallelism
   │  └─ stone = 5.1.execution.from_vision
   │
   └─ arrived? passed? mark the stone
```

minutes earlier, the same command reported:

```
   └─ halted, stone marked blocked
      └─ reason: .behavior/.../blocker/5.1.execution.from_vision.md
```

⇒ **no gate moved.** the nine lanes still returned `constraint ✋`, the corpus was still 602.0k, and
the human grant the halt asks for had not been given. only the passage log changed.

## .the gap, precisely

`rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close` states the contract verbatim:

> 🔴 **the converse holds equally and was never written down: a `.taken` does not lift the halt.**
> `--as contemplated` discharges a debt to a reviewer; `--as blocked` reports an absent grant.
> neither is the other's inverse — so answer points while halted, and do not read that progress as
> permission to re-arrive.

**mechanically, it does lift it.** `route.drive` reads the **latest** passage entry, so
`contemplated` supersedes `blocked` by recency alone. `passage.jsonl`:

```
{"stone":"5.1.execution.from_vision","status":"blocked"}
{"stone":"5.1.execution.from_vision","status":"contemplated","that":"repo-rules"}
… ×9
```

⇒ 🔴 **so the rule instructs a driver to perform, while halted, precisely the sequence that retires
their own halt — and the engine retires it on their behalf, silently.** the rule's own measured
incident (2026-09-07, `blocked → arrived → blocked → contemplated`) is the same defect read from the
other side: there, a driver re-arrived by choice. here, the engine does it for them.

⚠️ **it fails in the dangerous direction.** a driver who trusts `route.drive` re-arrives on a halt
whose gate never moved, spends a budget round, and the halt returns identically.

## .the shape of the fix

three options, and the third matches the rule's own model:

| # | the fix | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| A | a **sticky halt** — `blocked` persists until an explicit retire verb or a human act lifts it | a new verb, and a driver can strand themselves |
| B | a **loud lift** — `contemplated` still supersedes, and `route.drive` prints *"a prior halt was lifted by a contemplation"* | cheap, and it still lifts a halt a `.taken` may not lift |
| 🔴 **C** | read the **KIND**, never the recency — a `contemplated` entry discharges a reviewer debt and leaves a `blocked` entry standing; only an `arrived`, `passed`, or an explicit re-probe of the halt's own gate retires it | the correct model, and it touches the passage reader |

⇒ **C is what the rule already describes.** *"neither is the other's inverse"* is a statement about
two independent quantities, and the engine collapses them onto one ordered log. the repair is to
stop the collapse: a halt is retired by its own remedy, never by a neighbour's.

**where:** the passage reader behind `route.drive` — the operation that computes a stone's current
status from `passage.jsonl`. the rule's clause above is the specification; no new judgment is owed.

## .why it is not done in this round

- **SAFE = no.** it changes what `route.drive` reports for every route in the org, and every
  acceptance snapshot that renders a halt is an oracle over that output
- **CLEAN = no.** it ripples into the passage reader, the `route.drive` render, and the snapshots
  that pin both — none of which this stone's diff opens

⇒ so it is caught rather than fixed, and the round's own halt was **re-asserted by hand** after the
contemplations — which is the workaround this dream exists to remove.
