## .settled

**the articulation path drops its `rN` level and keys on `(stone, slug)` — the promise's own key.**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the path | `for.$stone._.r$index.$slug.md` | `for.$stone._.$slug.md` |
| the key | `(stone, slug, index)` — a third key in a two-key subsystem | `(stone, slug)` — the key the promise already uses |
| who derives the index | 🔴 three call sites, three different ways | 🌙 **no one. the quantity is gone** |

⇒ **the phrase *"the promise slug"* settles more than the fork asked.** the fork was *drop the
ordinal, or reconcile the three derivations of it*; the answer names **what the path keys on
instead**, which the drive had argued for and the wisher had not yet said. the articulation and the
promise now share one key, so a driver who can name the promise can compute the articulation.

🔴 **and it retires a class rather than an instance.** the off-by-one was one symptom; the cause is
that a **guard-assigned ordinal is typed by a driver**. remove the ordinal and the off-by-one, the
level pitfall, and the *"guess the level"* experience all become unreachable at once — **an absent
quantity cannot be derived wrong.**

## .the concept, stripped of this round

> **a key that carries a derived component invites several parties to derive it differently. the
> repair is to remove the component, never to reconcile the derivations.**

this is the round's central thesis, applied one level down. the hash was a derived component of the
trigger's key; the ordinal is a derived component of the articulation's. **both were removed by the
same argument, one utterance apart** — and in both cases the repair was to adopt a key that an
adjacent artifact in the same subsystem already used.

⇒ the general shape: **when two artifacts describe one subject and one of them is keyed more finely
than the other, the finer key is usually the defect.** the promise was keyed `(stone, slug)` and the
articulation `(stone, slug, index)`; the asymmetry is what let them disagree.
