# rule.always.say-only-what-is-needed

> **say only what the reader acts on. before you add a clause, doubt why it must be said at all.**

**how it applies the basis:** a clause with no read behind it is a claim you did not doubt. each clause you add is a claim you trust enough to send; a clause the reader acts on none of goes.

## .what

an extra clause is an extra claim, and an extra claim is an extra place to be wrong. the prose half (the fewest words that keep the full sense) is the mechanic's `rule.require.brevity` and `rule.forbid.rambles`. this rule adds the doubt per clause: a clause can be brief and still be one the reader never needed.

a read or a proof beside a claim is acted on: it spares the peer their own check. keep it, or move it to a cited evidence file (telepath `rule.require.dense-prose`).

## .the move

| step | the act |
|---|---|
| 1 | write what is necessary: the outcome, the owner, the hour |
| 2 | for each added clause, ask whether the reader acts on it |
| 3 | a clause the reader does not act on, or one two readers would read two ways, is cut |
| 4 | a clause that claims a bound keeps its source |

## .the test

> **rewrite the reply's claim in one line: what did the original hold that the line does not?**

## .enforcement

nitpick: a clause the reader does not act on, in an order or a report · an ambiguous clause left in · a clause with no source that claims to be a bound
