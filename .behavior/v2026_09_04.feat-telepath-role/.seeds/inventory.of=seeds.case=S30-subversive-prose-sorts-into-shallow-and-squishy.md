## .settled

**subversive prose is not one defect. it is two, and they take opposite repairs.**

| shape | reads as | what is absent | the repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shallow** | insubstantial | no substance beneath it — the sentence asserts none | **cut** it |
| **squishy** | unjustifiable | no justification reaches it — it conforms to whatever the reader wants | **replace** it with the mechanism it stood in front of |

⇒ so a rule that merely FLAGS is one notch too coarse. **it must SORT**, because a reader who is
told *"this is subversive"* cannot know whether to delete the line or to write a different one.

### the test is an OBSERVATION question, never a taste question

> **what observation would settle this sentence?**

🟡 **not *"could a reader disagree with it?"*** — that grades taste, and a metaphor passes it. you
may disagree with *"it took the cheaper of two doors"* and no observation on earth settles the
disagreement.

the same claim, three ways, and the coarse test separates only the first:

| the specimen | prose | is there a claim? | what settles it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| the hedge | *"the review flow has some issues worth a look"* | no | — |
| the metaphor | *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | yes | **none** |
| the mechanism | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStone…Unforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* | yes | open the file |

⇒ the metaphor is the expensive instance and the coarse test passes it.

### squishy is the dangerous shape

shallow prose reads as filler and a reviewer cuts it on sight. **squishy prose reads as INSIGHT** —
the author writes it and feels lucid, the reader nods, and the mechanism it stood in front of is
never stated. it takes the slot a settleable claim was owed.

### the sub-test for squishy

**substitute the sentence for the mechanism it describes.** if the reader can no longer act on it,
the sentence displaced rather than explained.

### the bound

the defect is **ambiguity**, never a rhetorical register. a metaphor that *accompanies* a stated
mechanism is fine; one that *replaces* it is not. and terse is not thin — a settleable claim stays,
however long it runs.

## .the lesson worth a keep, and it is about RULES rather than prose

> **a test that answers YES on both the good and the bad case is not a test — it is a filter one
> notch too coarse.**

before any rule ships: write the defect three ways — absent, plausible-but-wrong, and correct — and
confirm the test separates all three.

⇒ that is `rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` applied to a rule's **scope** rather than to a
word, and it is the sharper case: **a wrong word costs a rename; a wrong scope ships a gate that
passes the defect it was built for.**
