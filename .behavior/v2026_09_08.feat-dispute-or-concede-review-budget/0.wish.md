# wish: a driver must DISPUTE or CONCEDE a review verdict — and a dispute drives on

## .what

at the peer-review gate, a driver must declare one of exactly two signals against the judge's
verdict:

| signal | it means | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| `<dispute>` | *"I hold that this is fine to continue"* | 🔴 **the route drives on.** no halt, no budget spent |
| `<concede>` | *"the reviewer is right and I need more rounds"* | the route halts and asks for budget |

so a route halts **only** when the driver concedes. a dispute is trusted and the drive proceeds.

and settle the ownership question that sits under it: **a budget add is NOT a human-only
operation.** it is the driver's lever.

## .why

### divergent review rounds are expensive, and today only the budget bounds them

when a driver and a reviewer disagree on substance, more rounds do not converge them — they
re-argue. today the one force that stops that loop is the budget as it runs out, so the fleet
pays full review cost for every round of a disagreement that was never a defect.

⇒ **budget is the wrong instrument to end a disagreement.** it ends it by exhaustion rather than
by judgment, and it charges for every round along the way.

### the fulcrum council already reviews the disputes, at the end

`rule.always.itemize-the-fulcrums-you-best-guess` requires every judgment call to be itemized
as it is made, and `rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last` gathers them into one review at the
close. so a disputed verdict is **already** destined for human review — just later, and in a
batch, where it is cheap.

⇒ a halt mid-route to settle a dispute buys a review that would have happened regardless. it
buys it early, one at a time, at the highest possible price.

### the signal is the value, never the permission

a driver that silently proceeds and a driver that declares `<dispute>` do the same work. the
difference is that the second leaves **a record of a judgment**, which is precisely what the
council reads. today an unconverged blocker leaves an exhausted lane and an argument buried in
a `.taken`; this makes the driver's position explicit, binary, and countable.

## .acceptance

1. at the peer-review gate, a driver's response carries exactly one of `<dispute>` or
   `<concede>`. neither, or both, is refused
2. on `<dispute>`, the route **proceeds** — the stone is not held, and the disagreement consumes
   no budget
3. on `<concede>`, the route halts and surfaces a budget ask
4. a `<dispute>` is **itemized as a fulcrum**, so the end-of-route council reads it. a dispute
   that leaves no fulcrum row is the failure mode this whole change turns on
5. a budget add is reachable by the **driver**, and is documented as the driver's lever rather
   than a human gate
6. every one of 1–5 is covered by a test that goes **red** before the change and green after
   (`rule.require.clamp-edge-cases`)

## 🔴 .the extant rule this CHANGES, and it currently grades a blocker

⚠️ read this before you start, because the repo will argue with you.

- `rule.forbid.budget-top-ups` states *"the route's budget is the budget"* and grades a top-up
  a **blocker**. the supervisor's own babysit contract carries it as `⛔ NEVER`
- the wisher has now settled the ownership half: **a budget add is not human-only**

these are two different claims and only one is overturned. the wisher's directive settles
**WHO** may add budget. whether a top-up is still discouraged, and under which signal, is what
this behavior decides — and whatever it decides, **`rule.forbid.budget-top-ups` must be updated
or retired in the same change**, never left to contradict the new gate.

⇒ a rule left in force against a shipped behavior is the braided trail
(`philosophy.pavement-saves-nature`): the next traveler meets two paved paths and no way to tell
which is live.

## .the boundary — what is NOT in scope

- **do not remove the peer review.** the reviewer still runs and still renders a verdict; this
  changes what a driver may do in answer to one, never whether one happens
- **do not touch the SELF-review ladder.** `rule.always.bear-every-self-review` states outright
  that a self review cannot be overridden and has no budget — it is a debt, not a budget. a
  dispute signal carries no sense there
- **do not weaken the `.taken`-per-`.given` requirement.** a `<dispute>` is a *verdict* on the
  disagreement; it does not discharge the duty to answer each point
  (`rule.always.converge-with-reviewers.via-a-taken-per-point`)
- **do not make `<dispute>` the default.** an absent signal is refused, per acceptance #1. a
  driver that drifts into dispute by omission has declared no judgment, and the council would
  read a blank
