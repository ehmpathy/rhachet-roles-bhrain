# domain.term.reason: doc.kernel

## .etymology

a kernel is the seed inside a grain: the part that holds the whole plant in small. the thinker's
`kno301` briefs chose it for exactly that: the minimal carrier of one concept, richer than a ref,
smaller than a brief, able to grow into one. the garden metaphor (🌱 fertile) runs through the
thinker, researcher, and librarian roles, which all already use the word in this one sense.

rejected:
- `nugget`, `takeaway` — casual; they name what a reader keeps, not an atomic unit of one concept
- `insight` — names a novel realization; most kernels are plain facts
- `point` — overloaded (a point of a review, a point in time, a data point)
- `atom` — collides with `brain atom` vocabulary in token reports

## .boundary

"kernel, of what?" → of a doc. the thinker defines it as `doc.kernel` (`kno301.doc.kernelize`),
so the boundary is `doc`. a great's book, a paper, a docs page: each is a doc a course reads.

## .evidence

- in use, one sense, three roles: thinker (`kno301.doc.kernelize`, defines it), researcher
  (`probes.absorb` synthesizes yields into kernels), librarian (compression into atomic kernels)
- undeclared until the education suite made it a contract word: the pedigree entry lists each
  course's kernels and their vitro verdicts
- the split from `claim`: a course tests kernels in vitro, and a definition cannot be refuted
  in a sandbox. the refutable kind earned its own word (`term=doc.kernel.claim`); `kernel` stays
  the genus, unchanged

## .invariants

- one kernel holds exactly one concept
- every kernel a course records cites the doc and page it came from
