## 🔴 .the test that retired both tables

> **a question row earns its place when it carries URGENCY the board lacks, or when NO FULCRUM
> backs it.**

the two emptied for **opposite** reasons: the `C` rows were redundant with the board from the day
they were written; the `W` rows carried real urgency and were then all ruled.

⇒ **urgency is a property of the moment, never of the question.** a `W` table decays where a fulcrum
board does not — the board is the durable artifact, and a question table is a snapshot with an
expiry.

⚠️ **for a "may wait" row the answer is almost always no.** `rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last`
already gathers every fulcrum into one council at the close, so *"this may wait for the council"*
restates the default — and it lets the council meet one call twice, once as a row to rule and once
as a question to answer, and rule the two inconsistently.

## 🟡 .two process findings worth the next author's attention

- **an untagged question list is a `[wisher]` pile by default, and a default is not a triage.** the
  four sorts — *can logic settle it? can extant code? does it need research? does only the wisher
  know?* — moved two rows off `[wisher]` the moment they were actually run (W3, C2)
- **`W2` was tagged `[wisher]` correctly and SHAPED wrong.** it asked the wisher to pick between two
  predicates the author had imagined; the wisher answered with a mechanism neither named. ⇒ **a
  question put as a fork constrains the answer to the options its author could imagine**
- the two tables are lettered rather than numbered because they **collided on `4`** — a wisher who
  ruled *"on 4"* would have ruled on neither unambiguously
