# domain.term.choice.reason: squishy

## .etymology

plain english, and chosen for a **physical** property rather than a moral one: a squishy object
takes the shape of whatever presses on it and holds no form of its own.

that is exactly the defect. the sentence conforms to whatever the reader already believes, so every
reader finds their own view in it and none of them can check it.

the wisher named the word directly, 2026-09-06:

> i preffer shallow and squishy

## .the candidates, and why `squishy` won

| candidate | the case for it | why it lost |
|---|---|---|
| `unjustifiable` | the most precise gloss — no justification reaches the sentence | 🟡 a second sense in plain english, *morally indefensible*. a grade that used it would charge the author rather than grade the prose. kept as a gloss |
| `unfalsifiable` | a real term of art, and it names the test | it excludes the common case where an observation does exist and the author simply did not name it. that case is squishy and falsifiable at once |
| `slippery` | vivid, and the physical metaphor is right | it grades intent — a slippery author evades on purpose. squishy prose is usually written in earnest |
| `rhetorical` | names what the prose does | names a register, and the bound is ambiguity. a metaphor beside a stated mechanism is legal, and this word would forbid it |
| `mushy` | the same physical property | reads as a judgment of quality rather than of structure, and it composes worse |

⇒ **`squishy` is the only candidate that names the conformity with no claim about intent or
register**, and both of those are claims this grade must not make.

## .the evidence — it is the instance the original rule shipped past

the root `subversive` was coined 2026-09-04 with the test *"could a reader disagree with this?"*.
that test separates a real claim from `shallow` prose and **passes `squishy` whole**:

| prose | could a reader disagree? | what observation settles it? |
|---|---|---|
| *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | **yes** — so it passes | **none** |

⇒ so the expensive half of the defect survived the rule built to catch it, for two days, in the
canon whose declared subject is signal. that is the measured instance behind the split.

🟡 and the same passage is what the root's own measurement was drawn from — the rhetorical form ran
12 words against the mechanism's 13. so it is not long, not padded, and not caught by any
density rule either.

## .disputes

### dispute: unjustifiable — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (gloss only)

- raised.by  = the wisher, in `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain#444`
- claim      = *"`unjustifiable` states the defect exactly"*
- counter    = verbatim from the same issue: *"`unjustifiable` carries a second sense in plain
               english — morally indefensible — so `squishy` is the canonical term and
               `unjustifiable` is its gloss, never a synonym to use in a contract"*
- resolution = `squishy` canonical; `unjustifiable` recorded as a forbidden contract synonym and
               permitted as an explanatory gloss

## .the invariant

> **a squishy grade must name the mechanism the sentence stood in front of, or say that it could
> not find one.**

a grade of `squishy` with no mechanism named is itself squishy: it asserts a defect and names no
observation that would settle whether the defect is there. the honest form of that grade is
*"I cannot find the mechanism this stands in front of"*, which is checkable and which the author can
answer.
