# rule.always.trust-no-one-especially-not-yourself.example=the-acoustic-bass-guitar

> the deepest belief is the one never asked as a question. it reads as a fact about the world, so no doubt is aimed at it — in a human mind or a robot one alike.

## .what happened (2025)

- the human held, with full confidence, that an acoustic bass guitar cannot exist: physics forbids a bass smaller than a cello. a big bass, or none
- it was never a question to him. it sat as true as the sky is blue
- he explained to a friend why it was impossible. mid-explanation, he heard the claim as a claim: a belief he had never once put a question to
- one search refuted it. acoustic bass guitars exist, and have been sold for decades

## .what the read shows

- definition: "a bass instrument with a hollow wooden body similar to, though usually larger than, a steel-string acoustic guitar"
- first well-documented model: the Ernie Ball Earthwood, built with George Fullerton, "introduced in 1972"
- read at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_bass_guitar, 2026-11-27

## .the true kernel, and the leap

the belief was not baseless, which is what kept it unquestioned:

- the physics half holds. such a body is "too small to produce a resonance of acceptable volume at lower pitches"
- the leap was from *too quiet unplugged* to *cannot exist*. makers answer it with a somewhat larger body, or a piezo or magnetic pickup into an amp
- ⇒ two razors would have caught it, had a question been put:
  - `razor=false-dilemma` — "a big bass or none" omits a third case: a small body with a pickup
  - `razor=ladder` — the evidence sat on the rung *unplugged volume*; the claim spent the rung *the instrument cannot exist*

## .the lesson

- a belief held so long it reads as a fact is a first thought that has aged. age gives it no read
- the cue that fired was the explanation itself. to say a claim aloud, to a peer, makes it a claim again. that is why the role asks for the claim list before a reply leaves (`rule.always.send-no-claim-before-its-read`)
- the cost of the cure was one search. the cost of the belief was every year it stood, and every peer it was taught to
- self-skepticism binds humans and robots alike. a robot's deep beliefs come from the text it learned on, held with the same calm, and voiced with the same fluency

## .the phrase it left

the friend now warns him: *don't acoustic-bass yourself*. to **acoustic-bass yourself** is to explain, with full confidence, a belief you never once put a question to — until the explanation itself exposes it. a verb is a cue that fires faster than a rule.

## .the question that would have caught it

> **what would I expect to see if this were false — and have I ever looked?**
