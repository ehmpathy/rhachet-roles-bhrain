# 1.vision.yield — the telepath role

## .the outcome, in one line

**telepath owns the prose canon for every bhrain** — the traits and rules that ban subversive narration and chronological accretion while they enforce concise, clear, direct prose — **and a `review.by` rubric per rule makes that canon checkable.**

## .the deliverable, in priority order

per `S07`, the wisher's own words: *"the main priority is the enbriefment of the telepath role … to teach these traits … and rules … that can later be used by downstream usecases like routes and reviews … and to add this rubrics for review.by operation against the enbriefed rules … thats the primary deliverable."*

| # | the artifact | why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | the briefs — the canon | a role's deliverable is what it teaches. every consumer reads this |
| **2** | **telepath's own `review.by` skill + `rubrics.yml`** — required, not optional | a canon nobody can check is advice |
| 3 | a reviewer wired onto a guard | a downstream **usecase**, and separate |
| 4 | a subconscious that nudges on a live message | a second downstream usecase |

🟡 **the wired reviewer and the subconscious are consumers, never the purpose.** the canon is authored so that *routes and reviews* can use it later — that rank is the wisher's, and the intuitive read inverts it.

### the `review.by` skill is a REQUIRED deliverable, and it has three parts

settled by the wisher 2026-09-07 (`S38`): *"can we include the creation of a review.by skill … which has different focuses for the various reviews we have so it can be decomposed, but also so that it can just be run in bulk? that way, we can get smol brains to help us review."*

| part | what | state |
|---|---|---|
| `skills/review.by.sh` | the wrapper — a 6-line delegation to bhrain's base engine | absent |
| `briefs/reviews/rubrics.yml` | two entries: `{ slug, purpose, rules[] }` | absent |
| `getTelepathRole.ts` | `skills.dirs` already points at `skills/` | ✅ wired |

⇒ the base engine already gives **both** run modes at zero cost: `--for <slug>` runs one rubric, no `--for` runs every rubric. so the wisher's decomposed-and-bulk ask needs no mechanism — it needs the **decomposition**.

### the axis is TWO rubrics — `structure` and `efficiency`

settled by the wisher 2026-09-07 (`S38`): *"1 for structure, 1 for efficiency, on the rubrics front."*

| slug | the one question it holds | the rules it cites |
|---|---|---|
| **structure** | *is the concept built so it transfers whole and checkable?* | `require.domain-discovery` · `require.distillation` · `forbid.surface-speak` · `require.bulletize` · `forbid.narration` · `require.place-a-passage-by-its-subject` · `forbid.subversive-prose` · `require.disputable-claims` · `forbid.obfuscation` · `forbid.absolutes` · `forbid.width-ambiguous-glyphs` · `require.generic-governs-structure-never-voice` |
| **efficiency** | *does it cost the reader more than it must?* | `require.purpose-first` · `forbid.purposeless-passages` · `require.reflexive-condensation` · `require.archaeology-in-notes` · `forbid.emphasis-noise` · `forbid.diffusion` · `forbid.decode-friction` · `forbid.riddles` · `forbid.defensive-prose` · `require.dense-prose` |

⇒ it reverses **F02** on its `1 vs N` axis, and lands nearer the taken option than its counter: two rubrics, not one, and not the eight `grain` slugs the readme's canon table would produce.

🟡 the readme's grain axis is **kept as the canon's index and dropped as the rubric axis**, on the ask's own ground — *"we can get smol brains to help us review"*. eight slugs is one rule-cluster each and eight brain calls on a bulk run; two slugs is the affordable bulk, and each still holds one question.

### four calls the split does not settle cleanly, named rather than decided silently

| the rule | why it is borderline | where it went, and why |
|---|---|---|
| `forbid.diffusion` | it grades **findability**, which reads structural | **efficiency** — its pole pair `emphasis-noise` grades mark count, and the two are *two failures of one act*. split across rubrics, each reviewer's repair walks its subject into the other pole |
| `forbid.decode-friction` · `forbid.riddles` | the defect is ORDER and REFERENCE, which reads structural | **efficiency** — the metric is a **step count**, and `forbid.riddles` states the cost claim outright: *"a riddle spends the reader's time for the author's pleasure"* |
| `forbid.defensive-prose` | its subject is the author's **model of the reader**, which reads structural | **efficiency** — the measurable half is bloat. its own measured case is 3 lines that inform against 9 that defend |
| `require.elucidation` | it is the **root** both questions are surfaces of | **neither, and both** — each rubric's `purpose` field states it as the frame; no rubric grades against it |

🟡 and two booted rules are enforceable by **neither** rubric: `require.answer-what-they-meant` and `forbid.genie-answers` grade a live message read, and a reviewer reads files. that is **D5**, over a gap in the split — §5's `2b` argues it.

### 🟡 the skill RUNS the deterministic checkers, and grades their hits as nitpicks

settled by the wisher 2026-09-07 (`S39`): *"and should our rubric be sure to run those deterministic reviews too?"* · *"we can flag them as nitpicks."*

`skills/review.for/` carries a checker per mechanizable rule — emphasis density, glyph class, span stacks, diffusion flat/needleless/saturated, narration paragraphs, declare-once claims/enumerations, width-ambiguous glyphs, and three canon-integrity checks.

- a brain spent on a 🪨 solid skill's job is the entoolment ladder run backwards
- ⇒ so `review.by` **runs** them itself, then hands the brain only the residue

| shape | what it gets |
|---|---|
| the rubric **names** its checkers | a brain may run them, may not — so the cheapest half of the review is the likeliest to be skipped |
| the skill **runs** them, then hands over the residue | deterministic, zero tokens, unskippable — and the brain's subject shrinks, which is the failure the wish opens on |

🟡 **and `nitpick` is a property of what a mechanical predicate can know, never a courtesy.** a checker reads a shape and cannot read intent, so a hit is right in form and wrong in substance whenever its exempt set is short a case.

⇒ measured on the checker live when this was said: `review.for.width-ambiguous-glyphs.js` flagged four lines in `.dream/…entool.a-telepath-hook-refuses-a-blocked-glyph.md` — a dream whose **declared subject** is that glyph, so each of the four is a specimen it cannot state its subject without.

- at **nitpick** the driver disputes it in one `.taken` line, or repairs it
- at **blocker** an imperfect exempt set halts a stone, and the pressure is then to weaken the checker over to widen the exemption

🟡 the checkers sort mostly to **efficiency**, which is itself an argument for the split: the efficiency question is the more mechanizable of the two, so its rubric is the cheaper one to shrink over time.

### 🔴 a BLOCKED-GLYPHS HOOK is a deliverable too, and it is the cheapest surface on the table

settled by the wisher 2026-09-07 (`S42`): *"in the vision, lets add a hook that blocks that character"* · *"and include the hazard emoji in that hook too"* · *"blocked glyphs hook"*.

| the glyph | why it is refused |
|---|---|
| `·` U+00B7 | it fuses two clauses into a run-on the reader cannot parse — *"a fucked up way to make a gross runon sentence"* |
| the hazard mark, `U+26A0 + FE0F` | width-ambiguous — a terminal reserves one cell and an emoji font paints two, so every column right of it shifts |

🟡 **the second row names its glyph by codepoint and does not print it**, because this file is not exempt from the rule it documents — `review.for.width-ambiguous-glyphs.js` exempts a file whose *declared subject* is the glyph class, and this file's subject is the role.

⇒ the two ride one hook because they share a mechanism: a `Write|Edit` refusal at the moment the character is typed, which is the one surface that fires **before** the defect ships.

🟡 **it is the mechanism the canon has been short of, and the vision already named the gap.** §5's `2b` records the write-time term hook as a fourth mechanism the D5 table omitted, and prices it exactly here: *"a rule that reduces to a phrase belongs on the hook, and each one moved there is a rule the reviewer need not carry against its context budget."* a glyph is the smallest such phrase there is.

⇒ so this is the first entry in that class, and it is evidence for the class rather than an exception to it.

### the middot specimen is this canon's own, which is the argument for a hook over a rule

> came for the puzzle → it is the point · came for the subject → it is theft of their attention.

`rule.forbid.riddles`'s own boundary line. two full clauses, each with a subject and a verb, joined by a mark that carries no grammar — so the reader must guess whether the second continues the first or opposes it.

- **the mark reads as a list separator and was used as a sentence joiner**
  - a reader who has seen `·` part three short items meets it between two sentences and applies the wrong parse
- ⇒ and it was typed by the author of the rule against unparseable prose, in that rule
  - which is `rule.forbid.width-ambiguous-glyphs`'s measured pattern exactly: ten `U+26A0 + FE0F` in one round, in the repo whose canon forbade it
  - a brief-tier forbid is **rung 1** — it fires only for an author who recalls it, and the author who does not recall it is the one who needed it

## .the before/after that matters most

**a canon is what makes prose discipline transferable. a re-read is not.**

- an author cannot see their own prose — they re-read their intent, and the intent fills every seam
- so the discipline cannot live in a habit. it must live in rules a second reader can apply
- ⇒ that is why the briefs come first and the rubrics second: the rule states the standard; the rubric makes it checkable by someone who was not there

# 3 · the mental model

## .how an actor would describe it to a friend

> *"it is the role that makes a bhrain say it straight instead of a lap around it. and there is a reviewer that reads your writeup and tells you where you said the same claim twice."*

## .the analogy that fits

**a second reader with no memory of your edit.**

> a re-read re-reads the author's **intent**, and the intent fills the seam. a reviewer has no intent to consult, so the seam is all it sees.

## .their words vs ours

| an actor says | we say |
|---|---|
| "be less wordy" | `rule.require.brevity` · `define.wick-dense` |
| "you told me that already" | chronological accretion |
| "just answer the question" | lead with the answer |
| "i missed that you needed me" | the ask was not starred |
| "it repeats itself" | one claim declared twice |

🟡 **the actor's words are the better cue.** a rule named for the felt defect fires; a rule named for the taxonomy is looked past.

- two cues cannot disagree with each other — they can only both miss, or one catch
- ⇒ so a redundant detector costs one line, and a missed one costs whatever the defect costs
