## .settled

**a claim that cannot be disputed is not a claim.** prose built so that no reader can disagree with it has transferred no concept, so it costs the reader's attention and returns none.

the coinage is **subversive prose**: indirect language whose shape makes it unfalsifiable. it is subversive because it **reads as a claim while it commits to naught** — the reader spends the attention a claim would earn and receives no position they could act on or argue with.

### .the test the wisher stated

> **is it clearly disputable?**

- yes → it is a claim. it may be wrong, and that is what makes it worth the words
- no → **it said naught, and it spent the reader's time to do so**

### .this is a DENSITY rule, not a politeness one

the ask arrived inside a wish about terse prose, and it belongs there for a mechanical reason: **an unfalsifiable sentence has an information content of zero.** so it is the density defect in its purest form — maximum words, minimum signal — and it is invisible to every other rule in the set, since such a sentence is short, structural, well-placed, and refs out correctly.

⇒ **the extant rules grade the SHAPE of a passage. this one grades whether the passage asserts anything at all.**

### .the shapes it names

| shape | why it is unfalsifiable |
|---|---|
| the **hedge stack** — *"this should probably mostly work in most cases"* | every qualifier removes a way to be wrong |
| the **passive dodge** — *"it was determined that"* | no actor, so nobody can be disputed |
| the **both-ways** — *"X is good, though Y is also valid"* | it holds whichever way the reader leans |
| the **truism** — *"the right approach depends on context"* | true of everything, so it discriminates naught |
| the **unfalsifiable verdict** — *"this could be improved"* | names no defect, so no fix can be checked |
| the **retreat clause** — a claim, then a caveat that withdraws it | the sentence's own second half revokes the first |

🟡 **the retreat clause is the sharpest, because it survives every other rule.** it is brief, it is placed correctly, and it reads as rigor. what it does is let the author claim credit for a position and disown it in the same breath.

### 🟡 .the carve-out it must NOT eat — a stated uncertainty IS a claim

telepath's own extant canon prescribes the **stated read** — *"a stated read costs one line and converts a silent misread into a correctable one"* (`rule.require.answer-what-they-meant`). and this route's own artifacts carry confidence percentages and an unverified ledger.

⇒ those are **falsifiable**: *"80%, and here is the one read that would move it"* names what would change the answer. **a hedge hides a position; a stated uncertainty declares one, plus its bound.**

| the sentence | disputable? |
|---|---|
| *"this should probably mostly work"* | ❌ a hedge — no bound, no test |
| *"80% — a `Stop` hook may not receive the message; that one read settles it"* | names the doubt, the bound, and the test |

⇒ **the test is not "does it hedge?" but "could a reader disagree with it, and know what would settle it?"**
