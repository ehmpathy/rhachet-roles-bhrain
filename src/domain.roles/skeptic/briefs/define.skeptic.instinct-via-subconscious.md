# define.skeptic.instinct-via-subconscious

## .what

the skeptic's doubt becomes a reflex through a second brain: the **subconscious**, a cheap and highly cached brain that bhrain ships as its own role (`role=subconscious`). the skeptic declares five instincts; `rhx instinct.of --role skeptic` runs all, each its own ask, and `--to <slug>` runs one:

| instinct | it reads | it asks |
|---|---|---|
| `question-assumptions` | what the main brain said | what here was assumed, unquestioned? |
| `question-questions-of-self` | what the main brain said | what did it ask that it could have answered itself? |
| `question-questions-of-peer` | the peer message that opened the turn, then what the main brain said | what is the purpose of the question it was asked, beneath its surface, and did it answer that? |
| `question-solutions` | what the main brain said | what other answer was weighed, and why this one? |
| `question-fundamentals` | what the main brain said | was the concept understood deeply enough: its foundation beneath the case, overfit or bounded, converged or contrasted with other domains? |

neither reads a tool call, a tool result, or a reason block. a flag is a nudge: it reminds the main brain to do the instinct itself, and never does the read for it.

> **what here should have been skepticized?**

it flags two shapes of claim:

- a claim that was not skepticized: it moves work or reaches a peer, and no basis backs it
- a claim whose basis is not the kind the claim owes: the per-claim reads of `rule.always.trust-but-verify` set which kind each claim owes

## .why it holds where the word checks failed

| the cut word checks asked | the subconscious asks |
|---|---|
| was this thought checked? | does this claim state its basis? |
| invisible in words, so every flag was a guess | visible in words, so a read of the output is the right instrument |
| a regex, blind to sense | a brain, guided by the role's briefs |

- it judges whether a basis is **present and of the owed kind**, never whether the claim is **correct**. a model's judgment of its own correctness does not help (Huang et al. 2023, arXiv 2310.01798); a separate reader that classifies is a different task
- it is not the author of the draft. a reader that did not write a claim does not repeat its errors (the reason Chain-of-Verification works, Dhuliawala et al. 2023, arXiv 2309.11495)
- the duality is the lesson: a main brain that knows each unbacked claim gets flagged learns to state the basis of its claims. for a brain whose only levers are its context, a flag that recurs at a fixed moment is the nearest form of a habit

## .the durable record of skepticism

each skepticized claim persists as a record under `.skepticism/` (in the bound route, or the repo where none is bound):

- the record holds the claim, the read that backs it, and the source it read (path and content hash, or the command and its exit code)
- the main brain cites the record beside the claim; the subconscious receives those citations with the output and checks each against its record
- a citation of an absent record, or of a record whose source has moved since, is a recorded contradiction: it cannot cry wolf
- the records outlive the turn and the session, so a claim skepticized once is cited, never re-derived, until its source moves

## .its bounds

- **cheap and cached.** its inputs are fed the way `rhx review` feeds a reviewer brain: the role's briefs lead as the shared prefix, cached across turns; the output of the turn trails (`rule.require.name-the-shared-prefix-as-the-cache-anchor`)
- **its flag reaches the main brain as a message** into its own session (`rhx clone say`), the way the route reminder delivers its nudges; it never holds a turn. an idle seat meets the flag as a new turn, so the flag is marked as the instinct's, never the human's
- **one flag per claim, no chain.** the turn a flag opens is asked only of the flagged claim, and a claim flagged once in a session is never flagged again; else one unbacked claim spends turn after turn
- **every token it spends leaves a trail**: each call writes the log a review run writes, so the review meter reads its spend, graded, never estimated
- **its flag rate is measured before it speaks or blocks** (`rule.forbid.checks-that-cry-wolf`): on a labeled replay of real sessions; a block is earned by the replay
- **it asks only of claims that move work or reach a peer**, so the main brain does not learn to staple a basis to every sentence (`rule.always.say-only-what-is-needed`)
- **it is the skeptic's instinct, never its reviewer.** it does not judge the claim; it asks the main brain to have doubted it, and the main brain does the doubt
