## .the outcome

**a driver who has genuinely reviewed is never held by a clock.** the self-review challenge stops
to measure elapsed time as its gate. a promise clears on the strength of the articulation, never
on how many seconds passed since a hash appeared.

✅ **upheld in full** (`S11`). ⇒ and the sentence turned out to be more precisely true than it read:
the clause *"since a hash appeared"* names the actual mechanism, and it is the half that had to go
first.

## 🔴 .why — the timer punishes the driver who does the work

this repo already caught the defect and left it un-repaired:
`.dream/v2026_09_03.fix.selfreview-timer-punishes-the-driver-who-repairs.md`. read it first — it
holds the code cites and the reproduction.

`getSelfReviewChallengeDecision.ts` measures elapsed against **the artifact hash's** trigger
report, and mints a fresh report with the clock started when a hash is new. so:

| the driver who… | the guard |
|---|---|
| reads, finds no issues, waits 30s | **passes** |
| reads, finds 9 issues, **repairs each one** | 🔴 a new hash per repair, a restarted timer per hash, challenged again |

⚠️ **and the liveness hatch does not save them.** `attempts >= 3` is counted **per hash**, and a
driver who repairs is on a new hash every attempt — so the count never reaches 3. **the hatch is
unreachable for exactly the driver who reviews properly.**

⇒ the guard's own instruction is *"when you found issues, fix them before you continue."* a driver
who obeys it is held longer than one who does not. **measured: three `--as promised` calls over
~30 minutes of genuine review, each met with `the pond barely rippled`.**

🔴 **the lesson it teaches is the harm.** a driver who meets this a few times learns to batch every
repair until after the promise clears, or to make no repairs mid-review at all. both are worse
than what the guard asks for, and the second is precisely the daydream
`rule.always.bear-every-self-review` exists to forbid.

## 🔴 .the objection this wish must ANSWER, not skip

the dream closes with: ⚠️ ***"do not simply drop the timer. it exists for a good reason, and
`research.selfreview-effectiveness` is presumably its evidence."***

**that objection is noted and overruled by the wisher — and the overrule is not a licence to leave
it unaddressed.** read `research.selfreview-effectiveness` and state, in the yield, what the timer
was actually for and what now carries that weight instead.

⇒ our read is that the weight sits on the **articulation**, never the clock: that research measures
a cue's effectiveness over a bare principle, and a cue is written down rather than waited out.
**if the research says otherwise, say so and stop** — do not quietly keep a reduced timer to split
the difference.

## .what is NOT wanted

- 🔴 **a smaller threshold.** 30s down to 10s is the same mechanism with a lower number, and it
  bloats back the moment somebody argues a driver rushed
- **a cleverer clock** — elapsed measured from the articulation file, or carried forward across a
  hash change. those were the dream's candidate directions and they preserve the premise this wish
  rejects: that duration is evidence of review
- **a looser articulation gate.** requirement 2 is the bound, and a change that makes a promise
  cheaper to fake has traded one defect for a worse one
- **a deleted test.** update the snapshots; do not drop the cases

## .why it matters

a gate that holds the thorough driver longer than the hasty one does not merely annoy — **it
teaches**. every driver who meets it learns to review less visibly, and the guard then measures a
performance rather than a review.

⇒ the review is the work. the clock was only ever a proxy for it, and it is a proxy that got the
sign wrong.
