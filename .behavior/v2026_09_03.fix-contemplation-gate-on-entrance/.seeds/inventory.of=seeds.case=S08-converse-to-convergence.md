## .settled

**the driver's reply to a peer critique is a `converse`; a reviewer still owed one is `unanswered`.**

the act is two-party, so the word must be. `contemplate` names a solo, internal act and cannot carry
the second party — which is why every surface that used it also glossed it (*"reply to the review"*).

⇒ **the phrase is the test: "converse to convergence."** `con-verse` and `con-verge` are the same
root (*vergere*, to turn) — turn together, incline together. a loop governed by two `converge` rules
takes its act-word from that family, never from another register.

⚠️ **the state word need not share the root, and a hunt for one is how the pair breaks.**
`unconversed` is unreadable and `unconverged` overloads `converge`, which already names the
whole-ladder outcome. `unanswered` is plain, and it is what the act plainly leaves behind: you
converse **with** someone; until you do, they are **unanswered**.

## 🔴 .the general lesson, which outlives the two words

**a term's own etymology file can hold the refutation of its own choice.** this one opened
*"peer review in this domain is a conversation, not a verdict"* — it named the concept
`conversation` and then chose a word for a different act. nobody read the two lines against each
other for months.

⇒ **when a term needs a gloss wherever it is used, that gloss is the canonical word, unsurfaced.**
re-read the first line of a `.reason` file before you defend the word it settles.
