# domain.term.choice.reason: bulletize

## .etymology

`bullet` — from french *boulette*, "small ball". the typographic sense dates to the mid-20th
century: the round mark that heads each item of a list.

`bulletize` takes the `-ize` suffix that this repo's materialization verbs already share —
`itemize`, `catalogize`, `lessonize`. the morphology is the family's, so the word arrives already
conjugated into a practice that exists (`rule.always.reuse-pavement-before-improvise`).

⇒ the etymology carries the property the rule needs: **a bullet is a MARK THAT HEADS A LINE.** so
the unit is the line, and the line holds one concept. a verb built on it can mean no other act.

## .the coinage is the wisher's, twice

> 2026-09-04, first: *"and where's the bulletize howto ? i.e., the brief that teaches that
> structured .what, .why with sub bullet outline forms is the clearest communication structure; one
> dense line per concept, subconcepts = subbullets"*

> 2026-09-04, second: *"we should have a trait to BULLETIZE speach. that way, main concepts and
> subconcepts and subsub concepts naturally follow SINGLE TERSE SENTENCE outlines"*

⇒ `rule.require.ubiqlang` says adopt the wisher's word, never a synonym of it. **the word was used
twice, days apart, with the same sense** — which is the strongest possible signal the concept was
already theirs and needed only a home.

## .why not the rejected synonyms

- **itemize** — the sharpest rejection, because it is a declared term of this glossary and
  its etymology reads *"an item is not merely counted, it is given its own LINE"*
  (`term=itemization.itemize._.choice.reason.md`). that is close enough to collide.

  ⇒ the seam that parts them is declared by `src/domain.roles/telepath/briefs/define.bulletize.md`
  under `.the seam with itemize` — **an address against a position in a tree.**

  what this cluster adds is why that seam is load-bear rather than pedantic: an outline's third
  bullet has no path, cannot be globbed, and cannot report its own absences — the three properties
  an inventory exists to provide (`rule.require.inventory-for-enumerable-concepts`). so a verb that
  covered both would promise a census the outline cannot deliver.

- **outline** — the **noun** is correct and used throughout; the **verb** is the synonym. *"outline
  the argument"* in common english means *sketch it roughly*, which is the opposite of the
 precision this rule demands. a verb whose plain sense inverts the rule's intent is the worst
  available choice
- **decompose** — a real term of art, and already spoken for: `howto.dimensional-decomposition`
  (architect) and `rule.require.dimensional-decomposition` (behaver) use it for the walk of an
  orthogonal product. to reuse it here would be `rule.forbid.domain-term-ambiguity` — one word,
  two concepts
- **structure** — a noun pressed into verb duty, and it names the *result* rather than the *act*.
  every rule in the telepath canon governs structure; only this one governs the bullet form
- **format** — names a presentation choice, which is what the rule is **not**. bulletize is a check
  on the argument: a passage that will not fit a bullet is surplus, or the tree is wrong

## .evidence

- **code + contracts**: `rule.require.bulletize` and `rule.forbid.narration` (both booted say-tier)
  and `define.bulletize` (ref) each compose the word. `rule.require.domain-term-itemization` binds
  them
- **the boundary was in arrears when this word arrived**: `term=prose` did not exist, and the only
  prior mention of that boundary was the `enumerate` dispute's counter #2. ⇒ this term's declaration
  is what forced the head to be written (`term=prose._.choice.reason.md`)
- **invariant**: what bulletize produces is an **outline**, and an outline is a tree of lines. it is
  **not** an inventory — its entries carry no coordinates, so no gap check is expressible over it.
  a rule that wants a gap check wants `itemize`

## .disputes

none yet. 🟡 one is foreseeable and named here so it is not re-derived: **`enumerate` is DISPUTED
OPEN in this same boundary** (`term=itemization.itemize._.choice.reason.md`), and its claimed sense
— *"name the members inline, in place of a count"* — is adjacent to what a bulletized summary does.

⇒ they are distinct as the dispute stands: `enumerate` names members inline within one sentence;
`bulletize` gives each its own line at a depth. if the `enumerate` dispute resolves in favor of a
`term=prose.enumerate`, that boundary should be restated in both clusters.
