# domain.term.choice.reason: converse

## .etymology

peer review in this domain is a **conversation**, not a verdict: a reviewer's `.given` critique
invites the driver's `.taken` reply. **converse** names that exchange from the driver's side — a
genuine written answer to a named party, not a rubber-stamp and not an internal act.

⇒ **the root is the argument.** `con-verse` and `con-verge` are both *vergere*, to turn:
turn-together and incline-together. the two rules that govern this loop are
`rule.always.converge-with-reviewers` and `rule.always.converge-to-terminal`. an act-word drawn from
that family reads as one vocabulary; one drawn from elsewhere needs a gloss at every use.

**the phrase that settles it: "converse to convergence."**

chosen over:
- `contemplate` — the **superseded canonical word**, and the reason it moved is the sharpest evidence
  on this page. it names a **solo, internal** act, so it cannot carry the second party the exchange
  requires. its own etymology file opened *"peer review in this domain is a conversation, not a
  verdict"* — it named the concept `conversation` and then chose a word for a different act
- `consider` / `reflect-on` — passive; they do not imply the written `.taken` the gate requires
- `address` / `respond` — mechanical; they read as *"reply sent"*, short of the engagement demanded
- `acknowledge` — mere receipt, the opposite of the required engagement

## .the state word — `unanswered`, and it deliberately does NOT share the root

the act is `converse`; the state a reviewer sits in until you perform it is **unanswered**.

| candidate | why not |
|---|---|
| `unconversed` | unreadable — a reader stumbles on it, which is the one failure a status word may not have |
| `unconverged` | 🔴 an **overload**. `converge` already names the whole-ladder outcome in `converge-to-terminal`; a second sense at reviewer scope re-creates the `terminal-for-unlock` vs `clear-for-passage` trap `define.invariant.review.peer.passage` records as a hazard |
| ✅ `unanswered` | plain; already the word `rule.forbid.unanswered-exits-from-a-blocker` carries in its own title; and it is exactly what the act leaves behind |

⇒ **a shared root is a nicety, never a requirement.** you converse **with** someone; until you do,
they are **unanswered**. the pursuit of a root-matched adjective is what produced the two rejects
above, and either would have been worse than the mismatch it cured.

## .disputes

the dispute that moved the word from `contemplate` is recorded in full at
`term=route.guard.review.contemplate._.choice.reason.md`, under **dispute: converse — 2026-09-07**.
it is kept there rather than copied here: that file is where a reader who knows only the old word
will look, and a resolved dispute belongs beside the word it retired.

## .evidence

- **the term needed a gloss wherever it appeared** — `howto.drive-routes` said *"reply to the
  review"*; `1.vision.yield.md` carries a whole their-words/our-words table whose first row is
  *"reply to the review"* → `contemplate`. ⇒ **a term that must be glossed at every use is the
  gloss's canonical word, unsurfaced**
- **the original enumeration omitted it** — the rejects on record were `consider`/`reflect-on`,
  `address`/`respond`, `acknowledge`. `converse` survives every counter that killed those AND adds
  the second party none of them carry ⇒ `rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name`
- invariant: the gate requires a `.taken` for every reviewer's LATEST peer critique with
  blockers > 0 — a 0-blocker (clean or nitpick-only) critique owes no `.taken`
- invariant: a reviewer at an overruled (forgiven) level owes no `.taken` — the human took
  responsibility, so the driver's duty is discharged for that level
