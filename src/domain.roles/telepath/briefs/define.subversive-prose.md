# define.subversive-prose

the worked demos behind `rule.forbid.subversive-prose`. the rule carries the ten shapes and the
test; this carries the passage-length pair the table cannot hold.

## .why a paired PASSAGE is owed beside the table

the rule's table grades **sentences**, one row per shape. three of the ten only appear at passage
length, so a table of sentences can name them and cannot show them:

| shape | why a sentence cannot demonstrate it |
|---|---|
| absolution | it answers a charge, and a charge needs a prior passage to be absent from |
| pre-emptive concession | it shields against a reviewer, so the reviewer's likely strike must be in view |
| humility as advocacy | the modest frame only advocates once there is a call for it to protect |

⇒ so one paired passage is the minimum demonstration, and both halves must make the same claim
about the same gap — otherwise the pair grades content rather than form.

## .the pair

### 👎 bad — six shapes, one paragraph, and no sentence an observation settles

> *the review engine guarantees the review happens; the reviewer judges whether it was real. this
> is not a case of a broken transport — the mechanism was never at fault. it does not buy us
> sincerity, of course, and I make no claim that it does. still, on balance, I judged the guarantee
> sufficient and took it. it should hold in most normal cases. ✅ read `case=8` first for the
> strongest form of the argument.*

in order: aphorism · absolution · pre-emptive concession · humility as advocacy · hedge stack ·
self-issued verdict + read-order directive.

🟡 70 words, grammatical, and no rule about brevity or rambles fires on it. each word is
individually defensible. **not one sentence names an observation that would settle it.**

### 🟡 the pair also demonstrates the SORT, and the two halves of it repair differently

| the sentence | sorts as | its repair |
|---|---|---|
| *"the mechanism was never at fault"* | shallow | cut it. no reader charged it, so there is naught beneath it to promote |
| *"it should hold in most normal cases"* | shallow | cut it, and state the bound: *"70%"* |
| *"the engine guarantees it happens; the reviewer judges if it was real"* | squishy | replace it with the mechanism: the engine never opens the verdict file |
| *"I judged the guarantee sufficient and took it"* | squishy | replace it with the confidence and the counter |

⇒ so a grade that read *"this paragraph is subversive"* and stopped would leave the author with four
defects and one instruction. the sort is what makes the grade actionable
(`rule.forbid.subversive-prose`'s sort column).

### 👍 good — the same gap, bulletized and disputable

> **the engine does not check whether a review was read**
> - `runStoneGuardReviews` tallies the verdict file and never opens it
>   - ⇒ a reviewer that emitted `0/0` with no read reads identical to one that did
> - taken at **70%**
>   - counter: no cheaper check exists short of a second reviewer
>   - what settles it: feed a reviewer a deliberately unread artifact

## .what the pair measures

**54 words against 70 — sixteen FEWER.** the subversive form was the longer one.

⇒ that is the measurement the rule rests on, and it runs against the intuition an author brings:
**a cut alone would not have found this passage.** compression is the diagnostic, never the cause
(`#429`, and `S11` for the ambiguity half).

## .the repair was a container, not a word

`rule.require.bulletize` did the work. the outline forced each claim onto its own line, and a
truism has no place to hide there — *"the mechanism was never at fault"* sits naked, with no
connective to carry it and no peer to borrow weight from.

⇒ so the two rules compose: **bulletize renders the shape; the forbid grades what sits at each
node.** an author who reaches for this rule and skips the outline judges a paragraph whose claims
are still fused.
