# case=8 · the flip-flop refused

- **coordinates** = `concede × disputed × live`
- **feel** = sharp · **care** = **alterpath** (chosen) · **verdict** = forbidden → demoed

**acceptance #1's third clause, and the one a sequence would defeat.** *"neither, **or both**, is
refused."* two separate commands cannot produce "both" in one call — they can produce it in two.

## .why an alterpath is demoed at all

`care = freq × cost`. a stance reversal within one given is **rare**; its cost is **high** — F09
records that it re-opens the escape `rule.forbid.unanswered-exits-from-a-blocker` exists to shut.
⇒ below the critipaths, well above the itemized floor, and it encodes a rejection invariant no other
case carries.

## .the narrative

*driver, on `5.1.execution`, one round after a dispute*

I disputed the architect. two commands later the ergonomist raises a blocker that makes me think the
architect may have been right after all. I reach for a concede.

```
$ rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as conceded --with architect --about blocker.1
```

```
🦉 patience, friend

🗿 route.stone.set --as conceded
   ├─ stone = 5.1.execution
   ├─ with  = architect
   ├─ about = blocker.1
   ├─ refused — architect's blocker.1 is already disputed
   │
   ├─ declared against .reviews/peer/…architect…given.md
   │  └─ why = .fulcrums/inventory.of=fulcrums.case=F007-file-placement.md
   │
   └─ a stance is final for its given
      ├─ change the artifact and re-arrive, then take a stance on the new given
      ├─ or ask a human to void it now
      │  └─ rhx route.stone.set --stone 5.1.execution --as rewound
      └─ or let the council rule on F007 at the close
```

so the dispute stands for this generation, and I add what I learned to the fulcrum entry instead —
where the council will read it beside the argument I made when I was confident.

🔴 **the refusal line costs TWO ledger reads, and `F018` is what keeps it at two.**

| the refusal prints | how it is obtained |
|---|---|
| **which** reviewer holds the stance | ✅ `reviewer?`, the new optional |
| **which generation** it answers | ✅ the `.given` path — invariant 3 already keys on it, so it is on hand |
| ✅ its **fulcrum** | ✅ **the `--why` argument, stored verbatim.** no derivation, no scan |

🟡 **it names the GIVEN, never a hash.** a driver-authored entry records no stone hash, and
`computeStoneReviewInputHash` recomputes the *current* one, never the one a past stance was declared
against. ⇒ the given is both derivable and the truer referent — it is what the stance **answers**,
where a hash is the artifact set the guard happened to read.

⚠️ **without `fulcrum?` this refusal would have to open `.fulcrums/` and scan every entry for a
reviewer match** — an entry filename is `case=F$n-$slug`, where `$slug` is the fulcrum's, never the
reviewer's. argued at `1.vision.yield.md` § *the fields the ledger does not have*.

**the aha:** *a record that flips records no claim. the council's whole value is that it reads a
judgment made at a moment, with the reason as it stood — and a stance I could revise on a whim is
not that.*

## .the bdd timeline

```gherkin
given a stone `5.1.execution` with peer reviewer `architect`, budget 4, rounds 2
  and the architect's latest given raises blocker.1 and blocker.2
  and blocker.1 is `disputed`                                     # B = disputed
  and the stance stores --why …case=F007-file-placement.md VERBATIM  # 🔴 the path IS the record

[t0] when the driver runs --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1 again
     with the SAME --why path
     then it is ADMITTED, idempotently                            # ✅ rule.require.idempotent-operations
      and no second passage report is appended
      and NOTHING is written to .fulcrums/                        # 🔴 F018 — the command never mints

[t1] given blocker.1 were `conceded` instead
     when the driver runs --as conceded --with architect --about blocker.1 again
     then it is ADMITTED, idempotently                            # ✅ the mirror

[t2] when the driver runs --as conceded --with architect --about blocker.1
     then it is REFUSED                                           # 🔴 R3 — the contrary stance
      and the refusal names the extant stance, its given, and its fulcrum
      and it names the driver's own fix FIRST                     # 🔴 S03 — edit, re-arrive, re-declare
      and a human rewind SECOND, for a driver who wants it void now

[t2b] when the driver runs --as conceded --with architect --about blocker.2
      then it is ADMITTED                                         # 🔴 a DIFFERENT concern is no flip-flop
       and the lane now holds one disputed and one conceded concern
       and R3 keys on (reviewer, concern, given), never on the lane   # 🔴 S07 narrows the invariant

[t3] given blocker.1 were `conceded`
     when the driver runs --as disputed --with architect --about blocker.1
     then it is REFUSED IDENTICALLY                               # ✅ R3 is symmetric, exit included
      and it names the same fix: change the artifact, re-arrive   # ⇒ a new given re-opens the slot ([t5])

[t4] given the architect is `disputed` and the driver wants it re-read NOW
     when a human runs --as rewound
     then the stance clears and the lane is live again            # ✅ delStoneGuardArtifacts DELETES the givens
      and the peer meters reset to 0 rounds                       # ✅ resetRouteStoneGuardReviewPeerMeters
      and the `disputed` row STAYS in passage.jsonl               # 🔴 the ledger is append-only
      and the fulcrum entry STAYS, and naught marks it            # 🟡 hygiene, never correctness — see below

[t5] given a lane the driver took ANY stance on, then fixed, then re-arrived
     when the lane runs and raises a NEW blocker
     then a NEW .given lands and its stance slot is OPEN          # F09 — across givens it is free
      and the driver may take either stance on this one           # ✅ S03 — both lanes reach here now
```

🔴 **the `given` line keys the stance to a GIVEN, and a reader who reaches for the hash builds the
wrong design.** the debt is keyed by `getLatestPeerGivensPerSlug.ts:44-50` — `iteration`, then
`pathGiven`, explicitly *"across all hashes"*, so it **survives an edit to the artifact under
review**. a stance keyed to the hash would **lapse the moment any artifact moved**, whether or not
that lane had spoken again. ⇒ **the given key is what makes `S03`'s per-generation lapse fall out by
construction**: the lane speaks, a new given lands, the old stance no longer covers it.

🔴 **`[t0]` and `[t2]` together are the invariant.** the same command is idempotent when it agrees
and refused when it contradicts. a design that refused **both** would break
`rule.require.idempotent-operations`; one that admitted both would make acceptance #1's *"both is
refused"* unenforceable.

🔴 **and `[t2b]` is the bound `S07` put on that invariant: R3 keys on `(reviewer, concern, given)`.**
a lane-keyed R3 would refuse the commonest honest act there is — **agree with one point and argue
another** — which `case=3`'s driver performs on its own three blockers. ⇒ **the per-concern grain
does not merely refine the tally; it decides which second stance is a FLIP-FLOP and which is a
second opinion.**

🔴 **`[t0]`'s third line is `F018` in one clause: the command writes NOTHING to `.fulcrums/`.** an
earlier design minted the entry, so `[t0]` had to specify a **findsert** — and a findsert then owed
a key, which owed an argument about what happens after a rewind. **the driver authors the entry
now**, so the idempotence question never reaches the filesystem at all: two identical declarations
name the same path, and a path is idempotent by construction.

## ✅ .R3 is ONE rule with ONE message

**the refusal is symmetric and so is the EXIT**, because `S03`'s per-generation lapse returns every
lane:

| the move | the extant stance | can an artifact edit re-open the slot? |
|---|---|---|
| `[t2]` `concede × disputed` | **disputed** | ✅ **yes, since `S03`.** the lane returns next generation and mints a new given |
| `[t3]` `dispute × conceded` | **conceded** | ✅ **yes.** a conceded lane re-runs — it always could (`[t5]`) |

⇒ **one message, and it names a fix the driver can perform in either direction:** *change the
artifact, re-arrive, and take a stance on the new given* — with the human rewind kept as the second
line, for a driver who wants the stance void **now** rather than next round.

⇒ **a message that will not collapse across two cells is a real signal — and it points at the
DESIGN, not necessarily at the class.** the asymmetry here sat one axis away from the refusal class
everyone was inspecting, and it dissolved when that axis was re-ruled.

## .why it fails safe

**fast** — refused at the write, before any state moves · **loud** — names the extant stance, the
given it was declared against, and the fulcrum that holds it · **teaches the fix** — two ways
forward, and 🔴 **the first is the driver's own** (edit, re-arrive, re-declare), with the human
rewind kept for a driver who wants it void this round.

✅ **no deadlock.** the driver edits the artifact, the lane returns, and the stance lapses on its own.
⇒ **`F09` asks the narrow question that survives** — not *"is there an exit?"* but *"may a driver
revoke a stance **without a round**?"* — at 72%.

## 🔴 .what `[t4]`'s rewind LEAVES, and why the skip predicate turns on it

`rewindAffectedStones.ts` does four things per stone, and only the first two touch a stance:
`delStoneGuardArtifacts` (reviews, judges, promises, triggers) · `resetRouteStoneGuardReviewPeerMeters`
(*"fresh budget"*) · a yield archive · then `setPassageReport({ status: 'rewound' })` — an **append**.

✅ **so the stance clears for a reason this case owes its reader: the givens are DELETED.** invariant
3 keys a stance to the slug's latest given, and after a rewind there is none. ⇒ **the skip predicate
must be scoped to the current given**, never to `(stone, slug)` — an append-only ledger would answer
*"is the architect disputed?"* **yes** forever, and the lane would stay dark past its own undo.

✅ **the fulcrum survives the rewind, and under `F018` that is HYGIENE rather than correctness.**
there is no `.fulcrums/` cleanup in that operation, so the entry and its summary row persist
unmarked. **naught performs that mark.**

⇒ a council reads a live-seeming row for a dispute that no longer stands (`case=7`). the repair is a
**`rewound` state** on the entry — a `fulcrum.set --status` job, and the operation does not exist
(`.dream/v2026_09_09.entool.a-fulcrum-inventory-has-no-operation.md`). `F17` carries the row.

🟡 **the correctness half is MOOT, and it was the larger one.** under a mint, `[t0]`'s findsert would
**find the stale entry**, so a new dispute post-rewind would be filed under the dead rationale. there
is no findsert and no key — the driver names the path.
